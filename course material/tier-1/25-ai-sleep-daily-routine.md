# AI for Better Sleep & Daily Routine Planning

**Overview**

Your days are full — work, family, the Nairobi commute — and by night you are tired but wired. This course shows you how to use AI to design a daily routine that fits your real life, build small habits and reminders that stick, and find what is quietly wrecking your sleep and focus. Your win: two realistic changes this week, not a perfect life by Monday.

**Who this is for**

- Anyone whose commute and work schedule leave no time for themselves
- People who sleep badly and do not know why
- Those who keep failing at "new year routines" because they were too big
- Parents juggling family, work, and side hustles
- Night owls who want a calmer evening without rewriting their whole life

**What you'll be able to do**

- Design a realistic daily and weekly routine around work and family
- Build one or two habits that stick instead of ten that die
- Set up simple reminders and check-ins with AI's help
- Identify what is disrupting your sleep and your focus
- Make small changes and measure them without an app addiction

## Lesson 1: Build a routine around your real life, not an ideal one

Most routines fail because they start from fantasy — 5am runs, perfect meals, eight hours of sleep. Real Nairobi days have traffic, rush hours, and children who get sick. Start from reality.

Give AI your real constraints: when you wake, your commute time, work hours, family duties, and when you actually get home. Ask for a routine that fits the gaps, not one that rearranges your life. The best routine is the one you can follow on a bad day.

## Lesson 2: The commute is part of the plan

For many Kenyans, matatu or boda boda time is two to three hours a day. That is either dead time or useful time. AI can help you decide:

- Use part of the commute for low-energy tasks (replies, reading, learning)
- Or use it as deliberate downtime so you arrive less stressed
- Plan your "leave work" time backwards from a sleep goal, not forwards from the office

A commute is draining even when you are sitting. Budget some of it as rest, not guilt.

## Lesson 3: Habits that actually stick

Habit science is simple: start tiny, attach it to something you already do, and make it daily. Instead of "exercise more", try "after locking up, walk around the estate for 10 minutes." Instead of "stop using my phone at night", try "plug the charger outside the bedroom."

Ask AI to design 1–2 habits only, with the exact trigger, the tiny action, and the reminder. Two habits a month is a win. Twelve habits a year changes your life. One impossible list changes nothing.

## Lesson 4: Find what is disrupting your sleep

Common sleep thieves in Kenyan life: late-night phone scrolling, the WhatsApp group that never sleeps, caffeine after 4pm, irregular bedtimes (especially weekends), and the habit of working or thinking about work in bed.

AI can run a simple audit: answer its questions about your evenings, screen time, food, and stress, and it will flag likely culprits. Then pick ONE change — for example, a fixed bedtime, or no phone for the last 30 minutes of the day — and keep it for two weeks before adding another.

## Lesson 5: Realistic changes and simple tracking

Keep tracking boring on purpose: a weekly check-in with AI instead of a fancy app. Every Sunday, answer three questions — how was my sleep this week, what is the one thing I changed, what do I do next week. AI keeps the record and spots patterns.

Remember: sleep and health information from AI is general guidance, not medical advice. If you have ongoing sleep problems, see a professional. AI is your planner, not your doctor.

## Prompt Library

**Build a realistic daily routine**

Use when you want a routine that fits your actual schedule.

```
Design a realistic daily routine for me. My day: wake at [TIME], commute
[MINUTES] to [PLACE] by [MATATU/BODA], work [HOURS] at [TYPE OF JOB], commute
back, family duties in the evening [DESCRIBE], and I currently sleep around
[TIME]. Build a routine that protects [X HOURS] of sleep, keeps 30 minutes of
personal time, and assumes a bad-traffic day happens. No 5am workouts unless I
already do that. Keep it simple.
```

**Weekly schedule planner**

Use to see your whole week at a glance.

```
Create a weekly schedule from my inputs: work days [DAYS], commute [MINUTES],
family commitments [LIST], side project [HOURS], and one rest evening. Block out:
sleep windows, commute, work, family time, and 2 short habit slots. Format it as
a simple day-by-day list I can copy into Google Calendar.
```

**One habit design**

Use when you keep failing at big goals and need something tiny.

```
Design ONE habit for me that is ridiculously small. My goal is [E.G. sleep by
11pm / walk daily / less phone at night]. Give me: the exact trigger (something I
already do), the 5-minute action, and a reminder setup. Explain why starting this
small makes it stick. I will add a second habit only after this one is steady
for 2 weeks.
```

**Sleep audit questions**

Use when you are not sure what is wrecking your sleep.

```
Interview me about my sleep with 8 short questions: bedtime, wake time, phone use
in bed, caffeine and food timing, stress, noise and light in my room, weekend
patterns, and how I feel on waking. After I answer, list my top 3 likely sleep
disruptors and suggest ONE small change for each. Note that this is general
guidance, not medical advice.
```

**Evening wind-down routine**

Use to build a calm end to the day.

```
Design a 45-minute evening wind-down routine for someone who gets home around
[TIME] and wants to sleep at [TIME]. Include: stopping work thoughts, a phone-free
last 30 minutes, a small reading or music option, and a consistent "lights out"
signal. Keep every step realistic for a Kenyan household, including shared rooms
if needed.
```

**Commute time plan**

Use to turn matatu or boda boda time into something useful or restful.

```
My commute is [MINUTES] each way by [MATATU/BODA]. Design a commute plan: half my
mornings for low-energy learning or replies, half for rest and music, and evenings
for mentally switching off before I get home. Include one tip for protecting my
phone battery and one for arriving less stressed.
```

**Identify productivity blockers**

Use when you feel busy but achieve little.

```
Ask me 6 questions about my typical workday: when I feel most and least focused,
how often I check my phone or WhatsApp, how I start my morning, my biggest
interruptions, how I end the day, and my energy by hour. After I answer, identify
my top 3 productivity leaks and suggest one 15-minute fix for each.
```

**Weekly check-in**

Use every Sunday to review progress without an app.

```
This is my weekly check-in. My last change was: [DESCRIBE]. My sleep this week
was: [BRIEF]. My energy was: [BRIEF]. What went well, what slipped, and what is
the ONE thing I adjust next week? Keep your answer to 5 sentences and remind me
to celebrate the small win.
```

**Weekend routine that protects the week**

Use so weekends do not wreck your routine.

```
Design a weekend plan for someone who wants to rest but not undo the week's
progress: keep a similar wake-up time (within 1 hour), protect one rest morning,
plan one social activity, and set a Sunday evening wind-down that prepares for
Monday. Account for church or family commitments if I add them. Keep it flexible.
```

**Caffeine and food timing check**

Use when late-night energy is keeping you wired.

```
I usually take [COFFEE/CHAI] at [TIMES] and eat dinner around [TIME]. Explain how
caffeine timing affects my 11pm sleep goal, and suggest a realistic switch (for
example, last chai by [TIME], lighter dinner). Give me one change to try this week
and note this is general wellness guidance, not medical advice.
```

## Action plan

1. Open ChatGPT and run the "build a realistic daily routine" prompt with your real times.
2. Pick ONE habit from the routine — not all of them.
3. Run the sleep audit and choose one sleep fix to try for two weeks.
4. Set up a simple phone reminder or calendar block for the habit trigger.
5. Use the commute plan on your worst traffic day this week.
6. Do the Sunday weekly check-in prompt.
7. Adjust one thing next week. Repeat until two habits are steady.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Designing a routine for an imaginary, traffic-free life
- Starting ten habits at once and quitting all of them
- Using phone time as "rest" while claiming the phone is the problem
- Rewriting the whole week because one day went wrong
- Ignoring real sleep problems while self-diagnosing with AI
- Checking sleep progress obsessively every night
