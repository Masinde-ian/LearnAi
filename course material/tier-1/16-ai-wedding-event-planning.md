# AI for Wedding & Event Planning Basics

**Overview** — This course helps you plan a wedding or event like a pro using AI: build a day-of checklist and timeline, write invitations, programs, and MC scripts, budget with AI, and message vendors without awkwardness. The one clear win: a clear plan and budget before you pay anyone a deposit.

**Who this is for**

- Couples planning their own wedding on a budget
- Family members or friends helping organise a wedding or graduation
- First-time event organisers for church, chama, or community events
- Anyone who has been quoted "the market price" and does not know if it is fair

**What you'll be able to do**

- Build a day-of event checklist and timeline with AI
- Write invitations, programs, and MC scripts fast
- Budget a wedding or event and compare cost options
- Message vendors professionally (caterers, decorators, MCs, photographers)

## Lessons

### Lesson 1: Start with the plan, not the venue

Most events fail because people book the venue first and plan later. Start with the details AI can help you pin down: the number of guests, the date, the location, and the budget ceiling. From there, AI can generate the full checklist of what a Kenyan wedding or event needs — venue, catering per head, decor, MC, photographer, transport, and the small extras people forget (chairs, canopies, MC's PA system, offering or gifts, thank-you cards). Decide your guest count first. Everything else is priced per head.

### Lesson 2: The day-of checklist and timeline

Ask AI to build a timeline from morning to evening. A typical day-of structure looks like this:

- Morning: setup, caterer arrives, decor, sound check
- Midday: guests arrive, MC opens, program starts
- Afternoon: vows or ceremony, speeches, cutting the cake
- Evening: dinner, dancing, photos, packing up

Give AI your real times and it will slot each task in. Print the timeline and give one copy to the MC, one to the caterer, and one to a trusted relative who knows the family. The timeline is what saves you from "where is the MC?" panic calls.

### Lesson 3: Invitations, programs, and MC scripts

AI writes the words; you supply the details. For invitations, give names, date, venue, dress code, and RSVP contact. For the program, give the order of events and names. For the MC, give the flow and ask AI to write opening lines, transitions, and a warm closing — but keep the MC's jokes and personality in the MC's voice. Do the same for speeches if you want, but never make AI write a speech that is supposed to be personal; ask for an outline and fill in your own memories.

### Lesson 4: Budgeting and comparing cost options

Wedding costs in Kenya vary wildly by venue and town, so use ranges and never trust a single quote. Ask AI to build a budget table with columns: item, low estimate, high estimate, and notes. Then get at least two real quotes per vendor and feed them into AI to compare. A common pattern: venue and catering eat 50-60% of the budget. If you are over, ask AI what to cut first without ruining the day — usually favours, extra decor, and video packages.

### Lesson 5: Vendor communication templates

Many deals are agreed over WhatsApp. AI can write you professional templates: a request for a quote, a follow-up, a confirmation, and a polite decline. Always confirm price, what is included, and the payment schedule in writing before paying a deposit via M-Pesa. Keep the receipts and the chat history — they are your proof if there is a dispute.

## Prompt Library

**Event master plan**

**How to use it:** Start here, replace details in brackets.

```
I am planning a [wedding/graduation/event] for [number] guests in [town] on [date]. Budget is about KES [amount]. Build a master plan: what to book first, a complete checklist of items and vendors, and a rough timeline from 3 months before to the day. Keep it readable on a phone.
```

**Day-of timeline**

**How to use it:** Get a minute-by-minute running order.

```
Create a day-of timeline for my [event type] starting at [time] and ending at [time]. Include setup, guest arrival, MC opening, ceremony, speeches, cake, dinner, photos, and packing up. Leave room for delays. Keep it in a simple list format.
```

**Invitation message**

**How to use it:** Fill in your details and send as text or card.

```
Write a wedding invitation for [names] on [date] at [venue, town], starting [time]. Include a short joyful line, RSVP to [name/phone], and a note on dress code [dress code]. Keep it warm and under 120 words.
```

**Program order**

**How to use it:** Paste your event order for a clean program.

```
Turn this event order into a clean printed program with numbered items and times: [list order]. Format it so it fits a small card. Keep names and details exactly as I wrote them.
```

**MC opening script**

**How to use it:** Personalise the tone in the first line.

```
Write an MC opening for a [event type] in a warm Kenyan style: welcome the guests, introduce the couple/celebrant, state the day's flow, and hand over to the first item. Keep it 3 short paragraphs. Do not add jokes — the MC will add their own.
```

**Budget table**

**How to use it:** Get a compare-friendly budget sheet.

```
Build a wedding budget table for [number] guests in [town]. Columns: item, low estimate, high estimate, notes. Include venue, catering per head, decor, MC, photographer, transport, dresses, cake, and extras. Use ranges like "from about KES X" — never invent exact prices.
```

**Compare two vendor quotes**

**How to use it:** Paste both quotes and get a decision aid.

```
Compare these two quotes for [service, e.g. catering]. Tell me which is better value, what questions I should ask each vendor before paying, and whether the difference is worth it. Quote A: [paste] Quote B: [paste]
```

**What to cut if over budget**

**How to use it:** When the total exceeds your ceiling.

```
My wedding budget is KES [amount] and the total is about KES [amount]. List 5 ways to bring it down, ordered from least painful to most, and say what should never be cut. Keep it practical.
```

**Vendor enquiry message**

**How to use it:** Send to a vendor you have not contacted yet.

```
Write a WhatsApp message to ask a [caterer/decorator/MC] for a quote for my [event] on [date] for [number] guests at [venue]. Ask for: price, what is included, and availability. Be polite, clear, and professional. Keep under 100 words.
```

**Vendor confirmation**

**How to use it:** Lock in a verbal or WhatsApp deal in writing.

```
Turn these agreed details into a confirmation message I can send to my vendor: [paste details, e.g. price, date, deliverables, deposit]. Include a line asking them to confirm in writing, and note the M-Pesa deposit of KES [amount]. Professional and friendly.
```

**Polite decline**

**How to use it:** When you choose another vendor.

```
Write a polite WhatsApp message declining a [vendor type] after getting quotes, thanking them for their time, and leaving the door open for future events. No false promises. Under 80 words.
```

## Action plan

1. Set your guest count and total budget ceiling today.
2. Run the master plan prompt and pick the top 3 things to book first.
3. Build the budget table and get two real quotes for each major vendor.
4. Generate the day-of timeline and share it with your planner or MC.
5. Write invitations and the program.
6. Confirm all bookings in writing before paying deposits.

## Quick wins checklist

- I have a guest count and a budget ceiling
- I have a master plan and a day-of timeline
- My budget table compares at least two quotes per vendor
- My invitations and program are written
- My MC has a script and the day's running order
- All major vendors are confirmed in writing before deposits

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Booking the venue before deciding the guest count and date
- Trusting a single quote — always compare two or three
- Paying deposits without a written confirmation on WhatsApp
- Forgetting small line items (chairs, canopy, PA, transport, thank-yous)
- Letting the MC improvise a timeline nobody shared with them
- Letting AI write personal speeches and stories that only you know
