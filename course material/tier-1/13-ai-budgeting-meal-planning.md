# AI for Budgeting & Meal Planning on a Tight Income

**Overview**

Living on a tight income means every shilling has to work twice. This course uses AI as a budgeting assistant to build a simple weekly budget, plan affordable balanced meals around Kenyan staples, and track spending without fancy apps. You'll find ways to cut recurring costs and keep more money at the end of the month.

**Who this is for**

- Anyone paid weekly or monthly who runs out of money before payday
- Parents planning meals for a family on a tight budget
- People who find spreadsheets and budgeting apps too complicated

**What you'll be able to do**

- Build a simple weekly budget using AI as your assistant
- Plan affordable balanced meals with Kenyan staples
- Track spending without complicated apps or spreadsheets
- Cut costs on recurring expenses with AI suggestions

## Lesson 1: Build a weekly budget with AI

A budget is just a plan for your money. You give AI your income and fixed costs; it helps you divide the rest before you spend it.

Simple structure:

- **Income** — what comes in (salary, hustle income, M-Pesa from family)
- **Fixed costs** — rent, school fees, transport, M-Pesa loans/savings
- **Flexible** — food, airtime, personal, emergency

**Do this:** be honest about numbers. A budget that ignores your real spending is a plan you'll break by Wednesday.

**Not this:** budgeting a whole month if you're paid weekly — weekly budgets match weekly income better.

## Lesson 2: Meal planning around Kenyan staples

Eating well on a tight budget is about staples plus variety, not expensive ingredients. A balanced cheap plate has:

- **Starch:** ugali, rice, githeri
- **Protein:** beans, eggs, omena, ndengu, affordable cuts of meat or fish
- **Vegetables:** sukuma wiki, cabbage, kales, spinach, seasonal veg

AI can build a weekly meal plan, generate shopping lists, and even swap ingredients for what's cheap this season. Tell it your budget per day and how many people it's feeding.

**Do this:** plan meals before shopping. You buy less, waste less, and eat better.

**Not this:** asking for "healthy meals" with no budget — you'll get recipes using expensive ingredients.

## Lesson 3: Track spending without apps

You don't need a spreadsheet. The simplest tracking system is a WhatsApp chat to yourself or a note in your phone.

Every time you spend, type it. Once a week, ask AI to help you read the list and spot leaks.

**Do this:** add every M-Pesa deduction and cash purchase. Small "invisible" spending — airtime, snacks, kiosk shopping — is where the leaks hide.

**Not this:** relying on memory. You will forget the 50 shillings here and 200 there.

## Lesson 4: Cut recurring costs with AI

Recurring costs are the ones you pay every month without thinking: airtime bundles, data, subscriptions, transport, electricity, and loans. AI is great at reviewing a list and suggesting cuts.

Common Kenyan wins:

- Bundled data and airtime instead of pay-as-you-go
- Grouping trips to save on transport
- Negotiating or switching SACCO loans and insurance
- Buying staples in bulk with a chama or a neighbour

**Do this:** list every recurring cost and ask AI to rank the top 5 cuts with the biggest savings.

**Not this:** cutting food budget below what the family needs to stay healthy.

## Prompt Library

Copy, fill in the blanks, and adapt.

**Weekly budget from your numbers**

Use to build your first budget.

```
I earn KES [amount] per week. My fixed costs are: rent [X], transport [X], school [X], other [X]. Help me build a weekly budget. Divide the rest into food, airtime, savings, and emergency. Keep it simple, and tell me how much I can spend per day.
```

**Review my real spending**

Use at the end of the week with your spending list.

```
Here is everything I spent this week in KES: [paste list]. Sort it into categories, tell me my top 3 leaks, and suggest 3 specific ways to reduce spending next week without cutting food.
```

**Where did my money go?**

Use when money runs out before payday.

```
I get KES [amount] a month and I always run out before payday. Ask me the 5 questions you need to build a realistic budget (income, fixed costs, biggest unexpected costs, savings, loans), then build the budget with my answers.
```

**Emergency fund goal**

Use to start saving even on a tight income.

```
I can save KES [amount] per week. Set me a realistic emergency fund target for a family in [city, e.g. Nairobi] and tell me how long it will take. Suggest where to keep it so I'm not tempted (e.g. M-Pesa savings, SACCO shares, a chama).
```

**Weekly meal plan on a budget**

Use to plan family meals.

```
Build a 7-day meal plan for a family of [X] in Kenya on a food budget of KES [amount] per week. Use affordable staples: ugali, rice, beans, sukuma, cabbage, eggs, and cheap proteins like omena and ndengu. Breakfast, lunch, and dinner each day, plus a shopping list.
```

**Shopping list from the meal plan**

Use before you go to market.

```
Turn this meal plan into a shopping list with quantities for a week for [X] people: [paste meal plan]. Group it by market sections (vegetables, dry goods, meat/fish) and suggest which items to buy in bulk.
```

**Swap expensive ingredients**

Use when a recipe is too costly.

```
This recipe uses [ingredient] which is too expensive for me. Suggest a cheaper Kenyan alternative that keeps the meal balanced: [paste recipe]
```

**Pack lunches affordably**

Use to cut daily food spending.

```
Give me 5 cheap packed-lunch ideas for [adult/child] using leftovers, ugali, rice, beans, eggs, and whatever was on sale. Each under KES [amount] per serving.
```

**Cut recurring costs**

Use to find monthly savings.

```
Here are my monthly recurring costs in KES: [list, e.g. airtime 500, data 1000, transport 3000, subscriptions 0, electricity 2000]. Rank the top 5 ways to reduce them, with an estimate of monthly savings and effort level (easy/medium/hard). No cutting food below a healthy level.
```

**Airtime and data bundles**

Use to stop overpaying on airtime.

```
I spend about KES [X] a month on airtime and data. Recommend the best way to cut it in Kenya: bundles vs pay-as-you-go, and what to check on my network's app. Keep it practical, no unrealistic claims.
```

## Action plan

1. Write down your exact weekly income.
2. List all fixed costs — check your M-Pesa statement to be accurate.
3. Build your first weekly budget with AI.
4. Start a WhatsApp-chat-to-self or notes tracker for every spend.
5. Plan one week of meals and shop from the list.
6. At week's end, paste spending into AI and find 3 cuts.
7. Apply the biggest recurring-cost cut.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Budgeting on income you don't actually receive
- Planning meals without a budget or shopping list
- Forgetting cash purchases in tracking
- Cutting food or transport below what you need
- Treating a weekly budget like a monthly one
- Ignoring small daily spending that adds up
