# AI for Song Lyrics & Music Ideas

**Overview** — This course helps you generate lyric ideas and song structures with AI, beat writer's block on a verse or hook, stay original by not copying existing songs, and turn your lyrics into a rough demo using simple tools like Suno and Udio. The one clear win: you go from a blank page to a rough demo of your own song in one session.

**Who this is for**

- Aspiring songwriters who never know where to start
- Gospel, gengetone, and afro-pop fans who want to try making music
- Choir or praise-team members writing original pieces
- Anyone curious about AI music tools but scared of the copyright side

**What you'll be able to do**

- Generate lyric ideas and song structures with AI
- Beat writer's block on a verse or hook
- Understand originality and avoid copying existing songs
- Turn lyrics into a rough demo with Suno or Udio

## Lessons

### Lesson 1: Starting with structure

Every song has bones: verse, chorus (hook), verse, bridge, outro. Before writing anything, decide your song's shape. Tell AI the genre and mood and ask it to sketch the structure with the number of lines per section. For example, a gengetone track might use a short repeated hook and quick verses; a gospel worship song uses longer verses and a big chorus. Once the skeleton exists, filling it in is much easier than staring at a blank page.

### Lesson 2: Writing with AI as a co-writer

AI is best at giving you raw material, not finished masterpieces. Ask it for ten lines or a first draft of a verse on your topic, then pick what works, change the rest, and add your own memories and feelings. The human touches — a line your friend actually said, a place you know, a story from your life — are what make a song yours. Never submit AI's first draft as your final lyric; the best songs come from you and AI going back and forth.

### Lesson 3: Beating writer's block

Stuck on a hook or a verse? Give AI the one line you already have, even if it feels weak, and ask it to continue in the same style. Ask for five different options for a hook, or ask it to try a different rhyme scheme or a Kiswahili-English mix (very common in Kenyan music). Write something terrible on purpose — AI can help fix it. The goal is momentum, not perfection in one shot.

### Lesson 4: Originality and copyright

Here is the most important lesson: never ask AI to copy an existing song, and do not copy one yourself. Asking AI to "write a song like [artist]" produces something close to their work, which creates real legal and moral problems. Make your own songs, from your own ideas, and use AI only as a helper. If you are working with someone else's lyrics, you need their permission. If AI generated the melody and you plan to release the song commercially, check the tool's terms about who owns the output. Staying original keeps you safe and makes your music actually yours.

### Lesson 5: From lyrics to a rough demo

Once your lyrics are in good shape, you can turn them into a rough demo. **Suno** and **Udio** both have free tiers that generate full songs from your lyrics and a style prompt. Give them your written lyrics plus a one-line description of the sound ("upbeat Kenyan gengetone with a strong female vocal"). Listen, pick the best take, and save it. This is a demo, not a finished production — use it to test ideas, share with your producer, or post online. For release and distribution, tools like DistroKid, Boomplay, Audiomack, or Mdundo come later; this course stops at the demo.

## Prompt Library

**Song structure**

**How to use it:** Start every session here.

```
Create a song structure for a [genre] song about [topic]. Use this shape: intro, verse 1, chorus, verse 2, bridge, chorus, outro. Tell me how many lines each section should be and what each section should accomplish emotionally.
```

**First verse draft**

**How to use it:** Give AI your topic and a feeling.

```
Write a first draft of a verse for a [genre] song about [topic/feeling]. Keep the language natural and simple, leave one or two lines slightly incomplete so I can finish them with my own words.
```

**Hook ideas**

**How to use it:** When you need a strong, repeatable chorus.

```
Give me 5 different hook (chorus) ideas for a song about [topic], in a [genre] style. Each hook should be short, catchy, and easy to remember. Vary them: one short, one with a question, one in a Kiswahili-English mix.
```

**Overcome writer's block**

**How to use it:** Paste the line you are stuck on.

```
I am stuck on this line and need to continue the song: "[your line]". Continue it in the same mood and rhyme scheme, and give me two different directions the next lines could take.
```

**Verse from a real moment**

**How to use it:** Make it personal with your own memory.

```
Turn this memory into song lyrics: [describe a real moment, e.g. the day I left home for Nairobi]. Write it as a verse in a [genre] style, keeping it honest and simple.
```

**Kiswahili-English mix**

**How to use it:** The everyday Kenyan style.

```
Write a verse for a song mixing Kiswahili and English naturally, the way Kenyans speak. Topic: [topic]. Keep it catchy and make sure both languages flow together.
```

**Rewrite and improve**

**How to use it:** Polish a rough draft.

```
Here is a rough lyric draft of mine: [paste]. Keep my core ideas and improve the flow, rhythm, and word choice without changing the meaning. Point out the 2 strongest lines and the 1 weakest.
```

**Originality check**

**How to use it:** Before you commit to lyrics.

```
Read these lyrics and tell me if they sound too similar to any well-known Kenyan or international song, or if any phrase is copied word for word. If risky, suggest how to change it. Lyrics: [paste]
```

**Demo style prompt**

**How to use it:** Feed this style line into Suno or Udio.

```
Write a one-line style prompt for an AI music tool to produce: [describe your song, e.g. a mellow gospel worship ballad with piano, a high-energy gengetone beat]. Keep it under 20 words and describe the feel, not the lyrics.
```

**Release checklist later**

**How to use it:** Save for when the demo is ready to release.

```
List what I need to check before releasing a song I made with AI lyrics and an AI-generated demo: ownership terms of the tool, original lyrics confirmation, and platforms like DistroKid, Boomplay, Audiomack, or Mdundo. Keep it as a simple checklist.
```

## Action plan

1. Choose a topic and run the song structure prompt.
2. Draft your first verse — write at least half the lines yourself.
3. Pick a hook from the five ideas and make it yours.
4. Do an originality check on your lyrics before proceeding.
5. Run your style prompt and lyrics through Suno or Udio's free tier.
6. Save your best demo and share it with someone you trust for feedback.

## Quick wins checklist

- I have a song structure I can work from
- I wrote a verse and a hook that are genuinely mine
- I beat writer's block at least once with AI's help
- My lyrics passed the originality check
- I generated a rough demo with Suno or Udio
- I know what to check before any future release

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Copying an existing song or asking AI to mimic one
- Releasing AI's first draft unchanged as your final lyrics
- Using someone else's lyrics without permission
- Ignoring the tool's ownership terms before releasing commercially
- Fixating on making one perfect line instead of finishing the song
- Judging your first demo against finished studio tracks
