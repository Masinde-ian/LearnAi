# AI for Blogging & Content Writing Basics

**Overview**

AI can turn one rough idea into a full blog post — but if you paste its draft straight up, readers will know. This course shows you how to use AI for outlines, headlines, and keywords, and how to edit the draft so it sounds like you wrote it. You'll walk away able to publish posts people actually want to read.

**Who this is for**

- Kenyans who want to start a blog about their business or passion
- Writers who want to write more, faster, without losing their voice
- Anyone posting long content on Facebook, LinkedIn, or a website

**What you'll be able to do**

- Turn an idea into a full blog post outline with AI
- Write in your own voice instead of generic AI-sounding text
- Use AI for titles, headings, and basic SEO keywords
- Edit AI drafts so they don't read as obviously AI-written

## Lesson 1: From idea to outline

The fastest way to use AI is not "write me an article." It's "help me plan an article." A good outline saves you hours and keeps the post focused.

Give AI:

- The topic
- Who it's for
- What the reader should be able to do after reading

Ask for: a working title, an intro angle, 4–6 subheadings, and one takeaway per section.

**Do this:** review the outline first. Delete anything you don't know or don't care about — you'll write those sections poorly anyway.

**Not this:** letting AI pick topics you know nothing about. Your best posts come from your own experience.

## Lesson 2: Write in your own voice

Generic AI text sounds like it was written by a committee: perfect grammar, no personality, and phrases like "in today's fast-paced world." Your Kenyan readers want the opposite.

Ways to keep your voice:

- Write or talk about the topic first, then use AI to clean it up
- Feed AI examples of how you actually write
- Add your opinions, stories, and local examples AI can't know

**Do this:** tell AI "write this the way a Nairobi shop owner talks, not a textbook."

**Not this:** publishing first drafts with AI's default "in conclusion" closers.

## Lesson 3: SEO basics AI can help with

SEO just means helping people find your post when they search. The basics:

- **One keyword focus** per post (what people actually type, e.g. "best phone under 20k in Kenya")
- **Title with the keyword** and an H1 heading
- **H2 subheadings** that break the post into readable sections
- Short paragraphs — phone readers scroll fast

AI is great at generating keyword ideas, titles, and heading options. It's bad at knowing exactly what Kenyans search — check your own guesses against what people around you actually ask.

**Do this:** before writing, ask AI for 10 title options with your keyword. Pick the one a real person would click.

**Not this:** stuffing the same keyword into every sentence. It reads badly and helps nothing.

## Lesson 4: Edit so it doesn't read as AI

The rule: AI drafts, you finish. A quick edit pass catches the tell-tale signs.

Check for:

- Opening cliches ("In today's digital age")
- Perfect, robotic rhythm — break up long sentences
- "Furthermore / moreover / in conclusion" connectors
- No mention of Kenya, M-Pesa, matatus, real places, or real prices

**Do this:** read the draft out loud. Anywhere you stumble, rewrite that sentence in your own words.

**Not this:** publishing the draft as-is because "it's good enough." The editing is where your voice appears.

## Prompt Library

Copy, fill in the blanks, and adapt.

**Idea to outline**

Use at the start of every post.

```
I want to write a blog post about [topic, e.g. starting a small vegetable business in Kenya]. The reader is [audience, e.g. a first-time seller]. Give me a working title, an intro angle, 5 subheadings, and one key takeaway per section. Keep it practical, not academic.
```

**10 title options with a keyword**

Use when choosing a headline.

```
Give me 10 blog post titles for a post about [topic]. The keyword is "[keyword]". Make titles specific and clickable, not clickbait. Kenya context welcome. No emojis.
```

**Rewrite in my voice**

Use when the AI draft sounds like a robot.

```
Rewrite this paragraph in the voice of [yourself: e.g. a 30-year-old shop owner in Nakuru who writes short, direct sentences]. Keep all facts the same. Remove cliches and academic words: [paste paragraph]
```

**Keyword ideas**

Use to find what people might search for.

```
Suggest 8 keyword phrases a Kenyan might type into Google about [topic, e.g. cheap weekend trips from Nairobi]. Keep phrases how people actually speak, not marketing jargon.
```

**H2 headings**

Use to structure a draft.

```
Turn this outline into clear H2 subheadings for a blog post. Each heading should be a promise of what the reader will learn. No emojis: [paste outline]
```

**First draft from my points**

Use when you already know what you want to say.

```
Here are my rough points for a post about [topic]. Write a first draft from them in short paragraphs. Keep my points in order, add nothing new, and use a warm Kenyan tone: [paste points]
```

**Make it local**

Use to add Kenyan specifics the AI missed.

```
Add Kenyan context to this draft: mention M-Pesa payment options, local prices in KES, or common local challenges. Only add details that are realistic and useful: [paste draft]
```

**Remove AI-sounding phrases**

Use as a final cleaning pass.

```
Scan this draft and list every cliche, filler phrase, and overly formal sentence (e.g. "in today's fast-paced world", "furthermore"). Then rewrite the draft without them, in shorter sentences: [paste draft]
```

**Intro from a personal story**

Use to open with your own experience.

```
Write a 3-sentence intro for my post about [topic]. Start from a short personal experience I'll give you. Do not add a story I didn't share: [paste your story, e.g. the first time I sold online]
```

**Call to action and ending**

Use to close a post.

```
Write a short ending and call to action for a blog post about [topic]. Ask readers to share their experience or subscribe. Warm, not salesy. Under 3 sentences.
```

## Action plan

1. Pick one topic from your own experience.
2. Get an outline from AI using the first prompt.
3. Trim the outline — keep only what you can write with real knowledge.
4. Draft the post with AI, then rewrite the intro in your own words.
5. Add local detail: prices, places, M-Pesa, real examples.
6. Run the "remove AI-sounding phrases" pass.
7. Choose a title from your 10 options and publish.

## Quick wins checklist

- [ ] I have a one-keyword focus for my post
- [ ] Outline built and trimmed to what I know
- [ ] Title chosen from real options, not the first AI suggestion
- [ ] Draft edited in my own voice
- [ ] Kenyan examples added
- [ ] No "in conclusion" or robot phrasing left
- [ ] Post published somewhere

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Publishing AI's first draft untouched — readers can tell
- Writing about topics you don't know, in a voice that isn't yours
- Stuffing keywords everywhere for "SEO"
- Using AI to replace your opinions instead of organizing them
- Posting titles that sound great but the post doesn't deliver
