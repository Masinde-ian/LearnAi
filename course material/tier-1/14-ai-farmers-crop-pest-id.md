# AI for Farmers: Quick Crop & Pest Identification Help

**Overview** — This course shows you how to use AI to identify common crop pests and diseases from a photo or a simple description, get quick planting and weather-timing advice, and keep simple farm records. It also teaches you exactly where AI stops and a real agrovet or extension officer must take over.

**Who this is for**

- Smallholder maize and tomato farmers who see damage but do not know the cause
- Farmers who cannot reach an agrovet or extension officer quickly
- Anyone keeping a kitchen or shamba garden and wanting to learn faster
- Young farmers who already use a smartphone and WhatsApp daily

**What you'll be able to do**

- Use a photo or text description with AI to identify common pests and diseases
- Get planting and weather-timing guidance for Kenya's two rainy seasons
- Know when AI advice is enough and when you must see a local expert
- Keep a simple crop and spending record with AI as your assistant
- Verify seed and chemical authenticity using KEPHIS tools

## Lessons

### Lesson 1: Taking a good photo of the problem

AI photo tools only work as well as the photo you give them. Hold your phone steady and close enough that the damaged leaf, stem, or fruit fills most of the frame. Take one photo of the whole plant, then a close-up of the damage. Good light matters — early morning or late afternoon is best. If the plant has webbing, spots, holes, or a sticky residue, say so in your message. A clear description plus a photo beats a blurry photo alone.

**Do this:** "This is a maize plant, the leaves have long grey streaks and some are dead."

**Not this:** "My plant is sick." — Too vague for AI or a human expert to help.

### Lesson 2: Identifying the usual suspects

Three pests and diseases cause most of the damage in Kenyan farms, so learn their signs first.

- **Fall armyworm (maize):** small caterpillars that eat leaves leaving ragged "windowpane" holes, plus sawdust-like frass in the funnel of the plant. It has been a major maize pest in Kenya since 2016/17 and can cut yields by about one-third when uncontrolled.
- **Maize streak virus:** long, pale yellow or white streaks running along the leaf veins. It is spread by leafhoppers, not by the caterpillar you might be looking for.
- **Tomato blight and leaf curl:** brown or black spots on leaves and fruit that spread fast in wet weather, or leaves that curl up and thicken. Leaf curl is often spread by whiteflies.

Ask AI to compare your photo or description against these three before anything else. Nine times out of ten the answer is one of them.

### Lesson 3: Planting and weather timing

Kenya has two rainy seasons: the **long rains (March to May)** and the **short rains (October to December)**. AI can help you plan around them. Ask AI to build a simple calendar for your crop — when to prepare the seedbed, plant, weed, top-dress, and expect harvest — based on your county and the season you are entering.

AI can also draft a quick weather check for the week (look at weather apps or local radio reports) so you do not plant right before a long dry spell. AI does not have live weather data by default, so pair its planning advice with real local forecasts.

### Lesson 4: Where AI advice ends

AI is great at naming the problem and explaining it. It is not the final authority on treatment. For chemicals and rates, your **agrovet or county extension officer** is the expert. Always:

- Confirm the pest or disease before buying anything
- Buy inputs only from trusted agrovets (e.g. Twiga Chemicals, Lachlan, Kenya Seed) or verified digital shops like DigiFarm or iProcure
- Check the label is genuine — for seed, verify the KEPHIS label by SMSing the code to **1393**
- Follow the recommended dose and safety gear; never mix chemicals on your own guess

Bring the AI's diagnosis with you. It saves the agrovet time and helps you ask better questions.

### Lesson 5: Simple record-keeping with AI

You do not need a bookkeeping app. Use AI as your farm notebook. Each week, tell it what you planted, what you spent (seed, fertiliser, chemicals), what you harvested, and what you sold. Ask it to keep a running table and point out patterns — for example, "maize in plot B costs 20% more to grow than plot A." This is the same information that makes you a better negotiator with buyers and lenders later.

## Prompt Library

**Photo diagnosis prompt**

**How to use it:** Send this with a clear photo of the affected plant.

```
You are a plant doctor for smallholder farmers in Kenya. Look at this photo and the description below. Name the most likely pest or disease, give 3 signs that confirm it, and explain it in simple English. End with: "This is a suggestion, not a final diagnosis. Confirm with your agrovet or county extension officer before treating."
Description: [paste your description here]
```

**Description-only identification**

**How to use it:** When you cannot send a photo, describe the damage in detail.

```
A maize plant in Kenya has leaves with ragged holes and sawdust-like dirt inside the funnel where new leaves form. Which pest is most likely, and how does it spread? Answer in simple English with a one-sentence confirmation step.
```

**Weather and planting calendar**

**How to use it:** Replace county, crop, and season before sending.

```
I farm in [county, e.g. Kakamega] and I want to plant [crop, e.g. maize] this [long rains March-May / short rains Oct-Dec]. Build me a week-by-week farm calendar from land prep to harvest, and tell me the best planting window for my area. Keep it short enough to read on my phone.
```

**Agrovet visit checklist**

**How to use it:** Run this before heading to the shop, and take the output with you.

```
The suspected problem is [pest/disease name]. Make me a short checklist to discuss at my agrovet: questions to ask, what chemical groups to mention, and what to verify on the label (including KEPHIS verification). No specific product recommendations — I will confirm locally.
```

**Simple farm record keeper**

**How to use it:** Paste this once, then feed it your weekly updates.

```
You are my farm record keeper. Each week I will send you what I planted, spent, harvested, and sold. Keep a running table with totals. At the end of every month, give me a 5-line summary: biggest cost, best crop, and one money-saving tip. Start fresh — here is my first entry: [entry]
```

**Comparing cost options**

**How to use it:** Use before buying inputs or choosing between suppliers.

```
Compare buying [fertiliser/chemical/seed] from [agrovet A] versus [agrovet B] for a [size] farm. List what I should compare (price, transport, credit terms, delivery), and give me 3 questions to ask before paying. Use ranges like "from about KES X" — do not invent exact prices.
```

**Pest control plan (no chemicals first)**

**How to use it:** Get a starting point before you spend money.

```
For [pest/disease] on [crop], give me 3 non-chemical control steps I can try first, then tell me clearly: "If this does not improve in one week, see your agrovet." Keep it to under 150 words.
```

**Harvest and market planning**

**How to use it:** Near harvest time, plan selling.

```
I expect to harvest about [amount] of [crop] in [month]. Help me plan: when to harvest, how to store it, and 3 places in Kenya I could sell it (markets, cooperatives, platforms). Give me a simple "before harvest" checklist.
```

**Weekly farm summary**

**How to use it:** Quick end-of-week reflection.

```
Summarise this week on my small farm: [what you did]. What did I do well, what should I improve next week, and is there anything I should ask my agrovet about? Three bullets only.
```

## Action plan

1. Take one photo set of a damaged crop this week and run the photo diagnosis prompt.
2. If AI names a pest or disease, book one visit to a trusted agrovet to confirm.
3. Verify any seed you buy by SMSing the KEPHIS label code to 1393.
4. Ask AI to build your planting calendar for the next rainy season.
5. Start a weekly record entry — even two lines per week is enough.
6. Save your top three prompts in your phone's notes for reuse.

## Quick wins checklist

- I identified one pest or disease on my farm with AI's help
- I confirmed my diagnosis with an agrovet or extension officer
- I checked a seed or chemical label using KEPHIS
- I have a planting calendar for the next season
- I made at least one weekly farm record entry
- I know the difference between "AI advice" and "expert treatment"

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Treating the wrong pest because you skipped confirmation — fall armyworm and streak virus look different but both ruin maize
- Trusting AI's chemical dose suggestions; doses come from labels and your agrovet
- Buying from roadside or unverified sellers to save a few shillings
- Planting without checking the season timing and local forecast
- Asking AI for live weather without pairing it with a real forecast app or radio
- Giving up on record keeping because you think it needs an app
