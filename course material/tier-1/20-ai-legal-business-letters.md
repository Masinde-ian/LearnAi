# AI for Quick Legal & Business Letter Templates

**Overview**

Need a demand letter, a notice letter, or a quotation today, not next week. This course shows you how to draft common Kenyan business and legal letters in minutes with AI, understand which documents you can handle yourself, and know exactly when a real lawyer must step in. Your win: a starter pack of 10 ready-to-edit templates you can reuse forever.

**Who this is for**

- Small business owners who chase unpaid invoices and need demand letters
- Landlords and agents who need tenancy and notice letters
- Traders and vendors who need quotations and receipts fast
- Anyone who has been told "get it in writing" but does not know how
- People who want professional documents without paying a lawyer for every small letter

**What you'll be able to do**

- Generate a demand letter for unpaid goods or services in under 5 minutes
- Write professional notice letters (tenancy, termination, reminders)
- Draft simple quotations, receipts, and business agreements
- Know which documents need a lawyer's review and how to find one (LSK)
- Build and maintain your own library of 10 reusable letter templates

## Lesson 1: What AI can and cannot do

AI is brilliant at drafting, structuring, and rephrasing. Give it the facts and it will produce a clean, formal letter in seconds — saving you hours on the routine documents every business needs.

But here is the boundary: AI is not a lawyer and is not giving legal advice. For anything binding — contracts, tenancy agreements, anything where money or rights are at stake — a lawyer must review. The Law Society of Kenya (LSK) has a directory and a referral service. Your rule: AI drafts, a lawyer approves, you use.

## Lesson 2: The common letters you can draft today

Most Kenyan businesses repeatedly need the same four letters. Each takes minutes with AI:

- Demand letter — goods supplied, payment not received
- Notice letter — to a tenant, a supplier, or to end a contract
- Quotation — a clear price list and scope of work for a customer
- Reminder letter — the polite nudge before a demand letter

The trick is giving AI the facts: who, what, when, how much, and the deadline. Vague in, vague out.

## Lesson 3: Simple agreements, receipts, and records

Not every document is a contract. Many businesses only need:

- A receipt — goods sold, cash or M-Pesa received, simple and factual
- A simple agreement — scope of work, price, timeline, payment terms
- An acknowledgement — for a deposit or part payment made via M-Pesa

AI writes these well. Keep them short and factual. Add your business details (reg number from BRS if you have one), the date, and both parties' names. Keep copies in Google Drive or on your phone. Record the M-Pesa transaction ID on receipts and acknowledgements.

## Lesson 4: Where you must involve a real lawyer

Some documents are too important for AI alone:

- Contracts with long-term obligations or penalties
- Property and land documents (title deeds, LRA processes)
- Tenancy agreements where large deposits are involved
- Anything you are about to sign that commits your money or your freedom

The rule is simple: if a mistake would cost you money or freedom, get a human lawyer. In most routine cases, one consultation is enough to fix an AI draft.

## Lesson 5: Build your own template starter pack

Your one-hour win is a folder of 10 ready-to-edit templates. Use the Prompt Library below to generate each one, replace the square brackets with your real details, and save the clean version. Next time you need a demand letter, it is already 80 percent done.

Add one line to every template: "Draft — have a lawyer review before you rely on it." That one line protects you. Review templates yearly or when the law or your business changes.

## Prompt Library

Copy any prompt, fill in the square brackets, and paste into ChatGPT.

**Demand letter for unpaid payment**

Use when a customer owes you money.

```
You are a business letter writer in Kenya. Write a formal demand letter from
[BUSINESS NAME] to [CUSTOMER NAME] for unpaid goods/services worth [KES AMOUNT]
invoiced on [DATE]. Include: what was supplied, the agreed payment date, that
payment is now [DAYS] days overdue, a final deadline of [DATE], and a note that
we may refer the matter to a lawyer if payment is not received. Keep it polite
but firm. Sign-off: [YOUR NAME], [CONTACT].
```

**Tenancy notice letter**

Use to give or end tenancy notice.

```
Write a formal notice letter in Kenya for [GIVING/ENDING] tenancy of [PROPERTY
NAME] by [TENANT NAME] from [DATE]. State the required notice period of [X]
months per our agreement, the handover date, and any pending rent of [KES AMOUNT]
to be settled. Ask for confirmation in writing. Keep it simple and neutral. From
[LANDLORD/TENANT NAME].
```

**Business quotation**

Use whenever a customer asks "how much does it cost?"

```
Create a quotation template for [YOUR BUSINESS]. Header: business name and M-Pesa
till/paybill. Body: customer name and date, a table of items with quantity, unit
price, total, VAT if applicable, delivery or installation notes, payment terms
(50 percent deposit), and quote validity of [X] days. Footer: signature line and
a thank-you note.
```

**Simple business agreement**

Use for a one-off job that needs terms in writing.

```
Write a simple business agreement between [PARTY A] and [PARTY B] for
[SERVICE/GOODS]. Sections: scope of work, price [KES AMOUNT], payment schedule
[INSTALLMENT DETAILS], delivery or start date [DATE], what happens if either side
fails to deliver, and a clause stating this is a draft to be reviewed before
signing. Keep it under one page. End with signature lines.
```

**Receipt template**

Use to issue a clean receipt for cash or M-Pesa.

```
Create a receipt template for [BUSINESS NAME]. Fields: receipt number, date,
customer name, items and amount, total [KES AMOUNT], payment method (cash, or
M-Pesa with transaction ID), balance due if any, and a thank-you note. Leave a
signature line. Keep it clean and printable.
```

**Reminder letter before a demand letter**

Use as the polite first step before a demand letter.

```
Write a friendly but clear payment reminder to [CUSTOMER NAME] regarding invoice
[NUMBER] of [KES AMOUNT] dated [DATE], now [X] days overdue. Offer to discuss a
payment plan if cash is tight, set a deadline of [DATE], and mention that we
value the relationship. From [YOUR NAME/BUSINESS].
```

**Complaint letter to a supplier**

Use when a supplier delivers wrong or damaged goods.

```
Write a formal complaint letter to [SUPPLIER NAME] about order [NUMBER] placed on
[DATE]. State what was ordered, what was received (wrong, damaged, or incomplete),
what you want (replacement, refund, or credit of [KES AMOUNT]), and a deadline of
[DATE] for their response. Attach references: invoice number and M-Pesa payment
transaction ID. Polite but firm.
```

**Partnership letter of intent**

Use to test a collaboration before signing a binding contract.

```
Write a short letter of intent between [MY BUSINESS] and [OTHER BUSINESS] to
explore [COLLABORATION]. State that it is not a binding contract, outline the
rough role of each party, the expected start date, and the next steps. End with a
line that a formal agreement will be reviewed by lawyers before signing.
```

**Engagement letter for a simple job**

Use when you hire a freelancer for a defined task.

```
Write a one-page engagement letter from [BUSINESS] to [WORKER NAME] for the job
of [ROLE/TASK]. Include: scope of duties, pay of [KES AMOUNT] payable via M-Pesa
on [DATE/PERIOD], working hours, and a note that this is a task engagement, not a
permanent employment contract. End with acceptance and signature lines.
```

**Debt collection escalation notice**

Use when reminders and demand letters have failed.

```
Write a final notice to [CUSTOMER NAME] for outstanding balance of [KES AMOUNT]
on invoice [NUMBER]. State all previous reminders sent on [DATES], that the
account is now [X] days overdue, that we will refer the matter to our lawyer if
payment is not received by [DATE], and that legal costs may be added. End with
payment details (M-Pesa paybill or bank). Firm and professional.
```

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Treating an AI draft as legal advice — it is a draft, not a lawyer
- Signing a contract AI wrote without a human review
- Writing letters with no dates, amounts, or deadlines
- Copying templates from random websites that may be outdated
- Issuing receipts that do not record M-Pesa transaction IDs
