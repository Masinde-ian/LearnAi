# Using AI to Write Social Media Captions

## Overview

You know you should post on Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, and WhatsApp Status — but writing captions eats your whole day. This course shows you how to generate on-brand captions in minutes, match tone to your audience, and batch a whole week of posts in 15 minutes. Post more, stress less.

## Who this is for

- Small business owners who run their own pages
- Personal brands and content creators starting out
- Marketers who batch content for clients
- Anyone tired of staring at a blank caption box

## What you'll be able to do

- Generate captions for Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, and WhatsApp Status
- Match your tone to your audience — business page vs personal brand
- Batch-write a week of captions in one sitting
- Add platform-smart hashtags and strong calls to action
- Save a reusable caption system for every post

## Lesson 1: Captions for every platform

Different platforms speak differently. A WhatsApp Status is short and chatty. A TikTok caption is punchy and visual-first. Instagram allows longer storytelling. Facebook suits conversation and community.

Give the AI the platform in the prompt. Here is the formula:

```
You are a social media manager. Write [number] captions for
[platform] for my [business/type]. The offer: [offer].
Tone: [tone]. End each with a call to action.
```

**WhatsApp Status:** 1–2 lines, warm, direct. "Uko na matata ya password? We reset it in 10 minutes."

**TikTok:** Short hook line, then 1–2 lines. The video does the talking.

**Instagram:** Hook, story, offer, CTA. Can run 3–6 lines.

**Facebook:** Open a question or conversation. People reply, the post travels further.

## Lesson 2: Matching tone to your audience

A corporate business page sounds different from a personal brand. Do not copy-paste the same caption everywhere.

**Business page tone:** professional, trustworthy, clear on offers. "We deliver quality phone accessories across Nairobi within 24 hours."

**Personal brand tone:** personal, relatable, opinionated. "I quit my job with no plan. Here is what happened in 6 months."

**Community/organisation tone:** warm, inspiring, inclusive. "Join us Saturday for a free CV clinic. Everyone is welcome."

Tell the AI who you are talking to and how you want to feel to them. Try: "speak like a big sister", "sound like a trusted shopkeeper", "be bold and confident". The tone line is the single biggest lever in caption quality.

## Lesson 3: Batch-writing a week of captions in 15 minutes

Batching means writing many captions at once, then posting one a day. It takes one focused session instead of daily stress.

1. List your week's topics: product, testimonial, behind-the-scenes, tip, offer, question, announcement.
2. Paste the list into one AI prompt asking for one caption per topic.
3. Ask for a few options per topic and pick your favourites.
4. Save them in your notes app with the day you will post.
5. Schedule or post one daily — done for the week.

This works best when you keep the same business, tone, and platform in the prompt so every caption stays on-brand. Batching is the habit that separates pages that post daily from pages that post "someday".

## Lesson 4: Hashtags and calls to action with AI

Hashtags help new people find you. CTAs turn viewers into customers. Let AI do both.

Ask for hashtags separately — about 8–10 for Instagram, 3–5 for TikTok, fewer for Facebook. Mix broad ones (#NairobiBusiness) with specific ones (#PhoneAccessoriesKenya).

Every caption should end with one clear CTA: "Order on WhatsApp", "DM for price", "Comment 'price'", "Follow for daily tips". One action only. A caption that says nothing to do gets nothing done.

## Prompt Library

Copy, fill in the brackets, and adapt.

**Single Instagram caption**

```
Write an Instagram caption for my [business]. The post is about
[offer/topic]. Tone: [tone]. Under 80 words, end with a call
to action to [action]. No emojis.
```

**Week of Instagram captions**

```
I run a [business] in [town]. Write 7 Instagram captions, one
per day, for these topics: [topic1, topic2, topic3, topic4,
topic5, topic6, topic7]. Keep the same tone: [tone]. No emojis.
```

**TikTok caption**

```
Write a punchy TikTok caption for a video about [topic].
Start with a strong hook, keep it under 40 words, add 3
relevant hashtags.
```

**Facebook conversation post**

```
Write a Facebook post that starts a conversation. Topic: [topic].
Ask one question at the end. Target audience: [audience].
```

**WhatsApp Status**

```
Write a WhatsApp Status line (max 2 lines) announcing [offer].
Warm, short, end with "DM to order".
```

**Product launch caption**

```
Announce my new product, [product], priced KES [amount].
Instagram caption, excited but professional, mention M-Pesa
payment and delivery in [town]. No emojis.
```

**Testimonial caption**

```
Turn this customer review into a social media caption:
[paste review]. Add a short thank-you line and a CTA to
try my [service].
```

**Educational tip post**

```
Write a caption teaching my followers one useful tip about
[topic]. Tone: friendly expert. End with "Follow for more".
```

**Sale announcement**

```
Announce a [discount]% discount this weekend only. Add urgency
without sounding desperate. Platform: [platform]. End with an
order CTA via WhatsApp.
```

**Hashtag set**

```
Give me 10 hashtags for an Instagram post about [topic] in
Kenya. Mix popular and specific ones. Return them comma-separated.
```

## Action plan

1. Choose one platform to master this week.
2. Write 3 captions using the platform formula from Lesson 1.
3. Test two tones — business vs personal — and pick what fits you.
4. Batch 7 captions for next week in one 15-minute session.
5. Add hashtags and one clear CTA to every post.
6. Post daily for one week and track what gets replies.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Copying the same caption to every platform
- Leaving the AI "tone" line empty and getting bland text
- Writing captions with no call to action
- Stuffing 30 hashtags into every post
- Emojis everywhere — Kenyan business pages look better when used sparingly (and this course avoids them)
