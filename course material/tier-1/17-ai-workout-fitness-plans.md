# AI for Simple Workout & Fitness Plans

**Overview** — This course helps you generate a beginner workout plan based on your goals and the equipment you have — bodyweight first, no gym needed — plus basic nutrition guidance using affordable Kenyan foods and a simple way to track progress. The one clear win: a doable weekly plan you can start today from your own home.

**Who this is for**

- Beginners who have never followed a workout plan
- People who want to get fit but have no gym money or time
- Anyone on a budget looking for cheap, healthy eating ideas
- Parents or workers who need short workouts that fit a busy day

**What you'll be able to do**

- Generate a beginner workout plan matched to your goals and equipment
- Get basic nutrition guidance with affordable Kenyan protein sources
- Track progress simply, without expensive apps
- Know when to see a professional instead of relying on AI

## Lessons

### Lesson 1: Tell AI what you actually have

A plan only works if it matches your reality. Before you ask AI for a plan, decide four things: your goal (lose weight, build strength, feel more energy, or get fit for football), your free days, how long each session can be, and what equipment you have. For most Kenyans that answer is "none" — which is perfect, because bodyweight training works. Push-ups, squats, lunges, planks, and jumps need no equipment and no gym. Tell AI "no gym, no equipment, 30 minutes, 4 days a week" and let it build around that.

### Lesson 2: The beginner plan structure

A good beginner plan is simple and specific. Ask AI for:

- Which days to train and which days to rest
- Which exercises to do, and how many sets and repetitions
- How to warm up (5 minutes) and cool down
- A starting level, then how to progress each week

Start lighter than you think. Soreness on day two is normal and fine; sharp pain is not. If you can talk while exercising, the level is right. Increase reps or add a small difficulty (like a slower push-up) only when an exercise feels easy.

### Lesson 3: Basic nutrition with affordable proteins

You do not need expensive supplements to eat well. Good Kenyan budget proteins include **eggs, beans, omena, and sukuma wiki** with the right staples. Ask AI to plan simple meals around these foods, not exotic ones. A practical day might be: eggs or beans and bread/ugali for breakfast, beans and rice or githeri for lunch, and ugali with sukuma and omena for dinner. Tell AI your budget and what you already have in the kitchen, and ask for a realistic weekly menu. Drink water, reduce sugary drinks, and do not cut meals completely — that backfires.

### Lesson 4: Tracking progress without expensive apps

You do not need a fitness tracker. Keep it in your phone's notes or WhatsApp messages to yourself. Track three simple things each week:

- Your weight once a week, same day and time
- How many repetitions or minutes you completed
- How your clothes fit and how you feel

Once a month, paste your notes into AI and ask for a trend summary — is it working, and what one change to make? Pictures and measurements (waist, arm) often show change even when weight stays the same, so record those too.

### Lesson 5: When AI is not enough

AI is a great coach for the basics, but it is not a doctor or a physiotherapist. See a professional if you have a known condition (heart, blood pressure, diabetes, joint or back problems), if you are pregnant, or if any exercise causes sharp pain. For people with medical conditions, the right move is to ask a doctor first, then use AI to follow their guidance — never the other way round. Also, if you have not exercised in years, start with walking and let AI build up gradually before adding intense sessions.

## Prompt Library

**Beginner plan from scratch**

**How to use it:** Fill in your four details, replace the brackets.

```
Build a beginner workout plan for me. Goal: [lose weight / build strength / more energy]. I have [no equipment / [list equipment]] and [X] free days a week for [X] minutes. No gym. Write each day's exercises with sets and reps, a warm-up, and rest days. Keep it simple enough for a phone screen.
```

**Bodyweight-only workout**

**How to use it:** Strictly no equipment version.

```
Give me a 30-minute bodyweight-only workout I can do at home in [area, e.g. my living room]. Include a warm-up, 4 exercises with sets and reps, and a cool-down. Make it suitable for a complete beginner.
```

**Weekly schedule builder**

**How to use it:** Fit training around work or family.

```
I work [days/hours] and I am free [evenings/weekends]. Build a weekly workout schedule for me that fits this life and includes rest days. Be realistic — do not expect me to train twice a day.
```

**Progress the plan**

**How to use it:** When week one becomes easy.

```
This plan now feels easy for me: [paste current plan or your reps]. Make it slightly harder while keeping the same days and equipment. Explain one small progression per exercise, not a total overhaul.
```

**Budget meal ideas**

**How to use it:** Simple, affordable menus around a workout plan.

```
Give me a 7-day meal guide for a budget in Kenya using cheap proteins like eggs, beans, omena, and sukuma wiki. Show one meal per slot (breakfast, lunch, dinner), keep portions realistic, and note which days to increase protein after harder workouts.
```

**Protein ideas without meat**

**How to use it:** Vegetarian or cost-cutting days.

```
List 8 cheap protein sources I can buy in Kenya without meat, with rough portion sizes and how to prepare each simply. Keep it practical for a small budget.
```

**Progress check**

**How to use it:** Paste your weekly notes and get a verdict.

```
Here are my training notes for the last month: [paste notes with weight, reps, how you feel]. Tell me: is my plan working, what one change should I make, and should I increase difficulty? Be honest and brief.
```

**Short busy-day session**

**How to use it:** When you have only 15 minutes.

```
Write me a 15-minute workout for a busy day: 5-minute warm-up, 8 minutes of exercises, 2-minute cool-down. Bodyweight only. I want to feel like I did something but not be exhausted for work.
```

**Avoiding injury tips**

**How to use it:** First sessions, good form.

```
Give me 5 tips for a beginner to avoid injury, focusing on proper form for push-ups, squats, and planks. Keep it in simple language and explain what "sharp pain means stop" looks like.
```

**When to see a professional**

**How to use it:** If you have a condition or concern.

```
I [describe situation, e.g. have high blood pressure / am 40+ and have not exercised in years / feel knee pain doing squats]. Should I start exercising, and should I see a doctor first? Give me a clear yes/no answer and what to tell the doctor.
```

## Action plan

1. Decide your goal, free days, session length, and equipment (even if "none").
2. Run the beginner plan prompt and save the plan in your phone.
3. Do week one — start lighter than you think.
4. Set up a simple progress note in your phone.
5. Plan this week's meals around budget proteins.
6. Record your weight, reps, and feelings at the end of week one.

## Quick wins checklist

- I have a beginner plan built for my schedule and equipment
- I completed my first week of sessions
- I have a weekly meal guide using affordable proteins
- My progress notes exist and I know what to record
- I know which pain is normal and which means stop
- I know exactly when I should see a doctor first

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Starting too hard on day one and quitting by day four
- Following a random internet plan that assumes a gym
- Cutting meals or skipping carbs entirely to "lose weight fast"
- Buying supplements before fixing basic food and sleep
- Comparing yourself to videos of people who trained for years
- Ignoring sharp pain or training through an existing condition
