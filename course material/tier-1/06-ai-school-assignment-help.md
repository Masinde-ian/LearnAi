# AI Tools for School & Assignment Help

## Overview

Stuck on a topic? AI can explain it like a patient friend, summarise your notes, and even generate practice questions for exams. This course shows students how to use AI to actually understand work — not copy it. Learn the responsible way, and your grades and your knowledge both improve.

## Who this is for

- Secondary and university students drowning in assignments
- Students who do not understand a topic no matter how many times they read it
- Anyone doing exam revision who needs practice questions
- Parents who want to help their kids with homework

## What you'll be able to do

- Get any difficult topic explained at your level
- Summarise long textbooks and notes into clean study material
- Use NotebookLM with your own uploaded notes and PDFs
- Use AI responsibly without falling into plagiarism
- Generate practice questions for exam revision

## Lesson 1: Make AI explain it like you are in Form 2

The biggest study blocker is jargon. Textbooks use words that assume you already know the topic. AI can drop the jargon and meet you where you are.

The magic prompt:

```
Explain [topic] like I am a Form 2 student in Kenya. Use a
real-life example. Keep it under 150 words.
```

Try it with "inflation", "photosynthesis", or "Newton's first law". Then level up by asking for one step harder each time — start simple, then ask for more detail until you genuinely get it. This "explain, then go deeper" habit builds real understanding.

## Lesson 2: Summarising textbooks and notes

Long chapters are hard to revise. AI turns them into short, structured summaries.

```
Summarise this chapter into: 5 key points, 3 definitions I must
know, and 1 summary paragraph. Keep facts and dates exact:
[paste the text]
```

Copy-paste from a digital textbook, or type your handwritten notes into the chat (voice input helps). One chapter becomes one page. Revise from the summary, and return to the book only for the details you need.

## Lesson 3: NotebookLM — your personal study folder

NotebookLM is Google's AI that works on your own uploaded files. Unlike ChatGPT, it only answers from the notes and PDFs you give it — which means fewer hallucinations and answers that match your syllabus.

1. Go to notebooklm.google.com (free) on your phone or laptop.
2. Create a notebook and upload your PDFs, notes, or a past paper.
3. Ask questions and it answers using your sources, with citations.
4. It can also generate study guides, flashcards, and audio overviews.

This is the tool for exam season: upload your class notes once and revise from one place. Free tier is generous and covers most students.

## Lesson 4: Using AI responsibly — the line you must not cross

AI is a tutor, not a ghostwriter. Here is the line.

**Allowed:** understanding a topic, summarising your own notes, planning your essay, checking your grammar, generating practice questions.

**Not allowed:** making the AI write your assignment and submitting it word-for-word. That is plagiarism, it is detectable, and it cheats you out of the skills you need.

Kenyan institutions take academic honesty seriously. A copied assignment can mean a fail, a disciplinary case, or worse. The safe method: let AI explain, write the work yourself, then use AI to check and improve your draft.

## Lesson 5: Exam revision with AI-generated questions

Past papers are gold but run out. AI can generate unlimited practice questions from your notes.

```
Based on this topic, generate 10 practice questions like a KCSE
paper: 6 short answer and 4 essay. Then give me model answers I
can compare with my own: [paste notes]
```

Answer them closed-book, mark yourself, and re-read the sections you got wrong. Repeat until you score well. This active recall beats re-reading notes ten times over.

## Prompt Library

Copy, fill in the brackets, and adapt.

**Explain like I am a beginner**

```
Explain [topic] in simple terms, like I am in Form 2 in Kenya.
Use one example from daily Kenyan life. Under 150 words.
```

**Go one step deeper**

```
I now understand the basics of [topic]. Explain it at a deeper
level, but still in simple English, with one example.
```

**Chapter summary**

```
Summarise this chapter into 5 key points, 3 must-know
definitions, and 1 short summary paragraph. Keep facts exact:
[paste text]
```

**Notes to study guide**

```
Turn these notes into a study guide with headings, key terms,
and a mini-quiz of 5 questions: [paste notes]
```

**Difficult concept breakdown**

```
Break down [concept] into 4 steps a beginner can follow.
For each step, give a one-line example.
```

**Practice questions**

```
Generate 10 KCSE-style practice questions on [topic]:
6 short answer, 4 essay. Provide answers separately.
```

**Mark my answer**

```
Here is my answer to [question]. Mark it out of 10, point out
mistakes, and tell me what to improve: [paste answer]
```

**Essay plan**

```
Give me a 4-paragraph outline for this essay topic: [topic].
Include the main point for each paragraph. Do not write the
essay for me.
```

**Grammar and clarity check**

```
Check this draft for grammar and clarity. Suggest fixes but
keep my ideas and voice: [paste draft]
```

**Explain with a memory hook**

```
Explain [topic] and give me a Kenyan memory trick or acronym
to remember the key points.
```

## Action plan

1. Take your hardest subject and run the "explain like I am in Form 2" prompt.
2. Summarise one chapter of notes you are revising.
3. Set up a NotebookLM notebook and upload one set of notes.
4. Generate practice questions on a topic you have covered.
5. Answer them closed-book and identify your weak spots.
6. Re-read only the weak sections, then repeat.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Submitting AI text as your own work
- Copying answers without understanding the reasoning
- Using AI as the first step instead of trying the work first
- Asking for the answer instead of asking to be taught
- Trusting AI numbers and quotes without checking the source
