# AI for Debt & Savings Planning

**Overview**

Most people do not have a money problem; they have a money planning problem. This course shows you how to use AI to map every debt you owe, choose the fastest payoff method, set savings goals you will actually hit, and keep clean records for your chama or table banking. Your win: a simple debt and savings plan you can manage from your phone.

**Who this is for**

- Anyone with loans, fuliza, or shop credit they are juggling
- Chama and table banking members who want better records
- Salaried workers who want to save before the money disappears
- Small business owners with irregular income
- People tired of predatory loan apps eating their money

**What you'll be able to do**

- List all your debts in one place with interest rates and deadlines
- Compare snowball vs avalanche and pick the right payoff plan
- Set a savings goal and a weekly plan that fits your real income
- Keep chama and table banking records with AI-assisted tracking
- Spot hidden fees, loan app traps, and lifestyle creep early

## Lesson 1: Map every debt before you do anything

You cannot plan money you cannot see. Start by listing every debt: bank loans, SACCO loans, fuliza, M-Pesa loans, shop credit, money from family. For each, note the amount, the interest, the monthly payment, and the deadline.

AI makes this easy. Paste your list in and ask for a table. Do not guess the numbers — check your M-Pesa statements and loan messages first. The most common money mistake is ignoring a debt until it becomes a crisis.

## Lesson 2: Snowball vs avalanche — which payoff plan?

Two proven ways to pay off debt faster:

- Snowball: pay off the smallest debt first, then roll that payment to the next. Best for motivation because you get quick wins.
- Avalanche: pay off the highest-interest debt first. Best for saving the most money overall.

AI can run both scenarios with your real numbers and show the totals. There is no wrong answer — the best method is the one you will stick to. Many Kenyans start with snowball because finishing a small loan feels great, then switch to avalanche later.

## Lesson 3: Realistic savings goals and simple tracking

A savings goal that is too big fails in week one. Start with a number that hurts a little but does not break your week: KES 500 or KES 1,000 a week is enough to build the habit.

Give AI your income, your fixed costs, and your target, and ask for a realistic weekly plan. Track it in a simple Google Sheet or a notebook. M-Pesa statements are your friend here — you can see exactly where money goes and ask AI to summarize a month's statement into categories (airtime, transport, food, M-Pesa charges).

## Lesson 4: Chama and table banking records with AI

Chamas run on trust, and trust runs on records. For table banking or a merry-go-round, you need: who contributed, how much, when, and who borrowed or received the kitty.

Ask AI for a simple contribution table with member names, amounts, and dates. Update it after every meeting. At the end of the cycle, ask AI to produce a summary report. This works with a simple sheet on your phone — no fancy software needed.

## Lesson 5: Catch money mistakes early with AI

AI is good at spotting patterns you might miss:

- Hidden fees: ask it to review a loan offer line by line for charges you did not plan for
- Loan app traps: ask it to explain the real annual cost of a "KES 500 loan at 15 percent fee for 14 days"
- Lifestyle creep: ask it to compare this month's spending with last month's and flag where money silently grew

Rule: never borrow from an app just because it is convenient. If a loan's real annual cost is above 100 percent, it is eating your future. A SACCO or bank loan, or the chama kitty itself, is almost always cheaper.

## Prompt Library

**Debt inventory table**

Use when you need all your debts in one place.

```
Act as a personal finance assistant in Kenya. Create a table for my debts with
columns: lender, type (bank/SACCO/fuliza/loan app/shop credit/family), amount,
monthly payment, interest rate, and due date. Here are my debts: [LIST THEM].
Then ask me for anything missing before you calculate.
```

**Snowball vs avalanche comparison**

Use when you have the full debt list and want the best payoff order.

```
Using this debt list: [PASTE TABLE FROM ABOVE]. Calculate two payoff plans:
snowball (smallest balance first) and avalanche (highest interest first). For
each, show the order I should pay, the approximate months to finish if I pay
[KES X] extra per week, and the total interest saved. Recommend one and explain
in one sentence why.
```

**Weekly savings plan**

Use to turn a savings target into something you can actually do.

```
I earn [KES X] per month, my fixed costs are [LIST], and I want to save [KES X]
by [DATE]. Build me a weekly savings plan for a person in Nairobi, including how
much to move to savings every week, when to do it (right after payday), and how
to handle a week when income is low. Keep it simple.
```

**Savings goal tracker**

Use to set up a simple tracker you can update on your phone.

```
Create a simple savings tracker table with columns: date, amount saved, running
total, and days to goal. My goal is [KES X] and I save [KES X] per week. Then
write a short instruction on how I should update it every week using Google
Sheets on my phone.
```

**Chama contribution record**

Use at every chama or table banking meeting.

```
Design a contribution record table for a chama with [NUMBER] members. Columns:
member name, contribution [KES X], date, and notes (e.g. borrowed kitty, received
payout). Add a summary row that calculates the total collected each meeting. Write
it as a clean, copy-paste table I can put into Google Sheets.
```

**Chama monthly report**

Use before a meeting to summarise the month.

```
From this chama record: [PASTE TABLE]. Produce a simple monthly report: total
contributions, total paid out, loans given and repaid, and each member's running
balance. Keep it to one page and write it in simple English with a Kiswahili
heading option.
```

**M-Pesa statement summariser**

Use to see where your money actually goes.

```
Here are the transactions from my M-Pesa statement for last month: [PASTE
TRANSACTIONS]. Group them into categories: airtime/data, transport, food, M-Pesa
charges, shopping, other. Show totals per category and the top 5 expenses. Then
point out any two things that surprise me, like a subscription I forgot.
```

**Loan offer review**

Use before you accept any loan, especially from an app.

```
A lender offers me [KES X] repayable in [X] days with a fee of [KES X]. Calculate
the real annual interest rate and the true cost of the loan. Explain whether this
is expensive or cheap compared to a bank or SACCO loan, list any red flags like
unclear terms, and suggest safer alternatives.
```

**Hidden fees check**

Use to review a loan agreement or repayment plan for surprise charges.

```
Here is a loan agreement or recovery schedule: [PASTE OR DESCRIBE]. List every
fee, penalty, insurance charge, or fine a borrower could be hit with. Translate
each into plain Kiswahili and tell me which fees are avoidable.
```

**Lifestyle creep check**

Use each month to see where your spending grew silently.

```
Compare these two months of spending: [PASTE M-PESA OR BUDGET DATA FOR TWO
MONTHS]. Identify where spending increased (eating out, delivery, subscriptions,
shopping) and estimate the extra cost. Suggest 3 small cuts that would not feel
painful and what that money could do if saved for a year.
```

## Action plan

1. Open ChatGPT and generate your debt inventory table.
2. Pull your real numbers from M-Pesa statements and loan messages, not memory.
3. Run the snowball vs avalanche comparison and pick one method.
4. Set one small savings goal (KES 500–1,000 weekly) and create the tracker.
5. Create the chama contribution record before your next meeting.
6. Review one loan offer or your last month of M-Pesa using the prompts.
7. Save all prompts in a note on your phone for monthly use.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Planning from memory instead of real statements
- Paying the minimum on every loan instead of attacking one
- Setting savings goals too big for your real income
- Ignoring loan app fees until they balloon
- Treating the chama kitty as free money with no records
- Upgrading spending every time income rises (lifestyle creep)
