# AI for CV & Cover Letter Writing

## Lesson 1: How Kenyan hiring actually works

Big employers — banks, NGOs, telcos — receive hundreds of applications per role. To cope, they use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS): software that scans your CV for keywords, roles, and dates before a human sees it.

BrighterMonday is Kenya's largest job board and the default for many listings. Fuzu uses AI matching and gives feedback after rejection — a rare free resource, use it. LinkedIn matters for professional and corporate roles. NGOs often post on JobWeb Kenya and UN/ReliefWeb boards.

The lesson: your CV must be machine-readable AND human-readable. Clean format, plain section headings, real keywords from the job ad.

## Lesson 2: Turning rough history into a polished CV

Start with a brain-dump, not a perfect draft. List every job, task, and result you remember — messy notes are fine.

Then let AI do the polishing. Give it your rough text and the formula:

```
You are a CV writer for the Kenyan job market. Rewrite my work
history below into bullet points that show results and skills.
Use strong action verbs. Keep it one page. Here is my history:
[paste]
```

AI is best at turning "I was a teller" into "Processed over 200 customer transactions daily, maintaining a 100% cash accuracy record." That transformation — from duties to results — is what gets you interviews.

## Lesson 3: Writing a tailored cover letter per job

A generic cover letter screams "I sent this to 50 companies." A tailored one names the role, the company, and your specific fit. You should write a fresh one for every serious application — and AI makes that fast.

```
Write a cover letter for the [role] position at [company], advertised
on [BrighterMonday/Fuzu/LinkedIn]. My top skills: [skills]. One
achievement I am proud of: [achievement]. Kenyan professional tone,
under 250 words.
```

One template, three minutes, done. Repeat per job. The difference in response rate is enormous.

## Lesson 4: Formatting for ATS

ATS software is easily confused. Follow these rules:

- Use standard section headings: **Professional Summary, Work Experience, Education, Skills**.
- Save as PDF only if the job allows; otherwise use the plain format requested.
- Use a simple, single-column layout. Tables, images, and fancy graphics break ATS.
- Match keywords from the job ad exactly — if they say "customer service", write "customer service", not "client care".
- Put your phone number and an email you actually check at the top.
- Keep it to one or two pages. Fresh graduates: one page.

## Lesson 5: Common CV mistakes Kenyans make

**Too long.** A 5-page CV is never read. Cut ruthlessly.

**Duties, not results.** "Was responsible for sales" tells nothing. "Grew sales 20% in 6 months" tells everything.

**Wrong contact details.** An unprofessional email (kingkong24@) or a dead number loses the job instantly.

**Typos and formatting chaos.** ATS and recruiters both punish mess. Proofread with AI.

**Copy-paste applications.** One cover letter for every role is a fast no.

**Missing keywords.** If the ad asks for Excel, put "Excel" in your skills — and actually know it.

## Prompt Library

Copy, fill in the brackets, and adapt.

**Full CV polish**

```
You are a CV writer for the Kenyan market. Rewrite my rough work
history into a one-page CV with a professional summary, work
experience bullets that show results, education, and skills.
Use action verbs. Here is my history: [paste]
```

**Professional summary**

```
Write a 3-line professional summary for a [role] with
[skill/experience]. First-person, confident, no clichés like
"hard-working and dedicated".
```

**Results bullets**

```
Turn these duties into result-focused bullet points with numbers
where possible: [paste duties]
```

**Tailored cover letter**

```
Write a cover letter for [role] at [company], advertised on
[platform]. Skills: [skills]. Achievement: [achievement].
Professional Kenyan tone, under 250 words.
```

**ATS keyword check**

```
Here is my CV and here is the job ad. List the keywords from
the ad that are missing from my CV, then suggest how to add
them honestly: [CV] [job ad]
```

**CV shortening**

```
Shorten this CV to one page. Keep the most relevant experience
for a [role] role. Cut anything irrelevant: [paste CV]
```

**Address a career gap**

```
I have a [X months/years] gap in my CV because [reason]. Help me
phrase it positively in the professional summary or a short note.
```

**Red flags fix**

```
Review this CV for errors a Kenyan recruiter would reject:
typos, vague duties, weak opening, wrong format. List fixes:
[paste CV]
```

**LinkedIn summary**

```
Write a LinkedIn About section for a [role] based on this CV.
First-person, 4–5 lines, ends with what I am looking for:
[paste CV]
```

**Interview practice**

```
Based on my CV, ask me the 5 questions a Kenyan interviewer
would most likely ask for a [role]. Then score my answers.
```
