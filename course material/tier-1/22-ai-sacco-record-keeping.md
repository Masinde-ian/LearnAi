# AI for Boda Boda & Matatu SACCO Record Keeping

**Overview**

SACCOs run on trust, and trust runs on records that members can see. This course shows you how to use AI to set up daily income and expense tracking per rider or driver, generate simple reports for meetings, write clear member messages, and track loans and savings in plain tables. Your win: a working record system for your SACCO, from your phone, today.

**Who this is for**

- Boda boda SACCO treasurers who still use a worn-out notebook
- Matatu SACCO chairpersons who need reports for the monthly meeting
- Riders and drivers who want to know their own daily profit
- Committee members who want cleaner loan and savings records
- SACCOs not ready for paid software but tired of paper

**What you'll be able to do**

- Set up a daily income and expense sheet per rider or driver
- Produce a one-page report for the next SACCO meeting
- Write member reminders and notices in minutes with AI
- Track loans and savings in simple, clear tables
- Know when to upgrade to digital tools like Tekeleza or ChamaPro

## Lesson 1: Daily income and expense tracking per rider/driver

Start small: each rider or driver records daily income (fares or trips) and daily expenses (fuel, M-Pesa charges, food, float). Ask AI to build a simple table with one row per day. Update it at the end of the day — ten minutes max.

The goal is not a perfect accounting system. It is a habit: knowing your daily profit. A rider who earns KES 1,500 and spends KES 1,200 has a real profit of KES 300, not KES 1,500. Once every member tracks profit, the SACCO's numbers become real.

## Lesson 2: Simple reports for SACCO meetings

Before every meeting, someone has to explain where the money went. With AI, you paste the records and ask for a one-page summary: total income, total expenses, profit, loans given, loans repaid, savings collected.

Keep the report in simple language. The meeting needs clarity, not accounting jargon. If members can see the numbers, they stop guessing and start trusting.

## Lesson 3: Member communication with AI

SACCO life is full of short messages: contribution reminders, meeting notices, loan repayment warnings, policy updates. AI writes them in minutes, in the right tone — firm for money, friendly for announcements.

Send them through WhatsApp groups, which most SACCOs already use. Short messages read better on phones. Ask AI for a version in English and a version in Kiswahili, then post the one your members actually speak.

## Lesson 4: Basic loan and savings tracking

SACCOs lend the kitty and track member savings. Without records, these two things cause the most arguments. Set up:

- A loan table: member, amount, date given, repayment per week, balance
- A savings table: member, contribution per week, running balance

Ask AI to build both tables and to flag anyone falling behind. A simple formula does the math for you. Update after every meeting or collection day.

## Lesson 5: AI method vs full digital tools

This course shows the AI-assisted manual method: you do the recording, AI does the formatting and the math. It costs nothing extra and works on any phone.

But know that full digital tools exist and are worth looking at. Tekeleza and ChamaPro are popular with Kenyan SACCOs and chamas, and options like the Stanbic Chama App, ChamaConnect, and mychama.app offer free or low-cost plans. If your SACCO grows past a hundred members, switch to software. Start with the free or trial versions.

## Prompt Library

**Daily income and expense sheet**

Use to set up each member's daily record.

```
Create a daily income and expense sheet for a boda boda or matatu operator.
Columns: date, income (fares/trips), expenses split into fuel, M-Pesa charges,
food, and other, total expenses, and daily profit (income minus expenses). Add a
weekly total row. Write it as a clean table I can copy into Google Sheets, with
simple headings a rider can understand.
```

**Weekly summary per member**

Use at the end of the week to know each member's real position.

```
From this member's records: [PASTE TABLE]. Produce a weekly summary showing total
income, total expenses, profit, and the average daily profit. Write a one-
sentence comment on whether the week was good, fair, or poor, and one suggestion
to improve profit.
```

**Monthly SACCO report for the meeting**

Use before the monthly meeting.

```
Here are the SACCO records for [MONTH]: [PASTE]. Produce a one-page report with:
total income, total expenses, net profit, savings collected, loans given, loans
repaid, and arrears (members behind on payments). Write it in simple English and
then provide a Kiswahili version for members who prefer it.
```

**Member contribution reminder**

Use the day before a collection day.

```
Write a WhatsApp reminder for our SACCO members about the [WEEKLY/MONTHLY]
contribution of [KES X] due on [DATE] at [PLACE]. Tone: polite but clear. Mention
the M-Pesa till/paybill [NUMBER] for those who prefer to pay digitally. Keep it
under 4 lines. Give an English and a Kiswahili version.
```

**Meeting notice**

Use to announce the monthly meeting.

```
Write a short notice announcing our SACCO meeting on [DATE] at [TIME] at [VENUE].
Agenda: [AGENDA ITEMS]. Ask members to carry their contribution records. Tone:
warm but official. Keep it under 6 lines. Provide English and Kiswahili versions.
```

**Loan tracking template**

Use every time a member takes or repays a loan.

```
Create a loan tracking table for the SACCO. Columns: member name, loan amount,
date given, repayment amount per week, weeks to finish, balance remaining, and
arrears. Add a note column for the agreed repayment day. Then write a formula
instruction for Google Sheets to calculate the balance as repayments come in.
```

**Savings tracking template**

Use to record member savings every collection day.

```
Create a savings tracking table with one row per member: name, weekly
contribution, total saved to date, and target if any. Then write simple
instructions for updating the totals in Google Sheets after every collection.
Flag members below [KES X] saved, who may need encouragement.
```

**Arrears reminder**

Use when a member is behind on loans or contributions.

```
Write a private WhatsApp message to a SACCO member who is [X] weeks behind on
their [loan/contribution] of [KES X]. Tone: respectful, not shaming. Offer a
payment plan, remind them the matter will be raised at the next meeting if
unsolved, and invite them to talk to the treasurer. Provide English and Kiswahili
versions.
```

**Minutes summary from notes**

Use after the meeting to clean up handwritten minutes.

```
Here are my rough notes from our SACCO meeting: [PASTE NOTES]. Turn them into
clean minutes with headings: attendance, matters arising, decisions, and action
items with owners and deadlines. Keep it to one page. Flag any decision that
needs a follow-up vote or a lawyer's advice.
```

**Compare SACCO income month over month**

Use to show the group whether the SACCO is growing.

```
Compare these two months for our SACCO: [PASTE MONTH 1 DATA] and [PASTE MONTH 2
DATA]. Show a simple comparison of income, expenses, savings, and loan repayments,
with a verdict on whether we are improving. Write it for a meeting audience, no
jargon, and give a Kiswahili version.
```

## Action plan

2. Set up the savings tracking table before the next collection day.
3. Set up the loan tracking table with the treasurer.
4. Write this week's contribution reminder with AI and send it on WhatsApp.
5. Generate the monthly report from real records before the meeting.
6. Decide with the committee whether to trial a free digital tool like mychama.app.
7. Save all templates in a shared Google Drive folder for the committee.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Keeping records only in memory or a notebook no one else can read
- Using different formats per member so nothing adds up
- Recording income without expenses — profit is what matters
- Letting arrears build until a meeting becomes a fight
- Sending shaming messages that drive members away
- Buying paid software before the manual system works
