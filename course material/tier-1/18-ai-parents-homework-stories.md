# AI for Parents: Homework Help & Bedtime Stories

**Overview** — This course helps you use AI to explain homework topics in simple terms for your child, generate custom bedtime stories in English or Kiswahili, and set screen-time-safe rules so older kids use AI for learning, not copying. The one clear win: you become the parent who can actually help with homework and make storytime magical — in one evening.

**Who this is for**

- Parents whose children ask homework questions they cannot answer
- Parents worried about kids using AI to copy assignments
- Families who want bedtime stories with their child's name and favourite animals
- Any parent wanting a simple weekly learning routine at home

**What you'll be able to do**

- Explain homework topics in simple terms a 9-year-old understands
- Generate custom bedtime stories in English or Kiswahili
- Set safe rules for older kids using AI for schoolwork
- Build a weekly family learning routine with AI

## Lessons

### Lesson 1: Explain homework simply

When your child asks "how do plants make food?", you do not need to be a biology teacher. Ask AI to explain the topic **like to a 9-year-old** in simple English — or Kiswahili. The trick is asking for a short explanation, one everyday example, and one question to check understanding. Example: "Explain photosynthesis like a teacher talking to a 9-year-old, in simple English, with one everyday example and one question to ask my child." If the answer is still too hard, say "make it simpler, use shorter words."

### Lesson 2: Better questions, better answers

Teach your child — and yourself — that the quality of the answer depends on the question. Instead of "what is a fraction?", try "explain fractions using slices of a cake or chapati." Instead of "tell me about Kenya's independence", try "explain Mau Mau and independence in a way a 10-year-old can retell." You can also ask AI to break a topic into steps or to test your child with 3 simple questions afterwards. This builds real understanding, not just an answer.

### Lesson 3: Bedtime stories in English or Kiswahili

This is where AI shines. Give it three things: your child's name, a favourite animal or theme, and a lesson you want (like kindness or bravery). Ask for the story in English or Kiswahili, at your child's age level, and short enough for bedtime — 3 to 5 minutes read aloud. Example: "Write a short bedtime story for a 6-year-old named Amani who loves lions, in Kiswahili, where the lion learns to share. Keep it calm and gentle for bedtime." You can make a new story every night, or reuse favourites.

### Lesson 4: Screen-time-safe AI use for older kids

Older kids (10 and up) can use AI for schoolwork, but with rules. Set the ground rules together and write them down:

- AI explains, you write — no copy-paste of whole answers
- Always cite or check information against the textbook
- Homework first, then AI as a helper
- Use it in a shared space, not alone in a bedroom
- Ask a parent to review anything AI produced

The rule to remember: AI is a tutor, not a ghostwriter. When your child can explain the answer in their own words, they actually learned it. If the teacher would be surprised that a child wrote it, that is a red flag.

### Lesson 5: A weekly family learning routine

Keep it small so it sticks. Pick one evening a week — say Sunday after dinner. In 30 minutes: one AI explanation of something the child studied that week, one bedtime story, and one short conversation where the child teaches you what they learned. Do it in English one week and Kiswahili the next so the language practice alternates naturally. Small and consistent beats big and occasional.

## Prompt Library

**Simple explanation**

**How to use it:** The core homework helper. Replace the topic.

```
Explain [topic, e.g. photosynthesis] to a 9-year-old in simple English. Use one everyday example they would know, keep it under 150 words, and end with one question I can ask my child to check they understood.
```

**Kiswahili explanation**

**How to use it:** Same help in Kiswahili.

```
Explain [topic] in simple Kiswahili for a child aged [age]. Use one example from daily life and keep it short. End with one question to check understanding.
```

**Step-by-step breakdown**

**How to use it:** For topics with many steps, like maths or science.

```
Break down [topic or problem] into 5 simple steps for a [age]-year-old. Number the steps, keep each step to one or two lines, and give one worked example.
```

**Chapter-style retelling**

**How to use it:** History and social studies topics.

```
Explain [topic, e.g. Kenya's independence] in simple English that a 10-year-old can retell to a friend. Use a short story style with a beginning, middle, and end. Keep it under 200 words.
```

**Bedtime story (English)**

**How to use it:** Personalise every night.

```
Write a calm bedtime story for a [age]-year-old named [name] who loves [animal/theme]. The story should teach [lesson, e.g. sharing]. Make it gentle, positive, and about 4 minutes read aloud. End with a soft, sleepy closing line.
```

**Bedtime story (Kiswahili)**

**How to use it:** Swahili version of the same magic.

```
Andika hadithi fupi ya kulala kwa mtoto wa miaka [age] anayeitwa [name] anayependa [animal/theme]. Hadithi ifundishe [lesson]. Iwe tulivu, nzuri, na isomwe kwa dakika 4. Maliza kwa sentensi laini ya usingizi.
```

**Homework helper rules**

**How to use it:** Print or screenshot the family rules.

```
Write a simple list of 5 rules for how kids in our family use AI for schoolwork. The rules should cover: no copy-paste, always check facts, homework first, use in a shared space, and tell a parent before using it. Write it so a 10-year-old can read it.
```

**Weekly learning routine**

**How to use it:** Build the family habit.

```
Suggest a simple 30-minute weekly learning routine for a family with kids aged [ages]. Alternate English and Kiswahili weeks, and include: one homework explanation, one bedtime story, and a 'teach the parent' chat. Keep it easy to follow.
```

**Quiz me**

**How to use it:** After studying a topic, test understanding.

```
My child just learned [topic]. Give me 3 short questions to test their understanding, ordered from easy to harder, plus the answers. Keep questions suitable for a [age]-year-old.
```

**Correct without discouraging**

**How to use it:** When your child wrote something wrong.

```
Here is my [age]-year-old's answer about [topic]. Correct it gently: point out what is right, fix the wrong part in simple words, and give a short example of a better answer without shaming them. Answer: [paste]
```

## Action plan

1. Pick one homework topic from this week and run the simple explanation prompt.
2. Tonight, generate one personalised bedtime story and read it aloud.
3. Write down the five homework rules and discuss them with your child.
4. Set your weekly family learning evening on the calendar.
5. Try a Kiswahili explanation or story once this week.
6. Save your favourite prompts in your phone's notes.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Letting AI explain in adult language that confuses the child more
- Accepting AI answers without checking the textbook
- Letting kids copy-paste and call it homework
- Using AI alone in bedrooms without supervision or rules
- Making the routine too big and quitting after a week
- Reading AI stories without making them personal to your child
