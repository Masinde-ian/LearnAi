# AI Automation Agency Masterclass: Sell AI Services to Businesses

## Overview

This program turns you from a freelancer into an agency owner who sells automation to local businesses. You will learn which Kenyan businesses pay most for automation, build your first three offers (chatbots, lead capture, reporting), price them as retainers, and close your first clients with proven WhatsApp scripts. You will also learn how to deliver, document, and maintain client automations so you get paid monthly instead of chasing one-off jobs. You join a WhatsApp cohort with weekly office hours and leave with a full client delivery kit.

## Who this is for

- A freelancer already doing admin, social, or virtual assistant work who wants bigger monthly contracts.
- A developer or no-code builder who can wire tools together but has never sold to a business owner.
- A former business owner or salesperson who understands local businesses and wants to add automation services.
- A student who has completed free tutorials but needs a real business structure and clients.
- Anyone tired of one-off gigs and ready for KES 15,000-50,000 retainers.

## What you'll be able to do

- Identify which local businesses in your area most need automation and can afford it.
- Build and sell your first 3 offers: a WhatsApp chatbot, a lead capture system, and a reporting dashboard.
- Package and price services as retainers (setup + monthly fee) instead of one-off projects.
- Run a sales process with scripts for cold outreach, discovery, pricing, and follow-up.
- Deliver, document, and maintain client automations so clients stay and refer.
- Scale from solo operator to small agency with systems, and handle multiple clients without chaos.

## Module 1: Know Which Businesses Pay and Why

Not every business needs automation, and even fewer can pay. Your job is to target the intersection: painful problem + money to pay.

### The highest-value local sectors in Kenya

- **Clinics and health facilities.** Missed calls, appointment no-shows, patient follow-ups. Pain is daily and real.
- **Salons and barber shops.** Booking conflicts, missed calls, no reminder system. Loyal customer base, willing to pay small monthly fees.
- **Restaurants and food businesses.** WhatsApp orders, order status updates, customer follow-ups, repeat order reminders.
- **Logistics and couriers.** Delivery tracking updates, status notifications to customers, proof of delivery records.
- **Schools and tuition centres.** Parent communication, fee reminders, admission inquiry responses, report distribution.
- **Real estate agencies.** Lead capture from inquiries, property follow-ups, viewing scheduling, buyer-seller matching.
- **E-commerce sellers.** Order confirmations, delivery updates, abandoned-cart follow-ups, customer support.
- **SACCOs and chamas.** Member reminders, meeting notices, loan status updates, transaction confirmations.

### How to score a prospect

Ask four questions before you invest time in a prospect:

1. **How often does this business answer the same question?** (FAQ load = chatbot opportunity)
2. **How much money is lost when a lead or booking is missed?** (missed revenue = urgency)
3. **Who answers the phone and are they overwhelmed?** (overworked owner = pain)
4. **Does the business have steady income to pay a monthly fee?** (cashflow = ability to pay)

Score each prospect 1-5. Only pitch businesses scoring 15+. You will close far more by pitching 10 good prospects than 100 random ones.

### Research worksheet

Pick one sector in your area. Then:

1. List 20 businesses in that sector within reach (your estate, your town, your network).
2. For each, note how they take bookings/orders/leads today (from their WhatsApp status, catalogue, page, or a quick visit).
3. Mark the 5 with the clearest painful problem and steady cashflow.
4. Find the decision maker's name (the owner, or the manager who handles bookings).

## Module 2: Build Your First Three Offers

Keep it to three offers. Three is enough to fill a pipeline and simple enough for a solo operator to deliver well. Master them, then add more.

### Offer 1: WhatsApp AI chatbot

**What it does.** Answers the business's most common customer questions instantly on WhatsApp, captures leads, and hands over to a human when needed. Works on WhatsApp Business API through a BSP (business solution provider) or a simpler no-code chat tool.

**Who buys it.** Clinics (hours, booking, pricing), restaurants (menu, orders, location), salons (services, pricing, bookings), schools (admissions, fees, deadlines).

**Minimum setup.** A saved-replies system can fake it; a real bot uses a BSP. Start with the business's 20 most common questions and answers, then automate handoff to the owner.

**Deliverable.** Bot live on their WhatsApp, a "handoff to human" trigger, and a training note.

### Offer 2: Lead capture system

**What it does.** Turns scattered inquiries (WhatsApp, Facebook, calls) into one clean list with names, numbers, and follow-up timestamps, so no lead is forgotten.

**Who buys it.** Real estate, schools, clinics, e-commerce, and any business where "we forgot to follow up" means lost money.

**Minimum setup.** A form or chatbot that asks the 3-4 capture questions, pushes entries to a simple spreadsheet or CRM, and schedules a follow-up reminder.

**Deliverable.** The capture form, the live list, a weekly follow-up reminder, and a simple reporting sheet.

### Offer 3: Reporting dashboard

**What it does.** Gives the owner a weekly picture: leads captured, sales closed, busy days, response times. Owners pay for visibility into their own business.

**Who buys it.** Any business with a till, orders, or bookings. Restaurants, clinics, e-commerce, and SACCOs especially.

**Minimum setup.** A weekly automated summary sent to the owner on WhatsApp or email, built from their sales log or their bot's data.

**Deliverable.** The dashboard (even a Google Sheet styled simply) and the weekly summary message.

### The offer structure template

For each offer use the same skeleton:

- **The result:** one concrete outcome (e.g., "no booking inquiry goes unanswered").
- **The deliverable:** what you actually install and hand over.
- **The setup fee:** one-off build cost.
- **The monthly fee:** ongoing maintenance, updates, and reporting.

### Worksheet: your first offer pack

1. Pick your strongest sector from Module 1.
2. Write Offer 1 (chatbot) for that sector: result, deliverable, setup fee, monthly fee.
3. Write Offer 2 (lead capture) for the same sector.
4. Write Offer 3 (reporting) for the same sector.
5. Write one line for each explaining who the buyer is and what they lose today.

## Module 3: Price as Retainers, Not Projects

The single biggest pricing mistake new agency owners make is quoting a one-off price. A one-off project ends. A retainer pays you every month. In Kenya in 2026, the realistic market is:

- **Chatbot:** KES 15,000-30,000 setup, KES 4,000-8,000/month.
- **Lead capture:** KES 10,000-20,000 setup, KES 4,000-7,000/month.
- **Reporting:** KES 5,000-10,000 setup, KES 3,000-5,000/month.
- **Full stack (all three):** KES 25,000-50,000 setup, KES 8,000-15,000/month.

These are ranges, not laws. Adjust for your market, your proof, and the client's size. A clinic in Nairobi's CBD pays more than a salon in a smaller town. Do not invent exact numbers in your quotes unless you have a reason; anchor with a range and a clear value story.

### Why retainers win

- **Predictable revenue.** Three clients at KES 10,000/month is KES 30,000/month before you do anything.
- **You are invested in their success.** A one-off buyer forgets you in a month. A retainer client sees value every week.
- **Maintenance is real work.** Bots break, WhatsApp numbers change, menus update. Clients genuinely need monthly care.
- **Your reporting offer needs recurring data.** You cannot report on a system you no longer touch.

### The retainer pricing script (what to say)

- Anchor the problem first: "Right now every missed call costs you about KES X."
- Quote the setup as an investment: "A one-time build of KES 20,000 gets it live in a week."
- Frame the monthly fee as maintenance, not cost: "KES 6,000/month keeps it updated, and sends you a weekly report."
- Always bundle the trial: "First month includes free updates and priority support so you can see it work."

### Worksheet: pricing and packaging

1. Write your setup fee and monthly fee for each of the three offers in your target sector.
2. Write your full-stack bundle price.
3. Write the value story you will use: what the client loses per month today vs what you cost per month.
4. Write your discount policy (e.g., "pay 2 months upfront, get month 3 half price").
5. Write a 3-line retainer agreement summary you can send on WhatsApp.

## Module 4: Sales Scripts and Outreach

Selling to local businesses is relationship work, not volume spam. Here is the system.

### The outreach sequence

1. **Warm-up (day 1).** A personalised WhatsApp or call. Mention something specific about their business. Give one free useful tip. No pitch.
2. **The pitch (day 3-4).** Short message naming the problem you noticed and offering a free 15-minute audit.
3. **The audit call.** Ask questions, show the problem in their numbers, propose the fix. Close with a trial offer.
4. **The proposal (same day).** Send a simple one-page offer: result, deliverables, setup, monthly fee.
5. **Follow-up (days 5, 9, 14).** Value-add follow-ups, not pressure. Share a small tip or a relevant case study.
6. **Close.** Get a yes on the trial, then on the full retainer.

### The discovery call script (15 minutes)

- Open: "Thanks for your time. I want to understand how {business} runs today."
- Ask 4 questions: How do customers reach you? What happens when you are busy or asleep? What do you estimate you lose in missed sales? Would a weekly report of your numbers help you?
- Summarise: "So you lose about KES X a month to missed calls, and you have no record of who asked and what happened."
- Propose: "Here is what I can do for KES Y setup and Z per month. You get it live this week. First month includes free changes."
- Next step: "Send me the till number for the setup fee today and I start tomorrow."

### Objections and answers

- "It is too expensive." Reply: "Compared to the KES X you are losing every month, this pays for itself in the first week. What if we start with the reporting offer alone?"
- "I will think about it." Reply: "Fair. What specifically would you like to think about? Price, or whether it will work for your business?"
- "Let me ask my partner." Reply: "Great. When can I call you both together? I will bring a one-page summary."
- "I was scammed before." Reply: "I understand. That is why I offer a trial: pay half the setup, and if it does not work in 2 weeks, you pay nothing more."

### Worksheet: your scripts

1. Write your warm-up message for your 5 target businesses.
2. Write your pitch message (under 90 words).
3. Write your discovery call questions.
4. Write your objection answers for price, timing, and trust.
5. Write your close and your follow-up messages (all three).

## Module 5: Deliver, Document, Maintain

Agencies die in delivery. Over-promising, messy handovers, and abandoned bots destroy referrals. Do the opposite.

### Delivery checklist for every build

1. Build on a test account or test number first. Never experiment on a live client.
2. Test every flow: customer question, bot reply, handoff, capture, and report.
3. Hand over a one-page user guide in plain English (the owner will not read a manual).
4. Run a 30-minute training call and record it.
5. Document everything you built in a simple "system card": what it does, where it lives, how to change it, who to call.
6. Send the invoice via M-Pesa or invoice link the day you go live.

### Maintenance is your monthly income

Set a monthly maintenance routine for every retainer client:

- Update answers (new prices, new services, new hours).
- Check uptime and test 3 flows with real messages.
- Send the weekly report on time.
- Log anything the client asked to change.
- Have a "one change per month included" rule; extra changes billed separately.

### The system card template

Keep one card per client:

- Client name, sector, contact person.
- Offer(s) live: chatbot / lead capture / reporting.
- Platform and login details (in a password manager, never in chat).
- All connected tools and what they do.
- Monthly fee, payment day, and payment method.
- Last tested date and next test date.

### Worksheet: delivery system

1. Write your system card template.
2. Write your one-page user guide template for a client.
3. Write your monthly maintenance checklist.
4. Write your "changes are billed extra" policy in one polite line.
5. Test your first real build on a friend's business or a mock client before selling it live.

## Module 6: Scale the Agency

Scaling is not working harder; it is systemising so others can do the work. You scale in three moves.

### Move 1: Systemise

Write every task as a checklist. A checklist is a job an assistant can do. Document your build process, your onboarding, your monthly maintenance, and your sales follow-ups. If it is not written down, it is not a system yet.

### Move 2: Hire smart

When you have 3-5 paying clients and you are the bottleneck, hire:

- A **builder** (no-code, chatbot setup, reporting) for the technical work.
- A **maintenance person** for the monthly checklists and client care.
- A **sales closer** once you cannot do outreach yourself.

Hire part-time first. Pay per completed task or a small monthly fee. A reliable part-timer who follows your checklist is worth more than a brilliant one who improvises.

### Move 3: Package for leverage

- Create standardised packages so every new client buys the same thing. Custom work is a time trap.
- Build templates for each sector: a clinic package, a restaurant package, a real estate package.
- Raise prices as your proof grows. Every 3 testimonials, review your rates.
- Move to a simple client portal or CRM when you pass 5 clients. Spreadsheets break at scale.

### The one-person-to-agency math

- Client 1-3: you do everything.
- Client 4-6: you build and sell; a part-timer maintains.
- Client 7-10: you sell and manage; the team builds and maintains.
- Beyond 10: you run the business and the sales process, and your team delivers.

### Worksheet: scaling plan

1. Write the 3 biggest time drains in your current work.
2. Turn each into a checklist that a part-timer could follow.
3. Write your first job advert (part-time, task-based).
4. Write your standardised package for your main sector.
5. Set your price-review date (every 3 testimonials or every 60 days).

## Module 7: Case Studies

Real shapes, real numbers, names changed.

### Case study 1: Wanjiku, from salon worker to chatbot provider

Wanjiku worked at a salon and saw clients turn up with no appointment, or not turn up at all. She learned a simple WhatsApp bot builder, then pitched her own boss: "Pay me KES 3,000/month and I will set up a booking bot." Two weeks later, no-shows dropped and the salon took KES 2,000 more in weekly bookings. Wanjiku used that win to pitch three other salons in the same estate, each at KES 5,000/month setup plus KES 4,000/month. By month 3 she had 5 clients and hired a friend to do her maintenance visits.

The lesson: start with a sector you know personally, prove it on one client, then use that proof to pitch the same type of business.

### Case study 2: Otieno, the reporting specialist

Otieno was a freelance bookkeeper who noticed clients never knew their numbers until month-end. He built a simple weekly report: sales by day, top products, busy hours, response times to WhatsApp inquiries. He sold it as a KES 4,000/month add-on to his existing bookkeeping clients. Every client who took it stayed on it for a year. From that base he built a full lead-capture system for his best client, an e-commerce seller, at KES 20,000 setup plus KES 8,000/month.

The lesson: add-on offers to people who already pay you are the cheapest sales you will ever make.

### Case study 3: Mwangi, the school one

Mwangi targeted private schools and tuition centres. He offered a parent-comms bot: fee reminders, admission inquiry replies, and meeting notices, all on WhatsApp. One school took the KES 25,000 setup trial at KES 10,000. The head teacher was impressed, signed the full retainer, and referred him to two more schools at a parent-teachers meeting. By month 4 Mwangi had 4 school clients and a part-time builder handling maintenance.

The lesson: sectors where the decision maker talks to other decision makers (school heads, clinic managers) give you free referrals. Deliver brilliantly for one, and the network sells for you.

### What all three share

- They targeted one sector they knew and could walk into.
- They proved the result on one client before scaling.
- They sold retainers, not one-off projects.
- They documented their work and made maintenance a service, not a favour.
- They used one happy client to sell the next one.

## Module 8: The 90-Day Roadmap

Follow this and post your numbers every Sunday in the cohort group.

### Days 1-14 (Foundation)

- Complete Module 1 research. Pick your sector and list 20 targets.
- Complete Module 2 and build your three offers on paper with prices.
- Build your first real demo (a mock business or a friend's business) end to end.
- Write your scripts: warm-up, pitch, discovery, objections, close.
- Set up your delivery kit: system card template, user guide template, maintenance checklist.

### Days 15-30 (First clients)

- Send your warm-up messages to your 5 best targets.
- Book and run your first 5 discovery calls.
- Offer a discounted trial to your first client if needed to build proof.
- Deliver the trial brilliantly and log everything.
- Ask the trial client for a testimonial in writing.

### Days 31-60 (Consistency)

- Pitch the full retainer to your trial client and to 5 new targets.
- Target 2 closed clients on retainers.
- Build the weekly report habit for every client.
- Write your first case study from a real client result.
- Raise prices for new prospects once you have 2 testimonials.

### Days 61-90 (Scale)

- Reach 3-5 paying retainer clients.
- Systemise: turn your delivery and maintenance into checklists.
- Hire a part-time maintenance person for the client work.
- Run your outreach on a repeating cadence so the pipeline never empties.
- Set your month 4 target: more clients, higher prices, or both.

### Weekly rhythm

- Monday: plan outreach for the week.
- Daily: 30-60 minutes of outreach, delivery, or maintenance.
- Friday: run your client reports.
- Sunday: post numbers to the cohort group.

## Prompt Library

Use these with ChatGPT or Claude. Fill the blanks, edit the output, and make it sound like you.

**Sector opportunity scan**

Use to decide which sector to attack in your area.

```
I want to start an AI automation agency in {area, e.g., Nairobi / Eldoret / Mombasa} selling to local businesses. For each of these sectors — clinics, salons, restaurants, logistics, schools, real estate, e-commerce, SACCOs — list: the most painful recurring problem, what it costs the owner monthly, how likely they are to pay a monthly retainer, and how I can reach them locally. **Pain-point audit message**
```

Use as the warm-up message that adds value before you pitch.

```
Write a warm-up WhatsApp message to {owner name} of {business name}, a {type of business} in {area}. I noticed {specific observation}. Offer one free, genuinely useful tip related to their business that I can deliver with AI. No sales pitch. Under 80 words. Tone: respectful, knowledgeable, warm.
```

**Pitch message**

Use for the day-3 pitch.

```
Write a short pitch message to {owner name} at {business name}, a {type of business}. Their biggest problem is {problem}. Offer a free 15-minute audit where I will show exactly how much they are losing and how a {chatbot / lead capture system / reporting system} fixes it. Include a clear question to book the call. Under 90 words, no hard sell, no exclamation marks.
```

**Discovery call questions**

Use to prepare for the 15-minute audit call.

```
Generate a 15-minute discovery call script for selling a {chatbot / lead capture / reporting} system to {business type}. Include: an opening that builds trust, 4-6 questions that uncover the money being lost, a summary section where I restate their problem with their own numbers, and a transition into my offer. Keep questions short and natural.
```

**Problem quantification**

Use to put a KES figure on the problem during discovery.


## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Pitching every sector. Focus one sector, get proof, then expand.
- Quoting one-off prices. Sell retainers or you will never scale.
- Building before selling. Sell the trial first, then build.
- Over-customising. Standard packages are easier to deliver and maintain.
- Forgetting maintenance. Monthly care is your income, not a favour.
- Not documenting. Without checklists you cannot hire or scale.
- No follow-up. The second and third touches close most deals.
- Handling everything yourself. Systemise, then delegate, then grow.
