## Module 1: The Kenya e-commerce opportunity (and the real numbers)

Most people lose in e-commerce before they even open a store — they copy what worked in the US and ignore how Kenya actually buys online. Kenya is a mobile-first, M-Pesa-first market. Roughly 65% of e-commerce payments go through M-Pesa. Your buyers are on their phones, they pay with STK push, and they expect WhatsApp support. Design for that and you are already ahead of most online sellers.

The other reality: most traffic for marketplace sellers is concentrated. Jumia accounts for around 40% of marketplace traffic, and over 60% of orders come from Nairobi and its surrounds. That does not mean you must sell on Jumia — it means your own store must target where the money is, then expand outward. Delivery expectations are same-day or next-day in Nairobi; outside Nairobi you plan for courier and M-Pesa on delivery.

**The three layers of a Kenya e-commerce business:**

- **Store** — where you present products and take payment (Shopify or a local alternative)
- **Traffic** — where buyers find you (Meta ads, TikTok, WhatsApp, marketplace listings)
- **Operations** — how you fulfill, support, and report (AI handles most of this)

Most beginners obsess over the store and starve operations. The winners treat all three as one machine.

**Do this:** write down your current situation — products you have, money you can invest, time per week. That is your starting point for the whole program.

**Not this:** spending a month "perfecting" the store before you have even validated a product. Validation first, polish later.

## Module 2: Product sourcing and selection

The product decides everything: your margin, your ad costs, your repeat purchases. AI turns product selection from a gut feeling into a research process.

**What to look for in a first product:**

- **Problem or desire** — does it solve a pain or give status/pleasure? Kenya-specific problems win (phone accessories, home organization, beauty, baby gear, fitness, office setup)
- **Margin after all costs** — target at least 50% gross margin so you can afford ads and still profit
- **Light to ship** — heavy products eat profit in courier fees
- **Repeat or add-on potential** — one product that leads to a second purchase
- **Suppliable at volume** — can your supplier actually deliver 50, 100, 500 units?

**Sourcing routes in Kenya:**

- **Local wholesalers** — fastest delivery, no customs risk; margins thinner but you test fast
- **China suppliers (Alibaba, 1688)** — best margins at volume, but you wait 3–6 weeks and pay customs
- **Dropshipping** — no stock risk, but long delivery kills repeat buyers and refunds hurt
- **Making your own** — best control, but scaling depends on your capacity

Use AI to generate product ideas against your constraints, then verify demand with actual search and marketplace data before you spend a shilling on stock.

**Worksheet 2A — Product scorecard:** score every candidate out of 100 — demand evidence (25), margin after all costs (25), shipping weight (15), supplier reliability (20), competition intensity (15). Only products scoring 70+ move forward.

**Do this:** shortlist 10 products in week 1, score them, and commit to only the top 2–3 for validation.

**Not this:** falling in love with a product because you like it. Your opinion is not market demand.

## Module 3: Pricing for profit

Pricing is where beginners destroy their business. They price against the competitor down the street instead of pricing against their own costs. Price from the numbers.

**Your pricing stack (all in KES):**

| Item | Example |
|------|---------|
| Product cost | KES 800 |
| Shipping in (bulk) | KES 100 per unit |
| Payment fees (~1.5–3.5%) | KES 90 at 3% |
| Courier to customer | KES 250 |
| **Total cost** | **KES 1,240** |
| Selling price | KES 2,500 |
| Gross margin | 50.4% |
| Margin per unit | KES 1,260 |

That KES 1,260 margin must then cover ads, returns, packaging, and your profit. That is why you price at 2.5–3x your landed cost for products under KES 3,000. For bigger items (KES 5,000+) you can survive on a flatter multiplier because the absolute margin is bigger.

**The KES 4,000 free-shipping threshold.** Research and Kenyan store practice show that a free-shipping threshold around KES 4,000 lifts average order value. Set free shipping at KES 4,000 and add a "you are KES 900 away from free delivery" nudge at checkout. You will see buyers add one more item.

**Price anchoring:** show the crossed-out "before" price when a product has a genuine comparison, offer bundles ("buy 2, save 15%"), and keep a clear most-popular option in the middle. AI drafts all the copy that makes these prices feel like a deal.

**Worksheet 3A — Pricing calculator:** for each product, build a table with landed cost, payment fees, courier, target margin %, and suggested price. AI does the math if you give it the inputs.

**Do this:** run the margin prompt below for your top 3 products and set prices before you build the store.

**Not this:** offering free delivery on everything. Free delivery is a marketing lever, not a default.

## Module 4: Store setup with M-Pesa checkout

You do not need a complicated store. You need a store that loads fast on a phone and takes M-Pesa. That is the entire brief.

**Shopify route:** Shopify starts around KES 3,500/month (basic plan). Connect M-Pesa through a payment gateway like Pesapal or IntaSend, which process M-Pesa payments for fees in the 1.5–3.5% range. The buyer pays by STK push; money lands in your M-Pesa or bank account. You get a proper checkout, order tracking, and integrations with couriers like Sendy, Courier Plus, and Wellmax.

**Local alternative:** if Shopify's monthly cost and USD billing is a problem, some sellers run a WhatsApp catalogue plus a simple landing page and take M-Pesa directly to a paybill or till. That works, but you lose the cart and the tracking. Start with what you can run consistently, upgrade to a real store as revenue allows.

**Non-negotiables for a Kenya store:**

- M-Pesa checkout front and centre — never make a Kenyan buyer hunt for a payment option
- Mobile-optimized product pages that load in under 3 seconds
- WhatsApp Business linked so support is one tap away
- Clear delivery times and costs stated before checkout
- Trust signals: real photos, testimonials, a returns policy in simple English

**Worksheet 4A — Store setup checklist:** domain, payment gateway connected, courier selected, 3 test orders placed by yourself, WhatsApp Business connected, policy pages written.

**Do this:** in week 2, buy your domain and connect your payment gateway. Place your own test order end to end so you feel what buyers feel.

**Not this:** adding ten apps and widgets on day one. Every app slows your store and confuses buyers. Start minimal.

## Module 5: AI-driven ad creative and copywriting at scale

Advertising is a volume game. One ad is a test; twenty is a system. AI is the reason a solo seller in Nairobi can produce like a full creative agency.

**The creative stack:**

- **Product descriptions** — generate 5 versions per product, A/B test on the store
- **Ad copy** — short, benefit-first, Kenya-real language, one CTA per ad
- **Ad creative** — Canva templates for static ads, CapCut for short video ads, AI to write hooks and scripts
- **Angles** — every product has 5–10 angles (problem, results, social proof, urgency, price, comparison). AI drafts ads for each angle so you can test which one lands.

**Copy that converts in Kenya:**

- Lead with the outcome, not the feature ("Charge your phone in 25 minutes" not "20000mAh fast-charging power bank")
- Use specific numbers and prices
- Name the audience ("For long-distance matatu crews and daily commuters")
- Keep it short — phone screens, short attention
- One clear CTA: "Order now on WhatsApp" or "Shop now — M-Pesa accepted"

**Video ads:** the hook in the first 2 seconds is everything. Write 5 hooks per product with AI, film simple phone video or build slideshows in CapCut, and run 3–5 creatives per campaign. AI also writes your captions, subtitles, and the script so the whole ad is on-message.

**Worksheet 5A — Creative bank:** for your hero product, fill a table: angle, headline, body copy, visual idea, CTA. Aim for 10 rows. These become your first ad set.

**Do this:** produce 10 pieces of creative for your hero product before you spend a single shilling on ads.

**Not this:** running one ad and declaring "ads don't work." One ad is a single coin flip. Ten is a test.

## Module 6: Campaign optimization with real numbers

Most people lose money on ads because they guess. The fix is a spend ratio — the amount of sales money you generate per shilling of ad spend — and letting the numbers tell you what to do next.

**The metric that matters: break-even return on ad spend (ROAS).**

- If your margin is 50%, you need a ROAS of at least 2.0 to break even on ads (every KES 1 of ad spend must return KES 2 in sales, because half goes to cost).
- If your margin is 30%, you need a ROAS of 3.3.
- Below break-even, you are paying to lose money on every order.

**The campaign structure that scales in Kenya:**

1. **Testing phase** — 3–5 creatives, small daily budget (KES 500–1,500/day), broad audience
2. **Read the data** — after 3–5 days, cut what is not working, double down on winners
3. **Scaling phase** — increase budget in steps of 20–30%, not jumps of 300%
4. **Retargeting** — a separate set of ads for people who visited but did not buy

**Retargeting is where the profit hides.** Most visitors will not buy on the first visit. A retargeting ad that shows the product again, plus a small incentive ("free delivery this weekend"), recovers buyers you already paid to attract.

**Worksheet 6A — Ad tracker:** a weekly sheet with columns: campaign, creative, spend, sales, orders, ROAS, cost per order. Update it every Monday. If you do nothing else, you do this.

**Do this:** set your break-even ROAS before launch, and make every scaling decision against it.

**Not this:** doubling the budget on a losing ad hoping it turns around. Money follows proof, not hope.

## Module 7: Automating operations

Now the machine runs while you sleep. Customer service, fulfillment, and reporting are the three operations that eat your time — and all three are automatable in Kenya with the tools available.

**Customer service:**

- Build an FAQ from your 20 most common questions and load it into a chatbot or auto-reply on WhatsApp Business
- Use AI to draft instant responses to common messages — order status, delivery times, returns, price questions
- Escalate anything unusual to you, personally

**Fulfillment:**

- Write a fulfillment SOP (order received → confirm on WhatsApp → pack → courier pickup → send tracking → request review) and have AI maintain the templates
- Batch your packing times so you pick up once or twice a day
- Standardize packaging so every order looks the same

**Reporting:**

- Every Monday, paste your week's orders into AI and get a report: revenue, orders, best product, cost per order, ROAS, cash position
- Track a simple dashboard in Google Sheets so the numbers are never in your head

**Worksheet 7A — SOP library:** write out your three SOPs (customer service, fulfillment, weekly reporting) with AI's help. These are the playbook you hand to a helper or VA later.

**Do this:** in week 4, automate your WhatsApp replies and run your first AI weekly report.

**Not this:** answering "where is my order?" 40 times a day by hand. The 40th person gets a worse answer than the first. Automate it.

## Module 8: Scaling to multiple products and brands

Once one product has proven margin and repeat buyers, you scale — not by adding 50 random products, but by building a system that launches products on purpose.

**The scaling ladder:**

1. **First product proven** — consistent orders, positive ROAS, repeat buyers
2. **Adjacent products** — same customer, same sourcing route, same marketing angles
3. **Product lines** — group products into categories with their own store sections
4. **Multiple brands** — each brand gets its own positioning, its own audience, its own store

**When to launch the second product:** when the first product's profit can fund it without touching your personal money, and when you have a repeatable launch checklist. AI writes the entire launch — descriptions, ads, WhatsApp broadcast, landing copy — in one session.

**The reinvestment rule (real numbers):** take 30–50% of profit and put it back into ads and stock for the next product. The remaining profit is yours. This is how a KES 20,000 start becomes KES 100,000/month over time — the compounding comes from reinvestment, not from bigger loans.

**Worksheet 8A — Launch checklist:** validation → stock order → store listing → 10 creatives → WhatsApp broadcast → ad launch → fulfillment test → review. Reuse it for every new product.

**Do this:** before launching anything new, run the reinvestment prompt and confirm the numbers fund the launch.

**Not this:** spreading a small budget across ten products. One product done right beats ten products done halfway.

## Module 9: Case studies — what real Kenyan stores did

**Case study 1: The power-bank seller who found the margin.** A Nairobi seller imported power banks at KES 900 landed cost, priced them at KES 2,800, and ran Meta ads at KES 800/day with a target ROAS of 2.5. First week: KES 8,000 spend, KES 22,400 sales, ROAS 2.8 — profit after product cost of KES 5,600. He reinvested half into ads and half into a second product line (cables and car chargers) that shared the same buyers. Within three months his store had 12 SKUs and a monthly revenue around KES 150,000.

**Case study 2: The WhatsApp store that grew up.** A home-organizer seller ran everything on WhatsApp statuses and a paybill. She hit a ceiling because buyers could not see a catalogue and abandoned purchases. She moved to a Shopify store with M-Pesa via IntaSend, kept WhatsApp for support, and set free shipping at KES 4,000. Average order value jumped from KES 1,800 to KES 3,400 within a month — the threshold did the selling for her.

**Case study 3: The retargeting recovery.** A beauty-products store was spending KES 15,000/month on ads and breaking even. Adding a retargeting campaign — the same product, a free-delivery weekend incentive, shown only to people who had visited — recovered enough abandoned buyers to push overall ROAS from 2.0 to 3.1. The fix was not more creative; it was following up with people already interested.

Each of these started with the same discipline: validate a product, price from costs, test ads with numbers, and reinvest the profit. No luck involved.

## Prompt Library

Copy these into ChatGPT (or Claude/Gemini), fill the slots in brackets, and adapt. Each prompt is one job in your system.

**Product idea generation**

Use when you have constraints but no product yet.

```
Generate 15 e-commerce product ideas suitable for the Kenyan market.
I can invest KES [X] for initial stock, want a gross margin of at
least 50%, and the products should be light to ship and mobile-first.
For each idea give: the problem it solves, likely cost and selling
price in KES, and the risk in one line. End with the 3 you would test.
```

**Demand validation research**

Use before you buy stock.

```
I want to sell [product] in Kenya. Research the demand: search volume
trends, who buys it, competitors selling it in Kenya, and typical
prices. Then tell me the 3 strongest signs that demand is real and
the 3 biggest risks. If demand looks weak, suggest 3 similar products.
```

**Supplier outreach message**

Use to contact a supplier on WhatsApp or email.

```
Write a short, professional message to a supplier asking for a
quotation for [product]. I need: unit price, MOQ, delivery time to
Kenya, shipping cost, and payment terms. I am based in [town/city].
Keep it under 80 words and end with a clear question.
```

**Supplier negotiation script**

Use when the first quote comes back high.

```
A supplier quoted me KES [X] per unit for [product] with a MOQ of [Y].
Write a polite negotiation message that asks for a better price at a
higher volume (I can commit to [Z] units), asks what shipping
discounts are available, and asks about payment terms. Keep it
professional and pressure-free.
```

**Margin and break-even calculator**

Use for every product before pricing.

```
Calculate my margin for [product]. Landed cost is KES [X] per unit,
payment gateway fee is [Y]%, average courier cost is KES [Z], and
packaging is KES [W]. Show: total cost, and the selling price needed
for 40%, 50%, and 60% gross margin. For each, show break-even ROAS
(margin percentage means ROAS = 100 / margin).
```

**Pricing strategy with anchoring**

Use when setting final store prices.

```
My product costs me KES [X] all-in and comparable items sell between
KES [A] and KES [B] in Kenya. Suggest a pricing structure: regular
price, a bundle price, and a free-shipping threshold. Explain how to
display the prices so buyers see the value, and draft the "You are
KES [X] away from free delivery" message for my checkout.
```

**Store name and tagline**

Use before you buy a domain.

```
Suggest 10 store names for an online store selling [product/category]
to Kenyan customers. For each give: the name, a domain idea
(shopify or .co.ke), and a one-line tagline. Prefer short, easy-to-
spell names that work on a phone screen. Mark your top 3.
```

**Product descriptions at scale**

Use for every product listing.

```

outcome for a Kenyan buyer, mention price KES [X] and free delivery
over KES 4,000, include one social-proof line, and end with a clear

results, comparison, urgency, value. Keep each under 120 words.
```

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Building the store before validating the product
- Pricing from competitors instead of from your own costs
- Offering free shipping on everything and killing your margin
- Running one ad and concluding ads don't work
- Scaling budget faster than proof (jumps of 300% instead of 20–30%)
- Forgetting the customer is on M-Pesa — no STK push, no sale
- Answering support questions by hand instead of automating them
- Spending profit instead of reinvesting 30–50% into growth
- Launching ten products with the budget for one
