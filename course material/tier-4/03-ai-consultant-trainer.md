# Become an AI Consultant: Train & Advise Businesses on AI

## Overview

This program positions you as a credible AI consultant and trainer who sells to companies, not consumers. You will learn to run a company AI-readiness assessment, design and deliver training workshops for teams, write proposals that close, price consulting engagements by the day, and build a referral pipeline. You leave with a full client toolkit: assessment forms, workshop decks, proposal templates, and contracts. You join a WhatsApp cohort with weekly office hours, and you practise on real or mock companies from week one.

## Who this is for

- A professional (HR, operations, marketing, finance) who wants to advise companies on AI adoption.
- A trainer or teacher who wants to move from classroom training to corporate workshops.
- A freelancer who delivers AI services and wants to charge consulting rates.
- A tech person who wants to communicate with business leaders, not just engineers.
- Anyone with practical AI experience who wants a credible, structured consulting offer.

## What you'll be able to do

- Position yourself as a credible AI consultant and trainer in the Kenyan market.
- Run a structured AI-readiness assessment for a company and produce a readable report.
- Design and deliver half-day and full-day AI training workshops for teams.
- Write proposals, price consulting engagements by the day, and sign simple contracts.
- Turn client wins into case studies and build a referral pipeline.
- Keep your own AI knowledge sharp as tools evolve, so your advice stays current.

## Module 1: Position Yourself as a Credible Consultant

Nobody will pay you for "having heard of ChatGPT." Credibility is built deliberately, and in Kenya it is built on proof, referrals, and a clean professional presence.

### Your positioning statement

Write one sentence that says who you help, with what result, and how:

- Weak: "I train companies on AI."
- Strong: "I help Kenyan SME owners and managers adopt AI tools safely, so their teams save 5-10 hours a week without breaking the bank or their data."

### The credibility stack (build in order)

1. **Proof of skill.** 3-5 examples of real work: a workshop you ran, a process you improved, an assessment you completed. Even volunteer or mock work counts if it is real and documented.
2. **A simple professional presence.** A LinkedIn profile that names your niche, a WhatsApp business number, a one-page summary of your services, and a small library of free tips you share publicly.
3. **A free, visible offer.** A free AI-readiness checklist, a free "AI in your business" tip sheet, or a free mini assessment. Free value is the fastest credibility builder there is.
4. **Titles and proof artefacts.** Certificates from AI courses you actually took, and testimonials from anyone you have helped, even for free.
5. **Referrals.** One happy client introduces you to two more. Your whole pipeline should eventually run on referrals.

### The credibility do's and don'ts

- Do share what you are learning in public (short LinkedIn or WhatsApp posts).
- Do not claim expertise you do not have. Say "I specialise in AI adoption for SMEs, not in building models."
- Do name specific tools and specific results. Vagueness is the smell of a fake.
- Do not overuse buzzwords. Business leaders in Kenya want plain language and real numbers.

### Worksheet 1: Your positioning

1. Write your positioning statement (help, result, who).
2. List your 3 proof points with dates and results.
3. Write your one-page services summary.
4. Build your free offer (a checklist or tip sheet) using AI.
5. Set up or polish your LinkedIn and WhatsApp business presence.

## Module 2: The Company AI-Readiness Assessment

This is your flagship product. A company pays you to look at how it works and tell it exactly where AI helps, where it does not, and what to do first. It is the perfect first sale because it is low-risk, high-value, and leads straight to workshops and advisory retainers.

### What you assess (5 areas)

1. **Processes.** Which tasks are repetitive, manual, and frequent? (report writing, data entry, responses, scheduling)
2. **Data.** What data does the company have? Is it organised? Can AI touch it safely? (client lists, sales records, files)
3. **Tools and skills.** What tools does the team already use? How digital-literate are they?
4. **Risks.** What could go wrong? (client data leaks, wrong AI answers, staff misuse, cost blowouts)
5. **Wins.** Which 3 quick wins would show value fastest and with lowest risk?

### How to run the assessment

1. **Send a short pre-meeting questionnaire** (fill it with the owner or manager).
2. **Run a 60-90 minute session** on site or on a call. Walk through one real task end to end.
3. **Ask, don't guess.** Let them describe their own painful tasks; you listen and map.
4. **Write a short report** (2-3 pages): findings, the 3 quick wins, a simple 90-day adoption plan, and a risk warning.
5. **Present it and propose next steps:** the workshop that trains the team on the quick wins, or an advisory retainer that implements them.

### The report structure

- What we reviewed.
- What I found (plain language, 3-5 findings).
- The 3 quick wins (each with effort, cost, and expected value).
- Risks to avoid.
- Recommended next step (workshop, advisory, or both) with price.

### Worksheet 2: Assessment toolkit

1. Write your pre-meeting questionnaire (10 questions).
2. Write your 5-area assessment checklist.
3. Write your report template.
4. Write your "present the findings" pitch: how you move from assessment to workshop.
5. Run your first assessment on a real business or a mock company you invent for practice.

## Module 3: Design AI Training Workshops

Workshops are your volume product. Companies buy them because teams are scared of AI or using it badly. Your job is to convert fear into practical, safe, daily use.

### The workshop menu (start with these three)

- **Half-day: "AI Basics for Your Team" (4 hours).** What AI can and cannot do, the free tools, writing good prompts, and 3 safe daily uses for their specific business. Good for first-time buyers.
- **Full day: "AI at Work" (8 hours).** Half-day basics plus hands-on practice on the company's real tasks: drafting, data cleanup, reports, customer replies. Good for companies ready to actually adopt.
- **90 minutes: "AI for Managers."** How leaders oversee AI use, set rules, spot risks, and measure benefits. Good for leadership teams who will then buy bigger work.

### Design principles

- **Use their work, not your examples.** The workshop lands when a team rewrites their own reports, their own emails, their own processes.
- **80% practice, 20% talk.** People remember what they do, not what you say.
- **One tool per session.** Choose ChatGPT (or Claude/Gemini), and master one workflow: prompts, then a real task.
- **End with a takeaway.** Every participant leaves with a one-page cheat sheet and a "use it this week" assignment.
- **Collect feedback.** A one-page feedback form at the end gives you testimonials and improvement data.

### The workshop deck structure

1. Why AI now (2 minutes, plain language).
2. What AI is and is not (5 minutes, honest limits).
3. The 3 rules of safe use (data, verification, human review).
4. Live demo of 3 workflows on their real tasks.
5. Hands-on practice (most of the time).
6. Cheat sheet and assignment.
7. Q&A and next steps.

### Worksheet 3: Workshop design

1. Pick your first workshop (half-day basics is safest).
2. Write the agenda with timings.
3. Write the 3 real-task exercises using a sample business.
4. Write the one-page cheat sheet participants get.
5. Draft the feedback form (5 questions).

## Module 4: Deliver the Workshop

Delivery separates trainers from consultants. A company that sees your workshop as "just a training day" is a one-off sale. A company that sees you as the person who changed how their team works is a retainer.

### Delivery checklist

- Confirm equipment: projector, internet, a screen, participants with phones or laptops.
- Test your prompts live before the workshop. Demo failures happen to everyone; recover by saying "good, that's exactly the kind of failure to learn from."
- Pair participants and give each pair a real task from their own business.
- Walk the room. The best feedback is watching someone get their first good output.
- Time-box Q&A. Capture unanswered questions and answer them in a follow-up message after the workshop.
- Send the follow-up the next day: cheat sheet, recorded highlights, feedback summary, and your offer for the next step.

### Pricing your workshops

- **Half-day (4 hours):** KES 30,000-60,000 for a group.
- **Full day (8 hours):** KES 50,000-100,000 for a group.
- **Per-person alternative:** KES 1,500-3,000 per head for open workshops.
- **Advisory retainer after the workshop:** KES 40,000-80,000/month for ongoing implementation support.

These are ranges for the Kenyan SME market in 2026. Adjust for company size, your proof, and your location. Price by value: a workshop that saves a 10-person team 5 hours a week each is worth more than a "training fee."

2. Write your opening 2-minute "why AI now" talk.
3. Write your 3 rules of safe AI use.
4. Write the next-day follow-up message.

## Module 5: Proposals, Pricing, and Contracts

Most consultants lose deals on documents: the proposal is vague, the price is scary, the contract is either a joke or a wall of text. Fix all three.

### The proposal that closes

One page, five sections:

1. **Their problem** (echo their own words from the assessment or call).
2. **The result** (what changes after the workshop/engagement).
3. **What we will do** (the agenda or the deliverables list).
4. **Price and terms** (in KES, what it includes, payment schedule).
5. **Why me** (1-2 lines of proof: results, testimonials).

Send it the same day as the conversation. Speed closes deals.

### Pricing rules

- **Price by the day/workshop, not by the hour.** KES 30,000-100,000 per workshop day reads premium. KES 3,000 per hour reads casual.
- **Quote in ranges or clear numbers, never "it depends."** Give a number and a clear scope, then note what extra work costs.
- **Deposit before work.** 50% upfront, balance before or on delivery day.
- **Value framing:** the cost of the workshop vs the value of the hours saved and the mistakes avoided.

### The simple contract (what every clause must cover)

- Scope of work (what you will and will not do).
- Price, deposit, and payment method (M-Pesa till/paybill or bank).
- Dates and location.
- What the client provides (room, projector, participants, data access).
- Confidentiality (you will not share their data).
- Liability (you advise and train; they decide and verify; your advice is not legal/financial advice).
- Cancellation policy (48 hours notice, deposit rules).

You do not need a lawyer for a simple service agreement, but have one review your template once. A clean contract makes you look professional and protects you.

### Worksheet 5: Proposal and contract

2. Write your pricing sheet (assessment, half-day, full day, retainer).
3. Write your simple contract template.
4. Write your deposit and payment policy.
5. Draft your confidentiality and liability clauses in plain English.

## Module 6: Case Studies and Referral Pipeline

Consulting sells by proof. Every client becomes a case study, and every case study becomes the next sale.

### Turning a client into a case study

- Get permission in writing.
- Capture: the problem, what we did, the measurable result, the client's own words.
- Write it in plain English. Numbers beat adjectives ("saved 6 hours a week" beats "huge savings").
- Reuse it in proposals, on LinkedIn, and in your WhatsApp status.

### The referral system (ask on a schedule)

Referrals are not luck. They are a system:

1. **At delivery.** Ask every client: "Who else do you know who faces the same problem?"
2. **30 days later.** Send the case study to the original client and ask them to forward it.
3. **Quarterly.** Check in with past clients: results, needs, and referrals.
4. **The referral script:** "If you know one owner who could use this, would you introduce us? I will handle the rest."

### Your pipeline in numbers

- 1 assessment per week = 4 per month.
- Roughly 1 in 3 assessments converts to a workshop.
- Each workshop should lead to at least 1 advisory retainer or repeat workshop.
- A referral pipeline should deliver 50%+ of your new clients within 6 months.

### Worksheet 6: Referral system

1. Write your case study template.
2. Write your referral ask (delivery day version and 30-day version).
3. Write your quarterly check-in message.
4. List your first 3 target companies.
5. Write your monthly pipeline target (assessments, workshops, retainers).

## Module 7: Stay Current as AI Evolves

In 2026 tools change every few months. Your value as a consultant is that you know what is current and what is hype. Staying current is a system, not luck.

### Your weekly learning routine (2-3 hours)

- **Tuesday:** Read 2-3 trusted AI news sources and note anything that changes pricing, capability, or risk.
- **Wednesday:** Test one new tool or feature for 30 minutes on a real task.
- **Friday:** Write one short public post about what you learned. This builds your credibility and forces you to think clearly.
- **Monthly:** Redo your own workshop exercises with the newest tools so your demos are current.

### The signals to track

- Tool pricing changes (free tiers, API prices) — affects what you recommend to cost-sensitive SMEs.
- Model capability changes (what AI can now do well: images, video, analysis).
- Risk changes (data privacy, scams, AI-generated fraud) — this is where clients need you.
- New Kenyan-relevant integrations (M-Pesa, WhatsApp, local platforms).

### The honest consultant rule

If you have not tested something, say so. "I have not used this tool with your type of data yet — let me test it before I recommend it." That sentence builds more trust than any credential.

### Worksheet 7: Learning system

1. Choose your 3 trusted news sources.
2. Block your Tuesday/Wednesday/Friday learning slots in your calendar.
3. Write your first 3 "what I learned this week" posts.
4. List the tools you need to re-test this month.
5. Write your "I have not tested this yet" sentence.

## Module 8: Case Studies

Real shapes, names changed.

### Case study 1: Faith, from school teacher to corporate trainer

Faith trained teachers for 6 years. She saw schools drowning in manual reporting and parent communication. She ran a free AI basics session for her own school staff, got glowing feedback, and used that to pitch a private school: a half-day workshop for KES 40,000. The school then hired her for a full-day session and a KES 25,000/month advisory retainer. Her second client came from the first head teacher's referral at a district meeting.

The lesson: start inside an organisation you know, get proof, then let the sector network sell for you.

### Case study 2: Kamau, the operations consultant

Kamau was an operations manager who automated his own company's reporting with AI, saving his team 8 hours a week. His boss suggested he offer the same to suppliers and partners. He built a simple assessment (5 questions, one meeting, one 3-page report) and sold it at KES 15,000. Three of his first six assessments converted to half-day workshops at KES 50,000, and one converted to a KES 60,000/month advisory retainer. His secret: every report ended with a clear, cheap next step.

The lesson: real experience inside a business is your strongest proof. Package it, don't hide it.

### Case study 3: Njeri, the digital marketing freelancer

Njeri's clients kept asking "should we use AI for our marketing?" She stopped answering free and built a service: a 90-minute AI-for-marketers workshop at KES 30,000, plus a KES 40,000/month retainer where she set up their AI content pipeline. Her first two clients came from her freelancing network, and her third from a LinkedIn post describing exactly what she helped her first client achieve.

The lesson: monetise the questions you already get. If three clients ask the same question, it is a sellable workshop.

### What all three share

- They anchored on a niche they already knew (schools, operations, marketing).
- They built one flagship offer (the assessment) that led to workshops and retainers.
- They got proof fast with a low-cost first delivery.
- They let referrals do the heavy lifting after the first few wins.
- They stayed current by testing tools on real tasks every week.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Waiting for "enough" experience. Your first paid job builds it.
- Vague positioning. Name your niche and your result.
- Selling hours instead of outcomes. Price the workshop by value.
- A proposal that is vague or late. Send it the same day.
- No contract. A simple agreement protects you and looks professional.
- Forgetting follow-up. The workshop is the beginning, not the end.
- Recommending tools you have not tested. Say so honestly.
- Letting the pipeline go cold. Referrals need a schedule, not luck.
