## Module 1: Choose a Profitable AI Business Model

The first mistake beginners make is picking a model they love instead of one the market pays for. In Kenya, people pay for outcomes, not for "artificial intelligence." Here are the models with proven local demand in 2026.

### The shortlist

- **AI done-for-you content.** CVs, cover letters, social media content packs, product descriptions, WhatsApp catalogue copy, church and business notices. Low start cost, fast first sale.
- **AI writing and admin services.** Reports, proposals, grant drafts, letters, meeting summaries, data cleanup. Sells to small businesses, NGOs, and SACCOs.
- **AI-powered digital products.** Guides, templates, prompt packs, planners, checklists. Sell once, deliver forever, zero marginal cost.
- **AI local services.** Real estate listing packs, shop product descriptions, restaurant menu and promo content, school revision packs. Local niches pay well and refer well.
- **AI virtual assistant services.** Calendar, inbox, follow-ups, research, CRM cleanup. Sells to busy owners and the diaspora.
- **AI chatbots and automations.** For businesses that get the same questions daily. Higher ticket, needs a little more skill (see the Automation Agency course).

### How to choose (the 3-filter test)

Run every idea through three filters:

1. **Does it solve a painful, recurring problem?** People pay monthly for pain that repeats. A salon losing bookings to missed calls will pay for a booking system. A one-time CV buyer will not pay monthly.
2. **Can you sell it on WhatsApp in one message?** If your offer needs a 20-page explanation, it is too complicated. If it fits in one short message with a price, you can close today.
3. **Can you deliver it with AI in under a day?** Your job is to use AI to compress delivery time. The faster your delivery, the more margin you keep.

### The money math for a first offer

Use this formula when choosing:

- Price: KES 500 to KES 2,500 for a digital product or one-off service.
- To make KES 100,000/month you need roughly 40 to 200 sales, depending on price.
- At KES 1,000 each, that is 100 sales a month. That sounds hard until you sell a pack of 10 templates to a SACCO for KES 10,000.

You can also combine: a low-price product to bring people in, then a higher-priced service or package. Start low to get proof, then raise the price. Start high and you may never get your first 10 sales.

### Worksheet 1: Model selection

Answer on paper or in a note:

1. List 3 painful, recurring problems you have seen in your own community in the last month (name the people).
2. For each, write one offer you could deliver with AI in under a day.
3. Price each in KES based on what the customer currently loses by ignoring the problem.
4. Score each on the 3-filter test (1 to 5 per filter). Pick the highest score.
5. Write the name of the customer you will serve first (one specific type of person, not "everyone").

## Module 2: Build Your Offer and Price It

An offer is not a product. An offer is a promise. Here is the structure that converts:

- **The result.** What the customer gets after paying. Be concrete: "a CV that passes recruiter filters" not "a CV service."
- **The deliverable.** Exactly what lands in their hands: the file, the pack, the report.
- **The price and the payment method.** M-Pesa till or paybill number, clearly stated.
- **The guarantee.** Even a small one reduces fear. "Free revision within 48 hours." In a market where people have been scammed on WhatsApp, trust sells.

### Pricing rules for the Kenyan market

- **Use KES, not dollars.** Kenyan buyers think in KES and pay in KES. Anchoring to USD creates friction.
- **Use a low anchor.** Start with a clear entry offer (KES 500 to KES 1,500) so the first "yes" is easy. Then sell a premium package at 3 to 5 times the price.
- **Price by outcome, not hours.** A 2-hour job that saves a salon owner KES 30,000 a month in lost bookings is worth more than KES 2,000. Do not price by time.
- **Round numbers, clean message.** KES 1,000, KES 1,500, KES 2,500. Avoid awkward prices that slow down a WhatsApp reply.

### A simple 3-tier offer structure

- **Entry (KES 500-1,000).** A small, instant deliverable. E.g., one CV, one set of 10 social captions, one product description pack.
- **Standard (KES 2,000-3,500).** A bundle. E.g., CV + cover letter + LinkedIn rewrite + 2 free revisions.
- **Premium (KES 5,000-10,000).** A complete system or a package of deliverables plus delivery across a month. E.g., a monthly content pack for a business.

Your first job is to sell the entry offer and over-deliver. Then offer the upgrade inside the delivery message. This is how KES 500 becomes KES 5,000 in one conversation.

### Worksheet 2: Offer builder

1. Write your result in one sentence: "In 48 hours you will have ___."
2. List every deliverable the customer receives.
3. Write your three price tiers with the entry price first.
4. Write your guarantee in one line.
5. Write your till or paybill number and name, and test it once with KES 1 to make sure it works.

## Module 3: Payments, Delivery and Operations

You cannot build a business you cannot get paid for. In Kenya that means M-Pesa, and in 2026 it means doing it properly.

### M-Pesa basics every seller must know

- **Paybill:** Businesses use paybill for many payers. Account number is your customer's phone number or order number.
- **Till number (Lipa Na M-Pesa):** Simple for small sellers and one-off sales. Customer enters your till and amount.
- **STK push:** For apps and automated sales. You send a payment request to the customer's phone; they approve it and money lands in your account instantly. Built with the Daraja API.
- **From your own till number:** You must have a registered M-Pesa for Business account. If you do not have one yet, register through the Safaricom portal or a M-Pesa agent shop. This is your first operational task.

### Practical payment flow (manual, no code needed)

1. Customer messages you on WhatsApp.
2. You send a clear, short message with the offer, price, and your till/paybill number.
3. Customer pays and sends you the confirmation SMS.
4. You confirm the name and amount, then deliver.
5. You ask for a review and offer the upgrade.

Keep a simple log: date, customer, offer, amount, delivery sent (yes/no). Use a spreadsheet or even a notebook. You cannot scale what you cannot measure.

### Delivery of digital products

- **WhatsApp:** Send files directly for small deliverables.
- **Google Drive or email:** Send a link for packs, PDFs, and guides.
- **Gumroad or Payhip:** If you want an automated shop where buyers pay a card or PayPal and download instantly. In Kenya, most buyers will still pay via M-Pesa, so a WhatsApp ordering flow plus manual delivery is often faster to start.

### Automating later

When sales pick up, you can automate: a simple landing page with a payment link, then an auto-send of the product. The Darwin-based STK push approach lets you charge from your own site. For month one, do it manually. Manual is honest, builds trust, and teaches you what customers actually ask.

### Worksheet 3: Operations setup

1. Register or confirm your M-Pesa for Business till/paybill. Test it with a KES 1 payment from a friend.
2. Prepare your 3 delivery files (the product, the thank-you page/message, the upgrade offer).
3. Create your sales log (name, date, offer, amount, delivered).
4. Write your 3 saved WhatsApp replies: "pricing," "how to pay," and "delivered + thank you."
5. Test the full flow end to end: message a friend, they pay KES 1, you deliver a sample file.

## Module 4: Get Your First Customers

Customers come from three places: outreach you start, content that attracts, and referrals you ask for. In month one, outreach wins.

### Your acquisition funnel (simple version)

1. **A simple landing page.** One page: the result, the deliverables, the price, the till number, a WhatsApp button. Use a simple builder (Carrd, Canva page, or even a WhatsApp status). Do not build a website before you have sales.
2. **Traffic.** Two free sources to start: WhatsApp status and targeted direct messages. If you have a small budget, run a simple ad to a specific audience (e.g., "salon owners in Westlands") with the landing page link.
3. **The conversation.** People click through to WhatsApp. You respond fast with the saved pricing message and close.
4. **The delivery + review.** Deliver fast, ask for a short review or testimonial, and offer the upgrade.

### WhatsApp outreach that does not annoy

- **Personalise the first line.** Use their name and one fact about their business.
- **Name the problem, not your service.** "I noticed your shop has no product descriptions on its catalogue page." beats "I sell AI services."
- **Give one free, useful thing first.** A free tip, a free sample, a free audit. This builds trust before the ask.
- **Follow up twice, then stop.** Most sales happen on the second or third touch.

### Paid ads when you are ready

Once you have 10 sales and you know the exact message that works, you can put money behind it. Facebook and Instagram ads reach Kenyan buyers cheaply. Start with a small budget (KES 500-1,000/day), one message, one audience, and the landing page. Scale only what is working.

### Worksheet 4: Funnel setup

1. Build your one-page landing page with your offer, price, and WhatsApp button.
2. Write your outreach message and save it.
3. List 20 businesses or people who fit your customer. Write their names and a personalised first line for each.
4. Send 5 outreach messages today. Do not wait to feel ready.
5. Reply to every message within 30 minutes during business hours.

## Module 5: Case Studies

Real shapes, real numbers, names changed.

### Case study 1: Kevin, CV service to content agency

Kevin started with KES 0 and a phone. He offered CV rewriting at KES 500, delivered in 24 hours using ChatGPT for structure and drafting, then edited every draft by hand. His first 10 customers came from his own WhatsApp status and two university WhatsApp groups. He raised the price to KES 1,000 after 10 sales and added a KES 2,500 package (CV + cover letter + LinkedIn).

Month 1: KES 9,500. Month 2: KES 21,000. Month 3: he added a "graduates content pack" for businesses at KES 3,000/month and hit KES 47,000. The lesson: he upgraded the same customers instead of hunting new ones.

### Case study 2: Amina, digital product seller

Amina is a teacher who built a pack of 50 AI-assisted revision questions per subject for Form 4 candidates, priced at KES 300 per subject. She sold through teacher WhatsApp groups and church networks, charging KES 1,000 for all four subjects. Delivery was instant: a WhatsApp PDF link. In her second month she added a "study plan + weekly check-in" at KES 2,000/month and raised her average sale.

The lesson: digital products compound. The same PDF sold 300 times cost nothing extra to produce. She reinvested the profit into small Facebook ads for the school holiday season and doubled sales.

### Case study 3: Brian, salon booking service

Brian did the outreach grind: 60 personalised WhatsApp messages to salon and barber owners in one estate. Eight replied, two booked a trial. He built a simple WhatsApp booking flow (a spreadsheet plus saved replies, later a chatbot) that captured missed-call bookings. The salon owner saved an estimated KES 20,000/month in lost bookings and paid Brian KES 4,000/month to maintain it.

The lesson: high-ticket local services need a real relationship and a proven result. Once one salon paid, Brian used that salon as a case study to close three more.

### What all three share

- They started with one simple offer and improved it with real feedback.
- They used AI to compress delivery, not to skip doing the work.
- They sold first, then built bigger things. None started with a full website.
- They upgraded and referred existing customers before chasing new ones.

## Module 6: The 90-Day Roadmap

Follow this week by week. Post your numbers in the cohort group every Sunday.

### Days 1-14 (Foundation)

- Complete worksheets 1-3. Pick your model, your offer, your pricing.
- Register and test your M-Pesa till/paybill.
- Build your landing page (simple).
- Create your delivery files and saved WhatsApp replies.
- Make your first sale to a friend or family member for real money (even KES 100) to test the flow.

### Days 15-30 (First customers)

- Complete worksheet 4. Send 5 outreach messages daily.
- Post a daily WhatsApp status about your offer. Yes, daily.
- Deliver fast, ask every customer for a review and an upgrade.
- Track every sale in your log. Adjust your message based on what gets replies.

### Days 31-60 (Consistency)

- Raise prices once you have 15+ sales. Test the standard and premium tiers.
- Add a second offer that serves the same customers (referrals and upsells).
- Start collecting testimonials with photos or screenshots.
- If one channel is working (status, DMs, or ads), double down on it.
- Aim for a full week where you make a sale every day.

### Days 61-90 (Scale)

- Review your numbers: average sale, cost of your time, best channel.
- Reinvest a share of profit into small ads or better tools.
- Turn your best offer into a repeatable product or package.
- Systemise delivery so a friend or assistant could run it.
- Set your month 4 target. KES 100K/month is built by compounding good weeks, not one lucky post.

### Weekly rhythm (every week, all 90 days)

- Monday: plan the week, pick the 5 outreach targets.
- Daily: 1 hour of work. Post status, send messages, deliver.
- Friday: tally sales, log everything.
- Sunday: post numbers to the cohort group.

## Prompt Library

Use these with ChatGPT (or Claude/Gemini). Fill in the blanks with your details. Edit every output before sending it out — AI drafts, you polish.

**Business model brainstorm**

Use when you are stuck choosing a model. It forces the AI to work with your constraints.

```
I live in {city/town in Kenya} and I have {skill, interest, or job} and about {starting budget} KES to start a business. I can spend {hours} hours per week. I want an AI-powered business that solves a painful, recurring problem for a specific Kenyan customer. List 7 specific business model ideas with the exact customer, the exact offer, a realistic KES price, and the main risk of each. Then recommend the single best one and explain why.
```

**Offer clarity check**

Use to test whether your offer is understandable in one WhatsApp message.

```
Here is my business offer: {describe it}. Rewrite it so a Kenyan buyer with low patience understands it in one short WhatsApp message: what they get, what it costs in KES, how they pay via M-Pesa, and what happens next. Keep it under 60 words. Then list the 3 most likely questions a buyer will ask and draft one-line answers.
```

**Pricing consultation**

Use when you are unsure what to charge.

```
I provide {your service}. My target customer is {customer}. The result my customer gets is {result}. The problem costs them about {estimate in KES} per month. Suggest 3 pricing tiers in KES with the entry tier designed for an easy first yes, and explain what each tier includes. Add one guarantee I could offer that reduces buyer fear without exposing me.
```

**Landing page copy**

Use to build your one-page landing page.

```
Write the copy for a one-page landing page selling {your offer} to {customer} in Kenya. Include: a headline with the result, 3 bullet points of what they get, the price in KES, the M-Pesa payment instructions, a WhatsApp call-to-action button, and one short testimonial placeholder. Tone: warm, simple, trustworthy. Keep it short enough to read on a phone.
```

**WhatsApp outreach message**

Use to personalise your first outreach message per business.

```
Write a short WhatsApp outreach message to {business name}, a {type of business} in {area}, that is polite, personal, and not spammy. First line must mention something specific about their business. Then name one painful problem they likely have: {problem}. Then offer one free, useful thing I can do for them: {free thing}. End with one soft question. Under 90 words, no exclamation marks, no hard sell.
```

**Follow-up message**

Use for the second and third touch. Most sales happen here.

```
Write a friendly follow-up WhatsApp message for {customer name} who saw my offer ({offer}) but did not reply. This is the {second/third} follow-up. It should add new value, not nag. Mention one small new detail or a bonus, and end with a single soft question. Under 70 words. If this is the third follow-up, include a polite close so I stop contacting them.
```

**Objection handling**

Use to prepare answers before they come up.

```
List the 5 most common objections a Kenyan buyer will raise about {your offer} at a price of {price} KES. For each, write a one-line empathetic response that keeps the sale alive, plus a question that moves the conversation forward. Do not sound pushy or robotic.
```

**Sales conversation trainer**

Use to rehearse real conversations before sending them.

```
Act as a suspicious but genuinely interested Kenyan buyer named {name}. They run {type of business} and are worried about {worry}. I will pitch my offer: {offer}. Respond realistically to each of my messages. After 5 turns, tell me what I did well and the biggest mistake I made, and suggest better replies.
```

**Testimonial request**

Use to collect social proof without awkwardness.

```
Write a short WhatsApp message asking {customer name} for a testimonial after they received {deliverable}. Ask for 2-3 sentences in their own words about the result they got, and offer to send a simple template they can edit. Under 60 words, warm and grateful.
```

**Case study writer**

Use to turn one customer story into a short case study for your status or landing page.

```
Turn this story into a short case study I can share: customer was {situation}, they bought {offer}, the result was {result}. Write 3 versions: one for a WhatsApp status (under 60 words), one for my landing page (under 120 words), and one for LinkedIn (under 200 words). Include the lesson in each.
```

**Daily status ideas**

Use to never run out of content for your WhatsApp status.

```
Generate 14 WhatsApp status ideas for {your business/offer}. Each must be one short sentence or one simple image idea that sells the result, not the AI. Mix: proof posts, tip posts, behind-the-scenes, offers, and questions. No clichés, no emojis.
```

**Ad copy**

Use when you move to paid ads.

```
Write 3 short Facebook/Instagram ad texts for {offer}, targeting {audience} in Kenya. Each needs: a hook line, 2-3 benefit bullets, the price in KES, and a clear action (WhatsApp message or landing page). Keep each under 50 words. One ad should target busy business owners, one target price-conscious buyers, one target first-time buyers who fear being scammed.
```

**Product description pack**

Use to deliver faster for businesses selling on Jumia, Kilimall, or social media.

```
Write product descriptions for {product name} aimed at Kenyan online shoppers. Use simple English, mention size/material/use if relevant, include 3 benefit bullets, and end with a call to action. Tone: trustworthy, not hypey. Write 3 versions: short (2 lines), standard (5 lines), and detailed (8 lines).
```

**CV and cover letter pack**

Use for the classic CV service.

```
Rewrite this CV information for a {role} application in Kenya: {paste info}. Structure it clearly, quantify achievements with numbers where possible, keep it to one page, and optimise for ATS filters. Then write a matching cover letter under 300 words referencing the specific job and company: {job details}. Tone: confident and professional, not arrogant.
```

**Business document drafting**

Use for reports, proposals, and letters for small businesses, NGOs, and SACCOs.

```
Draft a {type of document, e.g., simple business proposal / SACCO meeting minutes / demand letter} for {organisation or person} about {topic}. Use plain English, a clear structure, and formal but friendly tone. Include placeholders in square brackets where I need to insert real names and numbers. Flag anything I should verify with a lawyer or accountant before sending.
```

**Social media content pack**

Use to sell a monthly content bundle to a local business.

```
Create a one-month social media content pack for {business}, a {type of business} in {location}. Include 4 posts per week: one educational tip, one promo, one customer-problem post, and one behind-the-scenes. For each: a short caption, 3 hashtags, and a simple image idea. Keep captions under 40 words each. All in simple English, no emojis.
```

**Simple customer research**

Use to understand a niche before you pitch it.

```
I want to sell {offer} to {customer group, e.g., salon owners in Nairobi}. List the 5 most common problems this group complains about, where they hang out online and offline, what they currently do to solve these problems, and what would make them try a new solution. Then suggest 3 ways I could reach them locally for free.
```

**Sales log and reporting**

Use to turn your messy notes into a clean weekly summary.

```
Here is my sales data for the week: {paste your log}. Summarise it: total revenue, number of sales, average sale value, best day, best offer, and the questions customers asked most. Then suggest 3 small changes to improve next week. Keep the summary short enough to share in a WhatsApp group.
```

**Refund and complaint handling**

Use to handle problems gracefully and protect your reputation.

```
A customer bought {offer} and is unhappy because {complaint}. Write a calm, respectful WhatsApp reply that: acknowledges their concern, offers a specific fix (revision, replacement, or refund), and preserves the relationship. Under 80 words. Then write one follow-up message to send after the fix to confirm they are satisfied.
```

**Payment reminder message**

Use to politely chase a customer who said they would pay but has not.

```
Write a polite WhatsApp reminder for {customer name} who agreed to pay {amount} KES for {offer} but has not paid yet. Assume it is an honest mistake, not a scam. Include the till/paybill number again and ask them to send the confirmation. Under 60 words, friendly, no pressure.
```

**Weekend review prompt**

Use in your Sunday check-in. Paste your weekly numbers.

```
Here is my week: {paste numbers and notes}. Act as my business coach. Point out my 2 biggest wins, the 1 thing that wasted my time, and give me exactly 3 actions for next week: one to get more sales, one to improve delivery, and one to improve my message. Keep it short and direct.
```

**90-day target planner**

Use to break your revenue goal into weekly numbers.

```
My target is {amount} KES per month by day 90. My average sale is {amount} KES. Work out: how many sales I need per month, per week, and per day. Then propose a realistic week-by-week ramp for 12 weeks assuming my sales grow from 0 to target, including when I should raise prices and when I should add a premium offer.
```

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Selling "AI" instead of a result. Customers buy outcomes, not technology.
- Building a website before making a sale. Validate with WhatsApp first.
- Ignoring the payment flow. If paying you is hard, you will not get paid.
- Copy-pasting AI output without editing. Unedited AI text reads robotic and kills trust.
- Pricing by the hour. Price by the outcome the customer gets.
- Chasing every idea. Pick one model and improve it for 90 days.
- Forgetting follow-ups. Most sales come on the second or third touch.
- Not logging sales. You cannot scale what you cannot measure.
