## Module 1: Choosing a profitable topic and validating demand

Most courses fail before a single lesson is recorded — the topic had no demand. Topic selection is a research problem, and AI makes the research fast.

**The topic sweet spot (three circles):**

- **What you know** — your skill, experience, or results
- **What people pay for** — a painful problem or a desired outcome
- **What you can teach** — you can explain it and prove a result

The best Kenya topics sit in the middle: people struggling with a real problem, willing to pay, and no one teaching it well locally. AI helps you find the overlaps by brainstorming against your experience and then stress-testing each idea.

**Validation before building (do this before recording anything):**

- Search volume and existing courses — if there are many buyers and weak local supply, that is an opening
- Talk to 20 real potential students (WhatsApp, LinkedIn, local groups) — ask what problem they want solved, not whether they'd buy your idea
- Build a waitlist landing page and drive a little traffic — people who join a waitlist are stronger evidence than people who say "that sounds nice"
- Price test — a small paid pilot or pre-sale tells you more than any survey

**Worksheet 1A — Topic scorecard:** score 10 candidate topics on personal fit, demand evidence, willingness to pay, and competition weakness. Only topics scoring 70+ proceed to validation.

**Do this:** in week one, produce 10 topic ideas and score them.

**Not this:** building the course you want to teach instead of the course people want to buy. Your taste is not the market.

## Module 2: Structuring the course — the outcome-first curriculum

A course is not a pile of information; it is a promise of an outcome, delivered in order. Structure it so a beginner can finish and get the result.

**The outcome-first design:**

1. **Define the outcome** — one sentence: "By the end, students can [do X]."
2. **Reverse-engineer the steps** — what must a student do to reach that outcome?
3. **Order for beginners** — each module depends only on what came before
4. **Module = one result** — each module should deliver one clear win
5. **Assignments and worksheets** — every module ends with something the student does, not just watches

**The module template:**

- **Lesson 1 of a module:** set the context and the win
- **Middle lessons:** the how-to, one concept at a time
- **Last lesson:** a demo, a worksheet, and a checkpoint

**Length rules for a modern course:** 5–8 modules, 3–5 lessons each, 5–15 minutes per lesson. Short lessons finish; long videos don't. Your course teaches through doing, not through marathon lectures.

**Worksheet 2A — Curriculum map:** outcome sentence, module list with one result each, lesson list, and one assignment per module. AI drafts this from your topic; you refine it.

**Do this:** write your outcome sentence and full curriculum map in week two.

**Not this:** structuring by "everything I know about X." That is a textbook, not a course, and nobody finishes it.

## Module 3: Scripting — writing lessons people actually watch

Scripting is 80% of course quality and it is the stage where AI saves the most time. A scripted lesson sounds professional; an unscripted one sounds like a ramble.

**The lesson script formula:**

- **Hook (30 seconds)** — the pain, the promise, why this matters to the student now
- **Teach (2–5 minutes)** — one concept, one example, plain language
- **Show (2–3 minutes)** — a live demo or walkthrough
- **Practice (assignment)** — the student does it, with a worksheet
- **Close (30 seconds)** — what was learned and what comes next

**Scripting with AI:**

- Give AI the lesson's outcome and your audience profile
- Get a first-draft script, then read it aloud and fix what sounds stiff
- Write in your voice: short sentences, Kenya-relevant examples, no corporate fluff
- Generate worksheets, quiz questions, and checklists from each script

**Scripting rules for phone consumption:**

- Keep lessons under 15 minutes, most under 10
- One idea per lesson — if two ideas appear, split the lesson
- Repeat key points twice: once in the teach, once in the close
- Write the on-screen prompts you'll show, not just what you'll say

**Worksheet 3A — Script template:** hook / teach / show / practice / close for each lesson, with the worksheet linked. This is your production checklist.

**Do this:** script module 1 fully in week three — read it aloud before recording.

**Not this:** recording first and scripting after. Fixing a bad recording takes ten times longer than fixing a script.

## Module 4: Producing the course — recording, slides, and editing

Production is where people quit. It does not need to be expensive — it needs to be consistent and clear.

**What you actually need:**

- A decent phone camera and a quiet room with good light (window light is free)
- A microphone that doesn't echo — a clip-on lav works
- Slides or screen recordings with a clean template in Canva
- CapCut for editing — trim silences, cut mistakes, add captions

**The recording workflow:**

- Read your script twice before recording
- Record in chunks per lesson so a mistake costs you two minutes, not twenty
- Do one take per chunk, restart only on a bad error
- Add captions — most students watch on phones with sound off

**The slide rule:** one idea per slide, big text, real screenshots over clip art. AI can generate your slide text, your lesson outline, and your talking points from the script.

**Worksheet 4A — Production checklist:** quiet room, light, mic test, phone storage free, slides done, captions plan, backup of every take. Check it before every recording session.

**Do this:** in week four, record and edit module 1 with captions.

**Not this:** waiting for perfect equipment. A clear phone recording with captions outsells a polished video nobody could finish.

## Module 5: Building your funnel — ads, landing pages, upsells, payments

Content is the product; the funnel is the machine that sells it. A funnel answers four questions: how they find you, what they read, what they pay, and what you sell them next.

**The four stages:**

1. **Traffic** — Meta and TikTok ads, WhatsApp broadcasts, your own content, joint ventures with other creators
2. **Landing page** — one promise, one offer, one button: the outcome, who it's for, what's inside, price, and a clear call to action
3. **Payment** — M-Pesa via paybill or till, or a course platform that takes M-Pesa; give the buyer the easiest possible route
4. **Upsell** — a more expensive offer right after purchase (community, coaching, advanced module)

**Payment platforms for Kenya:**

- **Global platforms** (Teachable, Udemy, Kajabi, Gumroad) — handle video, checkout, and delivery, but check whether they take M-Pesa directly
- **Direct route** — WhatsApp + M-Pesa paybill + Google Drive or a simple membership site; simplest for the Kenya buyer, you handle delivery yourself
- **Hybrid** — sell through WhatsApp/M-Pesa for local buyers, global platforms for international reach

**Landing page copy that sells:**

- Headline = the outcome ("Record, Edit, and Launch Your First Course in 30 Days")
- Subheadline = who it's for and the proof
- Body = what's inside, module by module, plus testimonials
- CTA = repeat the offer, the price, and the button

**Worksheet 5A — Funnel map:** traffic source → landing page → offer → payment → upsell → delivery. Fill each box before you build anything.

**Do this:** in week five, write your landing page copy and connect your payment route.

**Not this:** putting the course up and waiting for sales. A course with no funnel is a PDF with hopes.

## Module 6: Running a cohort and community for premium pricing

The course is the floor; the cohort is where premium pricing lives. A standalone course sells for KES 500–2,500. A course plus a cohort community with live office hours sells for KES 10,000 and up — you are now doing this course's own pricing model.

**Why cohorts command premium prices:**

- Accountability — students finish when other people are watching
- Live feedback — your attention is the product
- Community — learners motivate each other, which raises completion
- Urgency — a start date creates a deadline to join

**The cohort operations:**

- A fixed start date and a 4–6 week schedule
- Weekly live calls (office hours or a module walkthrough) recorded for those who miss
- A WhatsApp community with rules, pinned resources, and a weekly question thread
- Weekly assignments and a completion certificate
- You answering questions in batches, not 24/7

**Worksheet 6A — Cohort plan:** start date, duration, weekly live call schedule, community rules, assignment calendar, and the completion certificate design.

**Do this:** design your first cohort's 6-week calendar with dates.

**Not this:** running an open-ended "lifetime access + community" with no structure. Undefined communities die and refund requests follow.

## Module 7: Pricing, upsells, and the offer ladder

Pricing is positioning. A KES 500 course and a KES 10,000 course can cover the same topic — the difference is the delivery model and the perceived outcome.

**The offer ladder:**

| Level | Offer | Price |
|-------|-------|-------|
| Entry | Standalone mini-course or lead magnet | KES 0–500 |
| Core | Full self-paced course | KES 2,500–5,000 |
| Premium | Cohort + community + office hours | KES 10,000+ |
| Top | Coaching or certification add-on | KES 15,000–50,000 |

Buyers climb the ladder. The entry offer builds trust; the top offer captures the serious buyer. AI helps you draft the copy and the upsell sequence for each rung.

**Upsell mechanics:**

- Offer the premium option at the moment of purchase, not a week later
- Give the self-paced buyer a limited-time "join the cohort" upgrade after they finish
- Bundle: course + templates + community at one price
- Use scarcity honestly: cohort seats are limited by your attention, which is true

**Worksheet 7A — Offer ladder:** fill in your entry, core, premium, and top offers with prices, delivery, and the upsell trigger for each.

**Do this:** price all four rungs of your ladder before you launch.

**Not this:** pricing by how long the course took to make. Price by the outcome the student gets and the delivery model you run.

## Module 8: Scaling to multiple courses and passive income

One course is a product; five courses is a business. Scaling means reusing your system, not rebuilding from zero every time.

**The scaling playbook:**

1. **First course proves the funnel** — you know the topic, price, and audience that buy
2. **Second course serves the same students** — the next step in their journey, or a higher-value outcome
3. **Standardize the system** — your curriculum map, script template, production checklist, and funnel map are reused
4. **Batch the production** — script 3 courses, record 3 courses, launch 1 per month
5. **Automate delivery** — self-paced courses deliver themselves; the cohort is the only live part

**Passive income reality:**

- Self-paced courses are near-passive once built — the work is updates and support
- Cohorts are semi-passive — recurring income with a time cost
- Truly passive income comes from systems: a funnel that runs, content that brings traffic, and a library of courses that sell while you sleep

**Worksheet 8A — Course roadmap:** your current course plus 3 next courses mapped to the same audience, each with a launch month and a funding plan (profits from the previous course fund the next).

**Do this:** in week six, write your 12-month course roadmap.

**Not this:** starting a new course in a different topic before the first one sells. Serve the same audience deeper — that is where repeat revenue lives.

## Module 9: Case studies — course creators who built empires

**Case study 1: The WhatsApp + M-Pesa course seller.** A Nairobi digital-marketing trainer sold his first course for KES 2,500 through WhatsApp statuses and a paybill, with modules delivered via Google Drive. He built a funnel from a landing page, added a KES 10,000 cohort with weekly calls, and within two cohorts had a community of 80 paying students. His second course — for the same audience, on running AI ads — launched at KES 8,000 and sold to 40 of his existing students before he even opened ads. Same audience, deeper offer, repeat revenue.

**Case study 2: The platform-first creator.** A Kenyan data analyst published on Udemy and Teachable to reach an international audience, pricing at USD prices. His self-paced courses now sell on autopilot while he runs paid cohorts for local corporate clients at premium KES pricing. Two markets, two delivery models, one curriculum.

**Case study 3: The community that became the product.** A fitness coach started with a KES 500 mini-course, built a WhatsApp community, and slowly turned the community itself into the premium offer: a monthly membership with workouts, meal plans, and weekly check-ins. The membership now earns more monthly than the course ever did. The lesson: the community is an asset you own, not a side feature.

The pattern is identical in all three — validate demand, build with a system, funnel to a premium delivery, serve the same audience deeper.

## Prompt Library

Copy these into ChatGPT (or Claude/Gemini), fill the slots in brackets, and adapt.

**Topic brainstorm from your experience**

Use in week one.

```
I have experience in [your skills/industry] and these results [list
outcomes you've achieved]. Brainstorm 15 course topics I could teach
that combine what I know with what people in Kenya pay for. For each:
the student's pain, the promised outcome, and who would buy it.
Mark the 5 most promising.
```

**Topic scorecard**

Use to rank your shortlist.

```
Score these course topics for the Kenyan market: [list topics].
For each, score 1-10 on: my personal expertise, evidence of demand,
willingness to pay, competition weakness, and how teachable it is.
Total each score, rank them, and tell me the top 3 and why.
```

**Validation conversation script**

Use when interviewing 20 potential students.

```
Write a short WhatsApp/LinkedIn message asking potential students
about a course on [topic]. I want to know their problem, how they
tried to solve it, what they'd pay, and what result they want. Keep
it friendly, under 80 words, and make it easy to answer. Give me 3
versions with different angles.
```

**Validation summary**

Use after your 20 conversations.

```
Here are the responses from my potential students: [paste notes].
Summarize: the #1 problem mentioned, the most common desired
outcome, what people said they'd pay, any objections, and whether
demand is strong enough to build. Give me a yes/no verdict and the
3 strongest signals.
```

**Outcome sentence**

Use before structuring anything.

```
Write 5 possible outcome sentences for a course on [topic] for
[audience]. Each must be one sentence: "By the end, students can
[do X]." Make them specific, measurable, and different in focus
(beginner, career, business, confidence, speed). Recommend the best
one and why.
```

**Curriculum outline**

Use to build your module map.

```
Create a curriculum outline for a course teaching [outcome] to
[audience]. Structure: 5-8 modules, each with one clear result, and
3-5 lessons per module. Order it so each module depends only on the
previous. Add one assignment or worksheet per module. Keep lessons
under 15 minutes each.
```

**Lesson-by-lesson script**

Use to draft each lesson.

```
Write a script for this lesson: [lesson outcome], in my course on
[topic]. Format: hook (30s, pain + promise), teach (3-5 min, one
concept, one Kenya-relevant example), show (2-3 min demo), practice
(assignment), close (30s). Write in warm, plain English with short
sentences. No corporate fluff.
```

**Script rewrite for voice**

Use when a script sounds stiff.

```
Rewrite this lesson script so it sounds like a real person speaking,
not reading. Shorten sentences, cut jargon, add natural pauses, and
keep the teaching clear: [paste script]. Keep every fact and example.
```

**Worksheet generator**

Use to create practice material per module.

```
Create a worksheet for this module: [module outcome]. Include: 3
reflection questions, one step-by-step task with checkboxes, and one
"apply to your own situation" exercise. Keep it fillable on a phone
in under 20 minutes. Topic: [topic].
```

**Quiz questions**

Use for lesson checkpoints.

```
Write 5 multiple-choice questions for this lesson: [lesson outcome].
Each needs 1 correct answer, 3 plausible wrong answers, and a
one-line explanation. Keep the language simple: [topic].
```

**Talking points from script**

Use to make slides.

```
Turn this lesson script into slide talking points: [paste script].
Give me one slide per key idea, with a slide title, 3-5 short bullets
max, and what screen element to show (screenshot, example, diagram).
Keep it clean for a phone screen.
```

**Lesson hook variants**

Use to find a strong opening.

```
Write 5 different 30-second hooks for this lesson: [lesson outcome].
Vary them: a story, a surprising number, a question, a common
mistake, a before-after promise. Pick the strongest and rewrite it
for a phone viewer with captions.
```

**Course description**

Use on your landing page and sales messages.

```
Write a sales description for my course: [outcome], for [audience],
with [X] modules and [Y] worksheets, priced at KES [Z]. Structure:
headline, who it's for, the outcome, what's inside, the price, and
the call to action. Make it feel like a promise, not a brochure.
Keep it under 200 words.
```

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Structuring by "everything I know" instead of a student outcome

- Jumping to a new topic before the first audience is served deeply
- Handling 24/7 support instead of batching answers
