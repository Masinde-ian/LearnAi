## Module 1: The remote opportunity and where you fit

The world has more remote jobs than ever, and Kenya is a proven source of remote talent. The mistake most applicants make is applying to everything. The winners pick a lane and go deep.

**Why companies hire from Kenya:**

- Time-zone overlap with Europe (2–4 hours ahead) means real-time collaboration
- Strong English skills and a growing AI-savvy talent pool
- Cost-effective talent compared to US/EU local hires
- Remote-first companies literally do not care where you sit, they care what you ship

**Remote-first companies to study:** GitLab (fully remote, ~60+ countries), Shopify (digital by design, remote-first), Deel (remote-first HR/payroll), plus a long tail of startups that hire contractors from Africa. Each publishes how they work — read their career pages and remote handbooks before you apply. That research is what makes your application feel native.

**Which roles value AI-adjacent skills right now:**

- AI-assisted software development — building with AI tools in the stack
- Content and copywriting with AI workflows
- Data and prompt engineering, AI operations
- Digital marketing and SEO with AI tooling
- Customer success and support automation
- Design (UI/UX) using AI generation tools
- Technical writing, QA, and automation

You do not need to be an AI engineer. You need to prove you ship work with AI making you faster.

**Worksheet 1A — Lane finder:** list your current skills, cross them against roles that hire remotely, and mark where AI tools can lift your output. End with one target role for the next 90 days. One lane. No exceptions.

**Do this:** pick one target role and one target company type by the end of week one.

**Not this:** applying to "anything remote." Generalist applications read as desperate and get ignored.

## Module 2: Building the AI-adjacent skills that are in demand

Remote companies hire proof, not promises. The fastest proof is a portfolio of work that uses AI to produce results a non-AI candidate cannot match in the same time.

**The skill stack that travels:**

- **Core skill** — your profession (writing, development, design, marketing, ops)
- **AI layer** — the tools and workflows you use to be 2–3x faster
- **Evidence** — projects, case studies, and outcomes you can show

**Build the AI layer in 30 days:**

**Worksheet 2A — Skill gap map:** for your target role, list the top 8 skills from real job ads (AI can extract these), mark your level for each, and build a 30-day plan for the 3 gaps that matter most.

**Do this:** start one real project this week that you can publish as case-study evidence.

**Not this:** chasing every shiny AI tool. Pick one core skill, one AI workflow, go deep.

## Module 3: An internationally competitive CV

Recruiters at global companies spend seconds on a first pass. Your CV must survive both a human skim and an automated ATS scan. AI rewrites it so it reads native.

**The international CV rules:**

- **One page for under 10 years experience, two max.** Trim mercilessly.
- **Results before duties.** "Cut reporting time from 6 hours to 1 using AI automation" beats "prepared reports."
- **Numbers everywhere.** Revenue, users, time saved, percentages — international hiring managers love metrics.
- **No local-only jargon.** "Helped at the office" and vague titles weaken you. Use standard role titles.
- **ATS-friendly formatting.** Standard headings (Experience, Education, Skills), no tables or images, plain fonts.
- **Dates in a format the world reads** (Jan 2023 – Dec 2024), and role titles that map to global equivalents.

**The AI rewrite workflow:** paste your current CV into AI with the target role and ask for an ATS-optimized rewrite against global standards, then tighten every bullet with a results-first rewrite, then check it against the exact job description.

**Worksheet 3A — CV builder:** fill a table of your roles and, for each, 3 achievements with numbers. AI turns these into bullets. Your job is supplying the raw numbers — the tool does the wording.

**Do this:** rewrite your CV with a results-first structure this week.

**Not this:** sending the same CV to every company. A tailored CV for your target role beats ten generic CVs.

**Do this:** in week three, rewrite your headline and About, and publish one case study.

**Not this:** a LinkedIn that reads "Seeking opportunities" with no proof. That is a directory listing, not a recruiting magnet.

## Module 5: Navigating time zones, remote contracts, and interviews across borders

Remote work across time zones is a skill. Companies test it more than you think.

**Time-zone strategy:**

- Know the overlap window: Kenya is UTC+3, so a UK team (UTC+0) overlaps 9am–2pm UK = 12pm–5pm Kenya. US teams overlap in your evening.
- Answer the job ad's "what hours do you cover" honestly and specifically
- Build a buffer ritual: async updates by 9am their morning, replies within 24 hours
- Use time-zone tools (World Time Buddy, timezone converters) — never make a meeting mistake twice

**Contracts and roles:**

- **Employee (EOR)** — you are hired through an Employer of Record like Deel, payrolled properly, with your local taxes handled
- **Independent contractor** — you invoice and manage your own tax
- Know the difference in the ad before you apply; it changes your tax and benefits picture

**Remote interview culture:**

- Remote-first companies run async and sync interviews: written assessments, recorded video answers, and live video rounds
- They value written communication as much as technical skill
- Expect questions about how you work alone, communicate asynchronously, and handle ambiguity

**Worksheet 5A — Interview logistics sheet:** for your target companies, record time-zone overlap hours, interview format, whether they use an EOR, and the language of the culture (async-heavy vs meeting-heavy).

**Do this:** map your daily schedule to your target company's time zone before you apply.

**Not this:** applying without knowing their hours or contract model, then fumbling in the interview when asked.

## Module 6: Getting paid — international payment platforms

The money must actually reach you. Each payment route has different costs, and choosing wrong quietly eats your salary.

| Platform | Best for | Costs to know |
|----------|----------|---------------|
| Wise | Best FX rates, real mid-market rate | Around 0.5% conversion — the cheapest on most currencies |
| Payoneer | Upwork and many freelance platforms | ~USD 29.95/year account fee, plus transaction fees |
| PayPal | Common but expensive | ~4.4% plus a fixed fee per transaction |
| Deel (EOR) | Full payroll, benefits, tax handled | Paid by the employer; ideal for employees |
| Direct bank transfer | Large sums, some employers | Check your bank's FX spread; often worse than Wise |

**The rule:** whenever you control the method, prefer Wise for the best FX. When the platform dictates (Upwork → Payoneer), treat the fees as part of your rate math. If a company offers Deel as an EOR, you get payroll, tax, and benefits handled — that is the premium route.

**Get set up before you get the job:**

- Open Wise now and verify it
- Create your Payoneer account if you use Upwork
- Ask your bank about receiving USD into your KES account
- Understand what fees to name when you negotiate your rate

**Worksheet 6A — Payment setup checklist:** accounts opened and verified, fees written down, preferred method per client type, and a rate-calculation sheet that shows what you actually keep after fees and tax.

**Do this:** open and verify your Wise account this week.

**Not this:** letting a client decide your payment method by default — fee transparency is part of negotiating your rate.

## Module 7: KRA tax for self-employed and remote earners

Earning in dollars does not exempt you from Kenya Revenue Authority. Plan for tax from month one so there are no shocks.

**What you need to know:**

- If you are a contractor, you are self-employed: register for income tax and pay installments
- If you are employed through an EOR (Deel style), PAYE is usually handled for you — confirm with the payroll team
- Track expenses that reduce taxable income (equipment, internet, software, some home office costs) — keep receipts
- Register your business structure when income justifies it; a company (Ltd) can sometimes be more efficient than personal income tax at higher incomes — ask a registered tax advisor
- Pay estimates on time to avoid penalties and interest

**The AI workflow:** keep a simple income-and-expense sheet in Google Sheets, paste it into AI monthly, and get a running estimate of what you owe, so nothing surprises you at filing time.

**Worksheet 7A — Tax calendar:** filing deadlines, installment dates, your monthly income/expense tracker, and a list of receipts to keep. AI drafts the tracker; you keep it fed.

**Do this:** set up the income/expense tracker and note the key KRA deadlines this week.

**Not this:** treating tax as a "next year" problem. Remote income is visible, and penalties compound fast.

## Module 8: Interview preparation for remote-first companies

Remote interviews are trainable. Most candidates who fail simply never practiced the specific formats these companies use.

**The formats to master:**

- **Behavioral rounds (STAR)** — Situation, Task, Action, Result. Prepare 6–8 stories that prove results with numbers
- **Skills assessments** — take-home tasks or live coding/editing/design tests. Treat these as portfolio work: polish and document your process
- **Async video answers** — one-way recorded interviews; you must sound natural with no interviewer
- **Async work trials** — they give you real work tasks; show your communication as much as your output

**The remote-first interview edge:**

- Ask questions about how they work (async tools, meeting load, time-zone expectations, how they handle overlap) — these questions signal you already get remote culture
- Show written communication: respond to emails and chat threads clearly and fast
- Prepare a "how I work remotely" answer: your setup, your schedule, your communication habits

**Worksheet 8A — STAR story bank:** write 6 stories with numbers — a win, a failure you fixed, a conflict, a time you worked alone, a time you improved a process, a time you learned fast. AI polishes each into STAR format. These stories cover 90% of behavioral questions.

**Do this:** in week five, run a mock interview with your cohort and record it.

**Not this:** improvising stories in the interview. Your answers come from a bank you prepared, not from your memory under pressure.

## Module 9: Case studies — Kenyans who landed remote roles

**Case study 1: The developer who documented AI workflows.** A Nairobi developer had a solid but unremarkable resume. He spent 6 weeks building three small AI-assisted projects (a WhatsApp chatbot, a document-automation script, a data-cleaning pipeline), wrote each as a case study with the AI workflow visible, and put them on his portfolio. He targeted remote-first startups on a global job board. He landed interviews within a month and accepted a contract at roughly $48k/year, paid through Wise. The projects, not his years of experience, opened the door.

**Case study 2: The writer who became an "AI workflow" writer.** A Kenyan content writer was competing with thousands for generic writing gigs. She learned a documented AI editing and research workflow, rewrote her LinkedIn to "Content Writer | AI-Assisted Research and Editing," and built a case study showing a 10,000-word research report done in 3 days. A remote marketing agency hired her as a contractor through Payoneer at $2,800/month. Her edge was not faster writing — it was the documented, repeatable system.

**Case study 3: The EOR employee.** A customer-support professional applied to a remote-first company that hires through Deel. The interview focused on async communication and self-management, not location. She got the employee role — payroll, PAYE, and benefits handled through Deel — and her monthly salary is deposited in USD. She spends under an hour a week on the admin that used to take a day.

The pattern in all three: pick a lane, build proof with AI, optimize the online presence, practice the format, and get paid through the cheapest route.

## Module 10: Long-term career growth from Kenya

Landing the first role is the entry. Growing is a system.

**The compounding path:**

1. **First role** — accept a fair contract, build a track record
2. **Performance** — overdeliver, document wins, become the person they trust
3. **Rate growth** — raise rates 10–20% per year or at each renewal
4. **Diversify** — add a second client or move from contractor to employee where it suits
5. **Specialize** — deeper in one niche beats broad and shallow
6. **Your own brand** — a blog, a portfolio, a reputation that attracts offers instead of applications

**The remote-worker stack that compounds:**

- A portfolio updated after every project
- A skills roadmap reviewed every 90 days (AI research tells you what is trending)
- A savings and tax system that keeps money safe in KES and dollars
- A network — the best remote jobs come from referrals, not ads

**Worksheet 10A — Growth plan:** a 12-month plan with a monthly saving target, a quarterly skill review, and a rate increase schedule with the exact numbers you will ask for.

**Do this:** decide your 12-month income target and work backward to the role and rate you need.

**Not this:** treating the first job as the finish line. The first job is the vehicle; the career is the destination.
