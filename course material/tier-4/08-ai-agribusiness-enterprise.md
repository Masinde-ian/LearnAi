## Module 1: From farm to enterprise — the mindset shift

A farm that feeds a family is a livelihood. A farm that pays, records, forecasts, and scales is an enterprise. The difference is not the size of the land — it is how you run the numbers.

**The five pillars of a farm enterprise:**

- **Planning** — what to plant, how much, and when, decided before the season
- **Records** — every input, output, and shilling tracked in one system
- **Forecasting** — yield, prices, and cash flow projected before they happen
- **Market** — buyers identified and relationships built before harvest
- **Value** — branding and processing that lift the price above commodity level

**The enterprise question for every decision:** does this move the farm toward higher revenue, lower cost, or more stability? If it does none of those, it is not a business decision.

**The digital baseline:** you need a phone, WhatsApp, and Google Sheets. Everything else is an upgrade. AI tools like ChatGPT turn your records into forecasts and your notes into plans — but the records must be real.

**Worksheet 1A — Enterprise snapshot:** land size, crops or livestock, current costs, current revenue, buyers, and the one thing you'd fix first. This is your starting point for every later module.

**Do this:** write your current numbers honestly — including the ones you don't like.

**Not this:** pretending farming is "too complex for records." The complexity is exactly why the records matter.

## Module 2: Building the digitized farm operation

A digitized farm runs on one rule: everything is recorded, and the records drive decisions. AI turns that pile of records into planning and forecasting power.

**The record system — one sheet, four tabs:**

- **Planting / production** — crop, plot, planting date, seed cost, inputs
- **Costs** — every shilling spent, categorized (seeds, fertilizer, labour, transport, water, pest control)
- **Sales** — date, quantity, price per unit, buyer, channel
- **Notes** — weather, pests, problems, what worked

Set it up with AI, then keep it fed weekly — a half-hour on Friday beats a lost weekend at harvest.

**Planning with AI:**

- Season plan: land, budget, crops, expected yield, and profit, built before planting
- Kenya's two rainy seasons: long rains roughly March–May, short rains roughly October–December
- Staggered planting so harvests don't all collide and crash the price

**Forecasting with AI:**

- From your records, estimate next season's costs and yields
- Project cash flow: when money goes out (inputs) and when it comes in (sales)
- Model "what if" — what happens to profit if the price drops 20% or yield falls 30%? A business that knows its downside can survive it.

**Worksheet 2A — Farm system setup:** your Google Sheet built with all four tabs, your first month of real records, and your first season plan.

**Do this:** in week one, set up the sheet and enter everything you know about the current season.

**Not this:** recording sales but not costs. An enterprise that knows revenue but not cost is guessing at profit.

## Module 4: AI for market access and pricing strategy

The broker's power is information: they know the price, the buyer, and the timing. AI and research take that information back — and pricing strategy keeps it.

**Market access — know your buyers before you plant:**

- Wholesalers, mama mbogas, supermarkets, hotels, processors, exporters
- Their volumes, quality and grading requirements, and payment behavior
- Current farm-gate vs retail prices, and the price trend over recent seasons

**Pricing strategy — price from your numbers, not the rumour:**

- **Cost of production** — all costs divided by expected yield = break-even price
- **Target margin** — sell at cost plus a set margin, and know what that is before you negotiate
- **Season timing** — prices swing with supply; a staggered harvest and a stored product can sell into a higher window
- **Value positioning** — a graded, sorted, packed product commands a premium over a mixed bag

**The AI workflow:** research prices with Perplexity or ChatGPT search, cross-check with two people at your local market, and let AI build your price sheet with break-even, target, and minimum-acceptable columns.

**Worksheet 4A — Market and price sheet:** buyers with contacts, current prices by channel, your break-even, your target price, and your walk-away minimum.

**Do this:** in week two, build your price sheet and verify it at one market day.

**Not this:** accepting the first price offered because you don't know your break-even. A price below cost is a loss, however it's framed.

## Module 5: Buyer negotiations with AI support

Negotiation is where prepared farmers win. The preparation is a script; AI writes drafts, you bring the reality.

**The negotiation preparation:**

- Know your numbers cold: break-even, target, minimum acceptable
- Know your leverage: volume, quality, freshness, reliability, delivery
- Know the buyer's situation: what they need, their alternatives, their volumes
- Prepare responses to the five standard pushes: "too expensive," "the market is down," "my other supplier is cheaper," "pay me more later," "just this once at this price"

**Negotiation principles that hold in Kenya:**

- Name your price first when you have the facts — anchoring works
- Trade, don't just drop: "I can do KES X if you take all of it" or "at this price, delivery is separate"
- Never negotiate to a loss: your minimum is your minimum
- Build relationships, not just deals — a fair buyer who pays on time is worth more than a high price that never lands
- Put agreed terms in a WhatsApp message or simple note — memory is not a contract

**Worksheet 5A — Negotiation script:** for your main buyer, fill in your numbers, your leverage, their likely objections, and your prepared responses. AI polishes the language.

**Do this:** in week three, run one negotiation with your script and note what happened.

**Not this:** negotiating on emotion. Every number you quote comes from your sheet, not your mood.

## Module 6: Scaling to cooperative and aggregator models

Volume is the lever. A single farmer with 50 bags gets a farm-gate price; a group with 500 bags can call a processor, a supermarket, or an exporter. The work is coordination — and that is exactly what a digitized system solves.

**The two models:**

- **Cooperative** — members own it jointly, share the marketing, split revenue by contribution
- **Aggregator** — one buyer (you, possibly) buys from many farmers, grades, pools, and sells at volume to bigger buyers

**What scaling needs to work:**

- **A shared plan** — who plants what and how much, so the group supplies steady volume
- **Pooled records** — one sheet where every member's harvest, costs, and contribution are recorded
- **One selling voice** — one representative or one brand negotiates for the group
- **Fair sharing rules** — how revenue splits, agreed in writing before the first sale
- **Quality standards** — grading and sorting rules so the pooled product is consistent

**Worksheet 6A — Aggregation plan:** the volume you need to attract your target buyer, how many members that requires, the collection point and schedule, the grading rules, and the revenue split.

**Do this:** in week four, draft your aggregation plan and the revenue-sharing rules.

**Not this:** pooling produce without records and rules. Money arguments kill more groups than low prices do.

## Module 7: Branding, value addition, and premium pricing

The same produce, branded and value-added, sells for more. This is the difference between a commodity and a product.

**Value addition tiers (start small):**

- **Grading and sorting** — uniform size and quality sell for more and open supermarket doors
- **Packaging** — clean, labelled packaging with weight and origin turns a sack into a brand
- **Processing** — drying, milling, juicing, freezing extends shelf life and lifts price
- **Direct channels** — WhatsApp, farmers' markets, hotels, and offices pay more than wholesale

**Branding on a phone:**

- A name, a simple logo (Canva), and one line about what makes you different
- Consistent packaging and labels — no fake claims
- A WhatsApp catalogue and status updates that make your produce look consistent and trustworthy
- Story: how it's grown, by whom, where — provenance sells

**The premium math:** if your break-even is KES 40/kg and you sell graded, packed, branded produce at KES 90/kg, the extra KES 50 is pure margin — value addition pays for itself fast if you keep processing costs controlled.

**Worksheet 7A — Brand and value-add plan:** your brand name, one-line promise, packaging spec, value-addition step, premium price target, and the cost of the value-add per unit.

**Do this:** in week five, design your packaging and run the premium-price math.

**Not this:** claiming "organic" or "pesticide-free" without truth. One dishonest label can destroy a brand and a market.

## Module 8: Sustainable, AI-assisted operations

A farm enterprise must survive its own seasons. Sustainability here means two things: the business keeps producing, and the land keeps producing.

**Agronomic sustainability:**

- Soil health: rotate crops, add manure and compost, reduce dependence on expensive inputs
- Pest and disease awareness: fall armyworm, among others, can devastate maize — monitor early and act early, with local agrovet advice
- Two-season planning: plant for both the long rains (March–May) and short rains (October–December), and stagger so harvests spread
- Water and irrigation plans that survive dry spells

**Operational sustainability:**

- Cash-flow management: buy inputs when prices are low, sell into the best window, keep a buffer for a bad season
- A monthly system review: what worked, what failed, what to change next season
- Documentation for financing: clean records are what banks, SACCOs, and grant programs ask for
- Risk diversification: more than one crop or income stream so one failure doesn't sink the farm

**The AI-assisted operations loop:** records → AI report → decision → action → records. Every month the loop tightens the farm.

**Worksheet 8A — Sustainability audit:** soil and water plan, pest-monitoring schedule, cash-flow buffer target, and a monthly review date in your calendar.

**Do this:** in week six, run your sustainability audit and set your monthly review.

**Not this:** farming season to season on hope. The enterprise runs on the loop, and the loop runs on records.

## Module 9: Case studies — Kenyan agribusinesses that scaled

**Case study 1: The tomato farmer who stopped guessing.** A Kiambu tomato farmer tracked costs and sales in one sheet for a full season. He discovered his break-even was KES 45/kg, not the KES 30 he had assumed — he had been selling below cost for years when prices dipped. He staggered planting across three plots, negotiated with two hotels directly at KES 70–85/kg, and only used the broker to clear surplus. Same land, higher revenue, and a price sheet that ended the guesswork.

**Case study 2: The cooperative that got a real buyer.** A maize cooperative of 60 members pooled harvests into a shared record sheet, agreed a revenue split by contribution, and sent one representative to negotiate with a large buyer. The volume changed the conversation: the buyer took the whole batch at a premium over farm-gate, and the group reinvested part of the margin into better seed for the next season. The system, not luck, brought the buyer.

**Case study 3: The avocado aggregator who branded.** An aggregator who bought from small farmers started grading and packing avocados under his own label, with origin and weight on each box. Supermarket and export buyers who ignored ungraded produce responded to the consistency. The premium over ungraded pricing more than covered the sorting and packing cost — value addition turned a commodity into a supplier relationship.

Each case follows the same loop: real records, researched prices, prepared negotiation, and a system that runs beyond one person.

## Prompt Library

Copy these into ChatGPT (or Claude/Gemini), fill the slots in brackets, and adapt.

**Farm system setup**

Use to build your record sheet.

```
Create a Google Sheets layout for my farm. I grow/keep [crops and
livestock] on [land size]. I need 4 tabs: Planting/Production
(crop, plot, date, seed cost, inputs), Costs (date, category, amount),
Sales (date, quantity, price, buyer, channel), and Notes (weather,
pests, problems). Add a summary section that totals costs, revenue,
and profit per crop, and a formula for cost per unit. Explain setup
step by step.
```

**Season planning**

Use before each planting season.

```
I have [X] acres in [county]. Budget for inputs is KES [X]. Plan my
[long rains March-May / short rains October-December] season: which
crops to plant, land share per crop, estimated input costs, expected
yield, and rough profit at current prices. Include a staggered
planting schedule so I don't harvest everything at once.
```

**Weather and agronomy research**

Use before planting and during the season.

```
Research the current weather outlook and agronomy advice for
[county] this season for [crop]. What should a farmer prepare for
(rain onset, dry spells), and what input and planting decisions
follow? Give me a short action list, and flag anything I should
confirm with my local agrovet or extension officer.
```

**Pest and disease monitoring plan**

Use to protect the crop early.

```
I grow [crop] in [county]. Build a weekly pest-monitoring checklist,
focusing on the main threats (including fall armyworm awareness for
maize). For each threat: what to look for, when to act, and the
first response steps. Note that my agrovet confirms any chemical
use. Keep it simple enough to run every Friday.
```

**Monthly farm report**

Use monthly with your records.

```
Here is my farm record data for this month: [paste rows]. Total my
costs, revenue, and profit per crop. Calculate cost per unit and
profit per unit for [crop]. Compare to last month and tell me what
to adjust: more or less of a crop, input changes, or timing. Keep
it practical, one page.
```

**Yield and cash flow forecast**

Use before a season starts.

```
Using my past records: [paste yield and price history] and my plan
for next season [paste plan], forecast my yield, revenue, and cash
flow month by month. Show me when money goes out (inputs) and when
it comes in (sales), and tell me if I'll need a cash buffer and how
much in KES.
```

**Market price research**

Use before planting and before selling.

```
Search for the current farm-gate and retail prices of [crop] in
[county] this week, plus the trend over the last two seasons and
what is driving demand. List 3 ways a small farmer can get a better
price than the broker's first offer.
```

**Buyer research and targeting**

Use to build your buyer list.

```
I produce [crop], [quantity] per [week/season], from [location].
List the buyer types in Kenya who buy this (wholesalers, mama
mbogas, supermarkets, hotels, processors, exporters). For each:
volumes they want, quality and grading expectations, payment
behavior, and how I approach them. Suggest where to start with my
volume.
```

**Break-even and target price**

Use before you quote any price.

```
For [crop], my total input costs this season are KES [X] and I
expect [Y] kg yield. What is my break-even per kg? What should I
sell at for a [X]% margin? And what is my minimum acceptable price
below which I should hold or find another buyer? Show the math.
```

**Negotiation script**

Use before you meet a buyer.

```
I am selling [crop], [quantity], quality [grade], from [location].
My break-even is KES [X]/kg and my target is KES [Y]/kg. Write a
negotiation script: how to state my price first, my 3 strongest
leverage points, and responses to these 5 buyer pushes: "too
expensive", "the market is down", "my other supplier is cheaper",
"pay you later", "just this once at a lower price". Keep responses
firm but friendly.
```

**Price comparison across channels**

Use when choosing where to sell.

```
Compare selling [crop] through these channels: broker, local market
stall, mama mboga direct, hotel/restaurant, supermarket, processor,
and WhatsApp direct. For each: likely price per kg, volumes they
take, payment speed, and effort required. Recommend the mix that
maximizes what I keep per season with my volume of [X] kg.
```

**Aggregation plan**

Use to build your group model.

```
I want to aggregate [crop] from farmers in [area]. Volume needed to
attract [buyer type] is about [X] kg per [week/season]. Build a
plan: how many member farmers that needs, a collection point and
schedule, grading and sorting rules, and a simple way to record each
member's contribution in one sheet. Draft the message I send to
invite farmers to join.
```

**Revenue-sharing agreement**

Use before any pooled sale.

```
Write a simple revenue-sharing agreement for a farmer group pooling
[crop]. Cover: who sells, how revenue splits (by contribution), the
record-keeping rule, quality standards, how disputes are resolved,
and what happens if a member doesn't deliver. Simple language, and
note that a lawyer should review it before signing.
```

**Group coordination messages**

Use weekly to run the group.

```
Draft a WhatsApp message to my farmer group of [X] members for this
week: reminder of the collection point and time [details], what each
member should bring (quantity, grade), and any change in prices.
Keep it clear and short, and end with one call to action.
```

**Brand and packaging plan**

Use to design your product.

```
I produce [crop] and want to brand it for a premium price. Suggest:
a brand name, a one-line promise, simple packaging ideas (material,
size, label), and what to print on the label (origin, weight, date,
contact). Keep it honest and affordable on a small scale, and give
me the copy for the label in under 50 words.
```

**Premium product description**

Use for your WhatsApp catalogue and buyers.

```
Write a short product description for my [crop/product]: grown in
[location], [how it's grown], graded [grade], packed [size], price
KES [X], delivery terms [X], contact [number]. Make it sound
consistent and trustworthy, and give me 3 versions: for a WhatsApp
status, for a supermarket buyer, and for a hotel or restaurant.
```

**Value addition feasibility**

Use before you invest in processing.

```
I sell [crop] at KES [X]/kg. I want to add value: [option, e.g.
sorting/grading, drying, milling, packaging]. My estimated added
cost per unit is KES [X]. Research what the value-added product
sells for in Kenya, show me the added margin, the investment needed,
and whether it's worth doing at my volume of [X] kg. Give me a
go/no-go verdict.
```

**Premium pricing positioning**

Use to justify a higher price.

```
I sell [value-added product] at KES [X] while competitors sell the
raw [crop] at KES [Y]. Write the key selling points that justify
my premium: quality and grading, packaging, origin story, freshness,
convenience. Then write 3 lines I can say to a buyer who questions
the price. Keep them honest.
```

**Supplier and input management**

Use to cut input costs.

```
Here are my input purchases this season: [paste list with prices].
Analyze where I'm overpaying and suggest cheaper or better
alternatives available in Kenya (including buying in bulk or through
supply platforms like iProcure). Estimate the savings per season and
give me a purchasing plan for next season.
```

**Sustainability and soil plan**

Use to protect long-term productivity.

```
I farm [crop] on [land] in [county]. Build a simple sustainability
plan: crop rotation for the next 2 seasons, manure and compost use,
reducing input dependence, and water management for dry spells.
Keep it practical for a smallholder and note what to confirm with my
agrovet.
```

**Cash flow and financing readiness**

Use before approaching a bank, SACCO, or grant program.

```
From my farm records [paste summary], prepare a one-page summary for
a financing application: annual revenue, costs, profit, land and
assets, repayment plan from a loan of KES [X]. Also list the records
and documents I should have ready to show a lender or SACCO.
```

**Risk planning**

Use to survive a bad season.

```
Model a bad season for my farm: prices drop 20% and yield drops 30%
on [crop]. Using my records [paste], show my projected loss and the
3 actions I should take now to reduce it (diversification, storage,
forward selling, cost cuts). Give me a one-page risk plan.
```

**Monthly system review**

Use every month to keep the enterprise sharp.

```
Review my farm this month: [paste records and notes]. What worked,
what failed, and what I should change next month? Look at crops,
inputs, pricing, and buyers. Give me 3 decisions to make this week
and a one-line action for each.
```

**90-day enterprise plan**

Use at the start, then track weekly.

```
Build my 90-day agribusiness plan. I have [land/crops/livestock] in
[county], current revenue KES [X], and my goal is [goal, e.g.
increase revenue by 30%]. Structure it by week across: records and
digitization, market and pricing, value addition, aggregation or
cooperative, and sustainability. End with revenue and profit targets
for day 90.
```

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Recording sales but ignoring costs, then believing in fake profit
- Planting what everyone plants without checking prices
- Accepting the first price because you don't know your break-even
- Negotiating on emotion instead of from your numbers
- Pooling produce in a group without records and written sharing rules
- Labeling products with claims you can't prove
- Ignoring pest and disease early warnings (fall armyworm included)
- Farming season to season with no cash buffer for a bad season
- Trying to run everything from memory instead of the record sheet
