# AI for Fashion Designers & Tailors: Design, Market & Sell

**Overview**

Kenya's fashion scene is full of talented tailors and designers whose craft is excellent but whose marketing is missing. You sew beautifully — now learn to sell like a brand. This course shows you how AI generates design inspiration, mood boards, and marketing content, how to build an online catalog, how to take orders on WhatsApp and Instagram, and how to price custom versus ready-made work. The one clear win: a catalog of your work that sells while you are at the machine.

**Who this is for**

- Tailors and designers already selling on Instagram and WhatsApp who want to sell more
- Fashion students and graduates who want to turn skill into income
- Fundisha-style creators building a local fashion following
- Anyone making custom clothes, kits, or accessories who wants a real customer system

**What you'll be able to do**

- Use AI for design inspiration, mood boards, and pattern ideas
- Create product photography concepts and marketing content
- Build an online catalog and take orders via WhatsApp and social media
- Price custom vs ready-made pieces and manage production timelines
- Grow a loyal customer base with AI-assisted follow-ups

## Module 1: AI for design inspiration and mood boards

Designer's block is real, and it hits in the middle of your busiest week. AI gives you starting points in minutes: color stories, fabric directions, silhouette ideas, and entire collections to adapt — never to copy.

**How to use AI for design:**

- Ask for a capsule collection concept around a theme (a Kenyan wedding, office wear, a music video look)
- Ask for color palettes that fit your market and season
- Ask for garment details: necklines, sleeves, hemlines, pockets, closures
- Ask for fabric and pattern direction that suits your climate and price point

**From idea to mood board:**

- AI generates the concept and color palette
- You collect real reference images (Instagram, Pinterest, Google Images)
- Canva Magic Studio or a simple Canva board assembles the mood board
- The mood board becomes your design brief for sketching and cutting

**Do this:** treat AI as your design assistant, not your copy source. Adapt ideas to your skills, your fabrics, and your customers.

**Not this:** lifting another designer's work or reproducing a known brand's exact pieces. That is how small designers get flagged and lose trust.

**TEMPLATE: Design brief from AI**

```
DESIGN BRIEF
Theme: [e.g. modern Kitenge office wear]
Target customer: [age, style, occasion]
Colors: [from palette]
Garment types: [3-5 pieces]
Fabrics available: [what I can actually get]
Skill level: [what I can make well]
Price range: [KES target per piece]
Deadline: [collection date]
```

## Module 2: Pattern ideas and technical planning

Inspiration is nothing without a plan you can cut and sew. AI helps you move from concept to construction.

**The technical uses of AI:**

- Break a garment sketch into its pattern pieces and construction order
- Estimate fabric requirements by garment type (a dress, a shirt, a trouser)
- Suggest seam finishes and details appropriate to your fabrics
- Plan a small production line for repeated pieces (a school uniform order, a batch of shirts)

**The honest limit:** AI can describe patterns and construction, but it cannot see your fabric, your machine, or your hands. A pattern still needs your skill to draft or adapt, and testing on cheap fabric before cutting the real thing.

**Do this:** use AI to plan the steps and quantities, then verify the actual pattern against your measurements and experience.

**Not this:** sewing from AI output alone on expensive fabric without testing. Sample first, every time.

**TEMPLATE: Production plan**

```
PRODUCTION PLAN
Garment: [name]
Pattern pieces: [list]
Fabric needed per piece: [meters]
Notions: [thread, zips, buttons, lining]
Construction order: [1. 2. 3. ...]
Repeats in this batch: [number]
Time per piece: [hours]
Quality check points: [what to inspect at each stage]
```

## Module 3: Product photography concepts and content

Your catalog lives and dies on photos. You do not need a studio or a camera kit — you need a phone, good light, and a concept. AI plans the shoot; you execute it.

**Photography concepts AI can generate:**

- Shot lists for a mini product shoot (details, outfit, flat lay, in-motion)
- Backdrop and location ideas that fit your brand (a wall in your neighborhood, a clean bed sheet, a stairwell)
- Styling ideas — shoes, jewelry, bags that make the piece look complete
- Caption and hashtag sets for every photo

**The shooting basics that beat filters:**

- Natural window light is free and flattering
- Shoot against plain, clean backgrounds
- Capture the garment flat, on a hanger, and worn
- Take detail shots: stitching, fabric texture, buttons, fit
- Keep the same style across photos so your feed looks like a brand

**Do this:** build one repeating photo style and use it for every product. A consistent feed looks professional without a professional photographer.

**Not this:** posting blurry mirror selfies of every piece. Customers cannot judge fit, fabric, or finish from a bad photo — they will buy from someone clearer.

**TEMPLATE: Product shoot plan**

```
PRODUCT SHOOT PLAN
Garment: [name]
Goal of shoot: [what the photo must prove - fit, fabric, style]
Backdrop: [location and background]
Lighting: [window light / outdoor shade]
Styling: [shoes, accessories, hair, setting]
Shots needed: [flat lay, on model, detail x3, in-motion]
Captions and hashtags: [write after photos]
```

## Module 4: Build an online catalog and take orders via WhatsApp

A catalog is how customers browse without asking you 20 questions. Yours can be simple: an Instagram feed plus a WhatsApp Business catalog, or a lightweight site built with free tools.

**The catalog system:**

- Every product gets a standard listing: name, price, sizes, colors, delivery time, how to order
- Instagram feed and highlights organize your categories (dresses, shirts, kaftans, kits)
- WhatsApp Business catalog mirrors the same products
- Each listing ends with one action: "Order via WhatsApp"

**The WhatsApp order flow:**

1. Customer asks about a product or taps "order"
2. You confirm size, color, and quantity
3. You quote the price including delivery
4. Customer pays by M-Pesa till or paybill
5. You confirm payment, note the production timeline, and deliver

**Do this:** keep one Google Sheet with every order: customer, item, size, deposit, balance, timeline, delivery. A sheet is your memory when orders multiply.

**Not this:** taking orders in a scattered chat thread and hoping you remember sizes. You will sew the wrong size once, and that customer will tell ten others.

**TEMPLATE: Order tracking sheet columns**

```
ORDER TRACKING
Columns: Date | Customer name | Phone | Item | Size | Color |
Price | Deposit paid | Balance | Timeline | Status | Delivery date |
```

## Module 5: Price custom vs ready-made and manage timelines

Two different businesses hide inside one tailor shop: ready-made (you control everything) and custom (the customer controls the brief). They need different pricing and different timelines.

**Ready-made pricing:**

- Cost of fabric + notions + your labor time + a profit margin
- Price per piece is fixed across sizes (minor adjustments for extra fabric)
- Volume-friendly: the same price for 5 shirts is a bulk discount, not a new calculation

**Custom pricing:**

- Higher per piece because each order is a new pattern, fitting, and iteration
- Quote a range, not a fixed price, until the fabric and design are confirmed
- Add a fitting or consultation session into the price
- Take a deposit (typically 50%) before cutting

**Timeline management:**

- State the production time in writing before the deposit
- Build buffer days for fittings and corrections
- Track every order against its promise date
- If you are running late, tell the customer early — never on the promised day

**Do this:** write your timeline promise on every order: "Production 7-10 days from deposit, fittings by appointment."

**Not this:** quoting "next week" to five customers at once. Over-promising is how small tailors get a reputation for lateness, and it kills repeat business.

**TEMPLATE: Price quotation**

```
PRICE QUOTATION
Customer: [name]
Garment: [description]
Fabric: [type and cost]
Notions: [cost]
Labor: [hours x rate]
Total: [KES]
Deposit (50%): [KES]
Balance on: [completion / delivery]
Timeline: [days from deposit]
Fittings: [included / extra]
```

## Module 6: Grow a loyal customer base with AI follow-ups

Your best customers are people you already served. Follow-up turns a one-time buyer into a regular, and AI makes follow-up affordable at any scale.

**The follow-up system:**

- After delivery: thank them, ask for a photo of them wearing it, invite a review
- Weekly: a catalog or "new arrivals" status on WhatsApp and Instagram
- Seasonally: offers around events (December weddings, school opening, New Year)
- Personally: a message on a customer's order anniversary or major occasion

**The AI role:**

- Drafts the thank-you and review requests
- Writes new-arrival captions and statuses in your voice
- Suggests personalized notes for repeat customers ("you loved our kaftans, this new fabric is in")
- Plans a simple content calendar so you post consistently without stress

**Do this:** keep a customer list with their past orders and preferences. A good follow-up message references what they actually bought.

**Not this:** spamming every customer with the same discount every week. Over-messaging makes people mute you.

**TEMPLATE: Customer follow-up calendar**

```
CUSTOMER FOLLOW-UP
Day/Week | Channel | Message type | Audience
Week 1 | WhatsApp | Thank-you + photo request | Recent buyers
Week 2 | Instagram | New arrivals | All followers
Week 3 | WhatsApp | Care message + offer | Repeat customers
Month end | Status | Behind-the-scenes or pre-order | All
Seasonal | All | Event-driven offer | Target list
```

## Module 7: Assignment — launch your fashion catalog and order system

Build the full selling system for your existing work and test it.

**Assignment tasks:**

1. Choose 5 pieces you have already made and complete a product brief for each.
2. Plan and shoot a mini product session using the shoot plan template.
3. Build your catalog: post the 5 products on Instagram and add them to WhatsApp Business catalog.
4. Price each piece using the ready-made or custom formula, and show your working.
5. Set up your order tracking sheet with the exact columns from Module 4.
6. Write your follow-up sequence and customer calendar for the next month.
7. Take one real order through the full flow: inquiry, quote, deposit, production promise, delivery.

**TEMPLATE: Assignment checklist**

```
[ ] 5 product briefs complete
[ ] Product photos taken in one consistent style
[ ] Catalog live on Instagram and WhatsApp Business
[ ] All 5 pieces priced with margins
[ ] Order tracking sheet set up
[ ] Follow-up sequence and calendar written
[ ] One real order completed end to end
```

## Prompt Library

Copy, fill in the blanks, and adapt.

**Capsule collection concept**

Use when you need a direction for a new collection.

```
Create a capsule collection concept for a Kenyan designer. Theme: [wedding guest wear / office wear / kaftans / casual streetwear]. Target customer: [age, style, occasion]. Give me 5 pieces, a color palette that suits [season/climate], and a one-line description of each piece. Keep it wearable and sellable, not runway fantasy.
```

**Color palette generator**

Use to build a coherent range.

```
Give me 3 color palettes for a fashion collection in [season] for the Kenyan market. For each palette: the colors (hex names), the lead color, the neutral, the accent, and what customer it suits. Consider [skin tones range, local fabric availability like Kitenge, linen, cotton].
```

**Garment detail ideas**

Use when a design needs fresh details.

```
Suggest 10 design details for a [garment type, e.g. women's shirt dress] that a small Kenyan tailor can actually sew: neckline, sleeve, hem, pockets, closure, and fabric treatment options. Match them to my skill level [beginner/intermediate/advanced] and my fabric [fabric type].
```

**Pattern construction breakdown**

Use to plan sewing order.

```
Break down how to construct a [garment type] step by step: the pattern pieces needed, the order of assembly, seam finishes for [fabric type], and where to add ease. Assume a home setup with [machine type]. List the steps in order.
```

**Fabric and quantity estimate**

Use before buying fabric.

```
Estimate fabric needed for a [garment type] for sizes [range]. Give meters per size for [fabric width, e.g. 45 inches / 60 inches] and note where extra fabric is needed for patterns or lining. Suggest where to save fabric in cutting.
```

**Production line plan**

Use for batch orders like school uniforms or event kits.

```
I need to produce [X] units of [garment type] by [date]. Plan the production line: steps, time per step, how many I can cut/sew/finish per day, buffer for corrections, and a daily target. Assume I work [hours] a day with [helpers or alone].
```

**Product photography shot list**

Use before every shoot.

```
Write a shot list for a product shoot of [garment type]. Include: flat lay, hanging, on-model front, back, detail close-ups (stitching, fabric, buttons), and one in-motion shot. For each shot, note the angle, backdrop, and lighting I should use with a phone in [indoor/outdoor] light.
```

**Instagram caption and hashtags**

Use for each catalog post.

```
Write an Instagram caption for my [garment type] in [fabric/color] at [price]. Mention: the design, how it fits, occasions to wear it, delivery in [X] days, and order via WhatsApp [number]. Add 5-8 relevant hashtags including Kenyan fashion tags. Keep the caption under 120 words, no hype.
```

**WhatsApp catalog description**

Use for the WhatsApp Business catalog.

```
Write a short WhatsApp Business catalog description for [product] under 100 words: what it is, sizes, colors, price [KES], production time [days], and how to order. End with a call to action to send a message. Warm, clear, no emojis.
```

**Order quote message**

Use to quote a custom piece.

```
Write a WhatsApp message quoting a custom [garment type] for a client. Include: fabric options and prices [details], total quote [KES], 50% deposit via M-Pesa till [number], production time [days], and that I need their measurements for [fittings]. Clear and professional, under 120 words.
```

**Deposit and timeline confirmation**

Use when the deposit arrives.

```
Write a confirmation message for a customer who just paid a KES [X] deposit via M-Pesa for [garment]. Confirm: their item, the balance due [KES], the production timeline [dates], and when their fitting is. Keep it warm and specific, under 100 words.
```

**Production delay notice**

Use the moment you know you will be late.

```
Write an honest, apologetic WhatsApp message telling a customer their [garment] will be ready on [new date] instead of [old date] because of [reason]. Offer [a small compensation, e.g. free adjustment or delivery] and thank them for patience. Keep it under 80 words, no excuses that blame them.
```

**Thank-you and review request**

Use after delivery.

```
Write a thank-you WhatsApp message after delivering [garment] to a customer. Ask them to send a photo wearing it, invite a review or testimonial, and remind them about [next occasion offer]. Keep it short, warm, and specific to what they bought.
```

**New arrivals status**

Use weekly to stay visible.

```
Write a WhatsApp status and Instagram story text announcing new arrivals: [list 2-3 pieces]. Include prices, why each one is worth buying, and the order line. Keep the status under 40 words and the story text punchy. One clear call to action each.
```

**Personalized follow-up for repeat customer**

Use to win repeat orders.

```
A returning customer bought [past item] from me [X months ago]. They [mention preference, e.g. love kaftans in Kitenge]. Write a personalized follow-up message offering [new related piece/fabric] with a returning-customer note. Reference what they bought before. Under 80 words.
```

**Seasonal offer around Kenyan events**

Use for December, school opening, New Year.

```
Create a seasonal offer for [event, e.g. December weddings and New Year] for my [garment types]. Suggest: the offer (e.g. bundle 2 pieces, discount on pre-orders), a price, a deadline, and a WhatsApp promo under 70 words. Make it easy to order in one message.
```

**Customer care message**

Use to keep relationships warm without selling.

```
Write a short, non-sales WhatsApp care message to my customers asking how their [garment type] is serving them and inviting them to send photos. Mention I am taking orders for [next event/season]. Keep it under 50 words, genuinely caring, not pushy.
```

**Content calendar for a fashion page**

Use to plan a month of posts.

```
Build a 4-week content calendar for my fashion Instagram and WhatsApp Business. Include 3 posts per week: product, behind-the-scenes, customer photo or testimonial, and one reel/story idea each week. Match to upcoming [event/season]. Keep each idea one line so I can execute fast.
```

**Pricing review**

Use to check your margins.

```
Here is my pricing: [product, price, estimated fabric cost, time per piece]. Calculate my margin, hourly rate, and whether this price is sustainable. Suggest a new price that keeps me competitive locally while paying me fairly. Show the math in KES.
```

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Copying other designers' exact work instead of adapting ideas
- Sewing from AI descriptions without sampling first
- Posting poor photos and expecting sales
- Taking orders in chat threads without a tracking sheet
- Underpricing custom work because the deposit feels awkward to ask for
- Over-promising timelines and delivering late
- Spamming customers with the same offers every week
