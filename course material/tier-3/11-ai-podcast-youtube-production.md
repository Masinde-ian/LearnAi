# AI for Podcasters & YouTubers: Full Production System

**Overview**

Kenya is full of creators with good voices and empty channels. The problem is rarely talent — it is a production system. This course gives you a full pipeline: AI plans and scripts episodes, edits your audio and video, designs thumbnails, writes show notes, and keeps you publishing on schedule. The one clear win: a consistent publishing rhythm that grows an audience and attracts sponsorships, turning your channel into a real media brand.

**Who this is for**

- Podcasters and YouTubers who publish once and then disappear for a month
- Creators who spend hours editing and never have time to grow
- Niche creators (finance, faith, comedy, health, business) building an audience
- Anyone who wants to monetize a channel and pitch sponsors with confidence

**What you'll be able to do**

- Plan and script episodes and videos with AI
- Use AI for editing workflows, thumbnails, and show notes
- Build a consistent publishing and promotion schedule
- Grow an audience and pitch for sponsorships
- Turn a podcast or channel into a full media brand

## Module 1: Define your show and plan with AI

Before the first episode, decide what your show is and who it serves. A clear show identity makes planning, scripting, and pitching easier for years.

**The show definition:**

- **Topic and angle** — what you cover and how you are different ("Kiswahili business breakdowns", "Nairobi tech for beginners")
- **Audience** — who watches and what problem you solve for them
- **Format** — solo, interview, panel, story, tutorial
- **Length** — short (under 10 min), medium (20-30 min), or long-form
- **Frequency** — weekly, biweekly, or twice a week; pick what you can actually sustain

**How AI plans:**

- Generates 20-50 episode ideas from your topic and audience
- Builds a 3-month content calendar with hooks for each episode
- Suggests series ideas and seasonal themes (e.g. a "December money series")
- Flags trending angles in your niche for timely episodes

**Do this:** commit to one format and one frequency you can sustain for 3 months. Consistency beats occasional perfection.

**Not this:** starting 5 different shows at once. One channel, one identity, repeated until it works.

**TEMPLATE: Show definition**

```
SHOW DEFINITION
Show name: [name]
Topic and angle: [what + how we differ]
Audience: [who watches, what problem we solve]
Format: [solo / interview / panel / story]
Length: [minutes]
Frequency: [weekly / biweekly]
One-sentence pitch: [for sponsors and viewers]
```

## Module 2: Plan and script episodes with AI

Writer's block kills more channels than bad equipment. AI gives you an outline in minutes; you give it your voice, stories, and expertise.

**The scripting workflow:**

1. Start with a single episode idea and your real knowledge on it
2. AI produces an outline: hook, key points, examples, call to action
3. You feed in your own stories, facts, and opinions — the AI cannot know your experience
4. AI turns the outline into a script in your speaking style
5. You rewrite the parts that need your authentic voice

**The hook is everything:**

- First 10 seconds on YouTube, first 30 seconds on a podcast
- Hook with a question, a claim, a problem, or a story — not "welcome back to the channel"

**Do this:** write the hook first, and keep a hook bank of 20 openers you can reuse.

**Not this:** reading an AI script verbatim with zero personality. Your audience came for you — make sure they hear you.

**TEMPLATE: Episode plan**

```
EPISODE PLAN
Episode: [number]
Title (under 60 characters): [title]
One-line summary: [what this episode delivers]
Target viewer/listener: [who it serves]
Hook (first 15 seconds): [question/claim/story]
Key points: [3-5 points with my real examples]
Call to action: [subscribe, comment, share, follow]
Show notes plan: [bullets]
```

## Module 3: AI editing workflows that save your week

Editing is where creators quit. AI turns hours of raw footage into a finished episode in a fraction of the time.

**The core tools (2026 landscape):**

- **Descript** — edit audio like a document: delete words in the transcript and they disappear from the audio. Also removes filler words and mistakes
- **CapCut** — free video editor with auto-captions, auto-cut, and templates; runs on your phone
- **Riverside** — records remote guests in separate high-quality tracks, then AI edits and captions
- **Whisper / Otter** — transcription to start from text instead of audio

**The AI editing workflow:**

1. Record with Riverside (guests) or a simple mic (solo)
2. Auto-transcribe with Descript, Whisper, or Otter
3. Delete the ums, silences, and mistakes in the transcript
4. Auto-generate captions in CapCut or Descript
5. Cut a short clip for social from the best 30 seconds
6. Generate thumbnails with Canva Magic Studio and AI suggestions

**Do this:** set a batch editing time — record 2 episodes, edit both in one sitting.

**Not this:** editing every episode frame by frame like a cinema film. Your audience wants value, not perfection.

**TEMPLATE: Post-production checklist**

```
POST-PRODUCTION CHECKLIST
[ ] Filler words and silences removed
[ ] Intro and outro in place
[ ] Captions generated and checked
[ ] Thumbnail designed
[ ] Show notes written
[ ] 1-2 social clips cut
[ ] Episode scheduled for [date/time]
```

## Module 4: Thumbnails and show notes that get clicks

Two small pieces decide most of your growth: the thumbnail (gets the click) and the show notes (keep the platform happy and your audience informed).

**Thumbnails that win:**

- One clear subject, readable at phone size
- A face with a real emotion, or a strong product/object
- 3-5 words max of text
- High contrast and simple background
- Consistent style across your channel so people recognize you

**Show notes that work:**

- A 2-3 sentence summary with the key takeaway
- Timestamps for chapters
- 5-10 bullets of what was covered
- Links to anything mentioned
- A call to action (subscribe, comment, share)

**Do this:** write the thumbnail concept and show notes before you publish — same sitting as the edit.

**Not this:** publishing with a random screenshot as a thumbnail and no description. You are handing the platform no reason to recommend you.

**TEMPLATE: Thumbnail brief**

```
THUMBNAIL BRIEF
Title: [title]
Key emotion: [curiosity / shock / joy / anger]
Subject: [face / object / text]
Text overlay (max 5 words): [text]
Colors and style: [consistent with my brand]
Alternative concepts: [2 options]
```

## Module 5: Build a consistent publishing and promotion schedule

Growth is a rhythm, not a viral spike. The platforms reward consistency, and your audience learns when to expect you.

**The publishing system:**

- **Pick a day and time** — e.g. Thursday 7pm, when Kenyans are off work and scrolling
- **Batch produce** — record and edit 2-3 episodes in one session
- **Schedule releases** — upload ahead of time, not at midnight with panic
- **Cross-post** — one episode becomes: full video, audio version, 2-3 shorts, a WhatsApp status, and an Instagram caption

**The promotion cadence:**

- Release day: full post across platforms
- Day 2: a clip or a quote graphic
- Day 4: an audience question tied to the episode
- Weekly: a story or status asking what topic to cover next

**Do this:** create one content piece from each episode, then a second from the same episode. One episode, two weeks of content.

**Not this:** posting a video and forgetting it. The algorithm rewards the push, and so does your audience.

**TEMPLATE: Weekly publishing schedule**

```
WEEKLY SCHEDULE
Monday: [record/plan next episode]
Tuesday: [edit + thumbnail + show notes]
Wednesday: [final review + schedule upload]
Thursday 7pm: [publish episode]
Friday: [share clip + quote graphic]
Saturday: [story/status + audience question]
Sunday: [rest or plan next week]
```

## Module 6: Grow an audience and pitch for sponsorships

Audience numbers open doors, but sponsorships are won with a pitch and a media kit, not luck.

**Growing the audience:**

- Serve a specific niche deeply rather than everyone shallowly
- Ask viewers to subscribe and comment — platforms push channels that get responses
- Publish consistently for at least 3-6 months before judging results
- Collaborate with 2-3 creators in your niche
- Repurpose best episodes into short-form constantly

**YouTube monetization basics (2026):**

- Ad revenue requires 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours, OR 10 million Shorts views in 90 days
- Ad revenue is one income line, not the whole business — sponsorships pay more
- Disclose AI-generated content clearly; platforms require it and audiences trust honesty

**The sponsorship pitch:**

- Build a one-page media kit: your audience, niche, reach, engagement, and examples
- Pitch 5-10 brands that fit your audience — not random companies
- Offer a clear value: a mention, a dedicated episode, a review, social posts
- Give real numbers, never inflated ones

**Do this:** prepare your media kit before you pitch, and pitch brands your audience already cares about.

**Not this:** faking reach numbers. Brands verify, and a channel caught lying is finished.

**TEMPLATE: Media kit**

```
MEDIA KIT
Channel/podcast name: [name]
Niche and angle: [what we cover]
Audience: [demographics, what they care about]
Subscribers/listeners: [real numbers]
Average views/listens per episode: [real numbers]
Engagement examples: [comments, shares, screenshots]
Past collaborations: [any]
Episode/ad formats: [mention, dedicated episode, review, social]
Contact: [email/WhatsApp]
```

## Prompt Library

Copy, fill in the blanks, and adapt.

**Show identity**

Use once, at the start.

```
Define a clear show identity for my channel/podcast about [topic]. Suggest: a show name, a one-line angle that makes me different, the audience I serve, a format (solo/interview/etc.), an ideal length, and a sustainable frequency for a solo creator in Kenya. Make it specific to [audience, e.g. young Nairobi professionals].
```

**Episode ideas generator**

Use to keep a backlog of topics.

```
Generate 25 episode ideas for my show about [topic]. Audience: [who]. Vary the types: beginner explainers, hot takes, case studies, local examples, interviews, and mistakes to avoid. For each idea, give a one-line hook. Mark the 5 with the most potential.
```

**Episode outline**

Use to start scripting.

```
Create an outline for an episode titled [title]. My angle and knowledge: [paste your notes on the topic]. Structure: hook (first 15 seconds), 3-5 key points with my examples, one common mistake the audience makes, and a call to action. Keep each point to a line so I can script from it.
```

**Full script in my voice**

Use to draft the spoken script.

```
Write a spoken script for my episode on [topic] from this outline: [paste outline]. Use a conversational, natural voice — short sentences, contractions, no corporate language. Include [2-3 of my personal stories/facts: paste]. Length: [X] minutes of speaking. End with my call to action: [subscribe/comment/share].
```

**Hook bank**

Use to open episodes strongly.

```
Write 20 hook options for my show about [topic]. Types: a bold claim, a surprising question, a relatable problem, a story opener, and a myth-busting line. Keep each under 15 words and suitable for my Kenyan audience. I will choose and test.
```

**Interview question list**

Use when hosting a guest.

```
I am interviewing [guest name] who is a [role/expertise]. Write 12 questions for my show about [topic]. Start with a warm opener, then 8 substance questions that reveal their experience and opinions, and end with 2 fun/quick ones. Include follow-up probes for the strongest answers.
```

**Episode title options**

Use to maximize clicks.

```
Give me 10 title options for my episode on [topic]. Mix: curiosity-driven, benefit-driven, and listicle styles. Each under 60 characters and honest — no clickbait that the episode does not deliver. My platform is [YouTube/podcast].
```

**Editing with Descript**

Use to clean up an episode.

```
I recorded a [X]-minute episode on [topic]. Using Descript, what is the fastest workflow to: remove filler words, silence, and repeated mistakes; tighten the intro; and keep the natural conversational feel? Give me the steps in order, then a checklist for the final listen-through.
```

**Captions and social clips**

Use to get more from each episode.

```
From my episode on [topic], suggest: 5 short social clips (each 15-45 seconds) with a hook line and the timestamp where each starts, plus the best quote for a quote graphic. Make each clip work on TikTok, YouTube Shorts, and Instagram Reels.
```

**Thumbnail concepts**

Use before designing in Canva.

```
Give me 3 thumbnail concepts for my video titled [title]. For each: the main subject, the emotion to show, the text overlay (max 5 words), and the background idea. Style must match my brand [describe current style]. Keep them honest — no misleading thumbnails.
```

**Show notes writer**

Use for every published episode.

```
Write the show notes for my episode [title] on [topic]. Include: a 2-sentence summary, 5 bullet takeaways, timestamps for main chapters [list], links mentioned [list], and a call to action. Keep it skimmable and under 200 words.
```

**YouTube description**

Use on every upload.

```
Write a YouTube description for my video [title]. Include: a hook line, 5-7 bullet points on what is covered, timestamps, my channel intro line, and hashtags. Include a disclosure if I used AI tools for part of the content. Under 200 words, keyword-aware but natural.
```

**Publishing schedule builder**

Use to build your rhythm.

```
Build a weekly publishing schedule for a solo creator producing [1 episode/week] on [topic]. Include: planning day, recording day, editing day, publishing day and time (Kenyan audience), and promotion days. Keep it realistic with [X] hours available per week.
```

**Promotion post pack**

Use to promote each release.

```
Write a promotion pack for my new episode [title]: a WhatsApp status under 40 words, an Instagram caption under 120 words, a Facebook post, and a quote graphic text. All should drive to the episode link and sound like me, not an ad agency.
```

**Audience engagement question**

Use between episodes to build replies.

```
Write an audience question tied to my latest episode [title] to post on [Instagram/TikTok/WhatsApp]. It should invite comments and responses from [audience]. Give me 3 options, each under 30 words, and a short way to prompt more answers.
```

**Collaboration pitch**

Use to grow with other creators.

```
Write a short WhatsApp or email pitch to [creator name], who runs a channel about [their topic], proposing a collaboration with my channel [my topic]. Suggest 2 concrete collaboration ideas that serve both audiences. Keep it under 120 words, friendly, and low-pressure.
```

**Sponsorship pitch**

Use to reach brands.

```
Write a sponsorship pitch to [brand] for my channel/podcast. My audience: [real numbers and description]. Offer 3 formats: [a mention, a dedicated episode, social posts]. Explain why my audience fits their product. Include a clear ask (what I want to hear back). Under 200 words, no inflated numbers.
```

**Media kit content**

Use to build your one-pager.

```
Draft the content for my one-page media kit. Facts: [channel name, niche, audience description, subscriber/listener count, average views/listens, engagement examples]. Structure: who I am, who my audience is, what I offer sponsors, and my real numbers. Keep it scannable and professional.
```

**Sponsor episode integration script**

Use after a brand says yes.

```
Write a natural, honest sponsor segment for my episode about [topic]. Sponsor: [brand], what they offer: [product/offer], and any discount code: [code]. The segment should fit my show's tone, not sound like a scripted ad read, and be under 90 seconds. Keep the claim to what the brand can actually deliver.
```

**AI content disclosure**

Use to stay honest and compliant.

```
Write a short, natural disclosure I can include when AI tools helped me plan, script, or edit content. It should be honest, not apologetic, and placed where audiences can see it. Give a version for a video description and a spoken version for a podcast intro.
```
