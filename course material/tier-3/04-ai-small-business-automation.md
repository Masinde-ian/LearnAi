# AI for Small Business Owners: Automate Sales, Marketing & Customer Service

**Overview** — Your business is full of hours you spend repeating the same work: answering the same questions, writing the same captions, chasing the same receipts. This course maps your processes, finds the AI time-savers, and builds a 90-day automation roadmap for marketing, customer service, bookkeeping, and reporting. The one clear win: a running system where AI handles the repetition and you do the work only you can do.

**Who this is for**

- Shop owners, boutiques, salons, and kiosk-to-shop businesses in Kenya
- Service businesses (cleaning, catering, events, logistics) drowning in WhatsApp messages
- Anyone running a side business alongside a job and out of hours
- Small business owners who want better records without hiring an accountant yet

**What you'll be able to do**

- Map your business processes and find where AI saves the most time
- Automate marketing content, ad copy, and promotions
- Run AI-assisted customer service on WhatsApp and social media
- Keep simple, accurate books using AI on your M-Pesa statements
- Track inventory notes and produce reports without spreadsheets wizardry
- Build a realistic 90-day automation roadmap for your specific business
- See the money value of the hours AI saves you

## Module 1: Map your business processes

Every business runs on repeated processes: answering customers, posting offers, ordering stock, recording money in and out, following up, delivering. You cannot automate what you cannot see, so your first job is to write your processes down.

Take one week and list every task you repeat more than twice a month. Group them into five buckets: marketing, customer service, sales/follow-up, money records, and operations. For each task, note how long it takes and whether it needs a human judgment or is pure repetition.

A simple process map is enough — one sheet per bucket with the steps. Do not overthink it. The goal is a list of repeatable tasks you can hand to AI or a tool.

Ask AI to help you see the map: paste your rough task list and let it sort tasks into "automate now", "automate with setup", and "must stay human". The classification is the strategy; your week of notes is the data.

**Do:** capture tasks for a full week before judging what to automate.
**Not this:** automating a mess and making the mess run faster.

## Module 2: Find the time leaks — and their cost

The clearest money-saving target is the time you spend on questions. Many Kenyan small businesses spend 15 to 20 hours a week answering the same FAQs on WhatsApp and social media. At the value of your own time, that is roughly KES 400,000 a year of labour spent repeating yourself.

Do the calculation for your business: hours per week on repetition, times the value of an hour of your time (what you would pay an employee, or what your time earns you), times 52 weeks. That number is your automation budget — the amount of money it is worth spending to fix.

The second leak is marketing, where business owners write fresh captions and ads every week from scratch. That is pure repetition with a creative label. AI does not just save these hours; it raises the quality because you will actually post consistently.

Every leak you fix becomes capacity: more time for customers, for selling, for the work that actually grows the business. Automation is not a tech project; it is a cash-saving project.

**Cost check:** time wasted per week x your hourly value x 52. If the answer surprises you, the roadmap that follows is worth more than the course price.

## Module 3: Automate marketing content and ad copy

Marketing is the first automation win because it is visible, easy, and immediately usable. Build a content bank: your business details, your offers, your best selling points, and your photos, and let AI turn that bank into a month of captions.

Write the promotional calendar once: what sells when. Kenyan retail has predictable beats — back-to-school in January, M-Pesa salary days, festive season, sale weekends. Map your promos to these beats and AI drafts every piece from your offer details.

For ads, give AI the real facts: the product, the price, the discount, the audience, and the platform. It will draft ad copy for Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp status in the tone each platform needs. Then run a simple A/B test — two headlines, same ad — and keep the winner.

Always keep one human guardrail: read every AI caption for wrong prices, wrong dates, and tone. A business that posts "sale until 30th" on the 31st looks careless no matter how good the copy is.

**Do:** build one reusable prompt that knows your business and its offers.
**Not this:** prompting AI from scratch for every post — that is the repetition you are automating.

## Module 4: AI-assisted customer service on WhatsApp and social

Your customers already message you; your job is to answer fast without answering the same thing a hundred times. WhatsApp Business is the centre: set up quick replies for your top ten questions — price, location, opening hours, delivery, sizes, how to order, payment options, returns.

Write an auto-reply for after hours: "Asante for your message. We will respond by [time]. Meanwhile, here is our price list and how to order." The customer stays warm and you stay off the phone at 11pm.

When customers ask detailed questions, use AI to draft answers: paste the question, get a clear response, personalise it, and send. Keep an AI answer bank — every good answer becomes a saved reply for the next customer who asks the same thing.

Social comments and DMs follow the same pattern: AI drafts replies to common comments, you approve, and negative comments get a human reply with a personal touch. Never let AI handle an angry customer alone; that is the moment a human must step in.

**Do:** answer every inquiry within the hour you set — speed is the cheapest sales tool.
**Not this:** making your auto-reply so robotic customers can tell they are talking to a bot.

## Module 5: AI-assisted bookkeeping from M-Pesa statements

You do not need an accountant to know your numbers — you need a system. Start by downloading your monthly M-Pesa statement. That statement is already a record of money in and money out; AI can turn it into simple, understandable books.

Paste your M-Pesa statement into AI and ask it to classify the transactions: income by type, expenses by category, transfers, and withdrawals. Check the classification against your knowledge of your business — AI is fast, but you are the one who knows that a withdrawal was stock, not salary.

Build a simple monthly sheet: income, expenses by category, and your profit for the month. One sheet updated monthly tells you more than a shoebox of receipts. When AI produces it in twenty minutes, you will actually do it.

Keep the routine: the same day every month, download the statement, classify with AI, review, and update the sheet. This habit turns your business from guesswork into numbers you can plan with.

**Do:** separate business and personal money in your record, even if you use one phone.
**Not this:** classifying a money transfer as income because the sheet did not ask questions.

## Module 6: Inventory notes and simple reporting

If you sell stock, your biggest risk is money sitting in slow-moving items while fast sellers run out. You can track this simply: a stock sheet with item, cost, selling price, quantity, and how fast it sells.

Use AI as your analyst: paste your stock sheet and sales notes and ask which items give the best margin, which sit too long, and what to reorder next. AI turns your raw notes into a reorder suggestion you then check against reality.

For reports, ask AI to summarise your month: income, biggest expense, best-selling product, and one thing to change. A one-page monthly report, written in plain language, is what owners of larger businesses pay managers to produce — AI gives you the draft and you add the judgment.

Keep every report in one place — a Google Drive folder named by month. When you need to check a number for a loan, a tax query, or an investor, it is one click away instead of a panic search through receipts.

**Do:** tie inventory and money reports together — stock that does not sell is hidden expense.
**Not this:** running a business where no one, including you, knows the monthly profit.

## Module 7: Your 90-day automation roadmap

Automation works in waves, not overnight. Build a 90-day plan in three phases, each 30 days long, and start each phase only when the last is stable.

- **Days 1-30 — Foundation:** WhatsApp Business quick replies and auto-reply, your content bank prompt, and the first monthly M-Pesa bookkeeping run. These three take days to set up and save hours every week from day one.
- **Days 31-60 — Consistency:** a monthly content calendar producing a week of posts in one sitting, an AI answer bank for customer service, and a stock sheet if you hold inventory.
- **Days 61-90 — Growth:** ad copy with A/B testing, a monthly report you actually read, and one experiment — a new offer, a loyalty WhatsApp group, or a service tier — that the saved hours make possible.

Write your roadmap as one page: goal per phase, the automations to set up, the tools you need, and the owner of each task (probably you at first, a staff member later). Review every 30 days and kill anything that is not saving real time.

The roadmap works because it is small: three automations a month, done properly, beats a big automation project that never gets finished.

**Do:** measure before and after — hours saved per week is the success metric.
**Not this:** setting up twenty automations in week one and abandoning the whole system by week three.

## Prompt Library

**Process mapping**

```
Here are the repeated tasks in my small business: [LIST TASKS].
Sort them into four groups: automate now (quick win), automate
with setup (needs a tool or template), must stay human (needs
judgment), and stop doing (low value). For the automate groups,
name the tool or prompt I would use. My business is [TYPE].
```

How to use it: run with your week of task notes.

**Time-leak cost calculation**

```
I spend [HOURS] hours a week on repetition in my business, and my
time is worth about KES [VALUE] per hour. Calculate my yearly cost
of that repetition (52 weeks). Then rank these automations by
hours saved: [LIST]. Which two should I do first?
```

How to use it: use the number to justify every hour you spend on setup.

**Business facts prompt (the master prompt)**

```
You now know my business: [BUSINESS NAME, TYPE, PRODUCTS/SERVICES,
PRICES, LOCATION, OPENING HOURS, DELIVERY DETAILS, WHATSAPP
NUMBER, TARGET CUSTOMER]. From now on, write everything for this
business in a warm, professional Kenyan tone, no emojis, and never
invent prices or facts I did not give you. Confirm my facts back
to me first.
```

How to use it: run this once, then reuse for every marketing and service task.

**Monthly content calendar**

```
Create a 30-day content calendar for [BUSINESS TYPE] in Kenya.
Include [PAYDAY, BACK-TO-SCHOOL, SEASONAL EVENTS] as key dates.
Each day: platform, post type, topic, and a one-line caption idea.
Mix: offers, product highlights, customer wins, behind-the-scenes,
and value tips. No emojis.
```

How to use it: generate once a month, then batch-create the content.

**Ad copy generator**

```
Write ad copy for [PRODUCT/SERVICE] at [PRICE] targeting
[AUDIENCE] in [AREA] on [PLATFORM]. Discount: [DETAILS]. Include
a headline (under 60 chars), a 3-line body, and a clear call to
action. Tone: [URGENT/WARM/PROFESSIONAL]. Two versions for A/B
testing. Facts only from what I gave you.
```

How to use it: run two versions, post both, keep the winner.

**Promo post**

```
Write a WhatsApp status and Facebook post announcing [PROMO] for
[BUSINESS]. Include the offer, the dates, how to order (WhatsApp
[MESSAGE LINK]), and one reason to act now. Short and scannable,
under 100 words each.
```

How to use it: post the day before and the first day of every promo.

**FAQ quick replies**

```
Write quick replies for my WhatsApp Business covering these 10
questions: [LIST YOUR TOP QUESTIONS]. Each reply: under 40 words,
warm, answers the question, and ends with a next step (e.g. "Reply
with your location for delivery cost"). Kenyan tone, no emojis.
```

How to use it: paste each into WhatsApp Business quick replies.

**After-hours auto-reply**

```
Write an after-hours auto-reply for [BUSINESS]. It must: thank the
customer, state when I will respond, give one useful line (price
list link or opening hours), and invite them to leave their
question. Under 70 words, warm, no emojis.
```

How to use it: switch it on daily when you close.

**Customer question answer**

```
A customer asked: "[PASTE QUESTION]". Answer it for my business
[BUSINESS, with real facts: PRODUCTS, PRICES, DELIVERY, HOURS].
Clear, friendly, and end with a next step. Under 100 words. If the
answer needs a fact I did not give you, ask me instead of guessing.
```

How to use it: draft fast answers, then personalise before sending.

**Answer bank builder**

```
From my business facts [PASTE FACTS], create an answer bank for my
top 15 customer questions: price, sizes, delivery, payment
(M-Pesa), returns, order time, and stock availability. Each answer
under 50 words with a clear next step. I will approve each one
before saving.
```

How to use it: build the bank once, save as WhatsApp quick replies.

**Difficult customer reply**

```
A customer is unhappy about [ISSUE]. Write a reply that
acknowledges the problem, apologises sincerely without blaming,
states the next step, and offers to make it right. Keep my tone
calm and professional, under 120 words. Flag anything I must
decide myself before sending.
```

How to use it: use as the draft for sensitive replies, then add your human touch.

**M-Pesa statement classification**

```
Classify these transactions from my M-Pesa statement: [PASTE
STATEMENT]. Group as: business income (by type), business expenses
(by category), transfers, and personal items. For anything unclear,
list it separately and ask me to confirm. Output a simple table.
```

How to use it: run monthly, then move the results to your income sheet.

**Monthly profit sheet**

```
Turn my classified M-Pesa data [PASTE DATA] into a monthly report:
total income, total expenses by category, and profit. Show me a
simple table and a 3-line summary: the biggest expense, the best
income source, and one thing to improve. No invented numbers.
```

How to use it: run on the same date every month and save the sheet.

**Expense category setup**

```
Help me set up expense categories for my small business: rent,
stock, transport, marketing, staff, M-Pesa fees, utilities,
miscellaneous. For each, give a one-line description of what counts
so my AI bookkeeping is consistent every month.
```

How to use it: paste this description alongside every classification prompt.

**Stock analysis**

```
Here is my stock sheet: [PASTE ITEM, COST, PRICE, QUANTITY, SALES
NOTES]. Analyse it: which 3 items give the best margin, which 3
sit too long, and what should I reorder next. Then write a 2-line
summary for my monthly report.
```

How to use it: run when you update your stock sheet, before buying stock.

**Monthly report writer**

```
Write my monthly business report from this data: [PASTE INCOME,
EXPENSES, SALES NOTES]. Sections: money in, money out, profit,
best seller, biggest problem, and one recommendation for next
month. Plain language, under 200 words, so I can actually read it
and act.
```

How to use it: read it every month — this is the habit that builds a real business.

**90-day roadmap builder**

```
Build my 90-day automation roadmap. My business: [TYPE, DETAILS].
My repeated tasks: [LIST]. My tools: WhatsApp Business, ChatGPT,
Google Sheets, Canva. Structure: Days 1-30 (foundation), Days
31-60 (consistency), Days 61-90 (growth). For each phase, 3
specific automations, the tool, and the hours saved per week.
Keep it realistic for one person with a business to run.
```

How to use it: this becomes your one-page plan.

**Automation review**

```
Here is my roadmap after [NUMBER] days: [PASTE PLAN AND RESULTS].
Which automations are actually saving time, which are not worth
it, and what should I kill or change? Give me one decision per
item and a one-line reason. Be direct.
```

How to use it: run at the end of every 30-day phase.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Automating before mapping, so you automate the wrong things
- Trying to set up twenty automations at once and quitting
- Leaving marketing and ads to "when I have time" — that is the repetition you can kill
- Letting the auto-reply feel robotic and pushing customers away
- Classifying M-Pesa data without checking it against your real knowledge
- Hiding from your numbers — a business without a profit figure is a hobby
- Buying stock without a stock sheet, so money sleeps in slow items
- Never reviewing the roadmap, so it becomes wallpaper instead of a system
