# AI for Job Seekers & Career Switchers: Land a Tech/Remote Job

**Overview** — The 2026 job market pays for people who can use AI well, not people who fear it. This course identifies the in-demand AI-adjacent skills employers want, builds a project portfolio that proves you can actually do the work, prepares you for technical and behavioral interviews, and shows you how to find, land, and get paid for remote and international roles from Kenya. The one clear win: a portfolio plus an application system that turns "AI user" into "AI professional worth hiring."

**Who this is for**

- Kenyan job seekers aiming for tech or AI-adjacent roles
- Career switchers moving from non-tech fields into tech-adjacent work
- Professionals who use AI daily but cannot yet show it on a CV
- Graduates targeting remote and international roles from Kenya

**What you'll be able to do**

- Identify in-demand AI-adjacent skills employers are hiring for now
- Build a project portfolio with AI tools that proves real capability
- Write applications and resumes that survive ATS and human screens
- Prepare for technical and behavioral interviews with AI mock practice
- Find and apply to remote and international roles from Kenya
- Negotiate offers and understand salary expectations
- Handle remote work logistics, payments, and KRA tax awareness
- Turn your job search into a measurable pipeline

## Module 1: Identify in-demand AI-adjacent skills

Employers are no longer hiring "AI experts" as a mystery role; they are hiring people in normal roles who are expected to work with AI. The in-demand skills in 2026 cluster around: prompt engineering and AI workflows, AI-assisted content creation, data analysis, and AI tools in marketing and design.

The most practical entry point is to combine your existing knowledge with an AI skill. A marketer who can run AI-assisted campaigns, a designer who builds with AI tools, or an accountant who automates reports with AI is worth more than a generic "prompt engineer" with no domain context.

Study real job ads to confirm demand. Search LinkedIn, BrighterMonday, and remote boards for "AI-assisted", "AI tools", "prompt", and "automation" and note the skill combinations that appear repeatedly. That list is your curriculum — you do not need to guess what employers want.

Choose one skill to own and one project to prove it. Depth beats breadth: one demonstrable AI project in your target role beats a list of ten buzzwords you cannot defend.

**Do:** match your AI skill to a job family (marketing, data, design, admin).
**Not this:** listing "AI" as a skill without a single project or result behind it.

## Module 2: Build a project portfolio with AI tools

Your portfolio is the proof that replaces experience you do not have. It answers one question employers actually ask: "Can this person do the work?" You can build a real portfolio with AI tools and public platforms in weeks, not years.

Pick projects that mirror real work: a data analysis of a public dataset with an AI-written report, an AI-assisted marketing campaign for a real (or fictional but clearly labeled) Kenyan brand, an automation that turns a messy spreadsheet into a dashboard, or a prompt-system you designed and documented.

Show the process, not just the result. For each project, publish: the problem, the tools used (including which AI and how), the steps, and the outcome. Employers hire thinkers; a documented process proves you think.

Put it somewhere public and shareable: GitHub for code and data work, a simple portfolio site or Google site for marketing and design work, and a Canva PDF for anything visual. Link the portfolio from your CV, LinkedIn, and every application.

**Do:** write a 3-line "what I did and why it matters" summary for each project.
**Not this:** presenting a raw AI output as your skill — a prompt result is not a portfolio.

## Module 3: Write applications that get read

Recruiters and ATS screens decide in seconds, so structure your CV around the job ad's exact keywords. Build a master CV, then tailor per application: pull the summary, top bullets, and skills that match the ad. If the ad says "data cleaning", write "data cleaning" — not "tidying data."

The application pattern is: portfolio link prominent, two-line summary naming the role and your key AI skill, and 3-5 result bullets with numbers. One page for most roles; two only with serious experience.

Cover letters are dead; short "why me" notes live. Two to three sentences: who you are, your relevant project, and why you fit this specific role. AI drafts it; you check the facts and the tone.

Never let AI invent anything. A portfolio you cannot show or a skill you cannot defend fails the first technical question. Your edge is honesty plus real, documented AI work.

**Do:** apply with your best-matched project linked at the top of the CV.
**Not this:** sending the same generic CV and cover letter to fifty roles.

## Module 4: Prepare for technical and behavioral interviews

Technical interviews test whether you can think, not just what you memorised. Use AI as your practice partner: paste the job ad and your portfolio, and have it ask the technical questions that role attracts — then answer out loud and get scored.

For hands-on roles, prepare to walk through your project: why you chose the tools, how AI was involved, what broke, and what you would improve. A calm explanation of a real project beats a perfect recitation of theory.

Behavioral questions use the STAR method: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Build five STAR stories from real experience — a tight deadline, a mistake, a conflict, a success, a time you learned fast — and practise them under two minutes each.

Remote interviews add logistics: test your camera, mic, and data before the call, keep your background clean, look at the camera, and have your portfolio open on a second screen. Practise one full mock interview on video with AI so the format feels familiar.

**Do:** record yourself answering and review the playback — it is uncomfortable and it works.
**Not this:** answering from memory without practising out loud, then freezing on the call.

## Module 5: Find remote and international roles from Kenya

The best remote roles come from four channels: LinkedIn (filter by remote and by your region), remote job boards, African talent platforms, and freelance routes into full-time work.

Use remote-specific boards — Remote.co, AllRemote Jobs — and African platforms like Andela, which place Kenyan talent into global tech teams. LinkedIn is the workhorse: a strong profile, the right keywords in your headline, and applications to remote-first companies. Upwork and Toptal can turn freelance projects into long-term remote roles and provide portfolio income while you search.

Kenyan developers on the remote market average around $51k a year, and AI-adjacent remote roles are growing — but the pipeline is: prove skill (portfolio), apply consistently, and interview well. Expect several weeks of applications before traction; that is normal, not failure.

Treat your search like a pipeline: 10-15 tailored applications a week, a tracker with status and next actions, and a weekly review of what is getting replies. Consistency beats intensity, every time.

**Do:** optimise your LinkedIn headline for remote hiring managers' search terms.
**Not this:** spending all week on one "perfect" application while the pipeline stalls.

## Module 6: Negotiate offers

Most Kenyans never negotiate because nobody taught them it is expected — but for international roles, the offer is a starting point, not a ceiling. Research the role's range on platforms like Glassdoor and Levels.fyi, and know the lower bound you will accept before you start.

Negotiate salary, then location-pay, then extras. Remote roles sometimes pay regional or "cost-of-living adjusted" rates; ask directly what the pay basis is. Also negotiate non-salary items that matter for a Kenyan remote worker: paid leave, equipment budget, health cover, and timezone expectations.

Your leverage is your portfolio and your market research: "Based on comparable roles paying [range], and my project results in [area], I'd like to discuss [number]." One confident, polite counter that names a number is almost always respected.

Get the key terms in writing: role, salary in which currency, payment schedule, leave, and notice period. Written terms protect you months later when the money or the expectations shift.

**Do:** ask for the range before you give your number when possible.
**Not this:** accepting instantly out of fear, then learning you left money on the table.

## Module 7: Remote work logistics, payments, and KRA

Getting the job is stage one; getting paid reliably is stage two. International employers pay via bank transfer, platforms like Deel or Payroll, or services like Wise and Payoneer — each converts foreign currency into what you need.

Choose your money route by exchange rate and fees. Wise offers near-mid-market rates (fees around 0.5%) and works well for receiving; Payoneer is a solid option especially if you work via freelance platforms; PayPal is convenient but its 4.4% fee plus poor exchange rates make it the costliest choice for larger amounts. For local transactions and clients, M-Pesa remains the everyday channel.

Check how your employer wants to pay before you sign, and set up the account in the same month you start. Nothing kills a promising remote job like a payment setup discovered on day one.

Know your tax basics. If you earn as an employee of a foreign company, your employer's payroll arrangement determines what applies to you; if you earn as self-employed (freelance contracts, contractor gigs), register with KRA and file returns, and remember you are also personally responsible for your PAYE-type obligations as a contractor. A few hours with the KRA portal and a tax guide at the start saves real penalties later.

**Do:** set aside money for tax from the first paycheck, not the last minute.
**Not this:** accepting payment through the most expensive option because it was the easiest to set up.

Deliverables — complete all five this week:

1. **Skill decision:** your chosen AI-adjacent skill, confirmed against real job ads, with a one-line reason.
2. **Two portfolio projects:** built with AI tools, documented (problem, tools, steps, outcome), and published somewhere public.
3. **Tailored application kit:** master CV, one tailored CV against a real remote job ad, and your "why me" note.

## Prompt Library

**Skill demand research**

```
Analyse these job ads from [PASTE 5-10 ADS, PLATFORMS]. What AI-
adjacent skills appear most? What skill combinations are repeated?
What are the common titles? Recommend the ONE skill I should
develop and one project to prove it, given my background: [PASTE
EXPERIENCE]. Be specific and honest.
```

How to use it: run before choosing your skill direction.

**Portfolio project idea**

```
Given my target role [ROLE] and my skills [SKILLS], suggest 5
portfolio projects I can build in 1-2 weeks each using free AI
tools and public data. For each: the problem, the tools, the steps,
and the outcome to show. Pick the 2 that best prove I can do
[ROLE] work.
```

How to use it: pick two, build them, publish them.

**Project documentation**

```
Write the documentation for my project: [PASTE PROJECT DETAILS].
Structure: problem, tools used (including which AI and how I used
it), the steps, the result with numbers, and what I would improve.
Professional and honest, under 300 words. This goes in my portfolio.
```

How to use it: publish it beside the project itself.

**Master CV builder**

```
Turn my rough notes into a master CV for [TARGET ROLE]: [PASTE
NOTES]. Include: professional summary naming my AI skill, result-
focused bullets with numbers, skills, and education. Use action
verbs. Flag anything I must verify. Keep it ATS-friendly, no
tables or images.
```

How to use it: build once, tailor forever after.

**Role-tailored CV**

```
Tailor my master CV [PASTE CV] for this job: [PASTE AD]. Keep the
summary, top bullets, and skills matching the ad's keywords.
Output a one-page CV and note which of my projects to link.
No invented experience.
```

How to use it: run for every serious application.

**Why-me note**

```
Write a 3-sentence "why me" note for [ROLE] at [COMPANY] based on
my CV and portfolio [PASTE]. One line on my AI skill, one on my
most relevant project, one on why I fit this specific role. No
flattery, no cliches.
```

How to use it: paste into the application's notes field.

**Project walkthrough practice**

```
You are an interviewer. Based on my portfolio project [PASTE
DETAILS], ask me the 8 technical questions a hiring manager would
ask to test whether I actually did this work. Then score my answers
out of 10 with one improvement tip each. Start with the hardest.
```

How to use it: answer out loud, record it, review the playback.

**Mock technical interviewer**

```
Act as a senior technical interviewer hiring for [ROLE]. Ask me
technical questions one at a time about [TOPICS FROM THE AD],
wait for my answer, score it out of 10, and give one tip. Go
deeper on anything I answer weakly. Start now.
```

How to use it: run one full practice session before any technical interview.

**STAR story builder**

```
Turn this experience into a STAR story (Situation, Task, Action,
Result): [PASTE EXPERIENCE]. Keep it under 2 minutes spoken, end
with a number or clear outcome. Then suggest which of these job
behaviors it fits: [COMMON BEHAVIORAL TOPICS].
```

How to use it: build five reusable stories across different topics.

**Behavioral question bank**

```
Based on this job ad [PASTE AD], list the 8 behavioral questions
most likely asked and match each to my STAR stories [PASTE
STORIES]. Flag any story I still need to build.
```

How to use it: walk into behavioral interviews with mapped answers.

**Salary research**

```
Help me research a fair salary for [ROLE] in [COUNTRY/MARKET] with
[YEARS] experience and [AI SKILL]. Tell me: what range to quote,
what lower bound to accept, and how remote roles for Kenyan
candidates usually price this role. Then draft one negotiation line
I can use.
```

How to use it: run before the offer conversation, not during it.

**Offer response**

```
I received an offer for [ROLE] at [COMPANY]: [SALARY/CURRENCY],
[benefits]. Based on my research range [RANGE], write a polite
response that either accepts with clear terms or counters with one
specific number and a reason. Warm, confident, under 120 words.
```

How to use it: personalise the number before sending.

**Contract terms checklist**

```
Create a checklist of what to confirm in writing before accepting
a remote offer: role, salary and currency, payment schedule,
payment method (bank/Deel/Payoneer/Wise), leave, notice period,
equipment, and timezone expectations. Add any Kenya-specific items
I should watch for.
```

How to use it: work through it with every offer letter.

**Remote job search plan**

```
Build my weekly remote job search plan for [ROLE]. Channels:
LinkedIn, [REMOTE BOARDS], [AFRICAN PLATFORMS]. Target: [NUMBER]
tailored applications a week, [NUMBER] follow-ups, [NUMBER] mock
interviews. Give me a tracker format and a weekly review question
set to find what is working.
```

How to use it: run it every week and adjust with the results.

**Payment route comparison**

```
Compare how to receive international payments from an employer in
[COUNTRY]: bank transfer, Deel, Wise, Payoneer, and PayPal. Compare
fees, exchange rate quality, speed, and ease for a Kenyan
recipient. Recommend the best two for [AMOUNT] monthly. No
invented exact prices — describe cost tiers in words.
```

How to use it: decide your money route before your first paycheck.

**KRA tax starter**

```
Explain KRA obligations for a Kenyan earning [TYPE: remote
employee / freelance contractor] in [CURRENCY]. Cover: registration
(KRA PIN, self-employment), what to set aside, when to file, and
common mistakes. Keep it simple and practical, and flag where I
must verify details on the KRA portal.
```

How to use it: read it, then verify each point on the official portal.

**Application tracker**

```
Create an application tracker for my job search: columns for date,
role, company, platform, portfolio link used, status (applied/
replied/interviewed/offer/rejected), next action, follow-up date.
Add a rule for when to follow up (7 days) and what that message
should say.
```

How to use it: update it daily; the tracker is your job search, not the applying.
