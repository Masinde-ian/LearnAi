## Lesson 1: The proposal system — winning work with Claude

Proposals are where freelancers and consultants win or lose. The problem is not writing proposals — it is writing proposals that sound like everyone else's AI-generated output. Here is how to use Claude to write proposals that win.

**Step 1: Build a proposal Project.** Create a Claude Project called "Winning Proposals." Upload your three best past proposals — the ones that actually won work. In the Project instructions, write:

"You are my proposal writing partner. I work as a [role] in [speciality]. My clients are typically [describe them]. I win work when I am specific, demonstrate understanding of the client's problem, and propose a clear plan. Match the tone and structure of my uploaded winning proposals."

**Step 2: Analyse the job posting.** When you see a job or client opportunity, paste the entire posting into Claude and ask:

```
Analyse this job posting. Tell me:
1. What is the client's real problem (not just what they wrote)?
2. What skills and experience matter most to them?
3. What would make a proposal stand out?
4. What questions should I ask before writing the proposal?
5. What red flags or hidden requirements do I see?
```

**Step 3: Write the proposal.** Now ask Claude to draft the proposal using your Project context:

```
Write a proposal for this job. Use my winning proposal style.
Structure: (1) I understand your problem, (2) my approach to
solving it, (3) what I will deliver and when, (4) why I am
the right pick, (5) next steps. Under 400 words. Be specific
to this client — no generic filler.
```

**Step 4: Improve and personalise.** Review the draft. Add a specific detail about the client — something from their website, a recent project, a mutual connection. AI-generated proposals fail when they are generic. One personal sentence changes everything.

The system: analyse the posting, draft with your style, personalise with research, send. Repeat for every opportunity.

## Lesson 2: Contract and legal document analysis

You do not need to be a lawyer to use Claude for contract analysis. You need to be a careful reader with an AI assistant that catches what you miss.

**The analysis workflow:**

1. Paste the full contract or agreement into Claude
2. Use the contract analysis prompt from the Tier 2 prompt library
3. Ask follow-up questions about specific clauses
4. Create a risk summary you can take to a professional if needed

**Key prompts for legal documents:**

```
I am about to sign this [contract type]. Read every clause and give me:
1. Plain-language summary of what I am agreeing to
2. My obligations and deadlines
3. Payment terms and any conditional clauses
4. Clauses that favour the other party disproportionately
5. Missing protections I should ask for
6. A list of questions I should ask before signing
```

```
Compare these two versions of a contract. What has changed between
them? Are the changes in my favour or the other party's? Flag
anything that was removed that protected me.
```

```
This contract has a non-compete clause. Analyse it: what does it
restrict, for how long, and how geographically? Is this reasonable
for a [type of professional] in Kenya?
```

**Important:** Claude is not a lawyer. Use it to understand what you are reading, identify risks, and prepare questions — then consult a legal professional for final advice on anything that could cost you money or legal exposure.

## Lesson 3: Research and analysis for clients

Freelancers, consultants, and agencies often get paid to research. Claude makes you dramatically faster at producing research that clients can actually use.

**Market research workflow:**

1. Gather raw data from the web — Google searches, industry reports, competitor websites
2. Paste the data into Claude and ask for structured analysis
3. Use Claude to create a client-ready research brief

```
Here is raw research data on [topic] for [client's industry or need].
Organise it into a client-ready brief with these sections:
1. Executive Summary (3 bullets)
2. Market Overview
3. Key Findings (with data points)
4. Opportunities and Risks
5. Recommendations (3 actionable steps)
Write in professional, client-facing language.
```

**Competitive analysis:**

```
Here are descriptions of 5 competitors in [industry]. Analyse them:
1. What each one does well
2. Where each one is weak
3. Gaps in the market that my client could fill
4. Pricing benchmarks based on what is listed
5. One strategic recommendation for my client
```

**Grant and proposal research:**

```
I am writing a grant proposal for [organisation] focused on [cause]
in Kenya. Research and suggest:
1. The top 5 funding organisations that support this cause
2. What each funder typically finances
3. The typical grant size range
4. What makes a winning proposal to each funder
5. One angle my proposal should emphasise to stand out
```

**Industry brief creation:**

```
Here are 8 articles about [industry trend]. Create a 2-page industry
brief: what is happening, why it matters, the data points, and
3 implications for businesses in Kenya. Professional tone, suitable
for a board presentation.
```

The principle: Claude turns raw information into structured, client-ready deliverables. Your job is gathering the raw material. Claude's job is making it presentable.

## Lesson 4: Content strategy at scale

If you manage content for clients — social media, blogs, newsletters, or marketing — Claude can run your entire content engine from a single Project.

**Building a content strategy Project:**

1. Create a Project called "Content Strategy — [Client Name]"
2. Upload: the client's brand guidelines, past content that performed well, audience demographics, competitors
3. Set instructions: "You are the content strategist for [client]. Their brand voice is [describe]. Their audience is [describe]. All content should align with their goals: [list goals]."

**Weekly content planning:**

```
Plan next week's content for [client]. Their goals this month are
[goals]. Here is what performed well last week: [paste analytics
or notes]. Produce:
- 3 Instagram/Facebook posts (with captions)
- 1 blog topic with outline
- 1 WhatsApp broadcast message
- Content themes and hashtags
Match the brand voice from our past content in this Project.
```

**Content repurposing:**

```
Here is a blog post about [topic]. Repurpose it into:
1. 3 social media posts (different angles)
2. 1 WhatsApp broadcast
3. 1 email newsletter intro
4. 5 Twitter/X thread starters
Keep the key message but adapt the format and length for each platform.
```

**Performance analysis:**

```
Here are my content metrics for the past month. Analyse:
1. Which posts performed best and why
2. Which formats and topics underperformed
3. Audience engagement patterns
4. 3 specific changes to make next month
[paste metrics]
```

**Editorial calendar:**

```
Create a 30-day editorial calendar for [client]. Their content
pillars are [list pillars]. Post frequency: [frequency]. Platforms:
[list]. Include post topic, format, caption draft, and best
posting time for Kenyan audiences.
```

but clear. Not aggressive. Under 60 words.

## Lesson 7: Professional prompt engineering patterns

At the professional level, prompt engineering is not about tricks — it is about reliably getting outputs that are client-ready on the first or second try.

**The CHAIN method for complex prompts:**

- **C**ontext: Give Claude the background it needs
- **H**ypothesis: State what you believe or want to test
- **A**ction: Tell Claude exactly what to produce
- **I**nput: Provide the raw material
- **N**otes: Specify format, length, tone, constraints

Example:

```
Context: I am a digital marketing consultant in Nairobi. My client
runs an e-commerce store selling handmade jewellery. Their main
challenge is low website traffic despite good products.

Hypothesis: Their problem is not product quality but visibility
and trust.

Action: Create a 90-day marketing plan that addresses traffic,
conversion, and repeat purchases.

Input: Their current monthly traffic is ~2,000 visitors. Average
order value is KES 2,500. They are active on Instagram but not
running ads.

Notes: Format as a table with weeks as rows and channels as
columns. Include specific tactics, not just categories. All
budgets in KES. Target audience: women aged 22-35 in Nairobi.
```

**Chain-of-thought for analysis:**

```
Think through this step by step. I am analysing [problem].
1. First, list all the factors I should consider
2. Then, evaluate each factor with evidence from [data]
3. Then, identify the 3 most important factors
4. Finally, give me a recommendation based on that analysis
```

**Structured comparison:**

```
Compare these [options] across these dimensions: [list dimensions].
For each dimension, give a score (1-5), a one-line explanation,
and what it means for my specific situation. End with a
recommendation and why.
```

**Iterative refinement loop:**

```
Here is a draft of [deliverable]. Rate it on these criteria:
1. Accuracy (is the information correct?)
2. Completeness (are there gaps?)
3. Clarity (would a non-expert understand it?)
4. Actionability (can the client act on it immediately?)
For each criterion, give a score and specific improvement suggestions.
Then rewrite the deliverable incorporating your suggestions.
```

## Prompt Library

Each prompt below is professional-grade. Copy, fill the brackets, and test.

**Winning proposal writer**

```
I am writing a proposal for [client type] who needs [service].
Here is the job posting or brief: [paste].

Analyse the posting, then write a proposal using this structure:
1. I understand your problem (2-3 sentences showing I "get it")
2. My approach (specific steps, not vague promises)
3. What I will deliver (concrete outputs with deadlines)
4. Why me (relevant experience, not a bio)
5. Next steps (one clear call to action)

Match the tone of my uploaded winning proposals. Under 400 words.
No filler, no cliches.
```

How to use: paste into your proposal Project with winning proposals uploaded.

**Proposal personaliser**

```
I have a generic proposal draft for a [client type] job. Personalise
it by incorporating these details about the client:
- Their business: [describe]
- A recent project or post: [link or description]
- A specific pain point from their posting: [detail]
Add 2-3 sentences that show I researched them specifically.
```

How to use: run after drafting a proposal to add the personal touch that wins.

**Contract deep-dive**

```
Read this entire contract clause by clause. For each clause:
1. Plain-language explanation of what it means
2. What it obligates me to do
3. What rights it gives me
4. What risks it creates
5. Whether this is standard or unusual for [type of contract]

End with a negotiation priority list: what I should push back on,
what I should accept, and what I should add.
```

How to use: paste the full contract. Works for service agreements, NDAs, employment contracts, and partnerships.
