# AI for Teachers & Tutors: Building Digital Courses & Content

**Overview** — You already know how to teach; this course shows you how to package that knowledge and sell it beyond your classroom. Turn your existing lesson plans into structured digital courses, generate quizzes and study guides in minutes, record and edit lessons with phone-friendly tools, and sell to students and parents locally and online. The one clear win: a complete digital course — content, assessments, lessons, and a sales channel — built from material you already have.

**Who this is for**

- Primary and secondary teachers with ready lesson plans
- Tutors running private classes who want to scale beyond their hours
- University graduates and professionals who can teach a skill
- Anyone with classroom material who wants a second income stream

**What you'll be able to do**

- Turn an existing lesson plan into a structured, sellable course outline
- Generate quizzes, assignments, and study guides with AI in minutes
- Record and edit lessons efficiently with free tools
- Choose the right platform to host and sell your course
- Market your course to Kenyan parents and students, online and offline
- Deliver and support your students without burning out
- Build a repeatable course-creation system for future courses

## Module 1: Choose a course idea that sells

Your best course is the one you already teach well. List everything you can teach, then pick by demand: which subject or skill do parents pay for repeatedly? In Kenya, that is usually KCSE and CBC exam subjects — Math, English, Kiswahili, Chemistry, Biology — plus life skills like public speaking, coding, and study techniques.

Look at what parents already pay for: private tuition, mock papers, holiday revision classes. Any of these can become a digital course with a wider audience. "KCSE Chemistry Paper 2 revision" is a product; "Chemistry revision" is a vague wish.

Test your idea cheaply before building everything. Put a simple WhatsApp status or a social post: "Digital KCSE revision course coming — who is interested?" Real interest from real parents tells you to build; silence tells you to change the idea.

Choose one course to start. One well-built course beats five half-finished ones. Your first course establishes your name and a student base you can sell your next course to.

**Do:** pick a course with a clear exam or skill outcome parents can point at.
**Not this:** building a giant "everything I know" course with no clear promise.

## Module 2: Turn lesson plans into structured course content

Your lesson plan is a teaching outline; your course needs a learning journey. The structure is simple: welcome, modules, lessons, and a final outcome. Each module should answer one big question, and each lesson should deliver one clear takeaway.

Use AI to turn your existing notes into a course blueprint: paste your lesson plan and ask for a module breakdown, lesson sequence, and learning outcomes. AI organises your material; you keep the teaching judgment about what really matters for the exam.

For each lesson, produce: a 2-3 minute explainer, worked examples, and practice. The explainer is the core; examples and practice are where learning actually happens. AI can draft the scripts, the examples, and the practice questions from your notes.

A Kenyan touch sells: use KCSE/CBC paper structures, past-paper-style questions, and local examples. "Worked example from KCSE 2023" signals credibility no generic course can match.

**Blueprint template:**

```
COURSE: [Name] | AUDIENCE: [Form/CBC level or skill level]
OUTCOME: By the end, students can [specific skill or grade target]
MODULE 1: [Big question] -> Lessons [3-5 titles]
MODULE 2: [Big question] -> Lessons [3-5 titles]
MODULE 3: [Big question] -> Lessons [3-5 titles]
ASSESSMENT: [Quiz per module + final exam-style paper]
```

## Module 3: Generate quizzes, assignments, and study guides

Assessment is where AI saves you the most hours. A good quiz needs varied question types — multiple choice, short answer, and exam-style — with the right difficulty for the level. AI drafts all of it from your lesson content.

Paste each lesson's key points into AI and ask for a 10-question quiz with an answer key and clear explanations for wrong answers. Explainers on wrong answers are what make a digital course feel like a real teacher.

Generate assignments with a rubric: the task, the marking scheme, and what "good" looks like. Kenyan parents trust courses that look like school — mark schemes, grade bands, and exam references make your course credible.

Study guides are your marketing gift and your student resource. A one-page revision sheet per module — formulas, key terms, common exam traps — is shareable content that pulls parents to your course and a genuine help to students.

**Do:** check every AI question for KCSE/CBC syllabus fit before publishing.
**Not this:** using questions that reference the wrong syllabus or the wrong year.

## Module 4: Record and edit lessons efficiently

You do not need a studio. A quiet room, a phone, and good light give you 80% of professional quality, and your teaching voice is the last 20%. Record short — 5 to 10 minutes per lesson — and split long topics into multiple videos.

Record your screen for examples and past-paper walkthroughs with Loom or your phone. Use a free app like CapCut to edit: cut mistakes, add captions, and insert the example screenshots. Captions make your videos watchable on mute and accessible to more students.

Do not aim for perfect takes. Record in one flow, then edit out the pauses. AI can also generate the caption text and even the lesson summary shown with each video.

Batch recording is the professional secret: record five lessons in one sitting, edit them in one evening, and schedule them out over the week. Consistency without burnout is the system, not heroics.

**Recording setup:** phone at eye level, window light facing you, quiet room, a glass of water, and your one-page lesson notes off-camera.

## Module 5: Choose your hosting and selling platform

You have three routes, and the right one depends on how you want to sell.

- **Global course platforms:** Teachable, Udemy, and Kajabi host your course and handle payments. Udemy brings its own traffic but takes a large share; Teachable and Kajabi give you more control and branding for a monthly fee. Good if you want international reach and automated delivery.
- **Local WhatsApp route:** sell directly via WhatsApp and M-Pesa. Share the course as videos in a WhatsApp group or a simple folder, collect payments via M-Pesa till or paybill, and keep everything you earn. Best for selling to Kenyan parents who already trust you and pay with M-Pesa.
- **Hybrid:** launch locally via WhatsApp + M-Pesa for cash flow while a Teachable-style platform handles global sales and repeat customers.

Start where your students already are. If your audience is Kenyan parents on WhatsApp, the local route gets you paid fastest. Add the global platform when you want reach beyond Kenya.

**Decision rule:** local first for cash flow and trust, a proper platform when you have an audience and want automation, and Udemy only if you accept big fees for big reach.

## Module 6: Market to parents and students

Parents buy results, not videos. Your marketing must answer: "Will this improve my child's grade?" Market with proof: a free lesson, a sample study guide, a short video where you solve a past-paper question, and a clear promise of outcomes.

Give away a small, valuable freebie — a one-page revision sheet or a free introductory video — in exchange for a WhatsApp contact. That contact list is your real business asset; every future course sells to it.

Post weekly on the channels parents use: Facebook groups for parents, WhatsApp status, and TikTok or Instagram if your students are older. Show before-and-after: the confused student, your method, the improved grade. AI drafts the captions and scripts; your genuine results do the selling.

Ask happy parents for a short testimonial in their own words and share it (with permission). A parent's words about their child's improvement are more persuasive than any ad you write.

**Do:** price for the local market — parent-friendly ranges that compete with private tutoring.
**Not this:** building a beautiful course no parent ever hears about.

## Module 7: Deliver, support, and don't burn out

A digital course is not "set and forget." Students who feel supported finish the course and buy your next one. But support can scale: set a weekly live Q&A on WhatsApp or Zoom, batch-answer questions twice a day, and reuse answers across students.

Build a question bank as you go. Every real student question becomes content: add the answer to the course, post it as a study tip, or turn it into a future lesson. Your students teach you what to build next.

Track who finishes. A simple sheet of enrolled students, their progress, and their questions tells you who needs a nudge. A one-line WhatsApp nudge — "Karibu, did you try the Paper 2 mock?" — revives a stuck student and protects your reputation.

Automate the repetitive parts with AI: welcome messages, weekly reminders, and answer drafts. Keep the personal teaching moments — marking, feedback, encouragement — as the human heart your students paid for.

**Do:** set a fixed weekly support window so helping does not eat your whole week.
**Not this:** answering student questions at 11pm seven days a week until you quit.

## Assignment: Build your first digital course

Deliverables — complete all five this week:

1. **Course blueprint:** one course, outcome statement, and 3 modules with lesson titles, built from an existing lesson plan.
2. **Three recorded lessons:** one lesson per module, 5-10 minutes each, edited with captions.
3. **Assessment pack:** one quiz per module with answer key and explanations, plus one exam-style assignment with a mark scheme.
4. **Study guide:** one one-page revision sheet per module, ready to give away as your freebie.
5. **Sales setup:** your channel chosen (WhatsApp + M-Pesa and/or a platform), your price set, and one freebie + one welcome message ready.

**Self-check:** would a parent understand what their child will achieve? Can a student finish a module in one sitting? Can you sell this to the parents already messaging you?

## Prompt Library

**Course idea validation**

```
I am a [SUBJECT/TUTOR TYPE] teacher in Kenya. I can teach:
[LIST]. Ask me 5 questions to pick the single best digital course
idea, then rank my options by parent demand, exam value, and how
much content I already have. Recommend one course to start with.
```

How to use it: run before building anything.

**Lesson plan to blueprint**

```
Turn this lesson plan into a digital course blueprint: [PASTE
LESSON PLAN]. Output: course name, one-sentence outcome, 3 modules
each with 3-5 lesson titles, and the assessment for each module.
Align everything to the [KCSE/CBC] syllabus.
```

How to use it: this becomes the skeleton of your course.

**Lesson script writer**

```
Write a 2-3 minute explainer script for [TOPIC] for [LEVEL] students
preparing for [EXAM/SKILL]. Structure: hook with a student's
common confusion, the concept in simple steps, one worked example
(with numbers if relevant), and a one-line takeaway. Warm, clear,
examinable language, under 350 words.
```

How to use it: read it over your recording, then edit to sound like you.

**Quiz generator**

```
Create a 10-question quiz on [TOPIC] for [LEVEL] students. Mix:
multiple choice, short answer, and one exam-style question. Align
to [KCSE/CBC] style. Provide: questions, answer key, and a one-line
explanation for each answer, especially why wrong options are wrong.
```

How to use it: paste each lesson's key points in and generate per lesson.

**Assignment with mark scheme**

```
Create an assignment on [TOPIC] for [LEVEL] with: 3 tasks of
increasing difficulty, a full mark scheme with the points expected,
and a grade band (A-E) explained in one line each. Align to
[KCSE/CBC]. Make the tasks answerable in 40 minutes.
```

How to use it: add to the end of each module as the practice task.

**Study guide (revision sheet)**

```
Write a one-page revision sheet on [TOPIC] for [EXAM/LEVEL].
Include: the 5 key facts, 3 common exam traps, the formulas or
terms to memorise, and 2 exam-style quick questions with answers.
Phone-readable, no fluff, under 300 words.
```

How to use it: give this away free to collect WhatsApp contacts.

**Past-paper walkthrough script**

```
Write a script where I walk a student through this past-paper
question: [PASTE QUESTION]. Structure: read the question aloud,
restate what it asks, show the method step by step, solve it, and
give one "how to spot this in the exam" tip. Under 400 words.
```

How to use it: use as the voiceover for your screen-recording.

**Caption and summary generator**

```
Write captions and a 3-line video summary for a lesson video on
[TOPIC]. Captions: clear, short sentences a student reads quickly.
Summary: what was covered and the one takeaway, shown under the
video on [PLATFORM]. No emojis.
```

How to use it: paste captions into CapCut and the summary into your course description.

**Welcome message**

```
Write a welcome message for a student joining my digital course
[COURSE NAME]. It should: say what they will achieve, tell them
how to start (first lesson), set expectations for time per module,
and invite them to the WhatsApp group for questions. Warm, short,
under 120 words.
```

How to use it: send the day a student pays, every time.

**Weekly class reminder**

```
Write a weekly reminder for my course students. It must: name the
module/lesson for the week, give a 3-line nudge on what to submit
or practise, and offer the live Q&A time. Include a "reply to this
message if stuck" line. Under 100 words.
```

How to use it: send every Monday from a template you personalise in seconds.

**Student question answer**

```
A student asked: "[PASTE QUESTION]". Answer it clearly for a
[LEVEL] student preparing for [EXAM]. Explain in simple steps with
one worked example, end with one practice question for them to
confirm they understood. Under 150 words.
```

How to use it: answer fast, then add the question to your course question bank.

**Parent marketing post**

```
Write a Facebook post for parents of [LEVEL] students in Kenya
about my course [COURSE]. Message: my child's exam grade concern,
what the course covers, the freebie offer [FREEBIE], and how to
join via WhatsApp. Parent-focused, trustworthy, under 160 words,
no hype.
```

How to use it: post in parent groups and to your WhatsApp status.

**Testimonial request**

```
Write a message asking a happy parent for a short testimonial about
my course [COURSE]. Ask for: their child's subject, what improved,
and permission to share it. Keep it easy to answer with one or two
lines. Under 80 words.
```

How to use it: send to parents after a good result or a finished course.

**Course description**

```
Write a course description for [COURSE] to sell on [PLATFORM or
WhatsApp]. Structure: who it is for, the outcome (grade/skill),
what is inside (modules, assessments, study guides), the price in
KES, and a clear call to action. Honest, specific, under 200 words.
```

How to use it: paste into your platform page or as the WhatsApp sales message.

**Pricing help**

```
Help me price my digital course [COURSE] in Kenya. Comparable:
private tutoring in [SUBJECT] costs about [AMOUNT] per lesson, a
term's tuition is [AMOUNT]. Suggest a parent-friendly price range
for a self-paced digital course with [NUMBER] lessons and
assessments, and a launch discount strategy.
```

How to use it: set your price before you market, so you never undersell.

**Content repurposing**

```
Turn my course content on [TOPIC] into: 3 short Instagram/TikTok
study tips, 1 Facebook area post for parents, and 1 WhatsApp status
hook. All should point to my freebie [FREEBIE]. Keep each under 60
words and genuinely useful, not promotional.
```

How to use it: fill a week of marketing from one module's content.

**Student progress tracker**

```
Design a simple student progress tracker for my course: columns
for student name, enrolled date, module completed, quiz score,
questions asked, and next action. Add a rule for who needs a
nudge (no progress in 5 days) and what that nudge should say.
```

How to use it: keep it in Google Sheets and review it twice a week.

**Course review and next steps**

```
Review my course outline: [PASTE OUTLINE]. Is the outcome clear?
Are modules logically ordered? Are assessments aligned to the
outcome? Give me 3 specific improvements and 1 idea for a follow-up
course my students would buy next. Be direct and practical.
```

How to use it: run before launch and again after your first student cohort.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Building a huge course instead of one focused, sellable module
- Recording long, unedited videos students never finish
- Using AI questions that do not match the KCSE/CBC syllabus
- Publishing without a freebie, so no one has a reason to contact you
- Selling only on global platforms and ignoring WhatsApp + M-Pesa parents
- Supporting students 24/7 until you burn out
- Forgetting that marketing, not content, is where most courses die
