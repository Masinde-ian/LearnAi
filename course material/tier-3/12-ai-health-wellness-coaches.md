# AI for Health & Wellness Coaches

**Overview**

People in Kenya want wellness — fitness, nutrition, sleep, stress, and mindset support — and they will pay monthly for accountability and results. This course builds a professional coaching practice: AI-supported intake and goal-setting, personalized wellness content and check-in systems, clear marketing online and locally, and pricing that pays you fairly. The one clear win: a client-ready coaching practice with clear boundaries, where AI does the paperwork and you do the coaching.

**Who this is for**

- Fitness trainers, nutritionists, and wellness enthusiasts ready to coach seriously
- Coaches who work by memory and want structured intake and tracking
- Anyone building a health side business who needs professional boundaries
- Wellness content creators who want to convert followers into paying clients

**What you'll be able to do**

- Build client intake and goal-setting processes with AI support
- Create personalized wellness content and check-in systems
- Market a coaching practice online and locally
- Hold clear boundaries — AI as an assistant, never a substitute for medical advice
- Price and package coaching programs

## Module 1: The boundary that keeps you safe and professional

Before intake forms, before pricing, before marketing, understand the line you will never cross: AI is an assistant, never a substitute for medical advice — and neither are you unless you are a licensed professional.

**What this means in practice:**

- You coach lifestyle: movement, nutrition habits, sleep, stress, routines
- You do not diagnose, prescribe, or treat medical conditions
- Anyone with a medical condition, on medication, or unsure must get clearance from a qualified health professional first
- AI output is draft support for your professional judgment — never the final word on someone's health
- Every document carries a clear disclaimer: this is wellness coaching, not medical advice

**The coach's promise:**

- You refer out when something is beyond your scope
- You never guess on symptoms
- You document the boundary in your intake, your materials, and your conversations

**Do this:** write your scope of practice in one paragraph and keep it visible in your intake and your website.

**Not this:** coaching around a diagnosed condition because the client "does not want to see a doctor." Refer early and honestly.

**TEMPLATE: Scope and disclaimer statement**

```
SCOPE AND DISCLAIMER
My coaching covers: [movement, nutrition habits, sleep, stress, routines]
My coaching does NOT cover: [diagnosis, treatment, prescription]
I require medical clearance for clients with: [conditions, medication, pregnancy, recent injury, uncertainty]
Referral policy: [who I refer to and when]
This statement appears in: [intake form, website, program documents]
```

## Module 2: Client intake and goal-setting with AI

Every great coaching relationship starts with understanding the client. A structured intake turns "tell me about yourself" into data you can actually coach from.

**The intake must collect:**

- **Goal** — what they want (weight, fitness, energy, sleep, stress, a habit)
- **Motivation** — why now, and what would make them quit
- **Health context** — conditions, medications, injuries, clearance status
- **Current habits** — movement, food, sleep, stress, alcohol, screen time
- **Reality** — budget for food, time available, equipment, family duties
- **Preferences** — what they enjoy, what they hate

**AI's role:**

- AI drafts the intake questions in a Google Form format
- AI suggests follow-up questions based on answers
- AI converts intake answers into a client profile you review and approve
- AI helps set SMART goals you frame around their reality

**The goal-setting rule:**

- Goals must be the client's, not yours
- Small, measurable, weekly goals beat vague monthly dreams
- Review and reset goals every 4 weeks

**Do this:** refuse to coach a client who skips the health questions. This is not losing a sale; it is protecting both of you.

**Not this:** setting the goals for them. A goal you choose for a client is homework, not commitment.

**TEMPLATE: Client intake form outline**

```
CLIENT INTAKE
1. Name, age, location, contact
2. Primary goal [weight/fitness/energy/sleep/stress/habit]
3. Why now? What would make you quit?
4. Health: conditions, medication, injuries, clearance status
5. Current habits: movement, food, sleep, stress
6. Reality: budget for food, time per week, equipment, family
7. Preferences: enjoy / avoid
8. Medical disclaimer agreement [yes/no]
```

## Module 3: Personalized wellness content and check-in systems

Coaching is a relationship, and relationships run on contact. A check-in system keeps every client feeling supported without you messaging from memory all week.

**The weekly coaching rhythm:**

- **Monday** — the week's focus and one small goal
- **Mid-week** — a check-in and an encouragement or adjustment
- **Friday/Saturday** — the weekly review: what worked, what to change
- **Monthly** — a results summary and a new goal block

**Personalization with AI:**

- One master wellness approach, adapted per client: their foods, their schedule, their equipment, their taste
- AI drafts personalized weekly plans from the client's intake and progress
- AI writes check-in messages referencing the client's own answers
- AI prepares monthly summaries from check-in data — you review and send

**The check-in tool stack:**

- Google Forms for weekly check-ins (5 short questions)
- Google Sheets for per-client progress rows
- WhatsApp for delivery and messaging

**Do this:** keep every weekly touch short and specific to the client. "How did the Monday workout go?" beats a generic "how are you?"

**Not this:** sending the same generic message to all clients. A client who feels like a template stops showing up.

**TEMPLATE: Weekly check-in form**

```
WEEKLY CHECK-IN (5 questions, phone-friendly)
1. Sessions completed this week: [number]
2. Food went: [great / okay / rough]
3. Energy level: [1-10]
4. Sleep quality: [1-10]
5. One thing that helped or blocked you: [short text]
```

## Module 4: Marketing a coaching practice online and locally

Your coaching practice needs clients, and clients come from visibility. You have two marketing worlds: online (where Kenya discovers you) and local (where Kenya trusts you).

**The online marketing system:**

- A clear offer: who you coach, what you solve, the price
- A simple lead magnet — a free guide or a free 15-minute consult
- Content on Instagram, TikTok, and WhatsApp that shows real client results
- A booking link (Calendly or WhatsApp) so people can start easily

**The local marketing system:**

- Partner with gyms, salons, and wellness spaces near you
- Offer free or discounted workshops at churches, offices, and chamas
- Collect testimonials and photos (with permission) from real clients
- Ask every happy client for a referral

**The content rule for health:**

- Content educates and motivates; it does not diagnose or promise
- No "miracle weight loss in 7 days" claims — it is unethical and erodes trust
- Show your methods, your philosophy, and real client journeys

**Do this:** build one lead magnet (a free 7-day starter plan) and push all content toward it.

**Not this:** posting dramatic transformation content with fake before-and-after promises. Health audiences smell hype and so do regulators.

**TEMPLATE: Marketing offer**

```
MARKETING OFFER
I coach: [who]
Their problem: [what they struggle with]
My promise: [what they achieve in [timeframe]]
Offer: [program name, price, what's included]
Lead magnet: [free guide/consult]
Where I am visible: [Instagram, TikTok, WhatsApp, local partners]
How to start: [link / WhatsApp / consult]
```

## Module 5: Pricing and packaging coaching programs

People pay for results and accountability, not for PDFs. Package your coaching as a program with a start, a rhythm, and an outcome.

**Three package levels:**

- **Starter (per session)** — a single 45-60 minute coaching session (intake, plan, questions answered). Good for testing you out
- **Standard (monthly package)** — personalized program, weekly check-ins, WhatsApp support within stated hours, monthly review. Your main offer
- **Premium (higher package)** — everything in Standard plus video calls, deeper personalization, priority support, and more frequent reviews

**Pricing guidance for Kenya:**

- New coaches start lower and raise prices as testimonials and results accumulate
- Per session: roughly KES 1,500-4,000 depending on your market and credentials
- Monthly packages: roughly KES 3,000-10,000 depending on depth and support level
- Require a 4-week minimum for packages so clients commit, not sample and vanish

**The payment mechanics:**

- M-Pesa paybill or till for monthly subscriptions
- A set payment date each month
- Clear renewal rules in writing

**Do this:** write the delivery promise — what the client gets each week and when you reply. A clear promise beats "I'll be there."

**Not this:** pricing by your effort instead of the value. A client getting real results will pay more than the cost of your time.

**TEMPLATE: Coaching package**

```
COACHING PACKAGE
Package name: [Starter / Standard / Premium]
Who it is for: [client type]
Duration: [per session / per month / 3 months]
What's included weekly: [program, check-ins, support hours]
Delivery channels: [WhatsApp, calls, forms]
Price: [KES]
Payment method: [M-Pesa till/paybill number]
Minimum commitment: [rule]
Delivery promise: [exact weekly rhythm and reply times]
```

## Module 6: The monthly review and retention system

Retention is the quiet engine of a coaching business. A client who stays 3 months is worth far more than a client who comes once. Reviews keep clients, and reviews need numbers.

**The review rhythm:**

- Monthly: measure against the goals set at intake
- Show the client their own numbers: sessions, check-ins, weight or measurements where relevant, energy and sleep scores, and how they feel
- Celebrate two wins, be honest about one challenge, set next month's block

**The retention levers:**

- The client always knows what happens next week
- Adjustments happen quickly when life gets in the way (and it will)
- Check-ins get personal responses within your promised hours
- Payment reminders are friendly, not chasing

**AI's role:**

- Writes monthly result summaries from your check-in data
- Drafts personalized "here's what changed" messages
- Helps you plan the next goal block
- Suggests when a client's needs are out of scope — so you can refer honestly

**Do this:** show clients their own numbers every month. "Your energy went from 4 to 7, and you completed 9 sessions" keeps them subscribed.

**Not this:** tracking progress from memory. Track numbers, not vibes.

**TEMPLATE: Monthly review message**

```
MONTHLY REVIEW
Client: [name]
Month: [period]
Data: [sessions, check-ins, energy/sleep scores, measurements]
Two wins: [1] [2]
One honest challenge: [thing]
Next month's focus: [goal block]
Renewal: [date and payment reminder]
```

## Prompt Library

Copy, fill in the blanks, and adapt.

**Intake form questions**

Use to build your intake.

```
Write an intake questionnaire for wellness coaching clients in Kenya. Include a medical disclaimer agreement. Format for Google Forms or WhatsApp.
```

**SMART goal setting**

Use in the goal-setting session.

```
Help me set goals with a client who wants [goal]. Their reality: [time, budget, equipment, family, job]. Turn this into one 4-week goal and a small weekly goal for each week. Make each one specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound. Frame them so the client owns them, not me.
```

**Master wellness plan**

Use to build your base program.

```
Design a 4-week master wellness program for [goal, e.g. better energy and sleep] for a beginner in Kenya. Structure: weeks 1-2 foundation (build habits), weeks 3-4 progress. Include simple movement [X] days a week, sleep and stress habits, and affordable Kenyan foods (ugali, beans, eggs, sukuma, omena, avocado). Use small, sustainable changes. This is lifestyle coaching, not medical advice.
```

**Personalize for a client**

Use to adapt the master plan.

```
Here is my master plan: [paste]. Personalize it for this client: goal [X], schedule [X], food budget about KES [X] daily, equipment [X], preferences [enjoy/avoid], and health context [X]. Give me their full 4-week version with adjustments, keeping the structure. Flag anything that needs medical clearance.
```

**Weekly check-in form**

Use to build your tracking form.

```
Write a 5-question weekly check-in form for my coaching clients: sessions completed, food went (great/okay/rough), energy 1-10, sleep 1-10, and one thing that helped or blocked them. Format for Google Forms, short enough to answer on a phone, with a space for notes.
```

**Personalized check-in response**

Use mid-week, per client.

```
Here is a client's check-in for this week: [paste answers]. Their goal is [X] and last week they [progress]. Write a short personal response: acknowledge one real effort, address the blocker honestly, and give one small adjustment for the rest of the week. Under 100 words, encouraging, specific to them.
```

**Progress summary from check-ins**

Use monthly to close the loop.

```
Here is my client's check-in data for [month]: [paste]. Here is their starting data: [baseline]. Write a monthly progress summary: what improved (with their numbers), one honest challenge, and next month's focus. Frame it positively and truthfully. Under 150 words.
```

Use Mondays to open the week.

```
Write the Monday message for a coaching client whose focus this week is [focus]. Include: the one small goal, what they'll do each day (simple), a reminder of how to check in, and a warm opening line. Under 120 words, motivating and clear, ready to send on WhatsApp.
```

**Mid-week encouragement**

Use Wednesdays.

```
Write a short mid-week encouragement for a client who [is on track / missed a session]. Keep it non-judgemental, under 60 words, and end with one simple question to re-engage them for the rest of the week.

```

Note where they should seek professional help if sleep problems persist.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Coaches giving medical advice beyond their scope
- Letting AI output replace your professional judgment
- Coaching clients who skip the health questions
- Sending the same generic message to every client
- Promoting miracle results and fake transformations
- Pricing by your time instead of the client's value
- Promising 24/7 support and burning out
