# AI for Agribusiness: From Farm to Market

**Overview**

The Tier 1 course helped you identify crop problems fast. This course goes further — it turns your farm into a business. You'll build a simple record-keeping and planning system with AI, research buyers and pricing before you plant, time your sales to the Kenyan seasons, and sell directly to bypass brokers. The farmer who knows their numbers and their market always wins.

**Who this is for**

- Smallholder farmers who sell to brokers and suspect they're underpaid
- Farmers who keep records in their head or in a torn notebook
- Anyone starting commercial farming who wants to know what to plant and when
- Farmer groups and cooperatives that want to organize better

**What you'll be able to do**

- Keep simple farm records (crops, inputs, sales) using AI and Google Sheets
- Research buyers, pricing trends, and market timing before you plant
- Write marketing content to sell produce directly and bypass brokers
- Use AI-assisted coordination for cooperative and group selling

## Lesson 1: From quick help to a farm business system

A farmer who doesn't know their cost of production cannot price their produce. A farmer who doesn't track sales cannot negotiate. That's the gap this course closes.

Your farm system has four parts:

- **Records** — what you planted, what it cost, what you sold
- **Plan** — which crops, which season, how much land
- **Market** — who buys, at what price, when demand is highest
- **Sell** — how you reach buyers without handing profit to brokers

AI handles the writing and math. You provide the on-ground reality — your soil, your budget, your market.

**Do this:** start with one crop. Master the system on your main crop before expanding to all.

**Not this:** trying to track everything from day one. One crop, one season, done well.

## Lesson 2: Farm record-keeping with AI and Google Sheets

Records are the memory your farm business needs. Start simple — four things to track per crop:

- **Planting** — crop, date, seed cost, land size
- **Inputs** — fertilizer, manure, pesticides, water, labour, each with cost
- **Sales** — date, quantity, price per unit, buyer
- **Notes** — weather, problems, what worked

Set up a Google Sheet with one tab per crop, or a single tab with rows per entry. Use AI to design the sheet and to generate formulas that sum your costs and revenue automatically.

**The math that matters:**

- **Cost of production** — all inputs divided by expected yield = your break-even price
- **Profit per unit** — selling price minus cost of production
- **Net profit** — total sales minus total costs

If you don't know these three numbers, you're farming blind.

**Do this:** at the end of each month, paste your sheet into AI and ask for a profit summary.

**Not this:** recording sales but forgetting inputs. Under-counting costs makes you think you earned more than you did.

## Lesson 3: Plan your farm with AI

Planning beats hoping. Each season, answer five questions:

- **What** to plant — demand, price, and what grows in your area
- **How much** — land size, labour, and capital you can afford
- **When** — Kenya's two rainy seasons (long rains roughly March–May, short rains roughly October–December) and your local market days
- **Cost** — inputs you must buy before planting
- **Market** — who you'll sell to before the crop is ready

Give AI your land size, budget, and region, and it will draft a season plan with costs, expected yield, and rough profit. Treat AI as a planning partner — then confirm with your local agrovet or extension officer.

**Do this:** plan at least two months before planting so you can buy inputs when prices are low.

**Not this:** planting the same crop as every farmer in your area and then wondering why prices crash at harvest.

## Lesson 4: Research buyers, prices, and trends with AI

The broker's power comes from information. You can take that power back with research.

**Research your market before you plant:**

- **Who buys** — wholesalers, mama mbogas, hotels, restaurants, supermarkets, processors, exporters
- **What price** — current farm-gate price vs retail price, and the gap between them
- **Trends** — what prices did in the last two seasons, and what's driving demand
- **Grading** — what size, quality, and packing buyers expect

Use Perplexity or ChatGPT's web search to check current market prices, harvest forecasts, and reports from organizations like Kenya Agricultural and Livestock Research Organization and horticulture export bodies. Combine online research with real visits to your nearest market.

**Do this:** ask AI for "farm-gate price of [crop] in [county] this week" and cross-check with three people at your local market.

**Not this:** trusting one broker's word for the price. A price you didn't verify is a price you didn't get.

## Lesson 5: Time your sales to the seasons and market days

Timing is where small farmers lose or win.

**Two rainy seasons:**

- Long rains (around March–May) — main planting window, higher yields
- Short rains (around October–December) — a second planting window

Harvest timing affects price. Everyone harvesting at once floods the market and prices drop. Stagger your planting by two to three weeks so your harvest doesn't collide with everyone else's.

**Market days:** most Kenyan towns have designated market days (e.g. every Wednesday and Saturday). Knowing the day matters because buyers are present on those days, and prices can differ between them.

**Do this:** plan a 3-week staggered planting calendar so you sell over several weeks instead of one.

**Not this:** planting everything on the same day. One crop, one harvest, one price crash.

## Lesson 6: Sell direct and bypass brokers

Direct selling means more of the consumer's shilling lands in your pocket. It requires work, but AI reduces the workload.

**Direct channels that work in Kenya:**

- **WhatsApp groups** — produce-buyer groups, matatu and estate groups, your own status updates
- **Local markets** — take your own stall or sell to mama mbogas directly
- **Aggregation** — team with neighbours to assemble volumes that attract better buyers

For each channel, you need a selling message: what you have, how fresh it is, the price, and how to order. AI writes your WhatsApp posts, product descriptions, and flyer text.

**Do this:** build a small buyer list in your phone — even 10 regular customers change your farm.

**Not this:** quitting the broker on day one without a channel ready. Keep the broker while you grow your own customers.

## Lesson 7: Cooperative and group selling with AI

Groups get better prices than individuals. A buyer needs volume, and a group can supply it — but coordination is hard. AI handles the coordination.

**What the group system needs:**

- **A shared plan** — who plants what, in what quantity, so the group supplies steady volume
- **Pooled records** — one sheet where every member's harvest and costs are recorded
- **Shared selling** — one representative sells the pooled produce to a better buyer
- **Fair sharing** — clear rules for how the group splits revenue

AI can draft the group's WhatsApp messages, set up the shared tracking sheet, write a simple revenue-sharing agreement for review, and prepare reports for the group's meetings.

**Do this:** agree revenue-sharing rules in writing before the first sale, not after.

**Not this:** pooling produce without records. Money arguments kill more groups than low prices do.

## Prompt Library

Copy, fill in the blanks, and adapt.

**Set up my farm records sheet**

Use to design your Google Sheet.

```
Create a Google Sheets layout for my farm records. I grow [crops]. I need columns for planting date, crop, land size, seed and input costs, harvest date, quantity sold, price per unit, buyer, and notes. Add a summary section that totals costs, revenue, and profit, with a formula for cost of production per unit. Explain how to set it up in Google Sheets step by step.
```

**Monthly profit summary**

Use monthly with your records.

```
Here is my farm record data for this month: [paste rows]. Total my costs, my revenue, and my profit. Calculate my cost of production per unit and profit per unit for [crop]. Tell me which crop made the most profit and what to adjust next month.
```

**Season planting plan**

Use before planting season.

```
I have [X] acres in [county, region]. My budget for inputs is KES [amount]. Plan my [long/short] rains planting season: which crops to plant, how much land each, estimated input costs, expected yield, and rough profit at current prices. Include a staggered planting schedule so I don't harvest everything at once.
```

**Break-even price check**

Use before you accept any price.

```
For [crop], my total input costs this season were KES [X] and I expect to harvest [Y] kg. What is my break-even price per kg? And what should I aim to sell at to make a [X]% profit margin? Tell me what to do if market prices are below my break-even.
```

**Market price research**

Use before planting and before selling.

```
Search for the current farm-gate and retail prices of [crop] in [county, e.g. Uasin Gishu] this week. Also give me the price trend over the last two seasons and what is driving demand. List 3 ways a small farmer can get a better price than the broker offers.
```

**Buyer research**

Use to build your buyer list.

```
I grow [crop]. List the types of buyers in Kenya who buy this produce (wholesalers, mama mbogas, hotels, processors, exporters). For each, tell me the volumes they usually want, the quality and grading they expect, and how I can approach them from [location]. Suggest how to start with small volumes.
```

**Direct selling post**

Use to announce produce on WhatsApp and Facebook.

```
Write a WhatsApp status and a Facebook post selling my [crop]. Details: [quantity], harvested [date], price KES [X] per kg, available in [location], delivery terms [X], contact [number]. Make it short, honest, and fresh-sounding. Give me 3 versions with different angles: freshness, price, and urgency.
```

**Product description for a buyer**

Use when contacting restaurants, hotels, or stores.

```
Write a short, professional message to pitch my [crop] to a [restaurant/supermarket] in [town]. I produce [quantity] per week, can deliver [times], and my price is KES [X] per kg. Include what makes it good (freshness, how it's grown) and ask for a chance to bring a sample. Keep it to 5 sentences.
```

**Market day prep**

Use the day before market.

```
Tomorrow is my market day in [town]. I have [quantity] of [crop]. List what to prepare tonight: price ranges, packing, transport, and key talking points when buyers negotiate. Give me a simple negotiation script for when a buyer pushes the price down.
```

**Staggered planting calendar**

Use to avoid harvest crashes.

```
I want to plant [crop] over a period so I don't harvest everything at once. My planting window is [dates] and my land is [size]. Build a staggered planting calendar with 3–4 batches spaced 2–3 weeks apart, and estimate when each harvest will be ready.
```

**Group coordination message**

Use to organize your farmer group.

```
Draft a WhatsApp message to my farmer group of [X] members asking who wants to plant [crop] for the [season] season. Include a deadline to respond, a simple plan for pooling harvests, and an invitation to a planning meeting on [date]. Keep it short and clear.
```

**Group revenue-sharing agreement**

Use before pooled sales begin.

```
Write a simple revenue-sharing agreement for a farmer group of [X] members who pool [crop] for joint selling. Cover: who sells, how revenue is split (by contribution), record-keeping rules, how disputes are resolved, and what happens if a member doesn't deliver. Keep the language simple and note that a lawyer should review it before signing.
```

**Aggregation logistics**

Use when combining produce from several members.

```
Our group has [X] members each with [quantity] of [crop] to sell this week. Help me coordinate: a schedule for bringing produce to a collection point on [date], a check-off list to record each member's quantity, and a plan for one representative to sell it to [buyer]. Draft the message and the check-off sheet.
```

**Crop problem record**

Use when you notice a problem, to keep history.

```
I noticed [problem] on my [crop] in [field/plot]. Write 3 questions to ask the local agrovet or extension officer, and a short note to add to my farm records so I can see if this repeats next season.
```

## Action plan

1. Set up your farm records sheet with AI.
2. Record every cost from the current season — seeds, inputs, labour.
3. Run the market research prompts for your main crop.
4. Calculate your break-even price per unit.
5. Write your first direct-selling posts for WhatsApp and Facebook.
6. Build a buyer list of at least 10 contacts.
7. Attend one market day with your price ranges and negotiation script.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Recording sales but ignoring input costs
- Planting the same crop as everyone else without checking prices
- Accepting a broker's price without verifying the market
- Harvesting everything at once and crashing the price
- Selling in groups without written revenue-sharing rules
- Planning without confirming advice with a local agrovet or extension officer
