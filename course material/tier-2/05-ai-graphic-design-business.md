# AI Graphic Design Business: From Canva to Client Work

## Overview

You can already make great graphics — now get paid for it. This course turns your Canva skills into a real design business: a portfolio that attracts clients, clear pricing for logos, posters, and social kits, ways to find your first paying customers locally and online, and a professional delivery and revision system. Start small, get paid, grow.

## Who this is for

- Designers who make free graphics for friends and want to charge
- Small business owners who want design as a side income
- Freelancers adding design to their service list
- Anyone who wants their first paying design client

## What you'll be able to do

- Build a portfolio of AI-assisted designs that attract clients
- Price and package logos, posters, and social kits in KES
- Find your first paying clients locally and on freelance platforms
- Deliver professional files and manage client revisions cleanly
- Get paid reliably via M-Pesa and international payment methods

## Lesson 1: From hobby to paid designer

Clients do not pay for your hours; they pay for what the design does — a shop that looks established, a brand people remember, a poster that fills a hall. Sell the result, not the effort.

The mindset shift is the first step. You are a small design studio that uses AI to work fast and deliver good work. Speed is your advantage: a client's flyer in 24 hours beats a "big agency" in two weeks.

Start small and honest. You do not need to be a brand expert to design a price list or a poster. Take the work you can deliver well, deliver it fast, and let referrals build your name.

## Lesson 2: Build a portfolio that sells

Before you can ask for money, show what you can do. Build six to eight polished pieces on Canva — mock projects are fine if you do not have clients yet. Design a salon logo, a restaurant poster, a church event flyer, and a social media kit.

Present your work well: create a simple portfolio on Canva or Behance, and post your best three pieces on Instagram with a short line about the client goal. A messy profile loses trust; three clean pieces win it.

Add a before-and-after if you can. Showing an ordinary flyer next to your version proves your value faster than any price list. Keep the portfolio updated as real client work comes in.

## Lesson 3: Price and package your services

Charge in packages, not per hour, because you work fast. In the Kenyan freelance market, logo design typically ranges from about KES 3,000 to KES 15,000 depending on scope and client size; posters and flyers sit lower; social media kits in the middle. Use these as anchors, not rules.

Offer three levels so clients choose one: a basic poster, a standard poster plus two social versions, and a full kit with brand colours and a set of templates. Packages feel safe to buy and raise your average sale.

Quote in KES with a clear what-you-get list. When a client says it is too much, remove items rather than dropping the price — that keeps your value intact.

## Lesson 4: Find your first paying clients

Your first clients are closer than you think: shops, salons, churches, restaurants, and SACCOs in your own town. Walk in with one sample relevant to them and a simple price list. A salon needs a price list and social posts; a restaurant needs a menu and flyers.

Use WhatsApp Status and local WhatsApp groups to post one sample with a price. Ask every satisfied client for a referral — in Kenya, word of mouth moves faster than any ad.

Online, create profiles on Upwork and Fiverr. Pick a narrow offer — "logo and social kit for small businesses" — and send tailored proposals. The first client is the hardest; the second one comes from the first one's referral.

## Lesson 5: Sell with a simple offer sheet

You need two documents to sell cleanly: a one-page offer sheet (your packages and prices) and a client brief form (questions that tell you what they need). Make the offer sheet in Canva — it is a design, so it should look designed.

Before quoting, ask the right questions: what business, what the design is for, where it will be used, their colours and style, and the deadline. A short brief prevents redoing work and makes you look professional.

Send the proposal as a WhatsApp message or PDF with the package, the price, the timeline, and what you need from them to start. Clear scope at the start means no surprises at the end.

## Lesson 6: Get paid — M-Pesa, Payoneer, Wise

Local clients pay with M-Pesa. Give them a till number or use STK push, and never start work before a deposit — a 50 percent deposit is standard and protects you both. Collect the balance on delivery.

International clients on Upwork and Fiverr pay through the platform into Payoneer or Wise. Set up the account before you need it; the platform holds payment until approval, and withdrawal takes a couple of days.

Keep a simple record of every job: client, package, price, deposit, balance, deadline. A note on your phone is enough. You cannot grow a business you cannot see.

## Lesson 7: Deliver professional files

Delivery is where clients judge you. Send your work in the right formats: logos and posters as high-resolution PNG and PDF for printing, and editable Canva or PDF for clients who will reuse them. Name files clearly — "Salon-Logo-Final-2026.png", not "final2(3)".

Give the client a short delivery message: what is included, the formats, and how to request changes. Tell them how many revision rounds the package covers before they ask.

Offer a simple brand kit for clients who will keep posting: their logo, brand colours, fonts, and three template posts. It is a small extra that makes you their ongoing designer instead of a one-off.

## Lesson 8: Manage revisions without losing money

Revisions are normal, but unlimited revisions are a trap. State the revision rounds in your package and price extra rounds. When a client asks for changes, collect them in one list instead of piecemeal — "send me all changes in one message" saves everyone time.

Use AI to help: paste the client's feedback and your design description into ChatGPT to organise the changes and catch contradictions. Then deliver the revised version against the list.

Set boundaries politely. If feedback keeps growing beyond the agreed scope, offer the extra work as a new, priced round. Clients respect a designer who is clear, and clear scope keeps the work profitable.

## Prompt Library

Copy, fill the brackets, and reuse per client and per project.

**Portfolio project brief**

```
Give me a brief for a mock design portfolio project: a [business type]
needs [logo/poster/social kit]. Include their brand personality, target
audience, colours to use, and the problem the design must solve.
```

How to use: run to build realistic mock projects for your portfolio.

**Client intake questions**

```
I am taking on a design client. Write 8 questions to ask before
quoting: their business, what the design is for, where it will be
used, their style preferences, colours, and deadline.
```

How to use: send this brief form before every new project.

**Logo concept ideas**

```
My client runs a [business] called [name] in [town]. Give me 3 logo
concepts: a symbol idea, a font style, and a colour palette for each.
Target audience: [audience]. Keep them simple and memorable.
```

How to use: brainstorm concepts in ChatGPT, then execute in Canva.

**Poster copy and layout**

```
Write the copy for a [poster type] for [business]: a bold headline
under 6 words, 3 supporting lines, and a call to action. Suggest a
layout that suits a phone screen and print. Include a price or date
slot. No emojis.
```

How to use: get text and layout direction before opening Canva.

**Social media kit**

```
Outline a social media kit for [business]: 5 post types, their
purpose, and the key text and image style for each. Brand colours:
[colours]. Keep every post on-brand and simple to produce.
```

How to use: plan a kit as a package, then build templates once.

**Pricing recommendation**

```
I offer [logo design/poster design/social kits] in Kenya. Here are
typical market rates I know: [rates]. Recommend a 3-tier pricing
structure (basic, standard, premium) with KES prices, and what is
included at each tier.
```

How to use: build your package list with grounded pricing.

**Proposal message**

```
Write a short proposal for a design job. Client: [business].
Service: [service]. Price: KES [amount]. Includes: [what's included].
Timeline: [days]. Deposit of 50% before starting. Friendly and clear.
```

How to use: send via WhatsApp or email to turn a quote into a yes.

**Upwork proposal**

```
Write an Upwork proposal for this design job: [paste job post].
Under 120 words: name their need, show I understand it, mention
relevance to their business, and end with one question.
```

How to use: one tailored proposal per job, never copy-paste.

**Feedback organiser**

```
Organise this client feedback into a clear list of changes, group
similar ones, and flag any that contradict earlier requests:
[paste feedback]
```

How to use: paste revision comments to turn chaos into an action list.

**Revision scope check**

```
My client is asking for changes beyond the agreed scope of [scope].
List the changes that fit the scope and the ones that are new work,
then draft a polite message offering the extra work as a priced add-on.
```

How to use: protect your margins while staying friendly.

**Delivery message**

```
Write a professional delivery message for a completed design project.
Mention the files included, the formats, and how many revision rounds
are covered. Keep it warm and under 120 words.
```

How to use: send with every final file to look established.

**Payment reminder**

```
Write a polite WhatsApp message asking a client to clear the balance
of KES [amount] for the delivered [service]. Mention the M-Pesa
till/STK details. Friendly, not nagging.
```

How to use: send when the balance is due after delivery.

**Brand kit structure**

```
Structure a simple brand kit for [business]: logo, colour palette
with hex codes, font pairing, and 3 reusable post templates. Describe
each item and the rules for using them consistently.
```

How to use: build the kit once and give it to every client.

**Testimonial request**

```
Write a short WhatsApp message asking a happy design client for a
one-line testimonial. Include an example so it is easy for them:
"Got a logo from [name] and [result]."
```

How to use: collect reviews that win your next client.

5. Post one sample on your WhatsApp Status with prices.
6. Create an Upwork or Fiverr profile and send one tailored proposal.
7. Deliver your first paid job with a 50% deposit, clear files, and a revision limit.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Pricing per hour when you work in minutes
- Unlimited revisions with no scope
- Starting work before the deposit arrives
- Sending files named "final2(3)" with no explanation
- Designing what you love instead of what the client needs
- Ignoring referrals and testimonials
