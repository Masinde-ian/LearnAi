# AI for Church & Ministry Content at Scale

**Overview**

The Tier 1 course helped you write announcements and sermon summaries. This course builds the full ministry content system: a monthly content calendar for sermons, devotionals, and social posts; one sermon turned into YouTube, WhatsApp, and Facebook content; outreach and follow-up that actually welcomes new members; and a consistent, authentic voice across everything. Reach more people without burning out your few volunteers.

**Who this is for**

- Pastors and ministry leaders with limited time and volunteers
- Church communications teams who post "whenever there's time"
- Youth and media teams running the church's social media pages
- Anyone building a ministry or faith-based platform online

**What you'll be able to do**

- Build a monthly content calendar covering sermons, devotionals, and social posts
- Turn one sermon into content for YouTube, WhatsApp, and Facebook
- Use AI to support outreach and follow-up with new members
- Keep a consistent, authentic voice across all your content

## Lesson 1: From announcements to a content system

An announcement is a one-off. A system is content that works all week. Most churches post only when something happens — and then wonder why the page is quiet.

A ministry content system runs on three pillars:

- **Word** — sermons, devotionals, scripture reflections
- **People** — members, events, testimonies, prayer points
- **Outreach** — reaching those outside the church and welcoming new members

Every week you produce a small amount of content across these pillars. AI doesn't replace your message — it multiplies it. You create once, then AI helps you adapt it everywhere.

**Do this:** decide your church's content pillars and post on a rhythm — for example, three posts a week, one per pillar.

**Not this:** posting five times in one week and then nothing for two weeks. Consistency beats bursts.

## Lesson 2: Know your audience and your platforms

Your members live on different platforms, and each platform has its own culture.

- **WhatsApp** — your warmest audience. Status, groups, and broadcast lists. Short, personal, urgent.
- **Facebook** — the widest reach in Kenya, especially older members. Longer posts, event pages, photos, live video.
- **YouTube** — the sermon archive. Full messages, shorts, and series. Built over months, not days.
- **Instagram/TikTok** — the younger crowd. Short clips, reels, testimonies, memes with a message.

Before creating content, ask AI to help you write a short audience profile: who follows each page, what they need, and what time they're online.

**Do this:** match the message to the platform. A devotional that works as a 30-second WhatsApp status becomes a 3-minute Facebook post becomes a 15-minute YouTube short-form lesson.

**Not this:** copying the same text to every platform. It reads as lazy, and the platforms punish it.

## Lesson 3: Build a monthly content calendar

A calendar stops the Sunday-night panic of "what do we post tomorrow?"

**How to build one with AI:**

- List your recurring items — Sunday sermon, midweek service, devotionals, birthdays, events
- Add special items — crusades, harvest, choir events, weddings, memorials
- Ask AI to spread these across a month and fill gaps with devotionals and scripture posts
- Include the platform, the hook, and who writes each piece

Use a simple Google Sheet or a Notion page with one row per day. Assign only what your team can actually produce — a calendar that overcommits collapses in week two.

**Do this:** plan a month in two hours. Then each week is just production, not planning.

**Not this:** filling the calendar with generic posts you have no intention of making.

## Lesson 4: One sermon, many platforms

This is the heart of the system. One Sunday sermon becomes your week of content.

**The repurposing chain:**

1. **Record it** — a phone on a stand records the sermon. CapCut trims it.
2. **Summarize it** — AI turns the message into a clear summary and key points.
3. **Sermon recap** — a written summary for the church group and Facebook.
4. **3 devotionals** — pull one point from the sermon for each morning's devotional.
5. **YouTube** — upload the full sermon; make 3–5 Shorts from the strongest moments.
6. **Social posts** — quote cards in Canva, captions, and discussion questions.

One hour of sermon becomes a full week of content. AI does the summarizing, structuring, and rewriting — you add the Spirit and the truth.

**Do this:** ask AI to pull the single strongest 30–60 second moment from the sermon for a Short — moments of testimony and strong statement land best.

**Not this:** uploading the full sermon to social media without captions or context. People scroll past blurry hour-long videos.

## Lesson 5: Devotionals and daily encouragement

Devotionals are the backbone of daily engagement. A short, faithful devotional posted every morning builds a loyal following faster than anything else.

**The devotional formula:**

- A scripture verse
- A short reflection in plain language
- One practical takeaway for the reader's day
- A one-line prayer or question

AI can draft a month of devotionals from your sermon themes, your church calendar, or a book of the Bible you're studying. You review, adjust, and post.

**Do this:** write devotionals in your church's voice — if your church speaks Swahili or Sheng sometimes, let that come through. Authentic beats formal.

**Not this:** letting AI generate devotionals with no review. You must read, adjust, and pray over every piece before it goes out.

## Lesson 6: Outreach and new-member follow-up

Content isn't only about those already inside. It's about reaching those outside and welcoming them in.

**Outreach content:** testimonies, event invitations, open invitations to service, community help content. AI writes invitation messages, outreach posts, and scripts for visiting new areas.

**New-member follow-up — the golden window:** the 48 hours after someone visits. That's when a welcome message decides whether they return.

**The follow-up sequence:**

- **Same day:** a warm welcome message
- **Day 2:** a thank-you and invitation to the next service or a small group
- **Day 7:** a check-in and a personal invite to something specific
- **Day 30:** a follow-up — did they settle? What do they need?

AI drafts these templates. You personalize the name and the detail.

**Do this:** set up a simple WhatsApp follow-up routine for every new visitor, with a note in your phone or a simple spreadsheet.

**Not this:** sending the same message to everyone word-for-word with no personal touch. People can tell.

## Lesson 7: Keep a consistent, authentic voice

The biggest fear about AI in ministry is that content will sound robotic. That fear is valid — but avoidable.

**Build a voice guide.** Write 3–5 sample posts in your church's real voice, and paste them into AI's custom instructions. From then on, tell AI to match that voice. The result reads like you, not like a robot.

**Keep it authentic by:**

- Reviewing every AI draft before posting
- Adding personal details, names, and local references
- Keeping scripture accurate — always verify quotes and references
- Never letting AI write about sensitive pastoral matters without your full review

**Do this:** create your voice guide once and reuse it for everything — sermons, devotionals, social, WhatsApp.

**Not this:** publishing AI output unread, or claiming AI wrote something you didn't prepare. Transparency and oversight keep your ministry credible.

## Prompt Library

Copy, fill in the blanks, and adapt.

**Build my monthly content calendar**

Use once a month to plan.

```
Build a monthly content calendar for my church in [town]. Recurring items: Sunday sermon recap on [platform], 3 weekly devotionals, [event 1], [event 2]. Fill gaps with scripture posts and testimonies. Show it as a day-by-day table with the platform and the hook for each post. Keep it realistic for a team of [X] volunteers.
```

**Sermon summary and key points**

Use right after service, from your notes or transcript.

```
Here is my sermon on [topic]: [paste notes or transcript]. Summarize it in 5 sentences, list the 3 main points, and give me one memorable quote from the message I can use on social media.
```

**Turn the sermon into a week of content**

Use the repurposing chain.

```
Turn this sermon summary into a full week of content: [paste summary]. 1) A Facebook recap post, 2) 3 morning devotionals, 3) a WhatsApp status message, 4) 3 YouTube Shorts ideas with captions, 5) a discussion question for midweek. Keep it in my voice: warm, direct, and clear.
```

**YouTube video script from the sermon**

Use to turn a section into a video.

```
Write a script for a 10-minute YouTube video from this sermon point: [point]. Structure: a hook in the first 15 seconds, the teaching, a real-life illustration, and a call to action to subscribe and comment. Use simple, warm language that sounds spoken, not read.
```

**YouTube Shorts from strong moments**

Use to find clip-worthy moments.

```
From this sermon [paste transcript], pick the 3 strongest 30-second moments that would work as YouTube Shorts. For each, give the exact quote and a caption under 100 characters. Avoid moments that need too much context.
```

**Devotional series**

Use to write a month of devotionals.

```
Write 4 devotionals for next week based on [passage/theme]. Each should have: a scripture verse (verify it), a short plain-language reflection, one practical takeaway, and a one-line prayer. Write in a warm pastoral voice, suitable for [WhatsApp status/Facebook/print]. Keep each under 120 words.
```

**Facebook post from the sermon**

Use for the Sunday recap.

```
Write a Facebook post recapping today's sermon on [topic]. Include the main point, 2 supporting points, a Bible reference, and an invitation to join us next Sunday at [church, time, location]. Warm and welcoming, 150 words max, ending with a question to encourage comments.
```

**WhatsApp status and group message**

Use for quick, short reach.

```
Write a WhatsApp status (under 50 words) and a short group message about [event/theme] for my church group. Include the date, time, and location. Make it feel personal and urgent, like I'm inviting a friend, not announcing to a crowd.
```

**New member follow-up sequence**

Use for every new visitor.

```
Write a 4-message follow-up sequence for a first-time visitor to my church in [town]. Message 1: warm welcome on the day. Message 2: thank-you and invite to [next service/small group] on day 2. Message 3: friendly check-in on day 7. Message 4: follow-up on day 30 asking if they have settled and how we can help. Each under 60 words, leaving space for the visitor's name.
```

**Outreach invitation message**

Use for crusades, events, or area outreach.

```
Write an outreach invitation message for [event] to people who don't attend church. Tone: warm, humble, no judgement, no church jargon. Include date, time, location, and a line that says everyone is welcome as they are. Give me a version for WhatsApp and a shorter version for a Facebook post.
```

**Announcement set for the week**

Use weekly for practical updates.

```
Write this week's church announcements: [list items: services, events, needs, prayer points]. Format as a short paragraph each, then a single combined WhatsApp message under 200 words. Keep the tone warm and clear.
```

**Prayer request response**

Use to respond kindly to sensitive messages.

```
Write a compassionate, scripture-based response to a member who sent this prayer request: [paste request]. Keep it personal, non-judgemental, and under 100 words. Do not give medical, financial, or legal advice. Close by inviting them to connect with the pastoral team if they need more support.
```

**Voice guide for my church**

Use once, then keep in AI's custom instructions.

```
Here are 3 posts written in my church's real voice: [paste samples]. Write a style guide from them: tone, sentence length, words we use, words we avoid, how we address readers, and how we sign off. This is the voice to use for all our content.
```

**Testimony feature**

Use to feature members' stories.

```
I want to feature a member's testimony about [theme]. Here are their words: [paste notes]. Rewrite it as a 200-word Facebook post and a 60-second video script, keeping their voice and story intact. Add a question at the end to invite others to share.
```

**Content calendar review**

Use at month-end.

```
Here is what my church posted last month: [paste list of posts and results]. Which themes and formats likely worked best? Suggest next month's calendar around the 2 strongest themes, and tell me what to stop doing.
```

4. Record this Sunday's sermon and run the repurposing prompts.
5. Publish one devotional every morning this week.
6. Set up the 4-message follow-up for new visitors.
7. Review the month and refine next month's calendar.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Posting sporadically instead of on a calendar rhythm
- Copy-pasting the same text to every platform
- Publishing AI output without review
- Writing devotionals in a formal voice that doesn't match your church
- Missing the 48-hour window for new-member follow-up
- Letting AI write about sensitive matters without pastoral oversight
