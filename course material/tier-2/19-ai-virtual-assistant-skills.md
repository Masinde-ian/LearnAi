## Lesson 1: What a VA actually does

A virtual assistant helps a busy person run their business. The core tasks fall into four areas:

- **Scheduling**: booking meetings, managing calendars, setting reminders
- **Inbox management**: triaging emails, drafting replies, clearing clutter
- **Data tasks**: updating sheets, cleaning lists, simple research, reports
- **Social media**: scheduling posts, responding to comments, simple graphics

Clients hire VAs for one thing: to get hours of their life back. Your job is reliability — showing up, doing the task, reporting back. Rates for remote VA work vary widely; new VAs start low and grow fast with good reviews. Kenyan VAs are in demand precisely because they are reliable and hard-working.

## Lesson 2: Your skill stack, taught simply

You do not need to master everything at once. Start with these, in order:

1. **Email and calendar**: Gmail filters, labels, and Google Calendar basics
2. **Spreadsheets**: the data skills from the Tier 1 course
3. **Chat apps**: Slack or Discord basics for client communication
4. **Task tools**: Trello, Asana, or Notion to track what is due
5. **Social scheduling**: Canva for graphics and Meta Business Suite for posting

Learn one tool per week and practise on your own life first: set up your own inbox rules, schedule your own week, run your own task board. The skills you practise on yourself are the ones you can sell to a client.

## Lesson 3: The AI-assisted VA workflow

AI turns a one-person business into a one-person team. The pattern is always the same: AI drafts, you check, you send.

- **Inbox**: ask AI to draft replies from short notes you type
- **Scheduling**: use AI to suggest meeting times and draft the invite message
- **Data**: AI writes the formulas, cleans the lists, summarises reports
- **Social**: AI drafts post ideas, you approve, a scheduler posts them
- **Emails**: keep a template library AI personalises per client

Your value is judgment, not typing. Clients do not pay for keystrokes; they pay for a clean inbox and a calm calendar. AI does the grunt work; you decide what matters and what gets sent.

## Lesson 4: Portfolio and CV for VA roles

VA clients rarely ask for a formal CV. They want proof you can do the job. Build both anyway:

- **One-page CV**: title it "Virtual Assistant", list scheduling, inbox, data, and social skills, and one line on how AI makes you faster
- **Portfolio**: 3-5 real examples — a cleaned spreadsheet, a draft email system, a weekly schedule, a sample social calendar. Do the work on your own projects or for a friend, and screenshot the results
- **A welcome packet**: one page explaining how you work, your hours, and how a client can reach you

Give every example a short story: "Built this sales tracker for a Mombasa shop; the owner now sees weekly totals in one click." Stories sell skills.

## Lesson 5: Where to find remote VA jobs

The best platforms in 2026:

- **Upwork and Fiverr**: start with "virtual assistant" and "admin support" gigs; build reviews slowly
- **VirtualStaffFinder and OnlineJobs.ph**: specifically match assistants with long-term clients
- **LinkedIn**: set your headline to "Virtual Assistant", post one helpful post a week, and connect with small business owners
- **Remote job boards**: search "virtual assistant remote" on boards like Remote.co and We Work Remotely
- **Your network**: local business owners who now work online also need VAs

Apply daily, not weekly. VA work is a numbers game: 20 good applications is a normal week. Never pay to apply — legitimate platforms never charge you to start.

## Lesson 6: Landing the job

Your profile and proposals win the interview:

- **Profile**: clear headline, a short "how I work" paragraph, and a skills list with the tools you actually use
- **Proposal**: open with the client's problem in their own words, show you understood it, list exactly what you will do, and ask one question
- **Interview**: prepare answers for "how do you organise your day", "what tools do you use", and "how do you handle mistakes"

In the interview, ask about their tools and expectations. A good client wants a reliable assistant, not a genius — calm confidence and clear questions win more jobs than impressive claims.

## Lesson 7: Managing multiple clients like a professional

The skill that separates a VA from a hobby is organisation across clients:

- **Inbox rules**: one label and folder per client; rules auto-sort their emails
- **Templates**: a library of email replies, meeting invites, and status updates personalised per client
- **Task board**: one board with a column per client; everything due is visible in one place
- **Time tracking**: note hours per client (even in a simple sheet) so billing is honest
- **Weekly report**: one short message per client: what was done, what is next, anything needing their input

Never mix clients' data. Use separate folders, separate labels, and separate sheets. A client who feels like their work is handled is a client who stays.

## Lesson 8: Getting paid and growing

Local clients can pay via M-Pesa. International clients pay through the platform (Upwork, Fiverr) or via Payoneer — which can move money to your M-Pesa or bank.

- Set your rates: new VAs often start at $5-8 per hour, experienced VAs $10-20+
- Charge per hour for unclear scopes, per task or retainer for defined weekly work
- A monthly retainer (e.g. 20 hours at a fixed price) gives you stable income
- Keep a payment record per client and agree payment terms before starting

Grow by raising rates as your reviews build, and by converting good clients to retainers. One strong retainer client beats five one-off jobs — fewer gaps, steadier income.

## Prompt Library

**VA skill self-assessment**

**How to use it:** Find your starting strengths.

```
Ask me 10 questions to assess my virtual assistant skills across: scheduling, inbox management, data tasks, and social media. For each area, score me 1-5, then tell me my strongest skill to lead with and the one skill to practise this week. Keep the questions simple and practical.
```

**VA CV builder**

**How to use it:** A one-page CV in minutes.

```
Write a one-page CV for a virtual assistant with these skills: [SKILLS]. Format: headline "Virtual Assistant", a 2-line summary mentioning AI-assisted speed, a skills section with the tools I use, a "relevant work" section with 3 short story-based bullets, and education. Keep it ATS-friendly, no fancy layout, under 400 words.
```

**Portfolio outline**

**How to use it:** Plan the 3-5 examples to build.

```
Create a portfolio plan for a VA with 5 sample pieces: a sales tracking spreadsheet, a draft email/inbox system, a weekly schedule, a social media calendar, and a client welcome packet. For each, describe what to build, what screenshot or result to show, and a one-line story of the value delivered. I have no real client yet, so use personal or practice projects.
```

**Upwork profile bio**

**How to use it:** A headline and bio that get clicks.

```
Write an Upwork profile bio for a virtual assistant in Kenya. Headline should be: "Virtual Assistant | Inbox & Calendar | Data & Social Media | AI-Assisted". Bio: 150 words, friendly and specific, mention reliability, AI-assisted speed, tools I use [LIST], and that I respond fast. End with how a client can start with a small task to test me.
```

**Proposal template**

**How to use it:** The opening that wins jobs.

```
Write a job proposal template for a VA applying to an "admin support / virtual assistant" post. Structure: open by restating the client's problem in their words, list the 3 exact tasks I would do this week, state my availability, and end with one question that starts a conversation. Keep it under 180 words, no bragging, no emojis. Leave blanks: [CLIENT NEED], [TASKS], [AVAILABILITY].
```

**Interview prep**

**How to use it:** Ready answers for common VA questions.

```
Prepare 8 likely VA interview questions and short model answers. Cover: how I organise my day, which tools I use, how I handle mistakes, how I manage multiple clients, how I protect client data, why I use AI, how I communicate, and what my hours are. Keep answers under 60 words each, honest and calm.
```

**Inbox rule setup**

**How to use it:** Set up per-client email folders.

```
Write instructions for setting up Gmail labels, filters, and rules so emails from each client auto-sort into their own folder. Include: creating a label, creating a filter by sender, and marking a client email as important. Keep steps simple, numbered, and suitable for someone doing this on a phone or computer for the first time.
```

**Email reply drafts**

**How to use it:** Fast, professional replies from short notes.

```
Draft a professional email reply for a virtual assistant based on this short note from my client: "[NOTE]". Match a warm but professional tone, keep it under 100 words, and include a clear next step. Show the reply and a version that is even shorter for casual clients.
```

**Meeting scheduling message**

**How to use it:** Booking calls without back-and-forth.

```
Write a message to schedule a 30-minute call with a client. Offer 3 time options across two time zones (Kenya time and [CLIENT TIME ZONE]), include a Google Meet or Zoom link placeholder, ask them to confirm one option, and attach a short agenda: intro, tasks, tools, next steps. Keep it under 120 words.
```

**Weekly client report**

**How to use it:** Send the same update to every client.

```
Create a weekly client report template for a VA with sections: what I completed this week, what is in progress, anything needing the client's input, and next week's plan. Keep each section to 2 short lines and end with a question that keeps the client engaged. Make it fillable in under 5 minutes.
```

**Task tracking board**

**How to use it:** One place for every client's tasks.

```
Design a simple task board for a VA managing 3 clients. Use columns: Backlog, This Week, In Progress, Waiting on Client, Done. Give me the setup steps for a free tool like Trello or Notion, how to tag each card with a client name, and a rule for checking the board twice a day.
```

**Client welcome packet**

**How to use it:** Professional onboarding from day one.

```
Write a one-page welcome packet for a new VA client. Sections: a short thank-you, how I work (tools, hours, response time), how we communicate (email, Slack, WhatsApp), what I need from you (access to tools, decisions, feedback), and my first-week plan. Keep it warm, clear, and under 350 words.
```

**VA pricing setup**

**How to use it:** Decide hourly, per-task, or retainer.

```
Help me set VA pricing. I am new with [SKILLS] and plan to work remotely. Suggest: an hourly rate range (USD) for a new VA, a per-task price for a defined job like email cleanup or a weekly social calendar, and a monthly retainer for 20 hours a week. Explain when each pricing model suits the client, and how to adjust as reviews grow.
```

**Job application tracker**

**How to use it:** Never lose track of applications.

```
Create a job application tracker table with columns: date applied, platform (Upwork/Fiverr/LinkedIn/other), job title, client or company, proposal sent, status (applied/replied/interviewed/offered/rejected), follow-up date, notes. Explain how to maintain it in Google Sheets and why a follow-up rule (re-pitch every 3 days) improves results.
```

**AI-assisted daily routine**

**How to use it:** The workflow that keeps you fast.

```
Design my daily VA routine using AI: morning inbox triage (AI drafts replies, I approve), task board review, client reports, and social media scheduling. For each block, state which tool and which AI prompt to reuse. Keep it to 5 blocks a day, under 200 words, and note where I must do the human thinking myself.
```

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Applying without a profile, portfolio, or proposal tailored to the job
- Over-promising skills you have not practised (test yourself first)
- Doing client work without a written scope or payment terms
- Mixing clients' data in one inbox or folder
- Chasing many one-off gigs instead of converting one good client to a retainer
- Paying to apply for jobs — legitimate platforms do not charge
- Working without tracking hours, so billing and reports become guesswork
