# AI for Parents: Build an Educational Content Side Business

**Overview** — You already help your child with homework and craft bedtime stories with AI. This course turns that skill into a repeatable side business: worksheets, story-based lessons, and small tutoring content packs that other parents pay for. The one clear win: your first paid worksheet pack or tutoring session delivered this month, using tools you already own.

**Who this is for**

- Parents already helping kids with homework who want to earn from it
- Teachers, tutors, and former teachers looking for a side income
- Parents with a phone and M-Pesa who want a business they can run from home
- Anyone who wants a content business with no stock, no shop, and low start-up cost
- Stay-at-home parents who can dedicate a few hours a week

**What you'll be able to do**

- Turn your homework-help skill into paid tutoring content
- Build a library of AI-assisted worksheets and story-based lessons on a repeatable system
- Market to other parents in your community and online (WhatsApp groups, schools)
- Price and package your offer: per session, subscription, or packs
- Deliver content and collect payment entirely from your phone

## Lesson 1: Your homework-help skill is a product

Parents pay for three things: their child passing exams, less homework stress, and saving their own time. Your product is relief, not worksheets.

Think of what you already know how to do from the Tier 1 course — explaining fractions simply, writing a personalised bedtime story. Now package it:

- A worksheet pack is a product (KES 200-500)
- A one-on-one session is a service (KES 300-800 per hour)
- A weekly subscription is recurring income (KES 500-1,500 per month)

You are not competing with teachers. You are the affordable, convenient helper: a parent in the neighbourhood who understands the CBC syllabus and speaks the child's language. That positioning sells.

## Lesson 2: Build your worksheet library — the repeatable system

The key to a business that does not eat your evenings is a system you repeat weekly. Here is the one this course teaches:

1. Pick one topic from the child's level (Primary 1-6 works best)
2. Ask AI to generate a worksheet with questions, an example, and an answer key
3. Check every answer yourself — you are the quality gate
4. Add your name/brand and a clean layout in Canva
5. Save it to a numbered folder: "Worksheet Pack 01 — Fractions P4"
6. Schedule it for release on your content calendar

Make 5-10 packs before you sell anything. A small library makes you look established and lets you offer "packs" instead of single worksheets. Quality beats quantity: one accurate, well-designed pack is worth more than twenty rushed ones.

## Lesson 3: Story-based lessons kids love and parents buy

Parents do not buy "educational content" — they buy something their child will actually use. Story-based lessons sell because a child who loves the story will finish the learning.

A story-based lesson has three parts:

- A short story (AI drafts it, you personalise with the child's name or the buyer's child)
- A learning task woven into the story (maths hidden in the plot, comprehension questions)
- A takeaway the parent can show ("Today Amani learned multiplication")

This is your differentiator. Any parent can Google a worksheet; few can get a fractions lesson disguised as an adventure about a Kikuyu grandmother's farm or a Mombasa beach trip. Sell the story, deliver the learning.

## Lesson 4: Where your buyers are

Your market is closer than you think:

- WhatsApp parenting groups — ask to join local school and estate parent groups
- Schools — PTAs, class WhatsApp groups, and teachers who know struggling learners
- Church, mosque, and community groups — parents trust familiar faces
- Your own WhatsApp status and broadcast list
- Facebook and TikTok — short clips of your worksheets with a call to action

Rule: never spam a group. Share a free sample, offer genuine help, and let parents ask. "I made a free fractions pack for Primary 4 — anyone want it for their child?" builds trust. The paying offer comes after they see the value.

## Lesson 5: Pricing and packaging that works

Keep it simple and Kenya-real:

- Single worksheet pack: KES 200-500 per pack (a topic with 4-6 worksheets)
- Tutoring session: KES 300-800 per session (30-60 minutes, one child)
- Small group: KES 1,500-3,000 per term per child for weekly group lessons
- Subscription: KES 500-1,500 per month for a new pack each week plus homework help
- Bundle: 5-pack library for KES 1,500 (feels like a deal)

Start with packs and sessions. Add the subscription once you have repeat buyers. Announce prices clearly, accept M-Pesa (till or paybill), and record each sale in a simple notebook or Google Sheet. Consistency beats cleverness: parents re-buy when delivery is reliable.

## Lesson 6: A delivery system that runs on your phone

Your whole business lives in a few apps:

- WhatsApp Business — catalogue, auto-replies, status ads, order messages
- Google Drive — your worksheet library, organised by level and topic
- M-Pesa — payment with a till number and a recorded transaction ID
- Canva — turning AI text into neat, printable worksheets
- Google Sheets — your sales log: date, buyer, product, amount, delivery

A simple flow: parent messages you on WhatsApp, you send the M-Pesa till, they pay, you share the pack PDF from Drive. Keep the flow short — parents lose interest in long ordering steps. Screenshot or log every payment for your records and for the KRA if you grow.

## Lesson 7: Marketing that brings parents back

Paid content is a repeat business. After the first sale, aim to be remembered:

- Post a free worksheet sample on your status every week
- End every pack with "Want pack 2? It's on the farm adventure theme"
- Ask happy parents for a one-line testimonial and share it (with permission)
- Do a school holidays special: revision packs for the next term
- Send a follow-up message 2-3 weeks later: "How is the fractions pack going? Need the next level?"

Referrals are your cheapest marketing. One satisfied parent in a WhatsApp group brings you three more. Make a habit of asking every buyer to mention you to one other parent.

## Lesson 8: Your weekly production rhythm

A side business dies from burnout, not competition. Protect yourself with a small, consistent rhythm:

- Sunday: pick next week's topic, generate the pack, check the answers
- Monday: design and save the pack
- Tuesday: post the free sample on status
- Wednesday-Thursday: take orders and deliver
- Friday: log sales, ask for referrals, plan the following week

Two to three hours a week builds a library in a term. Do not scale until the system feels easy. When it does, raise your pack price, add the subscription, or partner with a school.

## Prompt Library

**What parents struggle with**

**How to use it:** Research your market before creating anything.

```
List the 10 most common learning struggles for Kenyan primary school parents, by class level (Primary 1-6). Include maths, English, Kiswahili, and science. For each, give the parent's likely frustration and a worksheet topic that solves it. Format as a table.
```

**Worksheet topic generator**

**How to use it:** Pick one topic and go.

```
Give me 8 worksheet pack ideas for a [Primary X] child in Kenya, based on the CBC syllabus. Each idea must have a clear title, the exact skill taught, and the age group. I want packs that solve a specific problem (e.g. 'Fractions for P4: Sharing chapati and mandazi').
```

**Maths worksheet with answer key**

**How to use it:** Your core product. Generate, then check every answer.

```
Create a maths worksheet pack for a [Primary X] student in Kenya on [topic, e.g. multiplication]. Include: 10 questions with realistic Kenyan examples (market prices, matatu distances, shambas), 2 word problems, a worked example at the top, and a separate answer key with brief working. Keep language simple and clear.
```

**Reading comprehension worksheet**

**How to use it:** Build reading packs quickly.

```
Write a reading comprehension worksheet for a [Primary X] student. Use a short story about a Kenyan child, followed by 5 questions (2 literal, 2 inference, 1 opinion). Provide a teacher/parent answer key. Reading age must match the class level.
```

**Kiswahili worksheet**

**How to use it:** Cover the language syllabus.

```
Create a Kiswahili worksheet for a [Primary X] student on [topic, e.g. msamiati wa shambani]. Include: a short passage, vocabulary matching, 5 fill-in-the-blank questions, and an answer key. Use correct Kiswahili sanifu and keep it suitable for a child.
```

**Story-based lesson plan**

**How to use it:** Turn any topic into a story lesson.

```
Turn [topic, e.g. fractions] into a story-based lesson for a [Primary X] child. Write a short story set in a Kenyan location with a Kenyan child as the hero, then 3 learning activities woven into the story (one maths, one writing, one thinking), and a one-line takeaway the parent can tell the child. Keep the story under 300 words.
```

**Personalised story pack**

**How to use it:** Add the buyer's child's name — this increases perceived value.

```
Rewrite this story so the main character is named [CHILD'S NAME], lives in [KENYAN TOWN], and learns [SKILL]. Keep the lesson identical, keep the story simple, and end with a short activity the parent can do with the child. Story: [PASTE STORY]
```

**Answer key and marking guide**

**How to use it:** Never deliver a worksheet without answers.

```
Here is my worksheet: [PASTE]. Create a full answer key with brief working for every question, and a simple marking guide (marks per question, total out of 20). Note any question where the answer could vary and how to mark it fairly.
```

**WhatsApp marketing message**

**How to use it:** The message that gets replies, not silence.

```
Write a short WhatsApp message promoting my [WORKSHEET PACK NAME] for [PRIMARY X] on [TOPIC]. It must: start with the parent's problem, offer a free sample, state the price [KES 200-500], mention payment via M-Pesa till, and end with a simple question. Keep it under 80 words, warm and not pushy.
```

**WhatsApp status ad**

**How to use it:** A shareable one-liner with a visual.

```
Write a WhatsApp status ad (2-3 short lines) for a fractions worksheet pack for Primary 4, priced KES 300. Make it grab attention in 2 seconds, mention the free sample, and add a call to action to message me directly. Keep it casual and Kenyan.
```

**School pitch message**

**How to use it:** When approaching a teacher or PTA contact.

```
Write a polite WhatsApp message to a [TEACHER/PTA MEMBER] proposing my worksheet packs as extra practice for [CLASS]. Offer to give the class a free sample pack, explain my packs match the CBC syllabus, and ask if parents would benefit. Keep it respectful, short, and not salesy.
```

**Pricing packages builder**

**How to use it:** Design your offer before you announce it.

```
Help me set up my educational content pricing in Kenya. My products: single packs, a 5-pack bundle, one-on-one sessions, and a monthly subscription. Suggest a simple price for each in KES (packs between 200-500, sessions 300-800), name each package, and write a one-line description a parent would understand. Also suggest a launch offer.
```

**Subscription content calendar**

**How to use it:** Plan a month of packs so delivery never stops.

```
Create a 4-week content calendar for my parent subscription at KES 1,000/month. Each week must deliver: one worksheet pack topic (vary maths, English, Kiswahili, science), one story-based lesson theme, and one WhatsApp status sample. Suggest what to post and on which day of the week.
```

**Sales log template**

**How to use it:** Keep your business records clean.

```
Create a simple sales log table for my educational content business with columns: date, buyer name, product/pack name, price (KES), payment method (M-Pesa/cash), transaction ID, delivery date. Add a row for notes. Explain how I can use Google Sheets on my phone to maintain it.
```

**Referral ask message**

**How to use it:** The message that turns one buyer into three.

```
Write a short thank-you message for a parent who just bought my worksheet pack. It must: thank them warmly, ask how their child found it, and gently ask them to mention my packs to one other parent who might need homework help. Include my WhatsApp number. Keep it under 60 words.
```

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Selling worksheets without checking the answers — one error destroys trust
- Copying other sellers' packs instead of making your own
- Spamming WhatsApp groups with ads instead of offering help first
- Pricing too low to be worth your time (below KES 200 a pack)
- Taking on a subscription before you can deliver weekly
- Using a personal number instead of WhatsApp Business with a catalogue
- Burning out on a huge production schedule instead of a small weekly rhythm
