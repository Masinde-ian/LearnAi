## Lesson 1: Know your options — free vs paid

Before building anything, understand the two worlds of WhatsApp automation.

**Free (WhatsApp Business app):** built-in quick replies, away messages, and catalogs. You can build simple menu flows that answer FAQs and collect orders. No coding, no monthly fees. Limits: one phone, no true AI answers, and limited speed.

**Paid (WhatsApp Business API):** real AI auto-responders with natural language. You need a Business Solution Provider (BSP) such as 360dialog or a local builder. Realistic Kenyan cost: KES 50,000+ for setup and KES 4,000-10,000 per month. Worth it only when your chat volume exceeds what one person can answer.

**The middle path:** a canned quick-reply menu system plus a spreadsheet to track orders. This is where most Kenyan small businesses should start — it costs nothing and solves 80% of the problem.

**Do this:** start with the free route. Design your flows first, prove they work, and upgrade only when demand forces you to.

**Not this:** paying KES 50,000+ for a bot before you know which questions your customers actually ask.

## Lesson 2: Map the conversations your business gets

Automation starts with a list, not with software. For one week, write down every WhatsApp message you receive. Then group them.

Most small businesses find the same clusters:

- FAQs: price, location, opening hours, delivery, "is it available?"
- Orders: "I want X, how do I pay, where do I send my details?"
- Bookings: "Do you have an appointment on Friday?"
- Chit-chat: greetings and thank-yous that need no bot

**Do this:** sort your clusters by volume. The biggest cluster is your first automation.

**Not this:** automating rare questions. If only 3 customers a month ask about refunds, do not build a refund flow yet.

## Lesson 3: Build a menu-based bot with free quick replies

The free WhatsApp Business app lets you create quick replies (saved responses triggered by a short command) and quick replies in chats — buttons customers can tap. Together they form a menu bot.

Design a simple menu:

- Press 1 for prices
- Press 2 for delivery
- Press 3 to place an order
- Press 4 to speak to a human

Each option replies with a saved answer. Customers navigate by tapping. It is basic, it is free, and it works.

**Do this:** put the menu in your greeting message and away message so every new chat starts with a clear choice.

**Not this:** forcing every customer through a menu. If the bot cannot answer, let them reach you quickly — a frustrated customer leaves.

## Lesson 4: Automate FAQs with keyword triggers

Quick replies are triggered by commands you type, but you can also guide customers with keywords. When a customer asks "price?", reply with the price answer. When they ask "delivery?", reply with delivery options.

Train your quick replies around the exact words your customers use. Write each answer with AI, then verify the facts yourself.

**Do this:** keep every FAQ answer under 3 lines with one clear next step. On a phone, short wins.

**Not this:** one long quick reply covering everything. Split answers so the customer gets exactly what they asked for.

## Lesson 5: Automate order taking and M-Pesa payment

The most valuable automation is the one that takes money. Design an order flow that collects what you need and moves to payment fast.

The order flow:

1. Customer says they want to order (or taps "place an order").
2. Bot asks: which product, quantity, and delivery location.
3. Customer sends details; bot confirms the total including delivery.
4. Bot sends your M-Pesa till number and asks for the name used.
5. Bot confirms "waiting for your payment confirmation" and hands off.

You confirm the payment on your side, then deliver. The spreadsheet tracks every order so nothing gets lost.

**Do this:** keep the flow to 4-5 questions maximum. Every extra question kills a sale.

**Not this:** having the bot promise delivery fees or stock levels that you have not updated. The bot must only repeat facts you set.

## Lesson 6: Automate appointment booking

If you run a salon, clinic, studio, or service, bookings repeat the same pattern. Automate it:

1. Customer asks for an appointment (or taps "book").
2. Bot shows your open days and time slots.
3. Customer picks a slot.
4. Bot confirms the booking and adds it to your log.
5. Bot sends a reminder message the day before.

A simple spreadsheet with your slots is enough at this stage. Later, a paid tool can sync bookings to your calendar.

**Do this:** send an automatic reminder the day before the booking — it cuts no-shows dramatically.

**Not this:** offering slots you have not blocked in your real calendar. Double-booked appointments destroy trust fast.

## Lesson 7: Hand off to a human at the right moment

The bot's job is to handle the routine and hand the important stuff to you. Clear handoff rules protect you from angry customers.

Hand off to a human when:

- The customer asks a question not in your flows
- They are unhappy or frustrated
- They ask for a discount, complaint, or refund
- They have paid and need confirmation
- They type "human", "agent", "help", or ask to speak to someone

**Do this:** every flow ends with a real person's phone number or an "I will reply shortly" promise. The bot should never trap a customer.

**Not this:** letting the bot argue with an angry customer. Escalate early — a human saves that relationship.

## Lesson 8: Consent and the Data Protection Act 2019

Kenya's Data Protection Act 2019 requires you to collect and use customer data lawfully. WhatsApp messages contain names, phone numbers, addresses, and payment details — that is personal data.

The practical rules:

- Tell customers why you collect their details (delivery, booking)
- Do not share their numbers with other businesses
- Delete details when a customer asks
- Store customer information only where you can control it

**Do this:** add a short consent line in your first automated message: "By continuing, you agree we use your details for [delivery/booking] only."

**Not this:** selling or sharing your customer list without permission. It is unlawful and destroys trust.

## Prompt Library

Copy, fill in the brackets, and adapt.

**Message audit**

Use before building anything — find your top flows.

```
I run a [business type]. Here are the WhatsApp messages I
received this week: [paste messages]. Group them into clusters,
rank the clusters by volume, and tell me which 3 flows to
automate first.
```

**Menu structure**

Use to design your free quick-reply menu.

```
Design a simple WhatsApp menu for my [business type]. Number
the options (1 prices, 2 delivery, 3 order, 4 human). For each
option, tell me what quick reply to save. Kenyan context.
```

**FAQ quick replies**

Use to write saved answers for repeat questions.

```
Write a short WhatsApp quick reply (under 3 lines) answering
[question, e.g. delivery cost]. My facts: [delivery fee, areas,
timeline]. End with one clear next step. Warm Kenyan tone.
```

**Keyword triggers**

Use to map the words customers actually type.

```
List the words and phrases a Kenyan customer would type to ask
about [topic, e.g. prices or availability]. Suggest the keyword
triggers I should set up in WhatsApp Business quick replies.
```

**Order-taking flow**

Use to automate orders.

```
Write the order-taking flow for my [business type]: the exact
questions the bot asks in order (product, quantity, location,
payment), and the confirmation message. Keep it under 5 steps.
```

**Payment confirmation flow**

Use to move an order to M-Pesa payment smoothly.

```
Write the messages my WhatsApp bot sends to confirm an order
and request payment via M-Pesa till [number]. Include the
amount confirmation and a message asking the customer to send
their payment name. Keep it short and clear.
```

**Appointment booking flow**

Use to automate bookings.

```
Write an appointment booking flow for my [service type]. The
bot should offer [days/times], take a name and phone number,
confirm the slot, and promise a reminder the day before.
```

**Handoff to human**

Use when the bot must pass the customer to you.

```
Write a WhatsApp message my bot sends when it cannot answer:
polite, apologetic, and giving the customer my number. Under
2 lines, warm tone.
```

**After-hours flow**

Use for when you are asleep or offline.

```
Write an after-hours away message that answers common questions
via menu and tells customers I will reply by [time]. Include a
way to leave an order so nothing is lost overnight.
```

**Consent message**

Use to collect consent under the Data Protection Act 2019.

```
Write a short consent line for the first message of my WhatsApp
bot. It should tell customers their details are used only for
[delivery/booking] and that they can ask me to delete them.
Keep it under 2 lines and friendly.
```

**Fallback message**

Use when the customer types something the bot does not understand.

```
Write a fallback message for when a customer types something my
bot does not understand. Ask them to rephrase or choose a menu
number, and remind them they can ask for a human. Short and kind.
```

**Bot tone and voice**

Use to make the bot sound like your business.

```
Rewrite these bot messages in the voice of a friendly Kenyan
small business owner: short sentences, a little warmth, no
robotic phrases. Keep all facts (prices, till number) identical:
[paste messages]
```

**Escalation rules**

Use to decide when the bot hands over.

```
Write me 8 clear rules for when my WhatsApp bot must hand the
customer to a human (complaints, refunds, unclear requests,
paid orders, etc.). Make them easy to apply day to day.
```

**Upgrade requirements doc**

Use when you are ready to consider the paid WhatsApp API.

```
I am considering a paid WhatsApp chatbot via a BSP like
360dialog. Write a short requirements list: what questions my
bot must answer, what volume of chats justifies KES 4,000-10,000
a month, and what to ask the provider before paying KES 50,000+
for setup.
```

## Action plan

1. Audit your messages for one week and cluster them.
2. Pick your top 3 flows (FAQs, orders, or bookings).
3. Build the menu structure with free quick replies.
4. Write the quick replies with AI and verify every fact.
5. Add your after-hours, fallback, and consent messages.
6. Set your handoff rules and test the whole flow with a friend.
7. Track results for 2 weeks, then decide if you need the paid API.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Paying for the WhatsApp API before you need it
- Building a bot before knowing which messages you actually get
- Making flows too long — customers abandon long menus
- Letting the bot invent prices, stock, or promises
- Trapping customers with no way to reach a human
- Ignoring data protection and sharing customer details
