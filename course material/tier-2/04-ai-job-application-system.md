# Land the Job: AI-Powered Job Application System

## Overview

Your CV gets you past the first screen; a system gets you the job. This course builds a repeatable application pipeline: a master CV you tailor per role in minutes, a LinkedIn profile that gets found, mock interviews with AI using the STAR method, and professional follow-ups that keep you in the running. Apply fewer, better applications and land more interviews.

## Who this is for

- Job seekers sending the same CV to every opening
- Graduates entering the Kenyan market for the first time
- Professionals switching roles or industries
- Anyone tired of applying and hearing nothing back

## What you'll be able to do

- Build a master CV and tailor it to any role in minutes
- Optimise your LinkedIn profile so recruiters find you
- Prepare for interviews with AI mock Q&A and STAR stories
- Follow up professionally after applying and interviewing
- Track every application and run your job search like a system

## Lesson 1: The job application pipeline

Treat job hunting as a pipeline, not a lottery. The stages are: prepare your master materials, find matched roles, tailor your application, prepare for the interview, and follow up. Each stage has a repeatable step you run in a fixed order.

Apply to fewer roles but apply well. Ten tailored applications beat fifty copy-paste ones, because tailored applications reach the interview stage far more often.

Track every application: the role, the platform, the date, and the stage. A simple spreadsheet — or an AI-organised list — tells you what to do next instead of guessing. This tracking is what turns applying into a system.

## Lesson 2: Build your master CV

The master CV is your full record: every job, project, skill, and result you have ever produced, in rough notes. It is longer than one page and nothing is cut yet. Its only job is to hold all your material.

Polish it with AI once: strong professional summary, result-focused bullets with numbers, clean sections, and clear skills. This is your reference version — the one you never delete and always return to.

Big Kenyan employers — banks, NGOs, telcos — screen with ATS software, so keep the master CV machine-readable: plain headings, single column, no tables or images. The ATS reads the tailored version too, so this formatting matters at every stage.

## Lesson 3: Tailor per role in minutes

Never send the master CV as-is. Tailoring takes the job ad's keywords and matching your strongest material to them. The method: paste the ad and your master CV into AI, ask for the keyword match, then pull the tailored summary, bullets, and skills into a fresh one-page CV.

If the ad says "customer service", write "customer service" — not "client care". ATS matches exact words. Only include skills you genuinely have; a claim you cannot defend fails the interview.

Cover letters follow the same pattern: name the role, the company, and your specific fit, under 250 words. One tailored letter per serious application, done in minutes with AI.

## Lesson 4: Optimise your LinkedIn profile

Recruiters search LinkedIn by keyword, so your profile must contain the words they type. The headline formula is simple: "Role | Skill | Location" — for example "Finance Analyst | IFRS | CPA Kenya | Nairobi".

Let AI draft each section: a headline from your skills, an About section in first person that ends with what you are looking for, and experience bullets that match your master CV. Keep your photo professional and your contact details current.

Use your headline for the role you want, not the one you have. Recruiters search for what they are hiring, and your profile should be findable for it.

## Lesson 5: Interview prep with AI mock Q&A

Interviews are skills you can practise. Ask AI to be the interviewer: paste the job ad and your CV, and have it ask the questions that role actually attracts. Then answer out loud, not just in your head.

Behavioural questions ("tell me about a time...") are best answered with the STAR method: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Build five STAR stories from your real experience — a tough deadline, a conflict, a mistake, a success, a time you led — and practise telling them in under two minutes each.

Practise with AI until your stories feel natural, and use your phone to record yourself. Reviewing your own answers is uncomfortable and effective.

## Lesson 6: Professional follow-ups

Most applicants apply and go silent. A polite follow-up keeps you visible. One week after applying, send a short message referencing the role and restating your interest. After an interview, send a thank-you within 24 hours that mentions one thing you discussed.

The follow-up after an interview is underrated: it reminds the panel who you are and shows professionalism. Keep every follow-up short, specific, and easy to reply to.

If you hear nothing after two follow-ups, move on gracefully. Persistence that becomes nagging hurts you; know when to close the file.

## Lesson 7: Interview formats — phone, panel, and virtual

Interviews in Kenya come in different shapes, and each needs different practice. A phone screen is short and about filtering: answer in under two minutes, keep your voice calm, and have your CV in front of you.

Panel interviews — common at banks, NGOs, and telcos — test composure under multiple eyes. Practise with AI acting as three panellists asking different questions in one session. Answer the question asked, look at whoever asked, and keep each answer tight.

Virtual interviews on Zoom or Teams are now standard for many roles. Test your camera, microphone, and data before the call, look at the camera not the screen, and keep your background tidy. Practise one full mock interview on video with AI so the format feels familiar on the day.

## Lesson 8: Weekly job-search rhythm

A system needs a rhythm. Run a weekly block: set targets for the week, search matched roles, tailor your top five, do one AI mock interview, and send your follow-ups. Consistent weekly motion beats irregular bursts.

Set realistic numbers: five quality applications, two follow-ups, one mock interview. More than that usually means quality drops.

Review at month end: which roles got interviews, which applications went nowhere, and what to change. Job hunting is feedback you can act on, and AI makes every step faster.

## Prompt Library

Copy, fill the brackets, and run per application.

**Master CV build**

```
You are a CV writer for the Kenyan market. Turn my rough notes into
a polished master CV: professional summary, experience bullets with
numbers, education, and skills. Use action verbs: [paste notes]
```

How to use: run once to create your reference CV.

**Role-tailored CV**

```
Tailor my master CV for this job. Job ad: [paste ad]. Keep the
summary, top 5 bullets, and skills that match the ad's keywords.
Output a one-page CV. No invented experience: [master CV]
```

How to use: run for every serious application, paste the fresh version into your CV.

**Keyword check**

```
Here is the job ad and my tailored CV. List the ad's keywords that
are missing from my CV, then suggest how to add them honestly:
[ad] [CV]
```

How to use: run after tailoring to catch ATS misses before submitting.

**Tailored cover letter**

```
Write a cover letter for [role] at [company] advertised on
[platform]. My top skills: [skills]. One achievement: [achievement].
Professional Kenyan tone, under 250 words, names the company and role.
```

How to use: one fresh letter per application, never reuse verbatim.

**LinkedIn headline**

```
Create 5 LinkedIn headlines for me using the formula
"Role | Skill | Location". My target role: [role]. My top skills:
[skills]. My location: [town/city]. Pick the strongest as number 1.
```

How to use: set your headline from the best option.

**LinkedIn About**

```
Write a LinkedIn About section in first person for a [role] based
on my master CV. 4-5 lines, confident, ends with what I am looking
for. No cliches like "hard-working": [master CV]
```

How to use: paste into your profile, then polish by hand.

**Company research brief**

```
Research this company and give me a one-page brief for an interview:
what they do, recent news or projects, their clients, and what
questions I could ask them. Company: [name].
```

How to use: run before any interview so you walk in informed.

**STAR story builder**

```
Turn this experience into a STAR story: Situation, Task, Action,
Result. Keep it under 2 minutes when spoken, end with a number or
clear outcome: [paste experience]
```

How to use: build your five reusable interview stories.

**Behavioural question bank**

```
Based on this job ad, list the 8 behavioural questions most likely
to be asked, and note which of my STAR stories fits each one:
[ad] [STAR stories]
```

How to use: map your stories to likely questions before the interview.

**Mock interviewer**

```
You are a senior interviewer in Kenya hiring for [role]. Ask me
questions one at a time, wait for my answer, then score it out of 10
with one improvement tip. Start with a behaviour question: [paste ad]
```

How to use: practise out loud until you score 8+ consistently.

**Post-application follow-up**

```
Write a short follow-up message (under 100 words) to [hiring contact]
about my application for [role] at [company], submitted [date].
Polite, brief, one line on why I fit. No pressure.
```

How to use: send one week after applying.

**Post-interview thank-you**

```
Write a thank-you message after my interview for [role] at [company].
Mention one specific thing we discussed: [topic]. Reaffirm my interest
in one line. Under 80 words.
```

How to use: send within 24 hours of the interview.

**Application tracker**

```
Here are my applications this week: [role | company | date | stage].
Organise them into a table and tell me the next action for each one.
Add a column for follow-up dates.
```

How to use: run weekly to keep your pipeline moving.

**Offer response**

```
I received an offer for [role] at [company] at KES [amount]. Write
a polite response that asks for [a small change/confirmation of start
date] without sounding ungrateful.
```

How to use: adapt when negotiating or confirming an offer.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Sending the same CV to every job with no tailoring
- Lying about skills ATS picks up but you cannot defend
- Using a headline that describes your old job, not your target
- Answering interview questions from memory instead of practising
- Going silent after applying or interviewing
- Applying to fifty roles and preparing for none
