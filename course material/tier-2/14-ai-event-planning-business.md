# AI Event Planning Business: From Idea to Paid Bookings

**Overview**

The Tier 1 course helped you plan one event. This course builds the business. You'll turn your event-planning skill into a bookable service, build a portfolio and pricing packages for weddings, birthdays, and corporate events, manage vendors and client communication with AI support, and market your business locally on WhatsApp and Instagram. Planning events was the skill — getting paid for it is the business.

**Who this is for**

- People who plan events for friends and family and want to get paid for it
- Aspiring event planners who don't know how to price or package their services
- Existing planners who want to stop underselling and start marketing properly
- Anyone wanting a side hustle that uses their organisation and people skills

**What you'll be able to do**

- Turn your event-planning skill into a clear, bookable service
- Build a portfolio and pricing packages for different event types
- Manage vendors and client communication with AI support
- Market your event planning business locally on WhatsApp and Instagram

## Lesson 1: From skill to service

Planning your cousin's wedding for free is a favour. Planning it for a fee is a business. The difference is clarity: what you offer, what it costs, and who it's for.

**Define your service by asking four questions:**

- **What** do you do — full planning, coordination on the day, or decoration direction?
- **For whom** — weddings, birthdays, corporate events, or a niche like church weddings?
- **Where** — your town and the counties you can travel to
- **How** — in person, or partially online with vendor calls

Write this as one clear sentence: "I coordinate weddings and milestone birthdays in [town], from planning to the last dance." That sentence becomes your WhatsApp about, your Instagram bio, and your pitch.

**Do this:** start with one event type and one town. Depth beats breadth when you're building a name.

**Not this:** offering "anything event-related" — vague offers attract cheap enquiries and no trust.

## Lesson 2: Package your services and set prices

People don't buy hours; they buy outcomes. Packages make that easy to understand.

**Three standard packages per event type:**

- **Essential** — planning checklist, vendor shortlist, and day-of timeline (light touch)
- **Standard** — planning calls, vendor booking support, client communication, day-of coordination
- **Premium** — everything, plus design direction, full day-of management, and post-event follow-up

Price from what the market pays, not from your expenses. Research what planners charge in your area — ask around, check event pages, join planning groups. For a starting point in Kenya, entry packages often range from a few thousand shillings up to hundreds of thousands for full premium weddings — price according to your market, scope, and proof.

**The deposit rule:** take a deposit (commonly 30–50%) before starting work, with the balance on a clear schedule. M-Pesa paybill or till makes this easy.

**Do this:** let AI help you draft the package descriptions and a simple price table, then confirm with real local research.

**Not this:** charging the same for a 20-guest birthday as for a 300-guest wedding. Scope changes price.

## Lesson 3: Build a portfolio that sells

Nobody books an event planner from promises. They book from proof.

**Build a portfolio without clients first:**

- **Plan a sample event** — use AI to plan a detailed hypothetical wedding, then present the plan as a sample
- **Volunteer for two free/low-cost events** — a friend's small event, a church event — in exchange for photos and a written testimonial
- **Document everything** — mood boards, timelines, vendor lists, budgets, and photos

**What your portfolio needs:** 3–5 event plans, real photos (even from your own styled table), client quotes, and your packages with prices.

**Do this:** turn every sample plan into a Canva PDF you can share on WhatsApp in one tap.

**Not this:** using other people's event photos without permission, or faking testimonials. One exposed lie kills the business.

## Lesson 4: Vendor management with AI

Vendors are your partners — caterers, decorators, MCs, photographers, makeup artists, venues. Managing them is most of the work.

**Build a vendor system:**

- **A vendor sheet** — Google Sheets with name, service, location, price range, reliability notes, and past event comments
- **Templates** — enquiry messages, price-comparison questions, booking confirmations, and reminder messages
- **Vendor briefs** — each vendor gets a one-page brief: date, venue, schedule, what they're responsible for, who they report to

AI writes all of it. Paste a venue's response and ask AI to draft the follow-up; paste a vendor's quote and ask AI to compare it against another.

**Do this:** keep a shortlist of 2–3 backup vendors per service. Events fail when one vendor lets you down and you have no plan B.

**Not this:** managing vendors from memory and scattered WhatsApp chats. One sheet saves you from a hundred confused calls.

## Lesson 5: Client communication that builds trust

Your clients are nervous and spending real money. Clear, calm communication is your product.

**The communication rhythm:**

- **Onboarding** — a structured intake call or questionnaire to capture the vision and budget
- **Weekly updates** — one short message with what's done and what's next
- **Vendor confirmations** — you confirm bookings, the client approves
- **7 days before** — a clear pre-event checklist sent to the client
- **Day of** — you run the day, client relaxes
- **After** — thank-you message, testimonial request, and a referral ask

AI drafts the templates and personalizes the details. You stay the human face.

**Do this:** always end your weekly update with one clear question — "confirm these 3 choices by Thursday" — so decisions don't stall.

**Not this:** leaving clients in silence for weeks. Silence reads as unprofessional and panic-inducing.

## Lesson 6: Day-of coordination

The event itself is where you earn the repeat booking. Structure it:

- **One master timeline** — arrivals, ceremony, meals, speeches, entertainment, send-off
- **The vendor run-sheet** — each vendor's time slot, location, and contact
- **Your phone sheet** — every key contact saved and labelled
- **Issue protocol** — who handles what when something goes wrong

AI builds the master timeline from your plan. Keep a simple printed or phone version with you at all times.

**Do this:** arrive 90 minutes before the first vendor and walk the venue with the timeline.

**Not this:** improvising on the day. A day-of emergency is normal; an unprepared coordinator is not.

## Lesson 7: Market your business locally

Your market is local, and local runs on WhatsApp and word of mouth.

**The local marketing loop:**

- **WhatsApp Business** — catalog with packages and prices, status updates of your events, saved replies for common questions
- **Instagram** — before/after venue shots, mood boards, vendor credits, client stories
- **Referrals** — ask every happy client for one introduction; offer a small referral reward
- **Local collaborations** — partner with one caterer, one decorator, and one MC; they send you clients and you send them work

AI writes your captions, catalogue descriptions, and follow-up messages. You provide the real photos and the genuine relationships.

**Do this:** post one "event of the week" on WhatsApp status every week — proof of work is your best ad.

**Not this:** waiting for clients to find you. Past clients, vendors, and one collaborating MC are your cheapest marketing channel.

## Prompt Library

Copy, fill in the blanks, and adapt.

**Define my service**

Use to clarify your offer.

```
I plan events in [town] and I'm good at [skills: coordination, decor, budgeting, MC booking]. Ask me the questions you need to define one clear bookable service (event types, location, what's included, what's not), then write my service in one sentence and as a short WhatsApp about.
```

**Build my packages**

Use to create pricing packages.

```
I plan [weddings/birthdays/corporate events]. Build me 3 packages — Essential, Standard, Premium. For each: what's included, how many planning calls, what the client handles, and what the planner handles. Suggest a pricing approach for [town] using ranges (deposit, balance schedule) and note what I should research locally before fixing prices.
```

**Sample event plan for my portfolio**

Use to build portfolio proof.

```
Create a detailed plan for a sample [wedding/birthday/corporate] event for [X] guests in [town] with a budget of KES [X]. Include: venue options, vendor list with estimated costs, day-of timeline hour by hour, and a mood/theme direction. This will be a portfolio sample, so make it complete and impressive.
```

**Portfolio description**

Use for your Instagram bio and catalogue.

```
Write a portfolio introduction for an event planner in [town]. 3 sentences: who I serve, what I handle, why clients trust me. Also write an Instagram bio under 150 characters and a WhatsApp Business catalogue description.
```

**Vendor enquiry message**

Use to contact new vendors.

```
Write a professional enquiry message to a [caterer/venue/other] about a [wedding/birthday] on [date] for [X] guests in [town]. Ask for availability, a quote, what's included, and payment terms. Keep it short and give space for their answers. I'll fill in my name and business name.
```

**Compare vendor quotes**

Use when two quotes come back.

```
Compare these two quotes for [service]. Quote A: [paste]. Quote B: [paste]. Tell me what each includes and excludes, which is better value for a [X]-guest [event] in [town], and the 3 questions I should ask each vendor before deciding.
```

**Vendor booking confirmation**

Use once a vendor is chosen.

```
Write a booking confirmation message to [vendor] for [event] on [date] at [venue]. Confirm: service, price, deposit of KES [X], balance due [date], arrival time [X], and their main contact. Also write a follow-up reminder message to send 7 days before the event.
```

**Vendor run-sheet**

Use for the day of.

```
Create a day-of run-sheet for a [event] at [venue] on [date]. List each vendor (caterer, decorator, MC, photographer, others) with arrival time, setup time, what they're responsible for, and who to contact if something is delayed. Format it so I can read it on my phone.
```

**Client intake questionnaire**

Use at the start of every booking.

```
Write a client intake questionnaire for a [wedding/birthday/corporate] event. Cover: date and location, budget range, guest count, vision and theme, must-haves, priorities (food, decor, entertainment, photos), and decision deadlines. Keep it simple so I can send it as a WhatsApp message and the client can answer quickly.
```

**Weekly client update**

Use every week before the event.

```
Write a weekly update message to a client for their [event] on [date]. Include: what's confirmed, what's still open (with a clear decision deadline), and one question they must answer this week. Keep it warm, calm, and under 150 words.
```

**Pre-event checklist for the client**

Use 7 days before.

```
Write a pre-event checklist message for my client with [X] days to go. Cover: guest list final, payments to vendors, outfit and key people, logistics on the day, and what they should NOT worry about because I'm handling it. Reassuring tone, under 200 words.
```

**Marketing post for Instagram**

Use weekly with event photos.

```
Write an Instagram caption for this event photo: [describe photo]. Mention the event type, venue, and that I handled [planning/coordination]. Add 5 relevant hashtags for Kenyan event planning. Keep it genuine, not salesy. I'll add the location.
```

**WhatsApp status event announcement**

Use weekly as proof of work.

```
Write a WhatsApp status update (under 50 words) announcing a [wedding/birthday] I coordinated in [town]. Include a thank-you to the couple/family, credit one vendor, and a soft invite for anyone planning an event to message me. Warm and short.
```

**Testimonial request**

Use after every successful event.

```
Write a message asking a happy client for a short testimonial after their [event]. Ask for 2–3 sentences on what they liked and whether they'd recommend me. Also write a version asking if they know anyone planning an event soon.
```

**Handling price objections**

Use when a client finds prices high.

```
A client said my [package] at KES [X] is too expensive for their budget of KES [Y]. Write a reply that: stays warm, explains what's included that protects their day, offers my Essential package or a reduced-scope option, and leaves the decision with them without pressuring. Under 120 words.
```

6. Post one event announcement on WhatsApp status and Instagram.
7. Ask one past organiser or vendor to refer you to someone.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Charging by hour instead of by package and scope
- Doing free work forever because you're afraid to charge
- Building a portfolio from borrowed photos or fake testimonials
- Managing vendors from memory instead of a sheet
- Going quiet between client updates
- Pricing without checking what your local market actually pays
