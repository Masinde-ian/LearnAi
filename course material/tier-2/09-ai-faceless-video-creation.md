## Lesson 1: Pick a niche and format that can actually grow

Your niche decides everything: the ideas you get, the audience you attract, and the money you can make. Pick a niche that is (a) interesting enough to keep making videos for months, and (b) monetizable.

Niches with proven audiences and decent RPM (revenue per 1,000 views) include finance, history, true crime, and education. Kenyan versions work too: personal finance in shillings, Kenyan history, campus-life tips, career skills.

Then pick a format you can sustain. Faceless formats include: narrated storytelling over stock footage, listicles ("5 money mistakes Kenyans make"), educational explainers, and top-10 compilations.

**Do this:** test one niche with 10 videos before judging it. Page growth is a months game, not a days game.

**Not this:** jumping niches every week because one video underperformed. Consistency beats luck.

## Lesson 2: Script the full video from one idea

A script is a plan for the whole video. One idea becomes a hook, a body, and a call to action.

- Hook (first 3-5 seconds): one line that stops the scroll
- Body: one main point, told with examples and tension
- Call to action: "follow for part 2" or "share this with someone who needs it"

For long-form (YouTube), aim for 3-8 minutes. For Shorts and TikTok, keep it under 60 seconds.

**Do this:** write the hook first. If the first line does not make you curious, rewrite it until it does — nothing else matters if the viewer leaves.

**Not this:** one video covering three topics. One video, one idea, one clear takeaway.

## Lesson 3: Generate the AI voiceover correctly

ElevenLabs' free tier gives you realistic voices without recording anything. Paste your script, pick a voice, download the audio. CapCut's text-to-speech is a quick free alternative.

Two rules matter here.

First, pick a voice that matches the content: calm and measured for finance and history, energetic for entertainment.

Second, disclose AI voices on YouTube. When you upload a video with an AI or synthetic voice, tick the "altered/synthetic content" box in YouTube's upload flow. Failing to do so risks strikes. This is not optional in 2026.

**Do this:** listen to the generated audio once before exporting. Cut pauses, repeated words, and mispronounced place names.

**Not this:** running AI voiceover with no script. Rambling audio loses viewers in seconds.

## Lesson 4: Source copyright-safe stock footage

Stock footage is the visual layer of faceless videos. Use copyright-safe sources so your channel never dies to copyright claims.

Free and safe options: Pexels, Pixabay, and Coverr stock libraries. These offer clips you can use in monetized videos under their licenses. Paid options like Storyblocks or Envato Elements give more premium footage once revenue supports it.

**Do this:** match each scene to your script with a visual plan first. A 60-second video can use 5-8 quick cuts, not one long clip.

**Not this:** ripping clips from other people's videos or movies. Copyright strikes can kill your channel before it grows.

## Lesson 5: Assemble in CapCut — voice, footage, captions, music

Now bring it together. The assembly order is always the same:

1. Add your AI voiceover track.
2. Lay footage scenes on top, cut to match the narration.
3. Add auto-captions and fix spelling — most viewers watch muted.
4. Add a subtle background music track (CapCut library).
5. Export vertical (9:16) for Shorts/TikTok or horizontal (16:9) for YouTube long-form.

**Do this:** keep captions short, centered, and readable on a small phone. Test your export on a phone before publishing.

**Not this:** exporting 16:9 for TikTok. Vertical or nothing on those platforms.

## Lesson 6: Grow the page step by step

Growth is a system, not luck. The step-by-step method:

1. Post on a fixed schedule — 3-5 Shorts a week or 1-2 long-form videos a week.
2. Batch: script 5 videos on Sunday, produce 5 during the week.
3. Study your analytics weekly: keep what gets views, cut what does not.
4. Reply to comments — engagement feeds the algorithm.
5. Use strong titles and thumbnails for long-form.

**Do this:** track one number only at the start: average view duration. If people leave early, your hook is the problem.

**Not this:** posting randomly "whenever inspiration hits." A schedule you can sustain beats bursts of energy.

## Lesson 7: Monetization — the real paths

Monetization is the goal, but the requirements are specific. Know them before you invest months.

**YouTube monetization (2026):** you need 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 watch hours in 12 months, or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days. Fan funding opens earlier at 500 subscribers — memberships and tips you can enable sooner.

**Other paths that do not wait for AdSense:**

- Affiliate marketing: recommend products (books, apps, courses) and earn commission
- Brand deals and sponsorships: brands pay for mentions once you have an audience
- Digital products: sell your own PDF guides, templates, or courses to your audience
- Memberships: subscribers pay monthly for extra content

**Do this:** plan one monetization path besides AdSense from the start, so you are not dependent on one income.

**Not this:** believing views equal money automatically. Views are the start — how you convert them decides your income.

## Prompt Library

Copy, fill in the brackets, and adapt.

**Niche picker**

Use before starting your page.

```
I am starting a faceless video page in Kenya. I can make
videos about [interests]. Suggest 5 niches that are interesting,
have proven audiences, and can be monetized. Rank by realism
for a beginner with a phone.
```

**Video ideas from your niche**

Use to get a backlog of video topics.

```
Give me 10 video ideas for a faceless page about [niche].
Mix formats: listicles, storytelling, and educational. Each
idea should be one sentence and aimed at a Kenyan audience.
```

**Long-form script**

Use for YouTube videos.

```
Write a full script for a faceless YouTube video on [topic].
Structure: hook, story or explanation, and call to action.
Target [X] minutes (about [Y] words). Conversational, written
for an AI voiceover. No emojis.
```

**Short from a long video**

Use to repurpose long content into Shorts.

```
Cut this script into a 45-second Shorts script with its own
hook, body, and call to action: [paste script]
```

**Hook variations**

Use to test opening lines.

```
Give me 8 different hooks for a video about [topic]. Each hook
under 12 words, written to make a Kenyan viewer stop scrolling.
```

**Scene and visual breakdown**

Use to plan your stock footage.

```
I am making a faceless video about [topic]. Based on this
script, suggest 8 stock footage scenes in order that match the
narration. Describe what each clip should show: [paste script]
```

**Voice style choice**

Use before generating the voiceover.

```
I will paste a script for an AI voiceover on [topic]. Suggest
the best voice style, gender, and pace, and why. Then I will
paste the script.
```

**On-screen caption text**

Use to add punchy captions.

```
Give me short on-screen caption text for each part of this
script. Keep each caption under 8 words, punchy, and readable
on a phone: [paste script]
```

**Title and thumbnail text**

Use for YouTube long-form.

```
Give me 5 title options and 3 thumbnail text ideas for this
video about [topic]. Titles should be specific and clickable,
not clickbait. No emojis.
```

**Weekly content calendar**

Use to plan a month of posts.

```
Build me a 4-week content calendar for a faceless page about
[niche]. Posting [X] times a week. Suggest a topic and format
for each day, with at least 2 formats rotating.
```

**YouTube description and tags**

Use when uploading.

```
Write a YouTube description and tag list for a video about
[topic]. Keep the description under 150 words, include a call
to action, and suggest 10 tags a Kenyan viewer might search.
```

**AI voice disclosure note**

Use when uploading AI-voiced videos.

```
Write a short note for my video description disclosing that the
voice is AI-generated/synthetic, as YouTube requires. Under 2
sentences, natural and not alarming.
```

**Affiliate call to action**

Use when recommending a product.

```
Write a natural call to action for a video about [topic] where
I recommend [product]. Mention it as an option, not a hard sell,
and tell viewers the link is in the description.
```

**Monetization plan**

Use to plan income beyond AdSense.

```
I have a faceless channel about [niche] with [X] subscribers.
Suggest 4 realistic monetization paths for my niche: affiliate
products, digital products, sponsorships, or memberships. Give
one concrete first step for each.
```

**Analytics review**

Use every week to improve.

```
Here are my video stats: [views, watch time, engagement per
video]. Tell me which videos to double down on, which format
is working, and one thing to test next week.
```
