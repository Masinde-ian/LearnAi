# AI for Translators & Transcribers: Build a Service Business

**Overview** — You learned to translate naturally with AI. This course turns translation and transcription into a real service business: a repeatable AI-assisted workflow, clear per-word and per-minute pricing, and a plan for finding clients in NGOs, media, and local businesses. The one clear win: your first paying translation or transcription job delivered this month.

**Who this is for**

- People who speak Kiswahili plus a local language and English fluently
- Freelancers who want steady, deliverable work instead of random gigs
- Former teachers, secretaries, and journalists with language skills
- Anyone who completed the Tier 1 translation course and wants clients
- People with a phone, M-Pesa, and a willingness to check AI's work

**What you'll be able to do**

- Offer translation and transcription as a paid freelance service
- Use AI (Whisper for transcription, ChatGPT/DeepL for translation) to work faster
- Keep accuracy and nuance while producing work quickly
- Find clients on freelance platforms and locally (NGOs, media, businesses)
- Price per word and per audio minute, and manage turnaround expectations

## Lesson 1: Two services, one skill

Translation and transcription are different products:

- **Translation**: converting written or spoken text from one language to another (e.g. English to Kiswahili). Sold per word or per hour.
- **Transcription**: turning audio or video into written text in the same language (e.g. a recorded meeting into a document). Sold per audio minute.

You can sell both, but start with the one you are strongest in. Most new freelancers start with transcription (it needs less language-pair skill) and add translation as they build clients. Both are real demand: NGOs, media houses, courts, and businesses need transcriptions and translations every week.

## Lesson 2: The AI-assisted workflow

Speed is your business model. The workflow for both services is the same pattern: AI does the heavy lift, you do the quality pass.

**Transcription:**

1. Use **Whisper** (free, runs online or offline) to transcribe the audio
2. Check and correct names, numbers, and local terms Whisper got wrong
3. Format it: paragraphs, speaker labels, timestamps if the client wants them
4. Deliver the clean document

**Translation:**

1. Use ChatGPT or DeepL for a natural first draft
2. Fix anything that sounds robotic with a second prompt
3. Check names, numbers, dates, and M-Pesa/paybill details are unchanged
4. Deliver with your glossary terms applied consistently

The rule: AI drafts, you decide. If you skip the human pass, you will be the freelancer who delivers errors and never gets a second job.

## Lesson 3: Accuracy and nuance — the human edge

AI translation can be very good, but it still fails on tone, idioms, and context. Your job is to catch those. Build these habits:

- Read your output aloud — if it sounds like nobody would say it, fix it
- Keep a glossary of your client's terms so the same word is translated the same way every time
- Respect register: formal Kiswahili sanifu for official work, casual language only when the client wants it
- Never change names, numbers, dates, or payment details — numbers do not translate
- For legal and medical content, translate as a draft and state clearly that a qualified professional must review it

Your reputation rests on nuance: "the customer said the payment is coming this week" is not the same as "the customer guaranteed payment this week." Capture meaning, not words.

## Lesson 4: Pricing that makes sense

Pricing has to be simple enough to quote in one message.

**Translation — per word or per line:**

- International rates are often $0.05-0.15 per word; Kenyan rates are lower
- A practical Kenya start: KES 1-5 per word depending on the language pair and difficulty
- For short jobs, set a minimum (e.g. KES 1,000 minimum fee) so small jobs still pay

**Transcription — per audio minute:**

- International transcription rates run roughly $0.50-1.00 per audio minute; Kenya rates are lower
- A practical Kenya start: KES 20-80 per audio minute, depending on audio quality and turnaround
- Charge more for: poor audio, multiple speakers, rush delivery

Always quote before you start, and state what the price covers (one pass, clean formatting, one revision). A clear quote prevents arguments later.

## Lesson 5: Finding clients

Your clients fall into four groups:

- **Freelance platforms**: Upwork and Fiverr list translation and transcription jobs daily
- **NGOs and community organisations**: they translate reports, brochures, and meeting notes, and transcribe interviews and focus groups
- **Media**: radio, TV, and digital outlets need transcriptions and subtitles; local stations need English-Kiswahili work
- **Local businesses**: shops and service firms need product descriptions, flyers, and customer messages in local languages

Approach locally first: WhatsApp status, one cold message to a small NGO or media house, one sample delivered free. A good free sample for one organisation becomes paid referrals for three more. On platforms, complete a few small jobs to build reviews before chasing larger ones.

## Lesson 6: Managing turnaround expectations

Clients will push for impossible deadlines. Know your real pace and protect it:

- Transcription of one audio hour takes roughly 3-5 hours of work (AI-assisted, faster)
- Translation of a one-page document takes 30-60 minutes plus your quality pass
- Never promise faster than you can deliver — missed deadlines kill reputations
- State your turnaround in the quote: "24 hours for one audio hour, 48 hours for complex audio"

Set a simple rule: quote a deadline, deliver early or on time, and tell the client immediately if anything changes. Reliability is the thing that makes clients re-order.

## Lesson 7: Quality checks before delivery

Build a standard gate you pass every deliverable through:

1. Back-translate translation to check meaning; re-listen to tricky transcription spots
2. Check names, numbers, dates, and payment details against the source
3. Run a spelling and grammar pass in the target language
4. Apply the glossary terms consistently
5. Format cleanly — paragraphs, speaker labels, timestamps where requested
6. Confirm the file opens and the right version is attached

A 10-minute check on every job is cheaper than one angry client. Add a note on legal/medical content stating it needs professional review — this protects both you and the client.

## Lesson 8: Running the business from your phone

The whole business fits on a phone:

- **Order flow**: client message, you quote, client pays M-Pesa till, you deliver via WhatsApp
- **Records**: a Google Sheet log with date, client, job type, words or minutes, price, and transaction ID
- **Files**: keep source and deliverable in Google Drive, one folder per client
- **Contracts**: a simple one-page service note stating scope, price, deadline, and that legal/medical content needs professional review
- **Growth**: ask every client for a one-line testimonial and a referral

Charge a minimum, collect payment up front for new clients (or half), and deliver on the agreed date. This small, consistent system is what turns a skill into a business.

## Prompt Library

**Natural translation draft**

**How to use it:** Your default translation first step.

```
You are a native [KISWAHILI/ENGLISH] translator. Translate this text into natural [LANGUAGE] for a Kenyan audience: [PASTE TEXT]. Do not translate word-for-word. Use short sentences, keep names, numbers, dates and M-Pesa details unchanged, and match a [FORMAL/CASUAL] tone. After the translation, list any terms you were unsure about.
```

**Fix a robotic translation**

**How to use it:** When the first draft sounds stiff.

```
This translation sounds robotic and unnatural: [PASTE]. Rewrite it so a Kenyan would naturally say or write it. Keep the meaning identical, shorten sentences, and remove phrases that look like direct word-for-word translation from English. Then explain the top 3 changes you made so I can learn.
```

**Register control**

**How to use it:** Formal vs casual for different clients.

```
Translate this text into Kiswahili in two versions: (1) formal Kiswahili sanifu for an official report, and (2) casual everyday Kiswahili for a WhatsApp broadcast. Keep the meaning identical and label both versions. Do not change names, numbers, or dates. Text: [PASTE]
```

**Client glossary**

**How to use it:** Consistency across all their documents.

```
Create a translation glossary for a [TYPE OF CLIENT, e.g. NGO] in Kenya. For each English term I list, give the Kiswahili equivalent, a note on when to use it, and any term to avoid. Terms: [LIST, e.g. grant, beneficiary, accountability, stakeholder, community]. Present as a simple table.
```

**Whisper transcription setup**

**How to use it:** Transcribe audio correctly the first time.

```
Explain step by step how to transcribe an audio file with OpenAI Whisper: where to run it (online tool or local install), what settings to use (language, output format), and what to do if the audio has background noise or multiple speakers. Write it for a beginner using a phone or laptop, in under 200 words.
```

**Clean transcript output**

**How to use it:** Turn Whisper's raw output into a document.

```
Clean this raw transcript: [PASTE]. Fix obvious mis-heard words, split it into clear paragraphs, add speaker labels where the speaker changes, and remove filler words if the client wants a clean transcript. Show the cleaned version and list any spots you were unsure about. Format as a Word-ready document.
```

**Timestamps**

**How to use it:** For interview and focus-group transcripts.

```
Format this transcript with timestamps every [X] seconds in [MM:SS] format at the start of each paragraph. Keep the text unchanged, keep speaker labels, and make sure timestamps match the actual audio times where I noted them: [PASTE TRANSCRIPT].
```

**Verbatim vs clean**

**How to use it:** Two transcript styles, one prompt.

```
Take this transcript and give me two versions: (1) verbatim, keeping every word including hesitations and repeats; (2) clean, removing filler words and correcting grammar while keeping meaning. Label both. Transcript: [PASTE]
```

**Transcription summary**

**How to use it:** Clients often want a short summary too.

```
Write a one-page summary of this meeting transcript: main decisions, action items with owners, and deadlines. Use a clear structure with headings and keep it under 300 words. Transcript: [PASTE]
```

**Quote calculator**

**How to use it:** Price jobs in one message.

```
Help me price a job. Inputs: type ([TRANSLATION] with [NUMBER] words, or [TRANSCRIPTION] with [NUMBER] audio minutes), my rate ([KES] per word / per minute), and a minimum fee. Calculate the total, add a rush option (+25 percent), and write a one-message quote I can send to the client with my turnaround. Assume translation minimum KES 1,000.
```

**NGO pitch**

**How to use it:** Your first cold message to an NGO.

```
Write a short, respectful WhatsApp or email pitch to a small NGO in Kenya offering translation and transcription services. Mention: I translate English-Kiswahili and [LOCAL LANGUAGE], I transcribe interviews and meetings with clean formatting, rates start at [KES X] per word / [KES X] per audio minute, and I offer one free sample of [200] words or [10] minutes. Keep it under 120 words.
```

**Media pitch**

**How to use it:** Approach radio, TV, or digital outlets.

```
Write a pitch to a media house in Kenya offering transcription and subtitle support. Mention I transcribe interviews and segments cleanly with timestamps, translate between English and Kiswahili, deliver within [24/48] hours, and can start with a small paid test piece. Keep it professional, under 120 words, and end with a simple question.
```

**Service terms**

**How to use it:** One page that protects every job.

```
Write a one-page service terms note for my translation and transcription business. It must state: what I deliver (translation per word, transcription per audio minute with formatting), turnaround expectations, that legal and medical content will be flagged for professional review, payment via M-Pesa before or on delivery, one free revision, and that I keep client files confidential. Simple English, under 300 words.
```

**Quality checklist**

**How to use it:** Run this before every delivery.

```
Create a 8-item quality checklist for delivering translation and transcription work: check names, numbers and dates against the source; back-translate or re-listen to tricky parts; apply the glossary; run spelling and grammar in the target language; check formatting (paragraphs, speaker labels, timestamps); confirm the file opens; verify payment details unchanged; and note if professional review is needed. One line per item.
```

**Client log**

**How to use it:** Records that build your reputation.

```
Create a client job log table with columns: date, client, job type (translation/transcription), volume (words or minutes), price (KES), payment method and transaction ID, deadline, delivery date, testimonial/feedback. Explain how to maintain it in Google Sheets on a phone and why recording the M-Pesa transaction ID matters.
```

**Rate card and packages**

**How to use it:** Present your prices like a professional.

```
Design a simple rate card for my translation and transcription business in Kenya. Translation: per word (with a KES 1,000 minimum), Transcription: per audio minute (with rates for good vs poor audio), a rush delivery option (+25 percent), and a monthly retainer for a client who sends regular work (e.g. weekly transcription). Name each package and write one line for each in simple English.
```

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Delivering raw AI output without a human quality pass
- Changing names, numbers, dates, or payment details during work
- Charging per word without a minimum for short jobs
- Pricing transcription by listening hour instead of audio minute
- Promising rush deadlines you cannot keep
- Taking legal or medical work and presenting it as final
- Skipping records — untracked jobs become untrustworthy work
