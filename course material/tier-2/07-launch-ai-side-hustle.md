## Lesson 1: From idea to offer

An idea is not something you can sell. An offer is. An offer has a clear name, a clear customer, and a clear result.

Take "I can do virtual assistance" — that is an idea. "I handle your business WhatsApp messages and calendar for KES 3,000 a month, so you stop losing orders to slow replies" — that is an offer.

Use AI to turn your idea into a proper offer statement.

**Do this:** state who the offer is for and what they get at the end. If you cannot say it in one sentence, you are not ready to sell it yet.

**Not this:** selling "services" in general. "I do social media, typing, and data entry" attracts nobody. One specific offer attracts your first customers.

## Lesson 2: Package it — what's included, what's not

A package defines the boundaries of the work so both you and the client know what "done" means. It prevents scope creep — the client asking for extra work at no extra cost.

Decide:

- What is included (deliverables, revisions, timeline)
- What is not included (extra hours, weekends, extra revisions)
- How delivery happens (WhatsApp, email, Google Drive)

**Do this:** keep your first package small and clear — one service, one delivery, one price. Small packages are easier to deliver well, and well-delivered small jobs bring referrals.

**Not this:** promising "everything you need" with no limits. Vague packages lead to endless requests and unpaid work.

## Lesson 3: Price it — starter KES packages

Pricing is the part most beginners get wrong in both directions: they charge nothing out of fear, or they copy a big agency's price and get no buyers.

Start with a simple test price that covers your time and is easy to say yes to. For most phone-based services in Kenya, that means starter packages in the low hundreds to a few thousand shillings:

- KES 500 for a single small deliverable (one flyer, one week of captions, one transcript)
- KES 1,500-3,000 for a monthly package (weekly captions, monthly reports)
- KES 3,000+ for ongoing services (WhatsApp handling, content management)

Your first few sales are about proof, not profit. Raise prices once you have testimonials and repeat customers.

**Do this:** test your price by asking 10 potential customers "would you pay KES X for this?" — the validation habit from Tier 1 continues here.

**Not this:** announcing your price in a comment section without testing. And never underpricing so low that you cannot sustain the work.

## Lesson 4: Create your service page or catalog

You do not need a website to look professional. In Kenya, your storefront is often a WhatsApp Business catalog plus a one-page PDF or Canva flyer.

Set up:

- WhatsApp Business profile with your offer as the "About" text
- A catalog with 2-5 services, each with a name, price, and one-line description
- A one-page service sheet (Canva) you can send to any prospect

**Do this:** write the catalog descriptions with AI, then check the prices and details yourself. The catalog should answer price, delivery, and payment method before the customer asks.

**Not this:** a catalog with no prices. Customers asking "how much?" on every item take longer to convert, and some never ask at all.

## Lesson 5: Land your first 5 customers

Your first customers come from people who already know you: WhatsApp groups, Instagram, and referrals. Cold strangers are the last step, not the first.

The method:

1. List 10 people or businesses who could use your offer.
2. Message them with a warm, specific pitch — not a broadcast.
3. Offer a first-timer price or bonus to reduce risk.
4. Ask every happy customer to refer one person.
5. Post your offer once a week on WhatsApp Status and Instagram.

**Do this:** talk to at least 10 potential customers before you even start, using the pilot outreach prompt. Every conversation teaches you what to fix in your offer.

**Not this:** spamming the same message to every WhatsApp group. Five personal conversations beat fifty broadcast messages.

## Lesson 6: Get paid via M-Pesa — cleanly

Getting paid is simple: customers send money to your M-Pesa till or paybill, and you deliver. But "cleanly" is the skill — meaning you know who paid, for what, and when.

Decide your payment method:

- Till number (Lipa Na M-Pesa): best for solo sellers, easy to share
- Paybill: good if you want a business account and records
- STK push: works when integrated with a platform; more setup

**Do this:** ask for payment before or on delivery, confirm receipt, then deliver. Send a short receipt message with the amount and what it covers.

**Not this:** delivering first and chasing payment later with no record of what was promised. A simple receipt protects both sides.

## Lesson 7: Basic bookkeeping that takes 10 minutes a day

You cannot grow a hustle you cannot read. Ten minutes of bookkeeping a day tells you what earns and what bleeds money.

Keep one simple log with columns: date, customer, service, amount in, cost out, balance. A notebook works; a spreadsheet works better; a free app works best.

**Do this:** at the end of each month, ask AI to review your income and expenses and spot patterns — which service sold most, which customer type pays fastest.

**Not this:** using the same M-Pesa account for business and personal spending with no separation. Even a separate note on your phone is a start.

## Prompt Library

Copy, fill in the brackets, and adapt.

**Idea to offer**

Use to turn your idea into a sellable offer statement.

```
I want to start [service idea]. Turn it into a clear offer
statement: who it is for, the result they get, and the price
range. One sentence, Kenyan context, no fluff.
```

**Package design**

Use to define what is included in your first offer.

```
Design a starter package for my offer [offer]. List what is
included, what is NOT included, how many revisions, and how
delivery happens. Keep it small and clear.
```

**Pricing test**

Use to build confidence before setting your price.

```
I plan to charge KES [amount] for [service]. Is that fair for
Kenya? Give me a starter, standard, and premium price, and a
cheap way to test the price with real people this week.
```

**Service page copy**

Use to write your one-page service sheet or About section.

```
Write the copy for a one-page service sheet for [service].
Include the offer, what's included, price, delivery, and that
I accept M-Pesa. Friendly, professional, under 200 words.
```

**WhatsApp catalog lines**

Use to write product lines for your WhatsApp Business catalog.

```
Write 3 catalog entries for my service [service]: a name, a
2-line description, and a price in KES for each. Include
delivery details and M-Pesa payment. Keep them short.
```

**Pilot outreach**

Use to test the offer with 10 potential customers.

```
I am testing my offer [offer]. Write a short WhatsApp message
asking 10 potential customers if they would pay for it, without
sounding like a sales pitch. Keep it under 60 words.
```

**Warm outreach message**

Use to message the first people on your list.

```
Write a WhatsApp message offering [service] at KES [price] to
[a specific type of person/business]. Mention a first-timer
bonus and M-Pesa payment. Friendly, not pushy, under 80 words.
```

**Instagram announcement**

Use to announce your offer once on your page.

```
Write a short Instagram caption announcing my new service
[service] at [price]. Include what it is for, a call to action,
and 3 hashtags. No emojis.
```

**Referral request**

Use after a happy customer completes a job.

```
A customer just received [service] and was happy. Write a short
message asking them to refer one friend or business who might
need the same, and offer a small thank-you discount if they do.
```

**Payment reminder**

Use to nudge payment politely after delivery.

```
I delivered [service] to a customer. Write a short, polite
WhatsApp message confirming delivery and reminding them the
payment of KES [amount] is via till [number]. Warm, not pushy.
```

**Receipt message**

Use to send a clean record after payment.

```
Write a simple receipt message to send after I receive KES
[amount] for [service]. Include date, service, amount paid,
balance owed if any, and a thank-you line.
```

**Income and expense tracker**

Use to set up your bookkeeping.

```
Create a simple income and expense tracking table for my side
hustle. Columns: date, customer, service, amount in, cost out,
notes. Add a monthly total row and a tip on filling it in.
```

**Delivery checklist**

Use before you hand over any job.

```
Make me a delivery checklist for my service [service]: what to
send, quality checks, what to confirm with the client, and how
to close with a referral ask. Keep it to 8 steps.
```

**Monthly review**

Use at the end of every month.

```
Here are my monthly records: [paste income and expenses]. Review
them and tell me: which service earned most, which cost me most
time, and 3 things to change next month. Kenyan prices.
```

## Action plan

1. Write your one-sentence offer using the first prompt.
2. Define the starter package: included, not included, delivery.
3. Set a test price and confirm it with 10 potential customers.
4. Set up your service page and WhatsApp catalog.
5. Message your first 10 prospects with the warm outreach prompt.
6. Deliver your first jobs, send receipts, and ask for referrals.
7. Log every transaction and run a monthly review.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Selling a vague "service" instead of a specific offer
- Underpricing out of fear and burning out on cheap work
- Delivering before payment with no record
- Spamming offers instead of having personal conversations
- Mixing business and personal money with no separation
- Forgetting to ask happy customers for referrals
