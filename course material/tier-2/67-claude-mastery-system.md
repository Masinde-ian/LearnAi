## Lesson 1: From one-off chats to a Project system

Most people use Claude the same way every time: open a new chat, type a question, get an answer, close the tab. That is like using a calculator and forgetting it can remember your last calculation.

The upgrade is Projects. A Project is a dedicated workspace where Claude holds your files, instructions, and every conversation you have inside it. You set it up once and it stays ready.

Here is how to think about it: every recurring task in your life or business deserves its own Project. Not because Claude needs it — because you need the organisation.

Start by listing your recurring work: client proposals, weekly reports, social media content, school research, business planning. For each one, create a Project with clear instructions and upload the relevant files. After a week you will have a set of workspaces that cover most of your professional life.

The key rule: once a Project works twice, it is a system. Stop recreating things from scratch.

## Lesson 2: Building your first power Project

A power Project has four components: a clear role instruction, uploaded reference files, a set of example outputs, and a conversation history that Claude can draw from.

**Step 1: Set the role.** Tell Claude who it is in this Project. Not just "you are a helpful assistant" — give it specifics. "You are my business development partner. You know my business sells organic skincare products in Nairobi. You write in a warm, professional tone. All prices are in KES."

**Step 2: Upload reference files.** These are the documents Claude needs to do its job well. Your price list, past proposals, brand guidelines, customer testimonials, product descriptions. The more Claude knows about your actual business, the better every output.

**Step 3: Give example outputs.** Paste examples of writing you have done well — a proposal that won a client, an email that got a positive reply, a social post that performed well. Tell Claude: "This is my style. Match this tone and format."

**Step 4: Have a first conversation.** Ask Claude to produce something using the context you have given it. Review the output, give feedback, and let Claude learn your preferences within this Project.

A good power Project takes 15 minutes to set up and saves you 2-3 hours every week after that.

## Lesson 3: Long-document analysis — the killer feature

This is where Claude leaves other AI tools behind. You can paste an entire document — a 30-page contract, a research paper, a 50-message WhatsApp thread — and Claude will read every word and give you exactly what you need.

**Contracts and agreements.** Paste the full contract and ask Claude to summarise the key obligations, payment terms, deadlines, and any unusual clauses. Ask: "What should I negotiate?" or "What are the risks I might miss?" Claude will flag things a non-lawyer would overlook.

**Reports and research.** Paste a full report and ask for an executive summary, key findings, or a comparison with another document. Claude handles the length and gives you structured output.

**Meeting transcripts.** If you have a long meeting transcript (from Otter, Whisper, or any transcription tool), paste it into Claude and ask for clean minutes with decisions, action items, owners, and deadlines.

**WhatsApp threads.** Copy a long WhatsApp conversation with a client or supplier and ask Claude to extract the key agreements, outstanding questions, and next steps. This saves you from scrolling back through hundreds of messages.

The technique is always the same: paste the full text, then ask a specific question or request a specific format. The more specific your ask, the more useful the output.

## Lesson 4: Controlling Claude's writing style

One of Claude's strengths is that you can teach it to write in your voice. This matters when every email, proposal, or social post needs to sound like your business, not like a robot.

**Tone instructions.** In your Project instructions or at the start of a conversation, tell Claude the tone you want. "Write like a friendly professional — not too formal, not too casual. Avoid corporate jargon. Use short sentences."

**Format preferences.** Tell Claude how you want output structured. "Always use bullet points for proposals. Start emails with a greeting. End WhatsApp messages with a call to action."

**Word and length control.** Be specific about length. "Keep this under 100 words," "Write exactly 3 paragraphs," "Give me a one-page summary." Claude follows length instructions well.

**Vocabulary and language.** If your audience uses Sheng or Kiswahili mixed with English, tell Claude. "Write in a mix of English and light Kiswahili, like how young professionals in Nairobi speak on WhatsApp."

**Voice matching.** Paste something you have already written — an email, a social post, a proposal — and say: "Match this voice for the next output." Claude will adapt.

The goal is consistency. Once you set the style in a Project, every conversation inside it produces writing that sounds like you.

## Lesson 5: Research and structured note-taking

Claude is a fast research partner when you feed it the right material. It cannot browse the web on its own, but when you give it articles, reports, or notes, it organises, analyses, and synthesises better than any tool.

**The research workflow:**

1. Search the web for your topic using Google, Perplexity, or Gemini
2. Copy the key articles or findings
3. Paste them into Claude and ask for structured notes: key facts, conflicting claims, practical takeaways
4. Ask Claude to identify gaps — what do you still need to find out?
5. Go search for those specific gaps
6. Repeat until your research is complete

This workflow turns Claude into a research analyst that works with the raw material you provide.

**Structured note templates:**

- "Organise these notes into: Key Facts, Opinions, Unverified Claims, and Action Items"
- "Compare these two articles on [topic]. Where do they agree? Where do they conflict?"
- "Turn these research notes into a 1-page brief I can present to my team"

**For students and researchers:**

- Paste lecture notes and ask Claude to create study flashcards or practice questions
- Upload reading list summaries and ask Claude to compare methodologies
- Ask Claude to outline a research proposal with clear sections: problem, objectives, methodology, expected outcomes

## Lesson 6: Writing systems — emails, proposals, content

The real value of Claude is not in writing one email. It is in having a system that produces consistent, high-quality writing across every task you do regularly.

**Email system.** Create a Project called "Client Emails." Upload your typical email patterns — follow-ups, quotes, thank-yous, complaints. In the instructions, set your tone, your business context, and the common slots (customer name, product, price). Now every time you need an email, start a conversation in this Project with: "Draft an email to [name] about [topic]. Use the tone from our past emails."

**Proposal system.** Create a Project called "Proposals." Upload your best past proposals, your service descriptions, your pricing. Claude will learn your format and style. When a new lead comes in, start a conversation: "New lead: [client name], [what they need], [budget range]. Draft a proposal." You get a first draft in minutes that matches your existing style.

**Content system.** Create a Project called "Content." Upload your content calendar, past posts that performed well, and your brand guidelines. Claude can plan weekly content, write captions, create carousel text, or draft blog posts — all in your established voice.

**Reporting system.** Create a Project called "Weekly Reports." Upload your past reports and tell Claude the format. Each week, brain-dump your rough notes into a new conversation and ask for a clean report. The format stays consistent because the Project remembers.

## Lesson 7: Your weekly Claude routine

The system only works if you run it. Set a weekly rhythm: one focused block where you use Claude for the tasks that repeat.

**Monday morning (30 minutes):** Open your email Project and draft the week's client emails. Open your content Project and plan the week's posts. Open your report Project and update last week's summary.

**Midweek (15 minutes):** Check any long documents that arrived — contracts, proposals from clients, reports from your team. Paste them into the appropriate Project and extract the key points.

**Friday (15 minutes):** Run a weekly review in Claude. "Here is what I accomplished this week: [list]. What should I prioritise next week? Where am I spending time that could be automated?"

Over a month, review which Projects you actually use. Cut the ones that sit empty. Add a new Project whenever a task repeats a third time. That is the entire skill: turning repetition into Projects until your routine runs itself.

## Prompt Library

Copy these templates, fill the slots in brackets, and reuse. Test each one and adjust until it fits your exact work.

**Client proposal from template**

```
You are my business partner at [business name] in [town]. We provide
[service/product]. Write a proposal for [client name] who needs
[what they need]. Our price is [amount] KES. Include: what we
will deliver, the timeline, why we are the right pick, and a clear
call to action. Professional but warm tone. Under 300 words.
```

How to use: fill the slots with each new client. The Project's existing context keeps the style consistent.

**Long document summary**

```
I am pasting a [document type — contract, report, article]. Read the
entire document and give me:
1. A 5-bullet summary
2. The 3 most important points
3. Anything unusual or risky I should pay attention to
4. Recommended next steps
```

How to use: paste the full document after this prompt. Works for contracts, research papers, policy documents, and reports.

**Contract risk check**

```
I am about to sign this contract. Read it carefully and tell me:
1. What am I agreeing to deliver?
2. What are the payment terms and deadlines?
3. Are there any clauses that put me at risk?
4. What should I try to negotiate?
5. Any missing protections I should ask for?
```

How to use: paste the full contract. Claude reads every clause and flags risks you might miss.

**Meeting minutes from transcript**

```
Here is a transcript of a meeting. Produce one page of minutes with:
- Key decisions made
- Action items (with owner and deadline)
- Open questions needing follow-up
- Any commitments or promises made
Keep names and numbers exact.
```

How to use: paste the full transcript. Works with Otter, Whisper, or any transcription output.

**Batch email drafts**

```
I need to reply to these messages. For each, write a reply in my tone
— friendly but professional, under 80 words. Match the style of my
past emails in this Project:
[paste the messages]
```

How to use: paste all incoming messages at once and get drafts in one go.

**Weekly report builder**

```
Write my weekly report for [role/business]. Structure:
1. What was completed this week
2. Challenges or blockers
3. Plans for next week
Here are my rough points: [paste points]
```

How to use: brain-dump the week in bullets and get a structured report.

**Research plan**

```
I need to research [topic] for [purpose]. Break it into 5 focused
questions. For each question, tell me what kind of source to look
for and what keywords to search. Keep it practical and specific.
```

How to use: run this first, then search each question with Google or Perplexity.

**Summarise multiple sources**

```
Here are notes from 3 articles on [topic]. Extract:
1. Key facts from each article
2. Where the articles agree
3. Where they conflict
4. One practical takeaway per article
Flag anything that needs verification.
```

How to use: paste article notes and get a clean, structured comparison.

**Messy data to table**

```
Organise this into a table with columns: [list columns]. Fix obvious
typos in names, flag missing amounts, and keep the original order:
[paste data]
```

How to use: paste order lists, customer notes, or any messy text and get a clean table.

**Email triage**

```
Here are 10 emails I received today (summaries). Sort them into:
- Reply today (urgent)
- Reply this week
- Delegate to [person]
- Archive / ignore
One line of reason for each decision.
```

How to use: paste short summaries and get an instant priority list.

**Custom style instructions**

```
Based on these 3 examples of my writing, write a style guide I can
use as Claude instructions. Cover: tone, sentence length, word
choices to use and avoid, greeting style, and sign-off style:
[paste 3 writing examples]
```

How to use: paste the output into your Project instructions for consistent style.

**Proposal improvement**

```
Here is a proposal I sent that did not win the client. Analyse it:
what is weak, what is missing, and what would make it more
compelling. Then rewrite the improved version:
[paste proposal]
```

How to use: run on past proposals to continuously improve your winning rate.

**Task repetition check**

```
Here are my tasks this month: [list tasks]. Which ones repeat weekly
or monthly? For each, suggest a Claude Project setup with the files
I would upload and the instructions I would set.
```

How to use: run this monthly to grow your system with new Projects.

**Weekly review**

```
Here is what I accomplished this week: [list]. Here is what I
struggled with: [list]. Analyse my week: what should I keep doing,
what should I stop, and what one change would save me the most
time next week?
```

How to use: run every Friday to improve your system.

**Template improvement**

```
Here is a prompt I use and an output I did not like. Suggest specific
edits to the prompt so the next output fits me better:
[prompt] [bad output]
```

How to use: run whenever a template produces a weak result.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Using Claude as a one-shot chatbot instead of building Projects
- Writing every prompt from scratch instead of reusing templates
- Not uploading enough context — Claude works best when it knows your business
- Trusting AI output on contracts, legal, or financial matters without professional review
- Building Projects for tasks you barely do — focus on what repeats
- Pasting confidential or sensitive data into any AI chat
- Giving up after one weak output instead of refining the prompt and context
