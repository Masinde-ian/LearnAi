# AI Fitness Coaching: Build & Sell Online Programs

**Overview**

The Tier 1 course helped you generate a workout plan for yourself. This course builds a coaching business. You'll design structured multi-week programs for different client goals, use AI to personalize those programs for many clients at once, package and price an online coaching offer, and deliver it through WhatsApp with simple progress tracking. Help people get fit while you build a monthly income.

**Who this is for**

- Fitness trainers and gym instructors who want to coach clients online
- Boda boda riders, hustlers, and parents who train people informally
- Anyone with a certificate or strong practical experience who wants a paid side hustle
- Nutrition and wellness enthusiasts who want structure and a disclaimer-aware system

**What you'll be able to do**

- Design structured multi-week programs for weight loss, strength, and general fitness
- Use AI to personalize one master program for many clients at scale
- Package and price an online coaching offer with monthly KES plans
- Deliver programs via WhatsApp and track client progress with simple forms

## Lesson 1: From one plan to a coaching system

A plan tells someone what to do this week. A coaching system keeps them showing up for months — and pays you monthly.

**The coaching system has five parts:**

- **Intake** — knowing each client's goal, health, equipment, and schedule
- **Program** — a structured multi-week plan matched to the goal
- **Personalization** — tweaking the program for each client's reality
- **Delivery** — sending workouts and check-ins through WhatsApp
- **Tracking** — simple forms and sheets that show progress

You don't need to be a superhuman coach with infinite memory. You need a system. AI handles the drafting and the math; you bring the judgment, the motivation, and the accountability.

**Do this:** start with one goal — weight loss, strength, or general fitness — and build the system for that one first.

**Not this:** selling "custom everything" to 30 clients with no system. You'll burn out and clients will notice.

## Lesson 2: The intake — know your client

Every good program starts with the right questions. The intake collects everything you need:

- **Goal** — weight loss, strength, general fitness, or sport-specific
- **Health** — injuries, conditions, medications, doctor's restrictions
- **Experience** — beginner, intermediate, advanced
- **Equipment** — bodyweight only, dumbbells, gym membership, ropes and tyres
- **Schedule** — days and minutes available per week
- **Motivation** — why now, and what would make them quit

Build this as a Google Form or a structured WhatsApp questionnaire. AI drafts the questions; you add the professional judgment.

**The medical rule:** AI is not a doctor, and neither are you unless you are. Anyone with a serious condition or who's unsure should get medical clearance before starting. You'll build this into your intake and your terms.

**Do this:** refuse to program for a client who skips the health questions. That's not losing a sale; it's avoiding a lawsuit.

**Not this:** training through an injury or condition because the client "doesn't want to waste time."

## Lesson 3: Design structured multi-week programs

A program is a series of weeks with a direction, not a random list of exercises.

**The standard structure:**

- **Weeks 1–2 (foundation)** — learning technique, building the habit
- **Weeks 3–4 (progress)** — adding load, volume, or intensity
- **Weeks 5–6 (push)** — harder sessions, closer to the goal
- **Week 7 (recover)** — a lighter deload week to let the body catch up
- **Weeks 8 (assess)** — measure results and plan the next block

For weight loss: combine strength work with added cardio and clear nutrition guidance. For strength: fewer exercises, heavier loading, more rest. For general fitness: balanced full-body sessions anyone can do.

**Do this:** build one "master program" per goal, then personalize per client. That's the key to scale.

**Not this:** writing each workout fresh for each client. You'll drown. One master, many tweaks.

## Lesson 4: Personalize at scale with AI

Here's the magic of AI in coaching: you write one master program, and AI adapts it for each client in minutes.

**The personalization variables:**

- Available equipment (bodyweight vs gym)
- Days and minutes available
- Current level and experience
- Injuries and modifications needed
- Taste — what the client enjoys doing
- Load and rep ranges based on their strength

You give AI the master program plus a client's intake answers, and it produces their version: their sets, their weights, their schedule, their exercise swaps. You review before sending — the AI does the typing, you do the coaching.

**Do this:** keep a template prompt with your master program inside it, so personalizing any client is a two-minute job.

**Not this:** sending AI output unread. You must check every program for safety, sense, and the client's specific constraints.

## Lesson 5: Package and price online coaching

People pay monthly for results and accountability, not for PDFs. Package your coaching as a monthly service.

**Three offer levels:**

- **Starter (KES 1,000–2,500/month)** — a program and a weekly check-in
- **Standard (KES 2,500–5,000/month)** — personalized program, weekly check-ins, nutrition guidance, priority WhatsApp access
- **Premium (higher range)** — biweekly video calls, full check-in form reviews, adjustments every week

Price by your market and experience. New coaches start at the lower range and raise prices with testimonials and results.

**The delivery promise:** what the client gets each week, and how fast you reply. Write it down — a clear promise beats vague "I'll be there."

**Do this:** require a monthly commitment or a 4-week minimum so clients don't buy one week and vanish.

**Not this:** pricing from your effort instead of the value. A client getting real results will pay more than the cost of your time.

## Lesson 6: Deliver via WhatsApp

WhatsApp is the coaching channel that works in Kenya — clients already live there.

**The weekly delivery rhythm:**

- **Monday** — the week's program, their goals for the week
- **Check-ins** — clients send a photo, weight, or a thumbs-up; you reply fast
- **Wednesday** — a quick encouragement or adjustment
- **Friday/Saturday** — the weekly review: what worked, what to change
- **Payments** — M-Pesa paybill or till on a set date each month

Use WhatsApp Business: saved replies for common questions, labels for clients (new, active, at-risk), and status updates to show results and attract new clients.

**Do this:** set a daily reply window (e.g. 8am–8pm) and tell clients when you're available. Boundaries keep you sane.

**Not this:** promising "24/7 support" — it burns you out and sets false expectations.

## Lesson 7: Track progress with simple forms

Progress is what keeps clients paying. You must be able to show them change.

**The simple tracking system:**

- **Start and monthly check** — weight, waist, photos, push-ups, how they feel
- **Weekly check-in form** — a Google Form with 5 questions: workouts done, food, energy, sleep, and how motivated they feel
- **A Google Sheet per client** — one row per week, so progress is visible to both of you

AI reads the weekly form responses and writes the client a progress summary with one adjustment. You review and send it.

**Do this:** show clients their own numbers every month. "You were at 5 push-ups, now you're at 12" keeps them subscribed.

**Not this:** guessing progress from memory. Track numbers, not vibes.

## Prompt Library

Copy, fill in the blanks, and adapt.

**Intake form questions**

Use to build your intake.

```
Write an intake questionnaire for online fitness clients in Kenya. Cover: goal (weight loss, strength, general fitness), current weight/height, health conditions, injuries, current activity, equipment available (bodyweight, dumbbells, gym), days per week and minutes per session, and motivation. Include a medical clearance question and a disclaimer note. Format for a Google Form or a WhatsApp message.
```

**Master program for weight loss**

Use to build your first master program.

```
Design an 8-week weight loss master program for a beginner in Kenya with [bodyweight only / home dumbbells / gym access]. Structure: weeks 1-2 foundation, 3-4 progress, 5-6 push, 7 deload, 8 assessment. Include full-body workouts [X] days a week, added cardio, and a simple nutrition approach using affordable Kenyan foods (ugali, eggs, beans, sukuma, chicken). Use sets, reps, and rest times.
```

**Master program for strength**

Use for strength-focused clients.

```
Design an 8-week strength program for an intermediate trainee in Kenya with [equipment]. Focus on compound lifts: squats, push-ups, rows, and lunges. Structure with foundation, progression, peak, deload, and assessment weeks. Include how to add load each week and how to set rep ranges. Assume [X] training days per week.
```

**Personalize a program for a client**

Use the core scaling prompt.

```
Here is my master program: [paste master program]. Personalize it for this client using their intake: goal [X], experience [X], equipment [X], [X] days per week, [X] minutes per session, injuries [X], and they prefer [exercise taste]. Give me their full weekly program with their sets, reps, and any exercise swaps. Keep the structure of the master program.
```

**Modify for limited equipment**

Use when a client only has bodyweight.

```
This client has no equipment, only bodyweight, and lives in [town]. Adapt this week's program: [paste]. Replace all weighted exercises with bodyweight progressions of equal difficulty (e.g. push-up variations, single-leg squats, pike-ups) and keep the session under [X] minutes.
```

**Exercise swap for injury**

Use when a client reports pain.

```
A client with [injury/condition] reports pain doing [exercise]. Give me 3 safe alternative exercises that target the same muscle without aggravating the issue, with instructions to swap in. Note that if pain persists they should stop and see a professional.
```

**Weekly check-in form**

Use to build your tracking form.

```
Write a weekly check-in form for my online coaching clients with 5 questions: workouts completed (of the planned), how food went this week, energy level, sleep quality, and motivation (1-10). Add one optional space for notes or photos. Format for Google Forms and keep each question short enough to answer on a phone.
```

**Progress summary from check-ins**

Use weekly to close the loop.

```
Here are this client's weekly check-in answers for [week]: [paste]. Here's their starting data: [weight, measurements, reps]. Write a short progress message: what improved, one honest challenge, and one adjustment for next week. Keep it encouraging and specific, under 120 words.
```

**Nutrition guidance prompt**

Use alongside programs.

```
Give simple nutrition guidance for a client doing [weight loss/strength] in Kenya with a daily budget around KES [X]. Use affordable foods: ugali, rice, beans, eggs, omena, sukuma, cabbage, chicken. Suggest a rough daily plate structure, not a strict diet. Include a note that this is guidance, not medical advice.
```

**Weekly program delivery message**

Use every Monday.

```
Write the Monday delivery message for a client. Include: this week's program (paste it), the 2 focus points for the week, and a reminder of how to check in. Keep it warm and motivating, under 150 words, ready to send on WhatsApp.
```

**Mid-week encouragement**

Use Wednesday, to keep clients on track.

```
Write a short mid-week encouragement message for a client who [is on track / missed a session]. Keep it motivational, non-judgemental, and under 60 words. Ask a simple question to get them re-engaged for the rest of the week.
```

**Monthly results report**

Use once a month to show progress.

```
Here is my client's monthly data: start [weight, measurements, performance] and now [current values]. Write a monthly results report for them: what changed, the 2 wins of the month, and the 1 thing to focus on next month. Frame it positively and truthfully. Under 200 words.
```

**Coaching offer and pricing**

Use to package your service.

```
Help me package an online fitness coaching offer in Kenya. I coach [goal] clients. Build 3 monthly packages: Starter, Standard, Premium — with what's included each week (program, check-ins, nutrition guidance, WhatsApp access) and suggested KES price ranges for a new coach. Include a 4-week minimum rule and a clear weekly reply promise.
```

**Sales message for new clients**

Use on WhatsApp and Instagram.

```
Write a sales message for my online coaching program. Mention: who it's for, the goal, what's included weekly, the price per month, and how to start (fill the intake form). Give a version for a WhatsApp status under 50 words and a longer Instagram caption with 5 hashtags. No hype, just clear value.
```

**Medical disclaimer**

Use in your intake and program documents.

```
Write a clear, simple medical disclaimer for my online fitness coaching. It must say: the program is educational fitness guidance, not medical advice; clients with health conditions or concerns should get medical clearance before starting; and I am not responsible for injury resulting from improper execution or ignored conditions. Keep it plain and non-intimidating.
```

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Designing every workout from scratch instead of using a master program
- Sending AI programs unread without safety checks
- Coaching clients who haven't answered the health questions
- Promising 24/7 support and burning out
- Pricing by your time instead of the client's results
- Ignoring tracking — progress you can't show is progress clients can't see
