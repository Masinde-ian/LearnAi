# AI Legal & Admin Document Business

**Overview** — You built a starter pack of letters in the Tier 1 course. This course turns that into a paid document-drafting service for small businesses: contracts, receipts, demand letters, quotations, and HR letters — produced fast with AI and delivered on a repeatable system. The one clear win: your first paying document client this month, with clear rules on when a lawyer must step in.

**Who this is for**

- Anyone who completed the Tier 1 letter templates course
- Former secretaries, admin staff, and PA experience holders
- Small business owners who want a side income from an admin skill
- Freelancers looking for a service local businesses pay for repeatedly
- People who are careful with detail and like written work

**What you'll be able to do**

- Build a library of reusable business document templates
- Offer a document-drafting service to small businesses (per document or retainer)
- Draft contracts, receipts, demand letters, quotations, and HR letters fast
- Understand liability and refer clients to a lawyer at the right time
- Run ordering, delivery, and records entirely from your phone

## Lesson 1: What you are really selling

You are not selling legal advice. You are selling drafting, formatting, and speed — getting a business a professional document today instead of next week.

Small Kenyan businesses constantly need the same documents: a quotation to win a job, a receipt to record payment, a demand letter to recover money, an appointment letter for a new employee. Most owners write these badly or avoid them entirely. Your service fixes that.

Define the boundary in your mind: you draft and organise, the client approves, and a lawyer reviews anything binding. This keeps your service affordable and keeps you out of legal trouble.

## Lesson 2: Build your template library — the system

The heart of this business is a library you reuse daily. Build 10-15 templates first, each saved in Google Docs and in a numbered folder:

- Quotation and invoice
- Receipt (cash and M-Pesa)
- Demand letter
- Reminder letter
- Simple supply agreement
- Tenancy notice letter
- Offer/appointment letter
- Warning letter (draft only)
- Acknowledgement of deposit
- Simple non-disclosure (draft only)
- Service terms and conditions
- Client intake form

Generate each with AI, fill in your clean formatting, and add the line at the bottom of every draft: "Draft for review by a lawyer before signing or reliance." A library of 15 solid templates lets you deliver any new document in minutes.

## Lesson 3: Your service menu

Keep your menu small and clear so clients know what to order:

- Quotations and invoices — KES 300-800 each
- Receipts and acknowledgements — KES 200-500 each
- Demand and reminder letters — KES 500-1,000 each
- Simple agreements and letters of intent — KES 1,000-2,500 each
- HR letters (appointment, warning, reference) — KES 500-1,500 each
- Document pack (quotation + invoice + receipt set) — KES 1,500-3,000
- Monthly retainer: up to X documents per month — KES 5,000-15,000

Every menu item should carry the note "draft for lawyer review before use" where it applies. Clients value the speed and the clarity; they do not expect you to be a law firm.

## Lesson 4: The drafting workflow

A repeatable workflow keeps quality high and delivery fast:

1. Intake — collect the facts with your intake form (parties, amounts, dates, deadline)
2. Draft — generate with AI from your library, then personalise to the client's facts
3. Check — verify names, amounts, dates, and the client's M-Pesa details
4. Review note — add the lawyer-review line and flag anything risky
5. Deliver — send a clean PDF and an editable copy via WhatsApp
6. Record — log the client, document, price, payment, and delivery date

Never skip intake. A document built on wrong facts is worse than no document. If the client cannot give you clear facts, stop and ask before drafting.

## Lesson 5: Liability — know the line

This is the most important lesson in the course. You draft documents; you are not a lawyer and you are not giving legal advice.

- Anything binding (contracts, tenancy agreements, anything committing money or rights) must be reviewed by a lawyer before signing
- Land, property, and court matters — refer to a lawyer, full stop
- HR letters with serious consequences (termination) — draft with caution and clearly recommend review
- Never guarantee an outcome, never promise a document is "legally sound"

The Law Society of Kenya (LSK) has a directory and referral service — recommend it to clients for anything beyond routine documents. Your own service terms should state your role clearly: drafting and formatting, not legal advice. This one habit protects your whole business.

## Lesson 6: Pricing and packaging

Price for the value, not the minutes:

- Per document: charge KES 200-2,500 depending on the document type
- Packs: bundle the three documents a new business needs (quotation, invoice, receipt)
- Retainer: businesses that need monthly letters pay KES 5,000-15,000 per month
- Payment: M-Pesa till or paybill, and record the transaction ID on every receipt you issue for yourself

Start with per-document pricing while you learn which documents clients order most. Move to retainers once you have repeat clients — they smooth your income. Announce prices up front so there are no surprises; the retainer works best for businesses that send weekly documents.

## Lesson 7: Finding clients and marketing

Your clients are all around you:

- Small traders and shops that send quotations and invoices
- Landlords and agents who need tenancy and notice letters
- Startups and SMEs hiring their first employees (appointment letters)
- Fellow freelancers who need contracts and proposals
- Chamas and SACCOs that need receipts, minutes, and records

Market with value, not ads: post a free quotation template on your WhatsApp status, offer one free demand letter to a business you know is owed money, and ask every client to refer you. A document business grows by referral because "someone wrote this for me properly" is the best advert.

## Lesson 8: Running it from your phone

The whole business lives in four apps:

- WhatsApp Business — catalogue, orders, delivery, and status marketing
- Google Docs/Drive — your template library and client files
- M-Pesa — payments, with transaction IDs on every record
- Google Sheets — your client log: name, document, price, payment, delivery date

Keep one folder per client in Drive with their documents and a copy of your service terms. Send every draft with the review note and ask the client to confirm they received it. Small, consistent record-keeping is what lets you raise prices later with confidence.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Presenting yourself as a lawyer or promising legal outcomes
- Delivering binding contracts without a lawyer review note
- Skipping intake and drafting on wrong facts
- Copying templates from random websites that may be outdated
- Issuing receipts without M-Pesa transaction IDs
- Taking on land, property, or court documents — refer those
- Building a huge menu before you can deliver well on the basics
