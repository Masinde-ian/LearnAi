# Advanced ChatGPT Mastery: Automate Your Work Tasks

## Overview

You already know how to talk to ChatGPT. This course turns that into a repeatable system that handles your recurring work tasks — emails, research, reports, and meeting summaries — in a fraction of the time. By the end, you will have a personal automation toolkit you can run every week, not just once.

## Who this is for

- Office workers drowning in repetitive emails and reports
- Business owners doing admin on top of running the business
- Team leads and managers who write summaries and updates
- Anyone who found ChatGPT useful but wants it to work like a system

## What you'll be able to do

- Build reusable prompt templates for tasks you do every week
- Research any topic and get source-backed, useful notes
- Turn messy meeting notes into clean summaries with action items
- Use custom instructions and memory so ChatGPT knows your context
- Cut the time you spend on routine writing by half or more

## Lesson 1: From single prompts to a system

A single good prompt saves you five minutes. A system saves you every week. The difference is that a system is a template you run again and again, not a prompt you write from scratch each time.

Start by listing the tasks you repeat weekly: drafting the same type of email, writing a report, summarising a meeting, updating a price list, replying to customers. These recurring tasks are the raw material of your system.

For each one, ask: what does the input look like, and what should the output look like? The answer becomes your template — a fixed prompt with empty slots you fill in each time. Once a template works well twice, it is yours forever.

## Lesson 2: Your first repeatable templates

A strong template has the same four parts you already know: role, context, task, format. The trick is locking the fixed parts and leaving only the slots blank.

Take a weekly customer email. The fixed parts: your business, your tone, the general structure. The slots: the customer's name, the specific issue, the date.

Fill the slots each week, keep everything else identical, and the quality never drops. Save every working template in your phone notes or a simple document called "My Work Templates", organised by task. After two weeks you will have ten templates covering most of your week.

Test each template three times before trusting it. Small fixes to one line of a template — "add a delivery date" — often make it work far better for your exact situation.

## Lesson 3: Research that you can trust

ChatGPT is a fast research assistant, but it is not a search engine. For recent facts, use its browsing feature (available on free and paid tiers) or pair it with a search tool like Perplexity or Gemini, which show their sources.

The right workflow: ask ChatGPT to plan the research and break the topic into questions, then search for answers, then use ChatGPT to organise what you found. Give it the articles and let it extract what matters instead of asking it to "know" everything.

Always ask for a list of sources you can check. Treat any number, price, or legal claim as unverified until you confirm it. Research with AI should make you faster to verify, not faster to believe.

## Lesson 4: Reports and meeting summaries

This is where ChatGPT saves the most time. Feed it your meeting notes — typed, pasted from WhatsApp, or transcribed — and ask for clean minutes: decisions, action items, owners, and deadlines.

For voice meetings, record the audio (with permission) and transcribe it with a free tool like Otter or Whisper, then paste the transcript into ChatGPT. The summary prompt below turns forty minutes of talk into one page of decisions.

Keep the same summary template every week. Your team will learn the format, and reading the output gets faster each time. This one habit makes you look organised without doing the organising.

## Lesson 5: Everyday workflows — email and data organizing

ChatGPT shines on the small stuff that eats your day. Draft emails in one place, then paste and send. Batch several replies in one session so you are not writing all afternoon.

For data, ChatGPT is a clean-up tool: it turns messy text into tables, lines up names and amounts, and spots missing entries. Paste a customer list or order notes and ask for a table you can copy into your spreadsheet. It will not run your books, but it organises the mess before you do.

Keep a clipboard workflow: copy raw input, paste into ChatGPT with the template, copy the clean output, paste where it belongs. Simple, repeatable, and it never fails.

## Lesson 6: Custom instructions and memory

Custom instructions tell ChatGPT who you are once, so you stop repeating it in every prompt. Set your role, your business, your tone, and your default format preferences. From then on, every answer comes already adjusted for you.

The memory feature lets ChatGPT remember facts you save — your job, your clients, your usual signing-off style. Save a fact once and it applies in later chats. You control it: you can view, edit, or delete what it remembers, and you can turn memory off entirely.

Use custom instructions for your permanent context and memory for things that change slowly. For confidential or sensitive material, do not paste it into any AI chat — keep that off the tool entirely.

## Lesson 7: Your weekly automation routine

The system only pays off if you run it. Set a weekly rhythm: one block of time where you run the templates for the whole week. Monday morning, for example: draft the week's emails, summarise Friday's meeting notes, update the report, organise the week's orders.

The block replaces the daily scramble. You do the same template work, in one focused hour, with ChatGPT doing the heavy lifting. Over a month, review which templates you actually use, cut the ones that do nothing, and improve the ones that save the most time.

Your system should grow as your work does. Add a template whenever a task repeats a third time. That is the entire skill: turning repetition into templates until your routine runs itself.

## Prompt Library

Copy these templates, fill the slots in brackets, and reuse. Test each one and adjust until it fits your exact work.

**Weekly customer email template**

```
You are my admin assistant. I run a [type of business] in [town].
Write a warm, professional email to [customer name] about [topic].
Mention [one key detail]. End with a clear next step. Under 120 words.
```

How to use: fill the three slots each time you send one of these emails.

**Batch email replies**

```
Here are the messages I need to reply to today. For each, write a
short reply in my tone — friendly but professional. Keep each under
80 words: [paste the messages]
```

How to use: paste all incoming messages at once and get drafts in one go.

**Meeting minutes from notes**

```
Turn these rough meeting notes into clean minutes with three sections:
Decisions, Action Items (with owner and deadline), and Follow-ups.
Keep names and numbers exact: [paste notes]
```

How to use: paste typed or WhatsApp notes straight after a meeting.

**Meeting minutes from a transcript**

```
Here is a transcript of a meeting. Produce one page of minutes:
decisions made, action items with owners and deadlines, and anything
needing follow-up next week: [paste transcript]
```

How to use: pair with Otter or Whisper transcription for voice meetings.

**Weekly report builder**

```
Write my weekly report for [role/business]. Section one: what was
completed this week. Section two: challenges. Section three: plans
for next week. Here are my rough points: [paste points]
```

How to use: brain-dump the week in bullets and get a structured report.

**Research plan**

```
I need to research [topic] for [purpose]. Break it into 5 focused
questions, and for each question tell me what kind of source to look
for. Keep it practical.
```

How to use: run this first, then search each question with browsing or Gemini.

**Summarise sources**

```
Here are notes from 3 articles on [topic]. Extract the key facts,
the conflicting claims, and one line of practical takeaway per
article. Flag anything that needs verification: [paste notes]
```

How to use: paste extracted article notes and get a clean, sourced summary.

**Messy data to table**

```
Organise this into a table with columns: [list columns]. Fix obvious
typos in names, flag missing amounts, and keep the order I gave you:
[paste data]
```

How to use: paste an order list or customer notes and copy the clean table out.

**Email folder triage**

```
Here are 10 emails from my inbox (summaries). Sort them into: reply
today, reply this week, delegate, and archive. One line of reason each:
[paste summaries]
```

How to use: paste short summaries and get an instant triage list.

**Rewrite for clarity**

```
Rewrite this paragraph to be shorter and clearer, keeping every fact
intact. Aim for a reading level of a smart 15-year-old: [paste text]
```

How to use: run on any dense writing before sending it out.

**Custom instructions starter**

```
Based on this description of me, write custom instructions for ChatGPT:
role: [your role], business/work: [describe], tone I prefer: [tone],
format defaults: [e.g. short paragraphs, no fluff]. Keep it under 150 words.
```

How to use: paste the output into Settings, then Custom Instructions.

**Memory check**

```
List what you currently remember about me and my work, then suggest
3 facts I should add that would make your answers more useful.
```

How to use: run occasionally to review and tidy your memory.

**Task repetition check**

```
Here are my tasks this month: [list tasks]. Which ones repeat weekly
or monthly? For each, suggest a reusable prompt template and the
input I would need to give it each time.
```

How to use: run this monthly to grow your system with new templates.

**Template improvement**

```
Here is a prompt template I use and one example of an answer I did
not like. Suggest specific edits to the template so the next answer
fits me better: [template] [bad answer]
```

How to use: run whenever a template produces a weak result.

## Action plan

1. List the tasks you repeat weekly or monthly.
2. Pick the three most painful and build a template for each.
3. Test each template three times and fix the weak lines.
4. Set your custom instructions with your role and tone.
5. Save one useful fact to memory.
6. Run your templates in one weekly block this week.
7. Review after a month and cut or improve what you keep.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Writing every prompt from scratch instead of reusing templates
- Trusting AI research without checking sources
- Asking ChatGPT to "know" current facts instead of browsing or searching
- Pasting confidential or sensitive data into any AI chat
- Building templates for tasks you barely do — system for what repeats
- Giving up after one weak answer instead of improving the template
